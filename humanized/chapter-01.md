# Chapter One: The Countdown Starts

Just me now alone at WattsUp this late. I had worked a cloth over the same area of the laminated countertop, running it in a straight line with a steady pull, and it squeaked faintly.

The counter had already been spotless for an hour. Outside, the canopy lights continued to hum above the three empty charge bays, their cables dangling loosely from their enclosures, the tall pylon sign in the front yard continuing to cycle through ads against the totally black sky. Beyond the old gas pumps, dark at the opposite side of the parking lot, and beyond the row of small flags attached to the canopy's edge, Shibuya was continuing to do what Shibuya does at midnight; all of the signs were lit, and the crossing areas were still filled with people; a low thumping beat was carried faintly across the parking lot.

An electric sedan had entered the center charge bay a little past 11 PM. The driver of the vehicle, a man wearing a wrinkled suit, was thumbing his phone while it charged with a soft double chime. He never came inside. Hardly anyone did these days. The card readers in the bays did most of the work anyway. I could not recall when someone last fed gasoline into one of the ancient pumps, hoses coiled and dusty. And the motor cars were disappearing from Japan, just as vintage video games disappeared when Virtual Reality took over. WattsUp had quickly removed the old gas signs and called itself a charging chain. The last person I knew who continually refueled a pump was my dad. His hybrid still used gasoline, and the hand controls had worn smooth under his palms. I pushed that memory away to where memories resided and returned to the counter. I glanced upward at the fluorescent light tubes humming overhead and rotated my neck until I heard something pop.

The cash register drawer ticked in the silence. A number I could quote without looking. Gato was a good boss, and the position provided steady income. Steady paid for rent and grocery shopping and barely enough for everything else. I attempted not to do the additional math.

I saw myself reflected in the dark portion of the glass separating me from the outside of the store between the counter and the doorway. Locs pulled back from my face using an elastic that had become soft due to multiple uses. A couple of stray locs fell free from my temple regardless of how many times I tucked them back. The thin, pale scar on my forehead, directly above my right eyebrow, appeared nearly new despite being eleven years post-accident that caused it. I sported a thin goatee along my jawline. A Black face under the store lighting, my mother’s dark brown skin gone sallow, the tired showing mostly around my eyes. I had inherited my mother’s height (six-foot-three) of long legs and arms, but my father’s hands, steady and accurate, capable of fixing rather than breaking items. There were some nights that I spent working night shifts like this that seemed like part of a legacy I hadn’t earned yet.

My cell phone vibrated loudly against the counter as Chiyo’s name lit up the display screen.

[ Tabuchi called again. Told them as usual. The lawyers are dealing with it, so if anything comes up at the hotel, I’ll handle it. Also Tochi’s submission deadline is the 23rd and the entrance exams are January 4th, I’m going to need your answer ASAP Tatsu.]

I read it twice: once for the words themselves, and once for what she chose to leave unsaid. Chiyo referred to it as “ordinary” debt collection, just like always, and brought up attorneys as if it was nothing. At some point, however, I stopped believing it was truly ordinary. The repeated requests kept referencing my father’s previous documents specifically, in ways that never fully correlated with “just pay us.”

*"Dad would not want her fighting this battle for him."* My chest constricted. *"This was never hers to carry. Never was."*

I typed back a thumbs-up emoji, because a thumbs-up cost nothing, and invited no further questions I didn’t know how to respond to. Then I placed my phone face-down on the counter.

The display screen over the register displayed what it always displayed: HGO, and I had not selected it. For most of the day, it had been sitting at the corner of my eye, a bright rectangular box that I had taught myself not to stare at, displaying the same logo, same clips, same voices hawking the same product. The WattsUp Corporation had purchased a sponsorship space within Hero’s Glory Online, just as dozens of smaller companies were attempting to place their logos upon one of the largest titles currently available while it remained in closed beta, and the corporation’s stock price increased with the game each week since then. Thus corporate had instructed each display in every location to continuously run it. Gato ceased complaining about it roughly a month prior to this date. Most evenings that meant some replay or guild video played quietly behind me while I worked; and I had learned well to ignore it. Corporate boosted the volume on all displays throughout their stores tonight for their final battle of the closed beta, and it became increasingly difficult to rationalize why I should continue ignoring it.

Next Yuna’s bright voice emerged above the looping clip, riding the crowd noise, describing the current battle.

“Aogiri is trapped inside that fortress,” Yuna declared loudly enough for me to cease pretending I wasn’t paying attention, and thus began watching intently. “And the Knights of the London Spire will not relent!” She raised her pitch slightly as she rode up into the roar of the crowd. “To everyone just joining in, I am Yuna, and this is Season Zero’s last battle! Live from Singapore! The Knights possess 10,500 season points, Aogiri trails by 250, and winning this battle gives possession of Thornwatch for 300 points! Thornwatch is Lunarest’s newest zone in Beta, an old fortress where the king once dwelled, abandoned and destroyed in Lunarest’s uncharted north, and any guild may attempt to claim ownership. In order to reach Thornwatch you must navigate Thornwatch Forest, and whatever resides in Thornwatch Forest is far worse than any guild. Considering how close we are to the end of this season, if either team loses this battle they lose everything, with only two hours remaining on their countdown timer. Aogiri must win this battle, and the Knights merely have to survive, under a seventy-two hour death lock which eliminates anyone who dies during this battle for good. Three days have worn this battle down to twenty Knights versus twelve Aogiri!”

“And that is what makes Hero’s Glory Online unlike every other MMORPG out there!” Yuna continued as the crowd swelled beneath her voice. “Hero’s Glory Online is an open-world Sandbox VRMMORPG, so many parts of it are created by its players. All NPC characters are living, all quests and raids continue indefinitely, and this is still only our closed beta period, so there is so much more to come! This is also true of your route, though a path such as mine is extremely unique.”

“But look who they’re fighting for?” Yuna exclaimed as the crowd roared louder beneath her voice. “The Knights fight for Prince Dorrin’s banner, Aogiri fights for Prince Maldren’s banner, and Prince Ilyan is absent from this battle in Lunarest City. If you’ve lost track of your storyline due to becoming too deeply entrenched in your grind, here is a brief summary of events leading up to today. I’ve seen and visited many taverns,” Yuna stated. “With the king ill, the princes moved in separate directions. That’s why they employed many members from various guilds for their respective causes. Tonight marks the first occasion those guilds have clashed head-to-head over territorial control, with a flag awaiting planting and with neither prince present, near the edge of Lunarest’s map where those trailers hinted at further regions! It is the biggest world quest anyone has cracked open so far, nobody knows where it ends, and tonight it decides which team wins Season Zero! Thanks again HGO for allowing me to host tonight’s event! Thanks again to every single one of my fans who have listened to my songs this far! New song coming very soon!”

There was a great deal of data being presented tonight. I found myself staring at the screen approximately one step closer than I intended to be when gazing at it; I shrugged one shoulder until my neck popped; and then I returned to the counter with rag hanging from my hand. *"Princes and banners. Not my concern."* My gaze drifted back toward screen before I consciously decided to look away again.

The screen showed the remnants of an ancient fortress near the edge of a large, snow-covered forest. There were several broken walls and towers that surrounded crumbled homes; however, the only structure remaining was an old hall. On its steps a banner was flapping. The camera feeds were being produced by the orb cameras created by HGO. These small, clear balls floated inside Eryndal above the walls and towers to allow viewers to watch the battle from multiple viewpoints. Yuna called it from Singapore. The overlay below the video feed read **ZONE: CONTESTED**, and the WattsUp lightning symbol sat in the middle of the sponsor bar between two logos I didn’t know. I chuckled a little, then shifted my shoulder toward the screen, like that would make me stop looking. It didn’t.

“They’re utilizing great strategy,” Yuna said softly, quickly. “Twenty Knights are cloaked under enchantments and hiding in groups, each group positioned to cover the breach from different areas in the wall. Hit and fade. Hit and fade. This is true guerrilla warfare. Twenty Knights versus twelve Aogiri is actually the number that determines the success of the battle. Because no matter how many Aogiri reinforcements come in and try to get through that forest to that breach, they are kills. And for each kill, London gets six. The only area that Aogiri uses for their defensive line is the old hall where Lin Haoyu, their guild leader, sits with two officers and nine hands. Behind those walls, Aogiri’s twelve players are dying. And magic isn’t used for entertainment purposes in Eryndal, so don’t think that the glowing ward spells and fancy equipment are merely for show.”

My thumb slid across the counter’s edge. *"Hit and fade. Finally someone is doing something right over there."*

The twenty Knights of the London Spire took refuge in partially destroyed buildings and in the shadows of a fallen tower. They were not defending a location. They were waiting. A lone Spellforger wearing midnight blue entered the breach, and immediately died after receiving fireballs and bolts from three separate windows at the same time. Another member of Aogiri was out of commission for seventy-two hours, and with only two hours left to go, out for good.

Lin Haoyu, Aogiri’s guild leader, along with the eleven other members of his team, remained locked inside the old hall with their banner flying high on the steps, and Haoyu’s name listed on the overlay as the protector of the banner. He remained POISONED, slowly losing health every few seconds, and all of the Shadowveil players who could have slipped a cure into the hall through the narrow way in had died early. Edmund Vane, London’s guild leader himself, guarded their flag from a tent set back from the breach, with the only entrance blocked off. All the Knights needed to do was keep the breach bleeding and let the poison do its damage.

It was easy to understand why everything read so cleanly. It was the tactics. It had always been the tactics, which was part of why I loved this game so much, back when I played with Eren and we spent entire nights designing them on our tablets and argued over them during class. That was why I kept my back to it now. I gazed at the dark glass until my reflection became stable, then deliberately breathed.

*"Boneheads."* He could pass the flag off to someone else and head out with a runner via another route, or leave with the eleven members of his team while a fresh wave attacked from the opposite side of the breach, but passing it off would show on the overlay and London would swarm whoever was holding it. Both of his officers were DPS and would die if they simply sat on the steps. Simply sitting there was the only move that couldn’t win. The incoming reinforcements were no better, wasting their resources in the forest while feeding into the breach individually rather than en masse. The breach was London’s single weak link too, one entry point and one exit point, a design flaw my Dad would have caught right away, asking where the redundancy should have been placed. Each minute he stayed planted there cost him health from the poison, and he was responsible for protecting the flag. If he fell, the flag would fall.

Haoyu did not stir. He sat on the steps of the hall with his shield across his knees and watched his health drain away as he waited for a fresh wave of attackers to hit the breach.

My palms rested against the counter.

“Choose,” I whispered aloud to no one, low-toned, already aware of how this would conclude, and I loathed that I had spoken it. I turned my back on the screen and returned to cleaning the exact same spot I had cleaned twice tonight. “Break out or run. Do something.”

Sound erupted from the speakers behind me, a double-kill chime that caused a massive roar from the crowd, and I scowled as the volume surged upward. Then the noise changed. Something had occurred on the steps of the hall.

“He’s dead!” Yuna screamed above the deafening cheer of the crowd. “Haoyu, the guild leader of Aogiri, is down, and so is his banner! The Knights own the zone completely now and the ten-minute clock has begun!”

I did not turn to face it. I continued cleaning that same spot, a third time and then a fourth, as the roaring grew louder and the countdown continued, until one tone sounded low, signaling that a ten-minute hold timer had expired.

“The zone is secure,” Yuna shouted above the roar of sound. “Thornwatch belongs to London Spire! The flag for London Spire is being raised over those ruins! And a quest line is opening up for the Knights of the London Spire! Once they complete it, the points go on the board, London Spire takes Season Zero, and forty million yen goes to them in front of a sold-out crowd here in Singapore!”

I managed to turn around in time to see the camera orbs disengage from Haoyu’s lifeless body and rise toward London’s flag over the ruins as usual, and the feed switched to Yuna on a virtual stage waving at her audience. *"Tough, losing like that."* I felt absolutely nothing about it.

*"Forty million."* The thought went exactly where it usually went. *"Even a tenth of that pays for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly filling gaps she thinks I’m not paying attention to. No more Tabuchi calls."*

The broadcast didn’t switch to credits, it switched to a soundstage draped in silver and blue, and the gigantic cartoon bear strolled out for applause, both from the arena and faintly from Gato’s little news speaker behind his counter whenever he worked the front.

Kazuku. Nobody knew what his real face looked like. Only the bear, round-bellied and smiling, with HGO’s compass-rose logo stitched across a vest that always looked freshly pressed regardless of how many times he wore it on camera, stood on an animated stage using holographic projection technology Singapore had developed for years, his avatar as real as though he had walked out into the arena himself.

“Huge congratulations to London Spire,” Kazuku said, arms spread wide as if he wanted to enfold the entire arena, his voice warm and genuine in a way that always made me suspicious. “And big kudos to all the guilds who competed for that stage tonight. You’ve once again shown us why we compete.”

There was a pause long enough for the applause to swell and then recede.

“And now, since you’ve been so patient with me.”

Gold light emerged across the screen behind him, a map pattern I recognized instantly, before my brain caught up. Five separate continents where there had only ever been one.

“Eryndal is expanding,” Kazuku said, and a larger roar burst through the arena noise. “The Hearthfire expansion will launch January first. Five brand new continents will be unlocked, each with its own advantages for the people born there. You’ll build your own kingdoms and cities in a world where native residents notice who you are and exactly what you’ve accomplished so far. Gain their respect and trust, or fail to get either. And before any conflicts begin, there’s a final surprise waiting at the end of Open Beta, with even more of the map unfolding behind it.”

My weight shifted slightly forward, like I could lean against the screen enough to see the map more clearly if I just got close enough. Then I regained control and stepped back, and found my hand resting on the counter’s edge again.

Suddenly a very shiny gold-colored access drive appeared, so big that Kazuku had to be at least ten feet tall to hold it. It could easily be mistaken for an overly elaborate movie prop, but it worked because he was made of CG.

“Christmas Day,” Kazuku said, “is our largest day. We give away prizes worth millions, and anybody that has made a new player can enter.” The crowd roared in agreement. He slowly spun around to face the camera while holding up the gigantic access drive. “We are opening up Beta tonight. Everything you earn from the moment you log in will count toward your Season total. Each point is awarded individually, but everything is set up to accommodate guilds, so please come with your guild. If you’re brand new to the world of Eryndal or wanting to get involved in the new economy, bring your business. We’ve never given people this inexpensive a way into the system. For the next seventy-two hours only, each HGO access drive and each rig is discounted fifty percent. As soon as the expansion launches, all characters currently in closed beta will freeze at whatever level they are at now. Your Legacy Points remain accessible to either use or trade once you’re ready. When you start playing on a new character, your old character will be retired.”

The number flashed on the screen underneath him, plain white letters on the black background: **50% OFF - 72 HOURS ONLY**.

I checked twice, the same way I checked Chiyo’s messages, to verify that the sale really was fifty percent and that my tired brain wasn’t hallucinating some sort of hell for me.

*"Half price. For three days."* Math problems about money always worked themselves out right away. Enough? Just barely. And prizes worth millions in the game I had vowed not to play?

*"Millions?"* The last prize pool was forty million yen, and a tenth of that would have covered the surgery. *"If this comes even remotely close to being that type of money, then it's worth trying to get into this game."*

I brushed it off. *"Tonight is work. Tomorrow is work too. A sale is only a sale."*

I dug my thumbnail into the top of the counter until it went white. Above me, the overhead lights hummed. But my mind kept running the math anyway, a tiny, unyielding motor that simply refused to shut down. I turned it off.

*"I don't earn enough money here."* This job had its limitations and I had hit them six months ago. Chiyo was covering more than she should. Mom’s bills weren’t decreasing. The Tabuchis kept calling us no matter how many times we ignored them. If I didn’t do something, Chiyo was going to make the Tochi decision for me, and I’d be walking back into that school with my head hung low in shame. I took a breath through my teeth and gazed out at the rain beginning to streak the glass.

I wasn’t going to risk losing what little I had on a game I detested hearing about. I grabbed the rag again and told myself that was where it ended.

Red-gold headlights rapidly passed across the front window of the store, and my stomach dropped half a second before I knew why. A black car slowly and deliberately parked in front of the charging bays.

Through the windshield I could make out a figure in dark clothing, driving calmly and gripping the steering wheel firmly as the vehicle came almost to a complete stop.

First, the driver’s side door opened and blond hair caught the streetlight before anything else registered. The black team jacket was zipped all the way up to his neck, a silver lotus crossed with two staves embroidered on his chest, and the logo caught the light.

*"Eren."*

**[END CHAPTER ONE]**
