# Chapter One: The Countdown Starts

Just me now alone at WattsUp this late. I had worked a rag over the same area of the laminated countertop, running it in a straight line with a steady pull, and it squeaked faintly.

The counter had already been spotless for an hour. I’d swept the lot after the last rush, and the cold was still on my hands. Out there the canopy lights hummed above the three empty charge bays, their cables dangling loosely from their enclosures, the tall pylon sign in the front yard continuing to cycle through ads against the totally black sky. From the far side of the lot, beyond the old gas pumps and the row of small flags attached to the canopy's edge, I’d watched Shibuya do what Shibuya did at midnight; all of the signs were lit, and the crossing areas were still filled with people; a low thumping beat was carried faintly across the parking lot.

An electric sedan had entered the center charge bay a little past 11 PM. The driver of the vehicle, a man wearing a wrinkled suit, was thumbing his phone while it charged with a soft double chime. He never came inside. Hardly anyone did these days. The card readers in the bays did most of the work anyway. I could not recall when someone last fed gasoline into one of the ancient pumps, hoses coiled and dusty. And the motor cars were disappearing from Japan, just as vintage video games disappeared when Virtual Reality took over. WattsUp had quickly removed the old gas signs and called itself a charging chain. The last person I knew who continually refueled a pump was my dad. His hybrid still used gasoline, and the hand controls had worn smooth under his palms. I pushed that memory away to where memories resided and returned to the counter. I glanced upward at the fluorescent light tubes humming overhead and rotated my neck until I heard something pop.

The cash register drawer ticked in the silence. A number I could quote without looking. Gato was a good boss, and the position provided steady income. Steady paid for rent and grocery shopping and barely enough for everything else. I attempted not to do the additional math.

I saw myself reflected in the dark portion of the glass separating me from the outside of the store between the counter and the doorway. Locs pulled back from my face using an elastic that had become soft due to multiple uses. A couple of stray locs fell free from my temple regardless of how many times I tucked them back. The thin, pale scar on my forehead, directly above my right eyebrow, appeared nearly new despite being eleven years past the accident that caused it. I sported a thin goatee along my jawline. A Black face under the store lighting, my mother’s dark brown skin gone sallow, the tired showing mostly around my eyes. I had inherited my mother’s height (six-foot-three) of long legs and arms, and my father’s hands, steady and accurate, capable of fixing rather than breaking items. There were some nights that I spent working night shifts like this that seemed like part of a legacy I hadn’t earned yet.

My cell phone vibrated loudly against the counter as Chiyo’s name lit up the display screen.

[Tabuchi called again. Told them as usual. The lawyers are dealing with it, so if anything comes up at the hotel, I’ll handle it. Also Tochi’s submission deadline is the 23rd and the entrance exams are January 4th, I’m going to need your answer ASAP Tatsu.]

I read it twice: once for the words themselves, and once for what she chose to leave unsaid. Chiyo referred to it as “ordinary” debt collection, just like always, and brought up attorneys as if it was nothing. At some point, however, I stopped believing it was truly ordinary. The repeated requests kept referencing my father’s previous documents specifically, in ways that never fully correlated with “just pay us.”

*"Dad would not want her fighting this battle for him."* My chest constricted. *"This was never hers to carry. Never was."*

I typed back a thumbs-up emoji, because a thumbs-up cost nothing, and invited no further questions I didn’t know how to respond to. Then I placed my phone face-down on the counter.

The display screen over the register displayed what it always displayed: HGO, and I had not selected it. For most of the day, it had been sitting at the corner of my eye, a bright rectangular box that I had taught myself not to stare at, displaying the same logo, same clips, same voices hawking the same product. The WattsUp Corporation had purchased a sponsorship space within Hero’s Glory Online, just as dozens of smaller companies were attempting to place their logos upon one of the largest titles currently available while it remained in closed beta, and the corporation’s stock price increased with the game each week since then. Thus corporate had instructed each display in every location to continuously run it. Gato ceased complaining about it roughly a month prior to this date. Most evenings that meant some replay or guild video played quietly behind me while I worked; and I had learned well to ignore it. Corporate boosted the volume on all displays throughout their stores tonight for their final battle of the closed beta, and it became increasingly difficult to rationalize why I should continue ignoring it.

Next Yuna’s bright voice emerged above the looping clip, riding the crowd noise, describing the current battle.

“Aogiri is trapped inside that fortress,” Yuna declared loudly enough for me to cease pretending I wasn’t paying attention, and thus began watching intently. “And the Knights of the London Spire will not relent!” She raised her pitch slightly as she rode up into the roar of the crowd. “To everyone just joining in, I am Yuna, and this is Season Zero’s last battle! Live from Singapore! The Knights possess 10,500 season points, Aogiri trails by 250, and winning this battle gives possession of Thornwatch for 300 points! Thornwatch is Lunarest’s newest zone in Beta, an old fortress where the king once dwelled, abandoned and destroyed in Lunarest’s uncharted north, and any guild may attempt to claim ownership. In order to reach Thornwatch you must navigate Thornwatch Forest, and whatever resides in Thornwatch Forest is far worse than any guild. Considering how close we are to the end of this season, if either team loses this battle they lose everything, with only two hours remaining on their countdown timer. Aogiri must win this battle, and the Knights merely have to survive, under a seventy-two hour death lock which eliminates anyone who dies during this battle for good. Three days have worn this battle down to twenty Knights versus twelve Aogiri!”

“And that is what makes Hero’s Glory Online unlike every other MMORPG out there!” Yuna continued as the crowd swelled beneath her voice. “Hero’s Glory Online is an open-world Sandbox VRMMORPG, so many parts of it are created by its players. All NPC characters are living, all quests and raids continue indefinitely, and this is still only our closed beta period, so there is so much more to come! This is also true of your route, though a path such as mine is extremely unique.”

“But look who they’re fighting for?” Yuna exclaimed as the crowd roared louder beneath her voice. “The Knights fight for Prince Dorrin’s banner, Aogiri fights for Prince Maldren’s banner, and Prince Ilyan is absent from this battle in Lunarest City. If you’ve lost track of your storyline due to becoming too deeply entrenched in your grind, here is a brief summary of events leading up to today. I’ve seen and visited many taverns,” Yuna stated. “With the king ill, the princes moved in separate directions. That’s why they employed many members from various guilds for their respective causes. Tonight marks the first occasion those guilds have clashed head-to-head over territorial control, with a flag awaiting planting and with neither prince present, near the edge of Lunarest’s map where those trailers hinted at further regions! It is the biggest world quest anyone has cracked open so far, nobody knows where it ends, and tonight it decides which team wins Season Zero! Thanks again HGO for allowing me to host tonight’s event! Thanks again to every single one of my fans who have listened to my songs this far! New song coming very soon!”

There was a great deal of data being presented tonight. I found myself staring at the screen approximately one step closer than I intended to be when gazing at it; I shrugged one shoulder until my neck popped; and then I returned to the counter with the rag hanging from my hand. *"Princes and banners. Not my concern."* My gaze drifted back toward the screen before I consciously decided to look away again.

The screen was showing the remains of an old fortress at the border of a very large, snowy forest. Some broken walls and towers surrounded some smashed houses. However, there was only one structure still standing, the old hall. Above the steps of the hall there was a banner blowing. The video feed of the battle was coming from the orb cameras, also known as the floating camera balls that HGO built to float inside Eryndal and allow viewers to look at battles from various angles. Yuna was calling in live from Singapore. Below the video feed there was an overlay that read: **ZONE: CONTESTED**. The WattsUp symbol was in the middle of the sponsor bar and between two logos I did not recognize. I laughed a little bit, then leaned my shoulder toward the screen as if leaning my shoulder toward the screen would help me quit looking. It didn’t.

“They’re using really smart tactics,” Yuna said softly, quickly. “Twenty knights are cast with spells to hide and split into smaller groups, and each group is strategically placed to cover the breach from a different angle in the wall. Hit and Fade. Hit and Fade. This is pure guerrilla warfare. Twenty against twelve is the real number, because no matter how many Aogiri reinforcements come in and try to make it past that forest and attack that breach, they are going to be killed. And for each kill, London gets six. The only place that Aogiri can use to protect themselves is the old hall where Lin Haoyu, their guild leader, and two officers, and nine hands are. Behind those walls twelve Aogiri are going to be killed. And magic isn’t used to entertain in Eryndal, so don’t think that all of the glowingly spelled wards and fancy equipment are simply decorations.”

I ran my thumb along the edge of the counter. *"Hit and Fade. Finally someone is doing something right over there."*

The twenty Knights of the London Spire were hiding in half demolished buildings and behind a fallen tower’s shadow. They weren’t defending a position. They were waiting. A lone Spellforger wearing midnight blue walked through the breach and was immediately killed by a combination of fireballs and bolts shot out of three separate windows simultaneously. One of Aogiri’s players was out for seventy-two hours, and with only two hours remaining, out for good.

Aogiri’s guild leader, Lin Haoyu, and the rest of his team (eleven), were stuck inside the old hall with their banner on the steps and Haoyu’s name marked as the guardian of the banner. He was poisoned, losing health every few seconds, and all of the Shadowveil players who could have snuck a potion through the narrow doorway into the hall had died early. Edmund Vane, London’s guild leader himself, was watching over their flag from a tent located back from the breach. The entrance to the tent was sealed. As long as the Knights maintained the bleed from the breach and allowed the poison to take its toll, they would win.

It was simple to understand why everything seemed so clean-cut. It was all based on strategy. Strategy was one of the reasons I loved this game so much, back when Eren and I spent hours talking about them and arguing over them on our tablets late at night and between classes. That’s why I kept my back to it now. I stared at the black glass until my image stabilized, then purposefully breathed.

*"Boneheads."* He could give the flag off to someone else, then run with a runner through another route, or run with all eleven members of his team while a new wave attacked from the opposite side of the breach. But giving it to someone else would display on the overlay and London would rush whoever held it. His two officers were DPS players and would die if they simply sat on the stairs. Sitting there was the only move he could make that couldn’t win. Every minute he stayed planted there he lost health because of poisoning, and he was ultimately responsible for defending the flag. If he fell, so would the flag.

Haoyu did not budge. He sat on the steps of the old hall with his shield laid flat across his knees watching his health dwindle away as he waited for a new wave of attacking forces to reach the breach.

My palms rested against the counter.

“Pick,” I whispered aloud to no one, soft-spoken and already knowing how this would conclude, and I hated having said it. I turned my back on the screen and began cleaning the same spot I had cleaned twice tonight. “Either break out or run. Do something.”

Noise exploded from behind me in the form of a double-kill chime that prompted an enormous roar from the crowd, and I frowned as the volume swelled higher. Then suddenly the noise changed. Something happened on the steps of the old hall.

“He’s dead!” Yuna yelled loudly above the thunderous cheering of the crowd. “Haoyu, guild leader of Aogiri, is down and so is his banner! The Knights own this zone totally now and the ten-minute clock has started!”

I still didn’t turn to look at it. I continued cleaning that same spot a third time and then a fourth as the roars grew louder and the countdown continued until one note rang low indicating that a ten-minute hold timer had expired.

“The zone is secure,” Yuna yelled above the screaming of sound. “Thornwatch belongs to London Spire! The flag for London Spire is being raised over those ruins! And a questline is opening up for the Knights of London Spire! When they finish it, all points will be put on the board for London Spire winning Season Zero, and forty million yen will be paid to them here in front of a sold-out crowd in Singapore!”

I managed to turn around just in time to see one of the orb cameras float away from Haoyu’s lifeless body, rising toward London’s flag over the ruins as normal, then Yuna appeared on a virtual stage waving at her audience for TV cameras. *"Tough luck on that loss."* I felt absolutely zero emotion about it.

*"Forty million."* The thought went directly where it usually went. *"Even a tenth of that is enough to pay for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly filling holes she thinks I’m not paying attention to. No more Tabuchi phone calls."*

The broadcast didn’t transition straight into credits, it transitioned into a sound-stage covered in silver and blue colors, then a giant cartoon bear came walking out onto applause from both the arena and faintly audible from Gato’s little news speaker behind his counter when he worked up front.

Kazuku. No one knew what his real face looked like. Only the bear, round-bellied and smiling, with HGO’s compass rose logo sewn onto his vest that always appeared freshly ironed no matter how many times he wore it for TV shows, walked out onto a virtual stage projected with holographic technology that Singapore had developed for years.

His avatar was as realistic as if he had walked out onto an actual stage itself.

“Huge congratulations to London Spire,” Kazuku said with arms stretched wide as if trying to envelop the whole arena, speaking warmly and sincerely in a manner that always made me skeptical. “Big kudos to all teams competing for this stage tonight. You’ve proven once again why we compete.”

He paused long enough for applause to grow loud then fade.

“And now, since you’ve been so patient with me.”

Gold light flashed across the screen behind him, an outline I instantly recognized as a five-continent layout, before my mind realized what it was.

“Eryndal is expanding,” Kazuku stated, and a huge roar erupted from within the arena noise. “The Hearthfire expansion will be released January first. Five entirely new continents will be open for development, each continent having unique benefits for the people born there. You’ll develop your own kingdoms and cities in a world where natives notice you and your accomplishments thus far. Get them to respect and trust you, or fail at getting either. Before conflict starts, there will be a last surprise awaiting at the conclusion of Open Beta with even more of Eryndal’s map revealing itself beyond it.”

My center mass shifted slightly forward like I could lean against the screen hard enough to see that map clearer if I just got closer. Then I regained control over myself and moved back, finding my hand resting upon the counter’s edge once again.

Suddenly a very shiny gold-colored access drive appeared, so big that Kazuku had to be at least ten feet tall to hold it. It could easily be mistaken for an overly elaborate movie prop, but it worked because he was made of CG.

“Christmas Day,” Kazuku said, “is our largest day. We give away prizes worth millions, and anybody that has made a new player can enter.” The crowd roared in agreement. He slowly spun around to face the camera while holding up the gigantic access drive. “We are opening up Beta tonight. Everything you earn from the moment you log in will count toward your Season total. Each point is awarded individually, but everything is set up to accommodate guilds, so please come with your guild. If you’re brand new to the world of Eryndal or wanting to get involved in the new economy, bring your business. We’ve never given people this inexpensive a way into the system. For the next seventy-two hours only, each HGO access drive and each rig is discounted fifty percent. As soon as the expansion launches, all characters currently in closed beta will freeze at whatever level they are at now. Your Legacy Points remain accessible to either use or trade once you’re ready. When you start playing on a new character, your old character will be retired.”

The number flashed on the screen underneath him, plain white letters on the black background: **50% OFF - 72 HOURS ONLY**.

I checked twice, the same way I checked Chiyo’s messages, to verify that the sale really was fifty percent and that my tired brain wasn’t hallucinating some sort of hell for me.

*"Half price. For three days."* Math problems about money always worked themselves out right away. Enough? Just barely. And prizes worth millions in the game I had vowed not to play?

*"Millions?"* The last prize pool was forty million yen, and a tenth of that would have covered the surgery. *"If this comes even remotely close to being that type of money, then it's worth trying to get into this game."*

I brushed it off. *"Tonight is work. Tomorrow is work too. A sale is only a sale."*

I dug my thumbnail into the top of the counter until it went white. Above me, the overhead lights hummed. But my mind kept running the math anyway, a tiny, unyielding motor that simply refused to shut down. I turned it off.

*"I don't earn enough money here."* This job had its limitations and I had hit them six months ago. Chiyo was covering more than she should. Mom’s bills weren’t decreasing. The Tabuchis kept calling us no matter how many times we ignored them. If I didn’t do something, Chiyo was going to make the Tochi decision for me, and I’d be walking back into that school with my head hung low in shame. I took a breath through my teeth and gazed out at the rain beginning to streak the glass.

I wasn’t going to risk losing what little I had on a game I detested hearing about. I grabbed the rag again and told myself that was where it ended.

Red-gold headlights rapidly passed across the front window of the store, and my stomach dropped half a second before I knew why. A black car slowly and deliberately parked in front of the charging bays.

Through the windshield I could make out a figure in dark clothing, driving calmly and gripping the steering wheel firmly as the vehicle came almost to a complete stop.

First, the driver’s side door opened and blond hair caught the streetlight before anything else registered. The black team jacket was zipped all the way up to his neck, a silver lotus crossed with two staves embroidered on his chest, and the logo caught the light.

*"Eren."*

**[END CHAPTER ONE]**
