The screen was showing the remains of an old castle at the border of a very large, snowy forest. Some broken walls and towers surrounded some smashed houses. However, there was only one structure still standing, the old hall. Above the steps of the hall there was a banner blowing. The video feed of the battle was coming from the orb cameras, also known as the floating camera balls that HGO built to float inside Eryndal and allow viewers to look at battles from various angles. Yuna was calling in live from Singapore. Below the video feed there was an overlay that read: **ZONE: CONTESTED**. The WattsUp symbol was in the middle of the sponsor bar and between two logos I did not recognize. I laughed a little bit, then leaned my shoulder toward the screen as if leaning my shoulder toward the screen would help me quit looking. It didn’t.

“They’re using really smart tactics,” Yuna said softly, quickly. “Twenty knights are cast with spells to hide and split into smaller groups, and each group is strategically placed to cover the breach from a different angle in the wall. Hit and Fade. Hit and Fade. This is pure guerrilla warfare. Twenty against twelve is the real number, because no matter how many Aogiri reinforcements come in and try to make it past that forest and attack that breach, they are going to be killed. And for each kill, London gets six. The only place that Aogiri can use to protect themselves is the old hall where Lin Haoyu, their guild leader, and two officers, and nine hands are. Behind those walls twelve Aogiri are going to be killed. And magic isn’t used to entertain in Eryndal, so don’t think that all of the glowingly spelled wards and fancy equipment are simply decorations.”

I ran my thumb along the edge of the counter. *"Hit and Fade. Finally someone is doing something right over there."*

The twenty Knights of the London Spire were hiding in half demolished buildings and behind a fallen tower’s shadow. They weren’t defending a position. They were waiting. A lone Spellforger wearing midnight blue walked through the breach and was immediately killed by a combination of fireballs and bolts shot out of three separate windows simultaneously. One of Aogiri’s players was out for seventy-two hours, and with only two hours remaining, out for good.

Aogiri’s guild leader, Lin Haoyu, and the rest of his team (eleven), were stuck inside the old hall with their banner on the steps and Haoyu’s name marked as the guardian of the banner. He was poisoned, losing health every few seconds, and all of the Shadowveil players who could have snuck a potion through the narrow doorway into the hall had died early. Edmund Vane, London’s guild leader himself, was watching over their flag from a tent located back from the breach. The entrance to the tent was sealed. As long as the Knights maintained the bleed from the breach and allowed the poison to take its toll, they would win.

It was simple to understand why everything seemed so clean-cut. It was all based on strategy. Strategy was one of the reasons I enjoyed playing this game so much when I played with Eren. We spent hours talking about them and arguing over them on our tablets late at night and between classes. That’s why I kept my back to it now. I stared at the black glass until my image stabilized, then purposefully breathed.

*"Boneheads."* He could give the flag off to someone else, then run with a runner through another route, or run with all eleven members of his team while a new wave attacked from the opposite side of the breach. But giving it to someone else would display on the overlay and London would rush whoever held it. His two officers were DPS players and would die if they simply sat on the stairs. Sitting there was the only move he could make that wouldn’t get him ahead. Every minute he stayed planted there he lost health because of poisoning, and he was ultimately responsible for defending the flag. If he fell, so would the flag.

Haoyu did not budge. He sat on the steps of the old hall with his shield laid flat across his knees watching his health dwindle away as he waited for a new wave of attacking forces to reach the breach.

My palms rested against the counter.

“Pick,” I whispered aloud to no one, soft-spoken and already knowing how this would conclude, and I hated having said it. I turned my back on the screen and began cleaning the same spot I had cleaned twice tonight. “Either break out or run. Do something.”

Noise exploded from behind me in the form of a double-kill chime that prompted an enormous roar from the crowd, and I frowned as the volume swelled higher. Then suddenly there was silence followed by noise again. Something happened on the steps of the old hall.

“He’s dead!” Yuna yelled loudly above the thunderous cheering of the crowd. “Haoyu, guild leader of Aogiri, is down and so is his banner! The Knights own this zone totally now and the ten-minute clock has started!”

I still didn’t turn to look at it. I continued cleaning that same spot a third time and then a fourth as the roars grew louder and the countdown continued until one note rang low indicating that a ten-minute hold timer had expired.

“The zone is secure,” Yuna yelled above the screaming of sound. “Thornwatch belongs to London Spire! The flag for London Spire is being raised over those ruins! And a questline is opening up for the Knights of London Spire! When they finish it, all points will be put on the board for London Spire winning Season Zero, and forty million yen will be paid to them here in front of a sold-out crowd in Singapore!”

I managed to turn around just in time to see one of the orb cameras float away from Haoyu’s lifeless body, rising toward London’s flag over the ruins as normal, then Yuna appeared on a virtual stage waving at her audience for TV cameras. *"Tough luck on that loss."* I felt absolutely zero emotion about it.

*"Forty million."* The thought went directly where it usually went. *"Even a tenth of that is enough to pay for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly filling holes she thinks I’m not paying attention to. No more Tabuchi phone calls."*

The broadcast didn’t transition straight into credits, it transitioned into a sound-stage covered in silver and blue colors, then a giant cartoon bear came walking out onto applause from both the arena and faintly audible from Gato’s little news speaker behind his counter when he worked up front.

Kazuku. No one knew what his real face looked like. Only the bear, round-bellied and smiling, with HGO’s compass rose logo sewn onto his vest that always appeared freshly ironed no matter how many times he wore it for TV shows, walked out onto a virtual stage projected with holographic technology that Singapore had developed for years.

His avatar was as realistic as if he had walked out onto an actual stage itself.

“Huge congratulations to London Spire,” Kazuku said with arms stretched wide as if trying to envelop the whole arena, speaking warmly and sincerely in a manner that always made me skeptical. “Big kudos to all teams competing for this stage tonight. You’ve proven once again why we compete.”

He paused long enough for applause to grow loud then fade.

“And now, since you’ve been so patient with me.”

Gold light flashed across the screen behind him, an outline I instantly recognized as a five-continent layout, before my mind realized what it was.

“Eryndal is expanding,” Kazuku stated, and a huge roar erupted from within the arena noise. “The Hearthfire expansion will be released January first. Five entirely new continents will be open for development, each continent having unique benefits for the people born there. You’ll develop your own kingdoms and cities in a world where natives care about you and your accomplishments thus far. Get them to respect and trust you, or fail at getting either. Before conflict starts, there will be a last surprise awaiting at the conclusion of Open Beta with even more of Eryndal’s map revealing itself beyond it.”

My center mass shifted slightly forward like I could lean against the screen hard enough to see that map clearer if I just got closer. Then I regained control over myself and moved back, finding my hand resting upon the counter’s edge once again.
