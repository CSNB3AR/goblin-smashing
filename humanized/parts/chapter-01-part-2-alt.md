The screen showed what was left of an ancient fortress near the edge of a large snow-covered forest. Broken walls and towers surrounded crumbling homes, but only one building remained, an old hall. A banner flapped from its steps. The camera feed came from orb cameras, small clear balls floating in the air above the walls and towers to give viewers multiple views of the battle. Calling it from Singapore was Yuna. The overlay below the video feed read **ZONE: CONTESTED**. In the middle of the sponsor bar, the WattsUp lightning symbol sat between two other logos I didn’t recognize. I laughed a little at that, then shifted my shoulder toward the screen like doing so would make me stop looking. It didn’t.

“They’re using great strategy,” Yuna said softly and quickly. “Twenty Knights have enchantments cast over them so that they remain hidden in groups, placed strategically around the breach throughout the wall. Hit and fade. Hit and fade. This is true guerrilla warfare. Twenty Knights versus twelve Aogiri determines the success of the battle, no matter how many Aogiri reinforcements come through the forest to the breach, because the Knights kill them. For every kill, London gets six. The area where Aogiri holds its defensive line is the old hall, where Lin Haoyu, guild leader of Aogiri, sits with two officers and nine hands. Behind those walls, Aogiri’s twelve players are dying. Magic isn’t used for entertainment purposes in Eryndal, so the ward spells glow brightly and the fancy equipment isn’t just for show.”

My thumb slid across the counter’s edge. *"Finally, someone is doing something right over there."*

The twenty Knights of the London Spire waited in the shadow of the fallen tower and inside partially destroyed buildings. They weren’t defending a location. They were waiting. A lone Spellforger wearing midnight blue entered the breach and immediately died after receiving fireballs and bolts from three separate windows at once. One Aogiri member was out of commission for seventy-two hours, and with only two hours left to go, gone for good.

Lin Haoyu, guild leader of Aogiri, and the eleven remaining members of his team remained locked inside the old hall with their banner flying high on its steps, his name listed on the overlay as protector of the banner. He was losing health slowly, every few seconds, from poison. All the Shadowveil members who could have slipped a cure into the hall through the narrow way in had died early. Edmund Vane, guild leader of London, guarded their flag from a tent set back from the breach, its entrance blocked off. All the Knights needed to do was keep the breach bleeding and let the poison do its damage.

I hated how clean everything was. It was all tactics, the easy-to-understand kind, the reason I loved this game so much, back when Eren and I spent entire nights designing them on our tablets and argued about them during class. That was why I kept my back to it now. I looked at the dark glass until my reflection became stable, then deliberately breathed.

*"Boneheads."* He could pass the flag to someone else and leave with a runner through a different route, or leave with the eleven members of his team while a new wave attacked from the opposite side of the breach, but either move would be visible on the overlay, and London would swarm whoever held it. Both officers were DPS and would die sitting on the steps. Sitting there was the only play that couldn’t win. The incoming reinforcements were worse, wasting resources in the forest and feeding into the breach individually rather than as a group. The breach was London’s weak link too, a single entry point, the kind of design flaw Dad would have spotted right away, asking where the redundancy should have been placed. Each minute he stood in that spot cost him health from the poison, and he was responsible for protecting the flag. If he fell, the flag would fall.

Haoyu did not stir. He sat on the steps of the hall with his shield across his knees, watching his health drain away and waiting for the next round of attackers to hit the breach.

My palms rested against the counter again.

“Choose,” I whispered aloud to no one, low-toned, already aware of how this would end, and I loathed that I’d spoken it. I turned my back to the screen and returned to cleaning the same spot I’d cleaned twice tonight. “Run or break out. Do something.”

Noise came from the speakers behind me, a double-kill chime that caused a crowd roar loud enough to drown out the soundstage, and I scowled as the volume went up. Then the noise changed. Something had occurred on the steps of the hall.

“He’s dead!” Yuna screamed above the arena roar. “Haoyu, guild leader of Aogiri, is down, and so is his banner! The Knights own the zone completely now, and the ten-minute clock has started!”

I did not turn to face it. I continued cleaning the same spot, a third time and then a fourth, as the roaring got louder and the countdown continued, until a last tone signaled that a ten-minute hold timer had expired.

“The zone is secure,” Yuna shouted above the roar. “London Spire owns Thornwatch! The flag is going up over the ruins! A quest line is opening for the Knights of the London Spire! When the Knights complete it, the points go on the board, London Spire wins Season Zero, and forty million yen goes to London Spire before a sold-out crowd here in Singapore!”

I managed to turn around in time to see the orb cameras disengage from Haoyu’s lifeless body and rise toward London’s flag over the ruins as usual, and the broadcast switched to Yuna standing on a virtual stage waving at the audience. *"Tough. Losing bad."*

My thoughts went exactly where they usually did. *"Even a tenth pays for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly filling gaps she thinks I’m not paying attention to. No more Tabuchi calls."*

The broadcast didn’t switch to credits, it switched to a stage draped in silver and blue, and a giant cartoon bear walked out for applause from the arena and, faintly, from Gato’s little news speaker behind the counter whenever he worked the front.

Kazuku. No one knew what his real face looked like. Only the bear, round-bellied and smiling, with the HGO compass-rose logo sewn onto a vest that always looked pressed regardless of how many times he’d worn it on camera, stood on an animated stage created using holographic projection technology Singapore had developed years ago.

“Huge congratulations to London Spire,” Kazuku said. He spread his arms wide like he was trying to embrace the whole arena, his voice warm and genuine in a way that always made me suspect him. “Big kudos to all the guilds competing for the stage tonight. You’ve proven again why we compete.”

There was a pause long enough for the applause to swell and recede.

“And now, since you’ve been patient with me.”

A map pattern emerged in gold light across the screen behind him, and I recognized it instantly, before my brain caught up.

“Eryndal is expanding,” Kazuku said, and a bigger roar burst through the arena noise. “The Hearthfire expansion launches January first. Five brand new continents will be unlocked, each offering its own advantages to the people born there. You’ll build your own kingdoms and cities in a world where the native residents notice who you are and what you’ve accomplished so far. Earn their respect and trust, or fail to earn either. And before any conflicts begin, there’s a surprise waiting at the end of Open Beta, with additional portions of the map unfolding behind it.”

My weight shifted slightly forward, like I could lean against the screen and see the map clearly if I just got close enough. Then I regained control, stepped back, and found my hand on the counter’s edge again.
