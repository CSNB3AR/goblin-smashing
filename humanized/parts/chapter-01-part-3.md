A large, gleaming access drive with a gold finish came out of nowhere, an over-the-top prop, which was exactly what it was, and it only worked because Kazuku was ten feet tall and made of CG.

“Christmas Day,” Kazuku went on, “is our biggest event. We give away prizes worth millions, and anyone who has created a new player can enter.” The audience boomed with approval. He turned around slowly and held up the giant access drive. “We will open Beta tonight. Everything you earn from the time you log in will go toward the season. All points are earned individually, but all finishes are designed for guilds, so come with your guild. Whether you are entering the world of Eryndal for the first time or you want to get started in the new economy, bring your business. No one has ever offered such cheap entry to the system. For the next seventy-two hours only, every HGO access drive and every rig is fifty percent off. Once the expansion releases, all characters currently in Closed Beta will lock at whatever their current levels were. Your Legacy Points are still available to spend or exchange whenever you feel ready. When you move onto a new character, your previous one will be retired.”

The number flashed on the screen below him, simple white lettering on the black background: **50% OFF - 72 HOURS ONLY**.

I double-checked it, twice, the same way I would double-check Chiyo’s texts, to verify that the discount was actually fifty percent and not some hallucination my tired brain had created to torture me.

*"Half price. For three days."* Math problems involving money always got solved by me immediately. If I bought it right now, was there enough? Yes. Barely. And prizes worth millions in the game I swore I wouldn’t play.

*"Millions?"* The last prize pool was forty million yen, and a tenth of that would have paid for the surgery outright. *"If this is anywhere close to that amount, then it's worth trying to get into this."*

I shook it off. *"Tonight isn't it. Not this game. Tonight is work. Tomorrow is work too. A sale is only a sale."*

My thumb found the edge of the countertop and pressed into it until my nail went white, and overhead the lightbulbs buzzed. My mind kept running the numbers anyway, a small, unrelenting engine that never seemed to know when to stop. I stopped it.

*"I don't have a high-paying job here."* Gato was a good person, but this job had limits and I had reached them six months ago. Chiyo was paying more than she should. Mom’s bills weren’t decreasing. The Tabuchis wouldn’t stop calling us just because we kept ignoring them. If I didn’t do something, Chiyo was going to make the Tochi decision for me, and I’d be walking back into that school with my tail between my legs. I breathed through my teeth and watched the rain start to streak the glass.

I wasn’t going to risk what little I had on a game I couldn’t stand hearing. I picked up the rag again and told myself that was where things ended.

Red-gold headlights moved quickly across the front window, and my stomach fell half a second before I realized why. A black car pulled up to the curb in front of the charging bays, slowly and deliberately.

Through the windshield, I saw a shape in dark clothing, hands firm on the steering wheel as the car slowed to a stop.

The driver’s side door opened first, and blonde hair caught the streetlight before anything else registered. The black team jacket was zipped up to her neck, a silver lotus crossed with two staves embroidered on her chest, the logo catching the light.

*"Eren."*
