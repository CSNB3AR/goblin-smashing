# Goblin Smashing — Story Premise

A LitRPG / VRMMORPG / Deck-Building Progression Story
By STR_B3AR (Bobby)

## Genre

Primary: **LitRPG** (visible stats, systems, and progression the reader can track on the page).
Secondary/blended: **VRMMORPG** (full-dive pod/helmet hardware, real physical stakes to being "in" the game) and **Deck-building progression fantasy** (Chaotic-TCG-flavored inventory system, never named directly on the page — players collect gear and deploy their earned kit in guild battles, described functionally).

**This is not an esports story.** HGO (Hero's Glory Online) is a whole living world and a business opportunity, not a competitive circuit. Crownfall is a guild-only Siege event, the Season One opener for seasonal points, that introduces the Siege battle royale (5 guilds per session, 20 players each, camps that grow into forts, 60 lives, 3 hours). A Siege Pro League is an active option from the start of Season One that Kazuku mentions for players who want that route, and Node Wars (weeks and months) are the separate, larger war. Tatsuya stays on the open-world path and is not a fan of the game (`codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`). Keep that distinction clear at all times.

**Point of view:** first person, past tense, locked inside Tatsuya's head (the Slime effect). See `style-guide/first-person-voice.md`.

## Elevator Pitch

Tatsuya Kazehaya is a broke teenage dropout working a convenience store register in Tokyo, carrying his dead father's debt and his sick mother's hospital bills. He has spent a year avoiding Hero's Glory Online because it is the game he and the friend who betrayed him once theorized about. Then Kazuku's Hearthfire announcement lands: a 72-hour discount on drives, open beta, and a ten million yen Crownfall prize pool. He shrugs it off. Then a failing scooter sends him to his father's manual, a letter falls out, and it points him at the bear on the screen. That is why he buys in. A hardware glitch locks him into the weakest possible starting position, on a character he can never reset. Everyone assumes that's the end of his story before it started. It isn't. This is a slow, earned rise told from inside his head, built through relationships, his father's design logic, and refusal to quit, not power fantasy. HGO is a new world, and he is one of many people who will try to make it big there.

## Three Core Genre Pillars (never drift from these)

Overcomplicating the premise (real-world politics, doomsday economics, too many simultaneous plot engines) is the exact failure mode to avoid.

1. **LitRPG** — visible stats, systems, progression the reader can track.
2. **VRMMORPG** — full-dive pod/helmet hardware, real physical stakes to being "in" the game.
3. **Deck-building** — players collect items and gear through play and deploy their earned kit in guild battles. Your permanent, earned inventory is your deck, and what you bring into a battle matters as much as your class.

## Tone & Inspirations (Understand the DNA — Do Not Replicate)

1. **King's Avatar** (structure reference only): a gifted player forced to rebuild from nothing, with brilliance shown through knowledge and tactical thinking, not power fantasy. Borrow the slow, earned rise, not the pro-circuit setting.
2. **Overlord** — monster-as-protagonist angle, unusual starting position, slow build of influence and respect, loyal companions earned rather than assigned.
3. **That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime** — weakest possible starting creature, real growth through intelligence and relationships, companions as found family, warmth without losing real stakes, and the first-person interior voice the whole story is written in.
4. **Log Horizon / Sword Art Online** — the game world is alive, reactive, has real social and political weight. Not just a backdrop for fights.

**Governing principle:** King's Avatar's earned rise, inside a world with Overlord's scope and Slime's warmth, told in Slime's first-person interiority. The rise is chapter-by-chapter earned. Companions are built through relationship, never command.

## Story Arc Structure (high level)

- **Chapters 1–6+:** Tatsuya alone. Real world (Tochi enrollment, Gato's store, Chiyo and the twins). Game world (starts in Lunarest Outskirts as a goblin, later migrates toward Myrrhwood, learning to be a goblin, building companion relationships one at a time, never immediately commanding anyone).
- Leon's sister Arya enters slowly, multiple arcade visits before she ever speaks to Tatsuya. She researches him and watches his gameplay before approaching.
- **The technical arc:** Tatsuya learns at Tochi, applies the knowledge, keeps the first-gen pods running, and Leon's business grows as a direct result.
- **Late game:** Tatsuya discovers his father's connection to Kazuku, the bear mascot's true identity, and the full circle of his family's legacy.
