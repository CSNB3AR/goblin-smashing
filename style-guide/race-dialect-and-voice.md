# Race Dialect & Voice Guide

Companion to `style-guide/craft-rules.md` and `style-guide/voice-technique.md`. Reconciled against the actual 8 locked playable races (`codex/lore/playable-races-and-character-creation.md`): **Human, Elf, Orc, Beastfolk, Goblin, Dwarf, Frostborn, Drakekin.** Not a blind import of a generic fantasy dialect reference, adapted specifically to what's already locked in this codex.

## Core rules

1. **Player characters can break racial pattern.** Tatsuya (and any future reincarnated/transplanted player character) is not bound by the below, he talks like himself, a modern person dropped into a goblin body, not like a goblin.
2. **NPCs stay in their racial lane, always.** This is part of what makes the world read as lived-in rather than uniformly modern-voiced.
3. **This does not override `craft-rules.md`'s dialogue-cue rule.** A goblin speaking in short, blunt fragments still gets a paired action/posture/tone cue from the narration. The fragmentation lives in the character's own words, not in how the prose constructs the beat around them. "Big thing coming. We hide." still needs its own visual cue sentence attached, the same as any other line of dialogue.
4. **Roleplay-register requirement, ties to `codex/lore/npc-tag-system.md`:** metagame or gamer-slang language from a player (saying "XP," "loot," "buff," "meta" out loud to an NPC) doesn't register as meaningful input to any NPC and won't move Affiliation or unlock deeper NPC reactions. Speaking in a register the NPC's own worldview can actually process does. The already-drafted Chapter 8 negotiation between Tatsuya and Sevish is the worked example: Tatsuya wins Sevish over with an in-world tactical case (easy kills make raiders bolder, not more cautious), never with a gamey word like "XP."

## Goblin

Split by clan status, both drawing from the low base Wisdom the race carries (`codex/races/goblin.md`), the mechanical seed for why goblin speech reads as blunt rather than eloquent regardless of clan.

- **Thrash clan (not feral)** — the only kind with named individuals on the page so far (Sevish, Wyx, Ru, Kesh, Rott, Nab). Blunt and practical, slightly more coherent than feral goblins, but never philosophical or lecturing, no lore-dumping about their own history or politics unprompted. Street-smart, not eloquent. Already the working model in the existing Chapter 8/9 dialogue, e.g. Sevish's "Careful. You've worn Shiv's mark for an hour. Doesn't make you chieftain of this camp," direct, clipped, no wasted words, no abstraction.
- **Feral/other-clan goblins (not on the page yet)** — short, fragmented, low-Wisdom-coded sentences if ever voiced: simple nouns, immediate needs, repetition. "Big thing coming. We hide. Or we die." When this is written, it still needs a real visual cue paired with it per Core Rule 3.
- **What every goblin, feral or Thrash, already knows:** their own place in the world's hierarchy. See `craft-rules.md`'s new rule, no goblin expresses naive surprise at being called a monster, that's lived reality for them, not a discovery.

## Other races (forward-reference only, none on the page yet)

Light notes for when these races actually get dialogue, not exhaustive:

- **Dwarf** — gruff, grounded, hard consonants, short solid sentences. Values craft, clan, and plain honesty. Forge/stone metaphors come naturally. Ties to the goblin trade company's future Dwarf-crafter relationship (`codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).
- **Elf** — fluid, measured, can run longer sentences naturally without it reading as over-written for them specifically. Patient, observant, can land as mildly distant or unhurried to shorter-lived races without being rude about it.
- **Orc** — direct, blunt, respect-is-earned-through-action framing. Short, purposeful sentences, little patience for hedging or word games.
- **Beastfolk** — practical and direct, sensory-grounded (scent, sound, movement come up naturally in how they describe things), pack-oriented framing when relevant. Region-mate to Goblin in Myrrhwood (`codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`), so their voice should read as a genuine contrast to goblin bluntness, not a reskin of it.
- **Frostborn** — clipped, spare, low emotional color, endurance and practicality over flourish.
- **Human** — the baseline, most flexible, no special filter needed beyond ordinary characterization.
- **Drakekin** — lowest priority, an endgame/milestone race (`codex/races/drakekin.md`), not browsed at character creation and unlikely to get significant dialogue for a long while. If it ever does: formal, measured, lineage/honor-conscious framing fits the "dragon-blooded" concept without needing a fully worked-out dialect yet.
