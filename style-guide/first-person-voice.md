# First-Person Voice (Tatsuya's Head)

The story is first person, past tense, locked inside Tatsuya's head, the way *That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime* sits inside Rimuru. This file records how the chapters were converted from the author's third-person draft and what must not change. The third-person originals are archived in `chapters-third-person/` and in git history.

## The rule

Keep the author's sentences, rhythm, word choices and punctuation. Change only what first person forces, and tighten only where first person makes a line redundant or clunky. No new jokes, no new metaphors, no polish that reads like someone else.

## Mechanical conversion

- he / his / him / himself and "Tatsuya" become I / my / me / myself. "Tatsuya said" becomes "I said."
- Body description of himself becomes his own sensation. `his jaw set` becomes `my jaw set`. A line about his own skin or locs survives only when he would feel or notice it.
- Anything he cannot perceive is cut or rerouted through what he sees, hears and infers. `Something in her expression settled` stays, because he reads it. `She was nervous` becomes what he sees her doing.
- Inner thoughts stay in quotation marks and are also italicized, the author's format: `*"Damn, she has me cornered."*`. This supersedes the earlier plain-narration rule, which was a mistake. Direct thoughts (short, self-addressed, in his voice) take the marks; reflection and analysis stay narration.
- Dialogue stays in quotation marks. System text stays in bold brackets. Texts and on-screen written text stay in plain brackets.
- Letters and notes from Dad appear as bracketed written text (not quotation marks), with the narrator's reaction in the next paragraph. OPEN: Chapter 1 sets texts and Dad's letter in bold brackets, while this rule and Chapter 8 use plain brackets; not decided.
- Tense stays past. Present-tense thought becomes past (`That's not good` becomes `That wasn't good`).

## What the story changes, beyond mechanics

These were story-level fixes made during conversion, listed in `outline/first-person-conversion-log.md`:

- HGO is not an esports story. Tatsuya avoids it because it reminds him of Eren. No analysts, scrims, pro talk or meta talk.
- The WattsUp Corporation sponsors HGO, which is why the finale plays on the store screen.
- Hearthfire, Season Zero, Crownfall (the guild-only Siege event that opens Season One and introduces the Siege battle royale), the 72-hour lock, shillings, Legacy Points, Lumi, patrons and open beta are used everywhere. Kazuku mentions a pro league once as an option in Chapter 1, and Tatsuya stays on the open-world path.

## Return to the body

**Return to the body after information.** After any tactical, mechanical or information-heavy passage (roughly every two or three paragraphs of analysis, system text, exposition, or another character explaining something), bring Tatsuya back into his body with a small specific cue, not only after dialogue: rubbing his eyes, squinting at the fluorescent light, a wince, a loc falling or a hand raked through his hair, a brow or jaw change, hands flat on a surface, a shift of weight, a breath. In-game, use the goblin body (cold, claws, smaller frame). Vary the cues and do not repeat one more than a couple of times in a chapter. Check `style-guide/physical-expression-thesaurus.md` first.

## Never changes

- The author's rough-draft phrasings, including deliberate roughness such as `the Loop icon buried on top of each other with some HGO Notifications floating`.
- No em dashes. No floating quotes. One speaker per paragraph.
- Real-world pressure (Ohema, Chiyo, the twins, the Tabuchis) never turns off.
- No metagaming: he cannot know anything he has not seen, and the first-person lock makes this automatic.
