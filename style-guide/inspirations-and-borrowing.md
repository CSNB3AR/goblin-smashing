# Inspirations and the borrowing rule (author-stated)

HGO and the story are inspired heavily by three sources. We may pull ideas from them as long as we reword and reformat them into our own system: take the mechanic or the feeling, never the names, wording, or character-specific material.

1. **Albion Online (heaviest influence).** A sandbox with player-driven economy and crafting, full-loot risk, weapon-based abilities (you are what you carry), tiered gear and masteries earned by use, guild territory and warfare, and escape or last-stand items (the Phoenix Elixir is built from the assassin jacket's near-death escape). Use for: how abilities are tied to weapons and Ways, mastery by use, tiers, crafting and market, guild-scale conflict.
2. **Log Horizon.** A game turned into a living world with real people and NPCs, economy and social systems that reward thinking over raw power, guilds as institutions, organised raids, tactics and teamwork over solo brawn. Use for: tactics and systems thinking, guild and territory politics, NPCs treated as people, the pleasure of working out how the world's rules can be used.
3. **Kingdom (the manga).** Large-scale warfare, strategists, formations (shield lines, flanking, bait and trap), generals who read terrain. Tatsuya's tactics (a shield wall, the high ground, a hit from behind) come from this register and from the Thornwatch broadcast, kept in his own words and never as a lecture.

Rules:
- Reword and reformat. Same idea, our names, our numbers, our scene.
- Tatsuya's knowledge stays his own: Roman and Qin shield lines, Kingdom, the Thornwatch breach, nights spent building tactics with Eren. No metagaming, no lore dumps; the source is always named on the page or in his head.
- The martial layer (teachers, trust, masteries) follows the Albion and Kingdom feel: you are only as good as the weapon you trained, and rare arts come from a master.
- Genre label for pitching: LitRPG with a Kingdom-style tactics core, an Albion Online sandbox, and a Log Horizon world.
