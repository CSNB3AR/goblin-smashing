# Concrete Detail and Figurative Language

The author's standing rule for prose. It serves Tatsuya's voice and the author's writing, never the reverse. If a line is in doubt, it stays as written.

## The rule

- Keep the reader inside Tatsuya's physical experience of the scene. Default to concrete detail: what he sees, hears, smells, feels and does.
- Use metaphors and similes sparingly. Only when they come from his own world and knowledge (circuits and tolerances, the till and counter, game systems, volleyball) and make the scene clearer.
- Stay in his way of noticing: exits, numbers, mechanisms, hands, the cold of the goblin body, what a charging station clerk clocks first.

## Avoid

- Abstract labels for actions or states ("filling the time", "has nowhere else to be", "the weight of the moment").
- Metaphors from outside his world or knowledge.
- Stacking several comparisons in one paragraph.
- Figurative language that explains what a plain detail already shows.
- Filter words that distance the reader (felt, realized, seemed, noticed) when the sensory fact can stand alone.

## When revising

1. Remove any metaphor or abstract framing that adds no concrete information.
2. Check that the remaining details keep the reader in the physical scene.

The author's own example: the chapter 1 opening line about cleaning that "filled time more than it filled a need" becomes the repeated stroke, the spot that does not change, the sound of the rag; "glowing like it had somewhere better to be" becomes the actual lights, crowds and noise.

## Limits on every editor (protecting the voice)

1. Touch only lines with one of the flagged habits above. Everything else stays byte for byte.
2. Smallest possible edit: delete the figurative clause or swap the filter word before rewriting a sentence. Keep the author's sentence length, rhythm, slang, dry humor, asides and rough phrasing.
3. Keep Tatsuya's own comparisons that sound like him (sarcasm, tech and engineering, till and counter, game systems, volleyball).
4. Replacement detail must already be on the page nearby. No new facts, beats, jokes or metaphors.
5. Never touch dialogue, plain-narration thoughts the author wrote, Lumi and system text, the letter, sheets, notifications, names, numbers or paragraph order.
6. Roughly one change per 150 words at most. A paragraph that needs more than two edits is left and listed for the author.
7. Every change is logged in `outline/concrete-detail-pass-log.md` (chapter, BEFORE, AFTER, reason) so the author can reject any line.
