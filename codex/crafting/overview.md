# Crafting & Gathering — Overview

**Status:** New system, first built out to support Chapter 8's real-world money/progression stakes and the raw-materials layer gear and consumables draw from. Not yet dramatized on the page.

## Core framing

Crafting is a real, opportunity-cost lifestyle skill, not a free bonus system layered on top of combat. A character has the same finite discipline points everyone else has (see `codex/lore/five-disciplines-stat-system.md`); every point sunk into a crafting tree is a point not spent on Power, Wisdom, Speed, or any other combat stat. A genuinely top-tier crafter and a genuinely top-tier PvP/PvE combatant are two different, largely incompatible investments, consistent with how the rest of the stat system already works, this isn't a new exception carved out for crafting. Most players who go deep into crafting are running a second, dedicated economy-focused character or accepting a real combat ceiling on their main.

## Crafting Points (CP)

A separate resource pool from Discipline Points, gained through a flat, slow drip tied to level (roughly 1 CP every 2 character levels) plus a larger, front-loaded grant if a player commits to a crafting profession at all (an initial 10 CP investment "unlocks" the tree, a real, felt decision point, not a free dabble). This keeps crafting meaningfully separate from combat progression while still gating it behind genuine time investment.

## The crafting professions

See `codex/crafting/professions.md` for the full six-profession breakdown (Smithing, Bonecrafting/Leatherworking, Alchemy/Poisoncraft, Enchanting, Cooking, and the Spirit Magic-gated Runecrafting/Spirit-Binding path). One player can actively level two at once, a hard cap, consistent with the opportunity-cost framing above. Goblins start with a real, felt edge in Bonecrafting and a gadget-flavored offshoot of Alchemy (see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`'s Goblin Gas/Flare family), consistent with the race's low-Wisdom-high-Fortune profile: goblins lean on salvage and gadgetry rather than raw magical crafting, at least until the Spirit Magic path opens.

## Tiered like every other skill tree

Crafting professions use the same Tier 1-8 structure as magic and Physical Arts (see `codex/magic/overview.md`, `codex/combat/physical-arts-overview.md`), unlocked with CP instead of Discipline Points, gated by character level bands rather than a stat threshold. A Tier 1 smith can nail together basic Common-rarity gear. Tier 8 smithing is the domain of the handful of named crafters who actually supply top pro-scene rosters, a real, visible economic ceiling, not flavor text.

## Success rate, the actual skill expression

Crafting isn't a guaranteed result off the right materials. Every attempt rolls against a success chance built from three inputs: the crafter's tier relative to the recipe's tier (attempting above your tier is a real gamble, attempting well below it is closer to a guaranteed pass), the quality of the specific material batch used (see `codex/materials/overview.md` for material grades), and a flat baseline improved by repetition, in-world "muscle memory," the same recipe attempted enough times slowly raises the floor on failure. A failed attempt doesn't just waste time, it consumes the materials, real, felt risk on top of the opportunity cost already baked into spending CP here instead of elsewhere. This is what makes crafting read as a genuine craft in-world, not a vending machine.
