# Materials: Blacksmithing and Farm Resources, Book One

**Status:** Author-requested extension of the materials layer, forward material, not on the page. Every name, price and drop rate below is a proposal unless it cites an existing file. It sits on top of `codex/materials/overview.md` and the four existing material files, and it does not replace any of them. Elixirs, Ember cores and Ember crystals are covered elsewhere and are not repeated here (see Cross-references).

## What this file is for

The author wants Tatsuya to have a decent, steady supply of things to farm and craft with in Book One, with enough variety to feed blacksmithing, enchanting, armor, and his own trapper, bomber and brigand kit (gas, flares, traps, light armor). The supply is built from three zones that already exist in canon, plus one that the author's latest notes place near them:

1. **The Outskirts and Bloodhurst belt** (`codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`): cheap, reliable, goblin-friendly.
2. **The Thornwatch Forest**, with the old fort on its edge (`codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`): the high-level zone with mythical beasts and the rare crafter materials.
3. **The Thornwatch high ground and the Dwarven approach**: the mountain side of the forest, closer to Dwarven and Frostborn land than to Elven land. Mines, hard metals, and the only place a dwarf contact is plausible in Book One.

## Ground rules used in this file

### Tiers and grades are two separate scales

- **Rarity tier** (the ladder in `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`): **Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary, World.** World is reserved for World Quest rewards, so no farmable material here is World. The ceiling for a farmed material is Legendary.
- **Material grade** (`codex/materials/overview.md`): **Rough, Standard, Refined, Pristine.** Grade is how good this particular piece is. Tier is what kind of thing it is. A Pristine Common material is still a Common material.
- Per the existing rule, finished gear rarity follows the crafter's tier and the grade of the batch, not the material's tier alone (`codex/materials/monocrystals-and-mono-steel.md`).

### Price convention (proposal)

- Values are **per unit, Standard grade, in shillings**, the in-game currency (10 shillings = 1 HGO Coin = 4 yen, so 1 yen = 2.5 shillings). A "unit" is named in each row (a sheet, a coil, a fang, a chunk).
- **Grade multipliers (proposal):** Rough x0.5, Standard x1, Refined x2.5, Pristine x6.
- **Anchor:** a Minor Elixir is 21,000 shillings, which is 2,100 coins, or 8,400 yen. That price is inflated, because the open-beta market refresh stacked standing buy orders and it settles within about a week (`codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). So the elixir is treated as the top of the Rare band, not the middle of it.
- **Proposed bands per unit:** Common 20 to 400. Uncommon 400 to 3,000. Rare 3,000 to 20,000. Legendary 25,000 to 150,000.
- The existing monocrystal note says a small cluster pays for a day's supplies, which fits an Uncommon at roughly 2,000 shillings.
- Values are gross. The auction tax is steep in canon and is not set here (see Open and conflicts).

## How goblins farm and mine these

### What a goblin brings

- **Scavenger's Eye** (`codex/races/goblin.md`) flags which nearby loot is worth picking up, which saves time on salvage piles, ruin floors and corpses. It does not identify node quality.
- **Nimble Hands** adds a proficiency with arcane tech and gadgets. It is the reason a goblin can run crafted tools that other races cannot.
- **Fortune** is the highest base stat of any race. In this file, Fortune raises the **grade roll and the drop chance**, not the tier of a drop. A Common node cannot yield a Rare, but a lucky harvest can yield a Pristine Common. This extends the Lucky Craft idea in `codex/crafting/crafting-stations-and-process.md` to gathering.
- **Weak Power and a small mana pool.** A goblin harvests slowly by hand and cannot afford a mana-heavy method. His answer, as the archetype file already says, is tools, bombs and troops.

### Methods

1. **Trap lines (Zone C).** Set snares of Hookvine Cord and Skitterling Silk along a game trail, then return and collect. The trap is a physical object in the world. It has to be walked back to, and it can be found and robbed by anyone passing through the forest edge, so a trap line is a bet and not a free income.
2. **Poison first, fire second.** Goblin Gas stacks to four and a thrown Flare then ignites it, killing a pack of Class I to II beasts cheaply (`codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`). Proposal: **ignition scorches hides and bark, dropping a drop one grade.** A gas-only kill keeps the full grade. So a goblin farming hides chooses poison, and farming Saltpeter or Pine Pitch does not mind the fire.
3. **Smoke cover for guarded nodes.** Gravebell Pollen and Emberpine Resin nodes are guarded by beasts or watched by other parties. Smoke bombs from Smokeleaf let him harvest and leave.
4. **Blasting instead of swinging.** A goblin with Power 4 mines slowly with a pick. A small charge of Cinderflint and Garrison Saltpeter Crust opens a seam faster. It costs materials, but it fits the bomber build, and it makes mining a use of his own kit.
5. **Troops as gatherers.** Goblin companions could harvest while Tatsuya holds the line. Whether the troop system lets troops gather at all is not set (`codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`).
6. **Salvage and rival drops.** Scavenger's Eye on the loot of any party he ambushes. Rival crafters carry processed stock, so a kill can pay out a finished Hallmarked Steel Bar.
7. **Buying and trading.** Hallmarked Steel Bar and the dwarf-side materials are bought or earned. This is the natural first supply line for the goblin trade company (`codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`) and the reason a Dwarf contact is worth cultivating.

### Access limits

- Only Thrash-clan goblins can enter villages and settlements (`codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`). Lunarest carries bad infamy for goblins and dwarves. A goblin selling Thornwatch loot in a Lunarest town has to expect friction, which is part of why Arya's auction connection matters (`codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).
- Crossing the forest solo burns supplies. A Portable Campfire Kit is the goblin default (`codex/crafting/crafting-stations-and-process.md`) and covers basic recipes in the field.
- Open-world PvP applies, and the Thornwatch Forest is full loot, so a loaded inventory is a target.

### Book One access order

In the story's short window, with the Christmas Day deadline and a low-level goblin, Tatsuya realistically moves in this order: Zone A first, Zone C once he has gas, flares and a few allies, a short, risky look at Zone B during the Crownfall run-up, and Zone D only with a party. Zone E arrives mostly through trade and the dwarfling smith until he can reach it himself (`codex/subplots/tabuchi-takedown-and-kazuhaya-brand.md`). Fallstone Iron and Elderbeast Marrow are the Legendary prizes that stay mostly out of reach in Book One.

## How enchanting layers on top

Enchanting takes the finished base item and adds a bonus line on top of it. In Tatsuya's case this is **Rune Priming**: he primes runes onto terrain and hand-inscribes a rune insignia directly onto tools, gadgets and weapons ahead of combat. It is a separate prep step, costs mana to seat, and has a real failure chance that rises with insignia tier (`codex/subplots/tatsuya-trap-bomber-brigand-archetype.md`, `codex/crafting/overview.md`).

### The layers, in order (proposal)

1. **Base material and its hold.** Each material has a hold, which is how well it keeps an enchant instead of bleeding it. Iron and most hides hold poorly. **Fort Bronze Plate, Mountain Silver, Hollow Heartwood and Mistantler hold well.** Mono steel and Fallstone Iron hold best. Mono steel's reason for existing is exactly this (`codex/materials/monocrystals-and-mono-steel.md`).
2. **The smith's work.** The crafter's tier and the batch's grade decide quality, so a poor smith wastes a good material.
3. **Insignia seats.** Item rarity sets how many insignias it can seat: Common 0 or 1, Uncommon 1, Rare 2, Legendary 3. A seat holds one insignia.
4. **Reagents and catalysts.** Common Spirit Dust, Prism Dust and Echostone are the existing reagents for ordinary insignias. Mountain Silver wire and Mistantler powder raise the success chance. **Elderbeast Marrow is the catalyst for a Legendary-tier insignia**, and nothing else will seat one.
5. **Modular attachments.** Gadget belt slots and spirit attachments sit on top of the item as already established (`codex/objects/goblin-gadget-belt.md`, `codex/crafting/recipes-spirit-line.md`).

### Rules this respects

- **Seating costs MP once, at the crafting step.** Using the finished item still follows the established SP rule: an item with a magical augment draws SP at an accelerated rate (`codex/lore/battle-items-and-battlegear.md`). Tatsuya's full MP bar is 32 on the page, so a high-tier seating is out of his reach until he grows or drinks Minor Elixirs. That is a real, felt limit and an honest use of the elixir.
- **Resistances come from materials.** Barrow Bear Hide gives Toxin resistance, which is the exact debuff pattern the existing framework asks for (`codex/crafting/debuffs-and-resistances.md`). Clearquartz in a mask lets him stand near his own gas, which the sidearm entry already requires.
- **Enchants stack on allies and troops.** Troop enchants multiply and last longer than on players (`codex/subplots/tatsuya-trap-bomber-brigand-archetype.md`). Greywall Iron weapons and Thornhide armor for goblin troops are what that support build needs.
- **Dwarves cannot cast.** Dwarves are fully locked out of magic (`codex/races/dwarf.md`). A dwarf smith can build the best base but cannot seat a rune. A goblin enchanter who can is therefore a real partner, not a customer. That gives the dwarfling smith and Tatsuya a genuine reason to need each other.

### Worked examples

- **Lingering Gas Bomb (Uncommon).** Scrap Tin Sheet casing, Hollowreed fuse sleeve, Gas Sac, Sporecap Dust. One seat: **Linger**, which extends cloud time. Cheap, sells reliably.
- **Hookvine Snare (Uncommon).** Hookvine Cord, Skitterling Silk, Thornwood Boar Tusk spikes. One seat: **Cling**, a brief Slow on a trap trigger. A short, resistible effect, in line with the build's no-hard-CC rule.
- **Thornhide Vest (Rare).** Thornwood Bark backing, Thornback Scale plates, Barrow Bear Hide outer layer. Two seats: **Toxin Ward** against the first Poison stack, and **Hush Step** to dull footfall.

## Legendary schematics (concepts)

A **legendary schematic** is a found, Legendary-tier recipe. It is a physical object, which has to be walked over and touched before it becomes a card in inventory, like any loot (`codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`). Reading it teaches the recipe to that character and binds the recipe to them. It does not make the schematic usable by another player. A crafted piece carries the **maker's mark**, which is how Tatsuya brands a line under Dad's name (`codex/subplots/tabuchi-takedown-and-kazuhaya-brand.md`). Building the line takes the materials above, a smith past his own tier (which is why he hires people further along), and the Enchanting layer on top.

**The recipes are proposals, and the line's brand name is open.** Daichi's manual has blank pages Tatsuya fills with two items of his own (`outline/canon-ledger.md`), so each schematic below is something he could also record there once he understands it.

1. **Caustic Rig Pattern.**
   - **Makes:** the steampunk sidearm and back tank, with a seal-fit mask (`codex/lore/battle-items-and-battlegear.md`). Switches between Goblin Gas and Flare ammunition.
   - **Needs:** Clearquartz (mask lenses), Hollowreed Stalk (hoses), Barrow Bear Hide (tank harness), Hallmarked Steel Bar (tank and fittings), Emberpine Resin (flare ammunition).
   - **Found:** carried by a hunter NPC in a Thornwatch fort camp, part of a chain quest. Possibly a reward for clearing the fort's powder store. Needs London Spire's tolerance.

2. **Tripline Array Pattern.**
   - **Makes:** six linked snares on one trigger, a field kit that lays a trap line in one action. Fits the trapper side of the build and the early PvP lesson that he needs fast, mobile tools (`codex/subplots/tatsuya-trap-bomber-brigand-archetype.md`).
   - **Needs:** Hookvine Cord, Skitterling Silk, Thornwood Boar Tusk, Cinderflint triggers, Mountain Silver wire.
   - **Found:** a hidden-room drop in the Thornwatch fort ruins, guarded by Stonehide Golem-kin.

3. **Sentry Flare Cartridge Pattern.**
   - **Makes:** a line of flare cartridges for the Rune-Primed Flare Launcher (`codex/crafting/recipes-spirit-line.md`). Signal, ignition, and a slow-burn marker variant that lights a gas cloud at range.
   - **Needs:** Garrison Saltpeter Crust, Emberpine Resin, Pine Pitch, Cinderflint, Clearquartz.
   - **Found:** the old fort's armory, sealed. Whoever holds the fort holds the key.

4. **Thornhide Skirmisher Set Pattern.**
   - **Makes:** a light armor set: helm, chest, legs, boots and gauntlets. Quiet footfall, a first-stack Toxin ward, and a set bonus for gas users.

## Open and conflicts

### Where I followed the author over an existing file

- **Mountains and dwarves near Thornwatch.** Existing files put the mountains, the dwarven houses and the monocrystal beasts in Valefrost, on a continent that only opens with Hearthfire (`codex/materials/overview.md`, `codex/locations/ashenveil-forest.md`). The author's latest notes place a Dwarven kingdom and Frostborn land beside the Thornwatch Forest. I put Zone E there and let Rimeiron appear in thin seams. The Greyvast and wind monocrystals stay in Valefrost, and I did not move them. Rimeiron's home continent and whether Valefrost is still the main source are not decided.
- **Dwarf contact in Book One.** The existing docs treat the dwarven refining knowledge as house standing (`codex/materials/monocrystals-and-mono-steel.md`). Zone E assumes a contact is reachable during Book One. That follows the author's dwarfling smith near the mountains. It is not yet in the page timeline.

### Conflicts and gaps in the existing files (not edited)

- **"Epic" is used in four existing material files, but the rarity ladder has no Epic.** The ladder is Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary, World. The "Epic" tier appears in `codex/materials/organic.md` (Gas Sacs, Ancient Bone Shards), `codex/materials/inorganic-and-salvage.md` (Rune Stone Fragments), `codex/materials/magical-and-spirit-tied.md` (Spirit Shards, Legacy Crystals) and `codex/crafting/recipes-spirit-line.md` (Whispering Gas Orb). I used the ladder as written. A sweep should probably turn Epic into Legendary.
- **Two active professions is a hard cap** (`codex/crafting/overview.md`). The author wants Tatsuya in blacksmithing, enchanting, gas and bombs, and armor. That is four. Book One choice for him is most plausibly Enchanting plus the gadget branch of Alchemy, with Smithing and Leatherworking done by NPCs, the dwarfling, or hired workers. The tabuchi file already says he hires people further along (`codex/subplots/tabuchi-takedown-and-kazuhaya-brand.md`). Ruling needed.
- **Dwarfling as a troop or as a player** is flagged OPEN in the Tabuchi file and stays open. This file works either way, but the troop route raises the gathering question below.
- **Stale cross-references.** `codex/crafting/overview.md` points to `codex/crafting/buffs.md` and `codex/crafting/debuffs.md`. The real files are `food-and-consumables.md` and `debuffs-and-resistances.md`.
- **The Thornwatch elixir, Kazuhaya core and market documents** that the ledger says were requested from other agents are not in the repo, so nothing here duplicates them, and none of it is checked against them.

### Open questions

1. **Prices and the auction tax.** The tax is described as brutal but has no number. Should shilling values here be taken before or after tax? Are the tier bands and grade multipliers acceptable?
2. **London Spire and the fort.** Do they let anyone salvage the fort? A fee, a standing requirement, or a flat ban would change Zone B and schematics 1 to 3.
3. **Mythical beast names.** The Mist Stag, Glassfang Lynx and Barrow Bear are mine. The Crownfall raid has three mythical beasts (defense, cleansing, attack) that are unnamed. Should these be the same animals, or separate farm beasts, and does the raid beasts' loot belong to Legendary?
4. **Elderbeast Marrow rarity.** Is Legendary the right ceiling for a farmed material, with World kept for World Quest items only?
5. **Wisps.** Wisp materials are left out on purpose. Wisps are how elves open the gate to the Elven Empire, and a goblin crafting from them would collide with that. Confirm that they stay out.
6. **Passive trap lines.** Is it acceptable for a trap line to produce loot while Tatsuya is away, given that nothing in HGO is pure abstraction and the zone is full loot?
7. **Troops as gatherers.** Can goblin troops harvest, or only fight?
8. **Does the fire-scorch rule stand?** It makes poison better for hides and fire better for fuel, which feels right but changes Chapter 3 and 4 combat only in what drops.
9. **Schematic ownership.** Can Tatsuya license one pattern to an employee smith, and does the maker's mark stay his? Who is the "partner" that titles the Kazuhaya line with him?
10. **Class tiers for the new beasts.** Zone C and D creatures need class entries in `codex/creatures/` if they are kept. The Thornwood Boar is flagged as the forest cousin of a Sunmarch beast, which may be a placement conflict.

## Summary

This file gives Book One thirty-two named, priced materials in five source zones that run from Common slag at Bloodhurst to Legendary Fallstone Iron and Elderbeast Marrow on the Thornwatch high ground. It covers gas, flares, traps, light armor, blacksmithing and enchanting, shows how a goblin can farm and mine each zone with his own kit, and explains how Rune Priming layers on top of a smith's work. It proposes eight Legendary schematics that would let Tatsuya build and brand his own line, from the gas rig and trap array to the construct and the turret. It follows the author over older files where the mountains and dwarves sit beside Thornwatch, and it lists the conflicts it found without editing them.
