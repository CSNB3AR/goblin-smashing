# Materials & Resource Nodes — Overview

**Status:** New system, built to support `codex/crafting/overview.md`. Story-present Eryndal is one supercontinent with six regions (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`), not yet five separate continents, that split only lands once the Crystal League expansion fully launches. This file builds per-region for the current state of the world; once the expansion lands, each region below maps cleanly onto its own future continent without needing to be rebuilt from scratch.

## Material grades

Every raw material drops in one of four grades, independent of the item-rarity ladder (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`) but feeding directly into a crafted item's odds of landing at a given rarity: **Rough**, **Standard**, **Refined**, **Pristine**. A Pristine-grade material in the hands of a low-tier crafter still caps out at what their tier allows, grade and crafter tier both gate the outcome, neither one alone is enough for a top-end result.

## Per-region nodes

### Lunarest (central hub, human-dominated)
Common, foundational materials, reflecting the region's role as the game's starting hub. **Ironvein Ore** (base-tier smithing metal, the default starting material for any new smith), **Hearthroot** (a common cooking herb, mild stamina-restoring food buffs), **Common Spirit Dust** (a low-grade enchanting reagent, drops off nearly anything). Nothing here is exotic, which is the point, it's the material base every other region's specialties get compared against.

### Valefrost (north, Frostborn and Dwarves)
**Rimeiron** (a frost-veined ore, smithing material that carries an innate minor Frost resistance bonus into any weapon or armor piece made from it, ties to Book of Frost-affiliated gear), **Glacial Marrow** (a rare alchemy reagent harvested from Valefrost's native fauna, the base component for any real cold-resistance potion), **Frostbloom** (a cooking ingredient, grows only in permanent-frost conditions, food buffs built around it grant temporary Frost resistance rather than raw stat boosts).

### Myrrhwood (west, Beastfolk and Goblin clans)
Tatsuya's own operating territory, tied to the already-locked Myrrhwood advantage (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`). **Bonewood** (a dense, dark timber that reads as an actual crafting material rather than just scenery, favored for staves and Dark/spirit-affiliated gear, ties to Book of the Veil), **Ashroot Fungus** (an alchemy reagent, the toxin-family base ingredient behind Goblin Gas and its evolved variants, see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`), **Clanmark Ore** (a low-grade but stubbornly common metal most other regions treat as junk, exactly the kind of underrated resource that fits the Myrrhwood-advantage theme, cheap to acquire, quietly useful).

### Sunmarch (south, Orcs)
**Sunforged Iron** (a naturally high-heat-tolerant ore, favored for Book of Embers-affiliated gear and any weapon meant to carry a burn enchant well), **Redroot Pepper** (a cooking ingredient, food buffs skew toward temporary Power/Courage boosts, aggressive, front-loaded rather than sustained), **Cracked Bloodstone** (an alchemy reagent tied to raw-damage potions, the region's signature aggressive alchemical export).

### Wraith Isles (east, ranked arena and tournament zone)
Deliberately thin on raw gathering nodes, most of the zone's economy runs through the arena/tournament structure instead (see `codex/arena/overview.md`). What does exist skews toward enchanting: **Echostone** (a rare enchanting reagent, harvested from the arena zone's own ambient magical residue, favored for gear meant for competitive/ranked use specifically, an in-world reason competitive gear has its own distinct flavor).

### Azure Expanse (north peaks, Elves)
**Skyglass Ore** (a light, naturally Wisdom-friendly metal, favored for staves and any gear meant to support high-Wisdom casters, ties to Book of the Arcane), **Moonpetal** (a cooking ingredient, food buffs skew toward mana regeneration and Wisdom rather than physical stats), **Prism Dust** (the highest-grade common enchanting reagent in the game, the material serious enchanters actually farm for once they're past the early tiers).
