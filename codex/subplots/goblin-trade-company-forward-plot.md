# Subplot: The Goblin Trade Company

**Status:** Forward plot thread, not drafted yet.

## The plan

Later in the story, Tatsuya builds a goblin trade company in Myrrhwood, using goblin-clan access to local materials to craft artifacts, a real extension of his father's tech legacy and his own tinkering instinct carried into the game itself. He partners with Beastfolk there and eventually earns a standing relationship with a Dwarf NPC willing to craft items for him on a regular basis.

**First real product: a goblin-built turret**, see `codex/subplots/tatsuya-trap-bomber-brigand-archetype.md` for the balance niche (weak in ranked 5v5, strong in GvG Wars and PvE/base defense). Not part of his personal kit, a company product that takes real in-world development time to exist at all.

## Origin, confirmed: PK_Wraith's blacklist is the direct trigger

This doesn't start as an abstract business plan. It starts with a real, felt disgust at how guild-based extortion and open-world PK lawlessness actually work, landed on the page in Chapter 8: PK_Wraith's crew dies to Tatsuya's own ambush, and PK_Wraith's response isn't a fair rematch, it's an informal blacklist, leaning on every guild that answers to him to freeze T-Drag out of normal channels rather than competing straight (see `codex/characters/pk-wraith.md`). Tatsuya watches gear he legitimately earned become untouchable through the ordinary market purely because one player with enough reach decided it should be, no rules body, no recourse, just reputation and muscle. That's the seed: he wants a trade mechanism that runs on transparent rules instead of whoever's loudest or most connected, the same instinct that already defines how he thinks about tech and fairness in the real world (his father's legacy, his own read on the Tabuchis' leverage-based tactics, see `codex/subplots/tabuchi-schematics-and-family-debt.md`).

**Arya's auction-house connection is the practical bridge, not a separate idea.** Her guild's black-market/auction-house contact, offered in Chapter 8 as the immediate fix for moving the blacklisted loot safely (see `codex/subplots/aryas-loop-discovery-and-reputation.md` and `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md`), is Tatsuya's first real look at how an alternative channel could actually work at scale. He doesn't build the company off this one transaction, but it's the concrete proof-of-concept he keeps coming back to once he's established in Myrrhwood: a real, rules-based trade structure instead of PK_Wraith's model of hoarding leverage and freezing out anyone who embarrasses him.
