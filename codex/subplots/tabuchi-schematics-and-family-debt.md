# Subplot: The Tabuchi Schematics & Family Debt

**Status:** Active, ongoing since before Chapter 1.

## The mystery

Not just the outstanding debt. The Tabuchis believe Tatsuya has copies of the first-gen schematics, or something else of his father's that could let them fix their second-gen hardware and finally compete with Kazuku's third-gen tech. That's their real endgame. Tatsuya doesn't know this yet, he just notices the questions don't add up to only being about money. See `codex/characters/kazuto-tabuchi.md` and `codex/characters/daichi-kazehaya.md`.

## Dad's letter

Daichi's letter in the manual says the Tabuchis pushed him hard to sell the business and he had not, that the manual holds what they need, and that Tatsuya should not give it to them and should speak to the bear (text in `codex/characters/daichi-kazehaya.md`). It gives Tatsuya enough to know the Tabuchis are behind the pressure, but not proof. Only Kazuku holds proof, which is what steers Tatsuya into HGO.

## What's known and by whom

Kazuto's one direct attempt at Tatsuya happened once, at Daichi's funeral, framed as help. Tatsuya refused and has had zero contact with the Tabuchis since, that refusal, not a warning from Chiyo, is why. This is Tatsuya's private knowledge, Chiyo doesn't know it, she treats it as debt collection and has hired lawyers. The debt pressure that shows up in scenes like Eren's WattsUp visits (see `chapters/chapter-01.md`) is what's escalated in place of that direct contact ever since. Tatsuya doesn't yet know why the demands keep circling back to his father's old paperwork specifically, only that it never fully squares with "just pay us."

## Escalation levers available going forward

- Eren personally leaning on Tatsuya (already active, Chapter 1).
- The Tabuchis' threat of arson against the Nakamura Grand (established, not yet deployed on the page).
- Chiyo's own protective ultimatum about the Tochi decision, tangential but related pressure (see `codex/subplots/tochi-academy-return.md`).
- Tabuchi complicity in Daichi's death and the collapse before it, confirmed as an active, intended direction. **Author-side truth:** see the section below. Reaches the reader only through Tatsuya's own inference (he is **consciously, privately suspicious**), never a direct reveal scene or confession. The exact mechanism of death stays unstated on the page even once this lands, it's a live suspicion, not a solved mystery. Eren is blind to it and acts only as Kazuto's messenger. Dad's manual carries a hidden letter (see `codex/characters/daichi-kazehaya.md`).

## Author-side truth, updated (Haya Sedan, yakuza, Chiyo, lawyers)

Stays suspicion on the page, never confirmed. There are **two separate accidents**: the childhood crash about eleven years ago (Tatsuya's scar, Daichi paralyzed, cause unstated) and the fatal accident about a year ago, months before HGO and the third-gen pods released. Daichi's own car, the **Haya Sedan**, was a **hybrid that still ran on gas** (only the Haya EB scooter is fully electric). The Tabuchis **hired yakuza to sabotage it** by pulling the plug-ins; it broke down and caused the accident that **killed Daichi**, and the **Tabuchis used it as a way to attack the business**, so people pulled every Haya product from the market. They expected it to bankrupt the family while Ohema was in the hospital. **Chiyo returning was not in their plans**: she began paying the fees. Kazuto hoped to take the family to court and win the **business and the first-gen pod schematics** that way, and it has not worked because **Chiyo has her own money**. She does not want to pay them and has **hired lawyers**, which is why her text in Chapter 1 says they called again and that she is handling it. **All Haya items were pulled from the market, the EB included**, which is why money came in thin. Chiyo's own money has gone into **bills and Ohema's care**, which is a second reason she is not paying the Tabuchis. She **does not know the details** of the sabotage, but she is a **yakuza daughter** (see `codex/characters/chiyo-nakamura.md`) and wants them to pay for what they did to her family in her own right, so her refusal is personal as well as practical.

## The debt, in figures (locked)

- Ohema's surgery costs roughly **¥4,000,000**, formalizing the math Tatsuya already runs in his own head in `chapters/chapter-01.md` ("forty million... a tenth of that pays for the surgery outright").
- The Tabuchi debt itself is roughly **¥3,000,000**.
- Combined, the family owes roughly **¥7,000,000**.

## Daichi's timeline, clarified

While Daichi and Kazuku were building the third-gen pods, before Daichi died, he **handed off some notes** to Kazuku as a partner. That stake **would have paid off Ohema's debt**, but he was killed in the sabotaged accident before it paid out, which is another reason his letter says to find the bear: Kazuku owes the family and holds what Daichi gave. He did not hand anything to the Tabuchis, and he refused them even when it might have cleared the debt. He didn't ask Ohema's side of the family for help, an act of the same self-reliant pride that defines Tatsuya's own characterization, he handled it himself. The money that did exist ran out over time, which is the real reason Chiyo has been personally absorbing hospital and rent costs since (cutting hotel staff, taking shifts herself, see `codex/characters/chiyo-nakamura.md`), not a sudden gap, a slow one.

## Why the pressure keeps escalating

The Tabuchis' current push is partly a legal-collections angle layered over their real motive: the debt survives Daichi's death the same way any loan does, giving them a legitimate-sounding reason to keep contacting the family regardless of what they're actually after. They're also under real financial pressure of their own (see `codex/characters/kazuto-tabuchi.md`), which sharpens the urgency. They target Tatsuya specifically for two compounding reasons, not one: Kazuto personally approached him at the funeral (the established origin point, unchanged), and as the eldest, Tatsuya inherited Daichi's estate outright, the Haya EB, the manual, presumed access to anything else that survived, making him the one who plausibly still has what they want. They don't escalate against Chiyo directly for the same reason already established in her own file, her yakuza-adjacent background means going after her risks a real street war, not a debt collection.

## Escalation, confirmed: the fake "unfinished discussion" bait (after Chapter 10, not yet on the page)

After Chapter 10, the Tabuchis shift tactics. Direct debt pressure and Eren's needling have gotten them nowhere concrete, so they try something more targeted: dangling the claim that Daichi left an "unfinished discussion" with Kazuto, something he meant to hand over or resolve before he died, that never actually happened. It's an invented hook, not a real thread, engineered specifically to bait Tatsuya into a conversation on their terms, one where getting him talking and reminiscing about his father is the real goal, an opening to extract details about the schematics or whatever else of Daichi's survived, under the cover of grief and closure rather than open pressure. A subtler escalation than the funeral approach or the WattsUp visits, playing on the one angle Tatsuya doesn't have full armor against, since refusing to talk about his father at all isn't really something he can do forever.

## Leverage shift, end of Act One

Once Tatsuya's a publicly revealed HGO star with real sponsorship/placement money behind him (see `outline/act-one-winter-bash.md`), the Tabuchis lose their actual leverage, not a secret (Ohema's condition was never hidden from them, Eren already references it openly in Chapter 1), but Tatsuya's isolation. Quiet debt-collection pressure only worked on someone with no money, no public standing, and no institutional backing. A star player with real income and a growing public profile isn't quietly pressurable the same way, which is exactly what pushes the Tabuchis toward riskier, more overt tactics, consistent with, and an escalation past, the already-locked "unfinished discussion" bait above.

## Payoff

Late in the story, Tatsuya discovers his father's connection to Kazuku and the full circle of the family's legacy, which likely also resolves or reframes this subplot. See `story/premise.md`.

## Backstory block (author-stated, kept off the page for now)

- **Tatsuya's nature.** Very homebound. He loves his siblings and his family. He knew his mom was already ill.
- **HGO and Daichi.** Daichi was under an NDA because he was on the HGO development team, one of its creators or early patent holders (not a developer of the game itself), based on his first-gen pod schematics. This is found out much later.
- **Pod generations.** VR1 (the first VR pod, like Meta's, for convenience, business and daily life, not full dive, one or two games, though Daichi did produce games for it) has been out four to five years. VR2 has been out about two years; the Tabuchis invested in VR2, which is when games became patent-driven. New now: HGO and the third-gen pod (lighter, not a full-body rig).
- **Daichi's company and the manual.** Kazuhaya Tech (the author wrote "Haya Interply"; read as the company). His line was two vehicles (the Haya EB and the Haya Sedan) and a VR pod. The manual holds his early work and schematics and helps repair the EB and, if it is ever running, the Sedan. He had a lot invested in Tochi.
- **Tochi.** Tochi is the one place in Japan where the prime minister has no influence: family and backing mean nothing and you enter on merit. Tatsuya got in on merit with his dad's brain and could have gone in on athletics but chose neither. His parents have Tochi ties: Ohema worked there (before she got sick she was the national volleyball team's coach, went to nationals three years and won twice, and is an alumna); Chiyo is an alumna on the council with a stake; Daichi was an alumnus who worked there; the Tabuchis also have a stake. OPEN: the earlier wording that Tochi is "connected to all politics" is superseded; OPEN: "his dad finally got him in" versus merit only; OPEN: Ohema "former player" versus "national team coach"; OPEN: first-year versus "maybe second year".
- **The courtyard and Eren.** At Tochi Tatsuya talked to nobody. One day in the courtyard, on his tablet, he was watching HGO trailers, three or four months before release. He watched because Dad loved gaming, and the ads were everywhere; HGO was a mindful distraction. He did not know what his dad was working on. Eren walked by, saw it, and the friendship grew over a few months. Eren did not know Tatsuya was Daichi's son; Tatsuya did not know his dad knew Eren's dad. The kids were not involved until Eren's dad got involved and turned greedy. Later Tatsuya hates HGO, until he gets in the game himself.
- **The loan.** Daichi sold everything and his business money and royalties went to his wife's hospital bills. As a last resort he took a loan from a loan shark he did not realize was Tabuchi Enterprises, believing her last surgery would cure her; she relapsed, costing more. The Tabuchis pressed him for three to four months. Eren's dad then said that since he could not pay, he should give them the designs and patents of VR1 as collateral, because he knew VR3 was coming.
- **Why he left Tochi.** The betrayal and the mocking, not wanting to be at Tochi because of his family ("why am I here?"), "I can go my own route," needing to make money, worrying about his younger siblings and about Chiyo covering everything, knowing the Tabuchis want more than money now. Chiyo has money and wants him in school.

They are not the company that made them and never had Daichi's base form, which is why they wanted the first-gen designs. First-gen pods are considered extinct and run only basic early VR games.

The bill is kept off the page until the hospital visit.
