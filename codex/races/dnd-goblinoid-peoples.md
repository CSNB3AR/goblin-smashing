# D&D Goblinoid Peoples, Translated for Eryndal

**Status:** Research and proposal file for the goblin, hobgoblin and "third people" baseline of Eryndal. Nothing here is canon unless it is tagged [CANON]. Written so the author can accept, reject or rework any piece later. Companion files: `codex/locations/thrash-camp-field-guide.md` (the camp) and `codex/combat/goblin-warfare-and-tactics.md` (how they fight).

## Tag legend (read this once)

- **[SRC](url)**: a claim paraphrased from a D&D or real-world source I read or searched. "(secondhand)" means the page is a fan guide, forum summary or review of a book I could not open. "(search summary)" means I only saw the claim in a search result and the page itself would not load.
- **[CANON file]**: already fixed in the repo (codex file, ledger entry, or a chapter on the page). Later ledger entries win over earlier ones.
- **[PROPOSAL]**: my invention for the author to accept later. Never treat as canon.
- **[OPEN]**: a question only the author can settle.

**The borrowing rule** (`style-guide/inspirations-and-borrowing.md`): take the mechanic or the feeling, never the names, wording or character material. D&D proper nouns in this file are research references only. Section 10 lists the names that must not reach the page.

## 1. What canon already fixes (the baseline everything else must fit)

- **Goblin is a weak race with a Wisdom floor.** Base stats 4/4/2/7/9/4, no resistances, magic restricted by the Wisdom-18 gate. [CANON `codex/races/goblin.md`]
- **Hobgoblin is an evolution tier, not a birth caste.** A goblin becomes one only by leveling steadily through combat without dying. Sevish never sends recruits out and the spawn zone has no high-level monsters, so almost nobody evolves. Sevish and Wyx do not know the mechanics. [CANON `codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`, "Why they never evolve"]
- **Hobgoblins on the page:** Sevish (L80, Berserker, two-handed axe), Wyx (L25, Blade Dancer, dual sabres), Zinka (L7, east-line scout, Toxic Breath), Ru (L5 after Shiv's blessing, formerly the runt). Tatsuya is a Shiv Goblin, a separate evolution. [CANON `codex/characters/thrash-siblings-ability-lists.md`, `codex/characters/pk-wraith-raiding-party.md`, ledger "Ru's title"]
- **The rank and file:** about 25 at the start, 23 after Tatsuya's two kills, 14 and then 10 during the raid. They are recruits off the Thrash ship; in game logic they are respawns, and the camp takes about a month to refill. [CANON ledger "Head count, audited"; `codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`]
- **Thrash law as stated on the page:** "The clan's rule is that we do not fight each other." Whoever brings word of Shiv stands as an ally. Sevish and Wyx are the walls and that is "the only law I keep". The dead stay dead. [CANON Chapters 6 and 10]
- **Feral stigma:** only Thrash goblins can enter villages and settlements; every other clan reads as feral and is barred. [CANON `codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`]
- **Myrrhwood's goblin side** is the Black Lands: many small camps fighting over untouched terrain and raw materials, no kingdom. There are six goblin provinces and seven Thrash-type clans, and Thrash owns no land because it gave it up. [CANON `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`, ledger "Myrrhwood control"]
- **Patron:** goblins are tied by race affinity to Seranis, goddess of demigods and creation, who has a sense of humor and picks odd bearers. A goblin may follow her without belonging to the Church. [CANON `codex/lore/patrons-and-world-weapons.md`]
- **Shiv:** a human noble, Shivaris Jutsuin, remade into a goblin by the court; not the first goblin, but the first to make the clans act like one people. He is now an ash-and-smoke spirit whose reach is fading. [CANON `codex/characters/shiv.md`]
- **Speech rules** (full table in section 8). [CANON `style-guide/race-dialect-and-voice.md`, ledger "Goblin speech and intelligence"]
- **NPCs call players "adventurers".** [CANON ledger "Chapter Ten revised", Chapter 6 line "the adventurers who rally to them"]
- **Ranks, the knot in canon:** Wyx says on the page "there are ranks in this clan. You earn them. Nothing is handed over." The ledger also says goblins have no formal ranks yet, so the page carries only "co-leader" and never a rank label. Both hold if ranks are informal. Section 4 proposes a ladder that keeps them informal until the author accepts names. [CANON Chapter 6; ledger "Chapter Ten: Tatsuya cuts in and sends Ru"]

## 2. The D&D picture in one page

**One people, three bodies.** In the 5e books the goblinoids were once separate peoples with their own gods and customs, until a conquering war god took all three and made them one, with their own deities broken, bound or forced into line. [SRC](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8532919) (secondhand, quoting Volo's) The newer book moves their roots to the Feywild and says the god conquered them when they crossed to the Material Plane. [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1248-exploring-the-new-goblin-hobgoblin-and-kobold)

**Goblins** are small, cunning and cruel in a fight and servile when beaten, and they bully anything they think they can. [SRC](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:goblin) They sit at the bottom of the goblinoid order: oppressed by hobgoblins and sometimes bugbears, and they pass the abuse down to pets, slaves and lower goblins. [SRC](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8528920) (secondhand) A tribe is traditionally led by its strongest member, ranges from a small gang to about four hundred, and the bigger ones ride wolves or ally with worgs and settle near civilization to raid for supplies. [SRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_%28Dungeons_%26_Dragons%29)

**Hobgoblins** are the organized people: war is central, cowardice is feared more than death, the society runs on a rigid code, and they are described as lawful. [SRC](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:hobgoblin) The 2022 book rewrote them as charismatic leaders who form strong bonds with comrades. [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1248-exploring-the-new-goblin-hobgoblin-and-kobold) Rank follows battlefield achievement, honor comes only from battle, the arts are discouraged, and individual lives count for little against the war. [SRC](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8528920) (secondhand)

**Bugbears** are ambush predators in small tribes, long-limbed and quiet for their size, who bully weaker creatures into working for them and put personal advantage above loyalty to their own kind. [SRC](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:bugbear) They are often called lazy because they tire on long exertion and need long rest. [SRC](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8532919) (secondhand)

**Where D&D is strong:** the three-peoples division of labor, the hobgoblin rank structure, the goblin caste and boss model, ambush and trap warfare, and the war-god pantheon. **Where D&D is thin:** diet, trade, home life, prisoners. The Eberron "empire" material (section 3) is the best source for goblinoids as a civilization rather than a threat.

## 3. Goblinoids as a civilization: the "empire" idea

### D&D baseline

- **Eberron's goblinoid empire** is the one place D&D treats the three peoples as a state rather than a raiding party. It dominated its continent before humans arrived, was broken by aberrant invaders and finished by civil war, and its heirs sealed themselves into deep vaults to keep the culture alive. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- Its society ran on a racial division of labor and a shared instinct to cooperate: bugbears supplied strength and courage, goblins cunning and finesse, hobgoblins discipline and organization. Goblins and bugbears followed hobgoblin leadership out of respect for how well it organized, not out of fear. Goblin labor was honored as vital, and slavery was rare. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- **Honor was two ideas:** duty to the whole, and personal honor. On the battlefield honor meant obeying orders, holding ground and showing skill. Ambushes, striking civilians when strategic, and killing the helpless were not dishonorable, because victory came first. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- **Their army** used small adaptable infantry units that could merge into large formations or split into small ones, plus cavalry and archers; chain weapons such as flails were common; bugbears often scouted; goblin rangers and rogues skirmished; a spy-and-assassin service existed; combat engineers built fortifications and siege works; and a class of storytellers (healers, diplomats, lore keepers, morale-builders) took the place of clerics, so spiritual life ran on true stories of past deeds, not on gods. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- **Their buildings** were often underground or cut into mountains, with few windows and archer slits, because goblins do not need much light. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- **The descendants** who lost the empire (driven into harsh lands, less refined, keeping slaves, with a clear hierarchy and punishments) show what a collapsed goblinoid civilization looks like. Their hobgoblins and bugbears are used to dominating their goblins. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)
- **Elsewhere:** in the Realms, hobgoblin society is described as more industrious and less savage than goblin or bugbear society, usually led by its strongest member, with bugbears sometimes taking over. [SRC](https://forgottenrealms.fandom.com/wiki/Hobgoblin) (search summary; the page returned HTTP 402) In Greyhawk, goblinoids rallied to a regional tyrant and helped overrun two border lands, and the Pomarj population was recorded as about 15 percent goblin and 10 percent hobgoblin against an orc plurality of about 43 percent. [SRC](https://greyhawkonline.com/greyhawkwiki/Pomarj) (search summary) The 5e book simply says hobgoblin legions are known for unity in both Eberron and the Realms. [SRC](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:hobgoblin)

### Translation for Eryndal

- **Eryndal has no goblinoid empire, and that is the point.** Shiv "united the goblins under one banner but never built a kingdom or a city" and tried to settle things at the negotiating table. [CANON `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`] D&D's lost empire is lost greatness. Eryndal's lost thing is lost unity, about forty-five years gone, remembered by Wyx's grandfather's generation. [CANON Chapter 6, `codex/characters/shiv.md`]
- **Working phrase for that era:** "the One-Fire years", when every clan sat at one fire. [PROPOSAL] Use it as a goblin saying, never as a calendar term.
- **Take from the Eberron model (idea level only):** roles by aptitude instead of birth; discipline as the thing that makes a hobgoblin respected; keepers of true stories instead of priests; a respected class of builders; fortification as habit. All five fit Thrash and are developed below. [PROPOSAL]
- **Leave behind:** empire scale, birth caste inside Thrash, and every proper noun. Eryndal's goblins are a few dozen recruits on a hill, not a state.

## 4. Caste, rank and leadership

### D&D baseline

- **Goblin tribes** run a four-tier caste order: a noble-and-enforcer tier, a warrior-and-rider tier, a food-trap-and-poison tier, and an outcast tier that does menial work and supervises any slaves. A family's standing follows how much it matters to the tribe's survival. The boss is a tyrant, bosses are often murdered, and the top families then fight for the post. Rare goblin spellcasters are held in high regard and often rise to the top. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand, summarizing Volo's)
- **The goblin boss, in the rules,** can swap places with a goblin next to it so the other goblin takes the hit. It is a perfect one-line picture of how a feral boss treats his people. [SRC](https://www.aidedd.org/dnd/monstres.php?vo=goblin-boss)
- **Hobgoblin legions:** a legion is a dynastic clan that doubles as a military unit, banners within it are led by captains, and ranks run from warlord down to soldier. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) A captain leads up to about fifty soldiers; a warlord commands whole legions and answers only to very powerful masters; kill a warlord and the hierarchy can fall into chaos until a new despot takes over. [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/263-how-to-play-hobgoblins-like-perfect-soldiers)
- **Hobgoblin code,** as the 5e race guide frames it: obey orders, honor the gods, answer insults, reward glorious deeds, put the legion above yourself. They respect competence even in a foe, dismiss the weak, and disagree out loud before following the order anyway. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/guides/hobgoblin-race-in-dnd-5e/) (secondhand)
- **A hobgoblin leader's one big trick** is Leadership: for a minute it can add a small die to the attack rolls and saves of allies who can hear it. [SRC](https://www.aidedd.org/dnd/monstres.php?vo=hobgoblin-captain)
- **Bugbears** live in small family groups under a dozen that act like gangs. A problem member is exiled and usually finds another group. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) In a mixed band a bugbear leader sits at the top. [SRC](https://dmdave.com/bugbears-1/) (secondhand)

### Translation: what Thrash does differently

- **Thrash replaced birth caste with earned marks.** It was "the first clan to accept Shiv" and the first to see that being feral was not the way. [CANON ledger "Thrash Clan and the quest trigger"] It follows that Thrash threw out the old kin-tier order and the slave trade that held it up. [PROPOSAL]
- **Feral clans keep the old order** in Myrrhwood. Four kin-tiers, in goblin idiom: **Hand-kin** (chiefs' families and enforcers), **Spear-kin** (fighters and beast-riders), **Pot-kin** (cooks, trappers, poison-makers, herb-gatherers), **Dust-kin** (outcasts who haul, dig and mind the captives). A boss rules by fear and is usually killed by Hand-kin kin. A feral clan's "spirit-listener" (someone who claims to hear spirits, as Wyx explains Tatsuya's talking to Leon) has high standing but is not a spellcaster, because goblins cannot cast. [PROPOSAL; casting gate CANON `codex/races/goblin.md`]
- **Thrash leadership is two people.** Sevish is the war leader (axe, honor code, no interest in politics). Wyx is first in command: she runs day-to-day discipline, hands out gear from her crate, and carries the clan's history. Both are gold tags that cannot level until they become Tatsuya's troops. [CANON `codex/characters/sevish-thrash.md`, `codex/characters/wyx-thrash.md`, `codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`] On the page the word is "co-leader", never a rank. [CANON ledger "Chapter Ten: Tatsuya cuts in and sends Ru"]
- **The smallest unit is the tent.** Four to six goblins who share a tent, eat from one pot and stand together on the stakes. Canon shows four tents around the big one plus a smaller pale one, so about five tents of about five fits the roster. [CANON ledger "Chapter 4 chase and camp layout"; PROPOSAL for the unit and its name] A Roman camp worked the same way, with fighters housed in facing rows of tents by unit. [SRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra)
- **Succession is not the danger.** Sevish and Wyx retreat at about 20 percent HP and cannot be killed in the rally. [CANON ledger "Sevish, Wyx and the monthly rally"] The danger is absence: they are the walls, so when they leave, the stakes hold nothing. [CANON Chapter 10]

## 5. Religion: Shiv, a legend, versus a war god

### D&D baseline

- **The chief goblinoid god is a god of war and rulership** who conquered the three peoples and merged them into one. The newest book says he corrupted them from a fey form and was never their original ancestor. [SRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_%28Dungeons_%26_Dragons%29)
- **Older editions give him rites:** a sign of blood running down an axe blade, read as his way of speaking; a full-moon holy day with sacrifices by axe, ideally of dwarves or gnomes; temples in caves; shamans in grey-green robes and pointed hats; priests who serve as a war chief's right hand or run a tribe themselves and must keep defending their authority against undisciplined goblins. [SRC](https://www.aidedd.org/univers/pantheon/maglubiyet/) (older-edition material)
- **The lesser gods** are a ladder of tempers. A goblin god of slavery, oppression and morale whose priests capture rather than kill and teach by public torture. A goblin god of cooperation and territory, the "peacekeeper" who forbids war between goblin tribes so they can turn on outsiders together. A hobgoblin god of war and authority. Bugbear gods of violence, of hunting and surprise, and of fear (who is not worshipped, only appeased). One outlier god of patience, tolerance and craft whose faith centers on sharing food from decorated bowls. [SRC](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Grankhul.html) (Wikipedia mirror, older editions)
- **Hobgoblins** build small shrines rather than temples, and their priests train warriors. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) They want to die in battle so they can join the endless war on the god's home plane. [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/263-how-to-play-hobgoblins-like-perfect-soldiers) Their saying is that a hero in death becomes a hero eternal. [SRC](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:hobgoblin)
- **Bugbears** keep no priests, worship through combat and murder, and offer slain enemy leaders' heads. Cowardice is blamed on possession by a bogeyman and those who flee are shunned. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand); [SRC](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8532919) (secondhand)
- **The Eberron empire is the odd one out:** no clerics, no gods, and faith in unseen forces is alien to them. Storytellers who recount true deeds carry the spiritual role, and what lasts after death is the honor you earned and the example you set. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)

### Shiv against the war god

| | D&D war god | Eryndal's Shiv |

The closest D&D match to Shiv is the **cooperation god**, the one who forbids tribe-on-tribe war so the goblins can face outward together. Shiv is that idea as a mortal: no conquest, no sacrifice, just a law that the clans do not fight each other. [SRC](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Grankhul.html) That is a stronger fit than the war god, and it explains the page: Thrash is "the first clan to accept Shiv", and goblins fighting each other is its cardinal sin. [CANON ledger "Thrash Clan and the quest trigger"]

### How the faith is lived (PROPOSAL)

1. **Remembered, not worshipped.** Elders say "Shiv said" the way a veteran says "my grandfather said". Nobody prays to him. The proof of belief is behavior. [PROPOSAL; echo of the empire's storytellers [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)]
2. **Keepers, not priests.** Whoever carries the old words is the keeper. In Thrash that is Wyx, with Shiv's notebook. Fireside telling is the whole liturgy. [PROPOSAL, built on CANON Chapter 6]
3. **Bone totems as witnesses.** The carved poles at the war tent carry blocky lines like the ones on Wyx's arms. They are clan memory, not idols. Beast bone only, never people. [PROPOSAL meaning; poles and lines CANON ledger "Chapter Six, the tent meeting, REBUILT"]
4. **Names at the fire.** When a goblin does not get up, his name is said at the pot. It answers permanent death, and it fits the clan-lead naming rule. [PROPOSAL; naming CANON ledger "Naming, the clan lead, and the logout lock"]
5. **The bowl is the clan.** The pot is the one daily ceremony. A stolen bowl is a small sacrilege against the "no blood inside the fence" law, which is why a bowl fight in Chapter 4 can set off a world quest. [PROPOSAL; trigger CANON ledger "Trigger (author-stated)"; echoes the outlier god's shared bowls [SRC](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Grankhul.html)]
6. **Sayings.** "Teeth at the door, not at each other." "Walls first." "Say the name." [PROPOSAL]
7. **Spirits.** Low-Wisdom goblins believe small spirits speak to people, which is how Wyx explains Tatsuya talking to Leon ("Spirits do that"). Texture only, not a pantheon. [CANON Chapters 6 and 10; PROPOSAL for the extension]
8. **Seranis.** [OPEN] The author has given her humor and odd bearers but no rites. Until she has some, goblins can swear by "the one who picks the odd ones" and leave it there.

## 6. Food, tools, homes and trade

### 6.1 Food

**D&D baseline.** The only official-flavored line I found says the gatherer tier cooks and does the little farming goblins manage, hunters hunt, and bugbears forage and hunt but make others farm. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) Fan sources describe goblins as meat-first omnivores that will eat rats, snakes and carrion. [SRC](https://spelljammer.fandom.com/wiki/Goblin) (search summary, low confidence) D&D gives no menu.

**Canon.** One stew pot in the middle of camp, tended by a goblin with a long spoon who tastes it and adds something. Rotten stew in wooden bowls. Strips of meat. Half-burned meat on the air. A water barrel. [CANON Chapter 4] Wyx's "farm tools" line says there are no fields. [CANON Chapter 6] The day-to-day food item in the codex is the Clanmark Trail Ration. [CANON `codex/crafting/food-and-consumables.md`]

**Proposals.**

### 6.2 Tools and craft

**D&D baseline.** Gatherer-tier goblins usually carry only knives or slings; the top tier holds the knowledge of smelting, forging and mining; bugbears use rudimentary tools. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) In the stat blocks a goblin carries a scimitar and shortbow, a hobgoblin a longsword and longbow, a bugbear a morningstar and javelins. ### 6.3 Homes

**D&D baseline.** Goblin lairs sit in forests or caves, often old mines, with low ceilings, narrow heavily trapped passages, wolves on the perimeter and lookouts in trees. Bugbears den in caves or abandoned dens. Hobgoblins build military-style encampments in secluded spots. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) A hobgoblin command post can be a stone fortress, a palisaded command tent or a fully stocked war camp. [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/263-how-to-play-hobgoblins-like-perfect-soldiers) The classic published goblin base has a blind at the cave mouth, a wolf kennel, a bridge, a flood trap and a bugbear boss whose room the whole tribe walks through. ### 6.4 Trade

**D&D baseline.** Goblins steal from settlements before they try to take people. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) Bigger tribes settle near civilization to raid for supplies. [SRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblin_%28Dungeons_%26_Dragons%29) Hobgoblins count finding ore veins and magic items as honor. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand) The Eberron empire traded little with outsiders, and modern city goblins either fall into crime or build honest trades under heavy prejudice. [SRC](https://keith-baker.com/tag/goblins/)

**Eryndal.** The clan "came with what they raided with, and it came over on the Thrash ship". [CANON `codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`] Thrash goblins can enter villages; other clans cannot. Lunarest carries bad infamy for goblins. Coin sits in the war tent chest, never on a belt. The clan's wealth is its ship, its gear and its crate. [PROPOSAL]

## 7. Prisoners, loot and slaves

**D&D baseline.**
- Goblins enslave any creature they can subdue (wolves, rats, now and then humans or dwarves), treat favored captives like pets while still beating them, and build traps meant to catch passers-by for enslavement. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand)
- The goblin god of oppression has priests who capture rather than kill and use public torture as instruction. [SRC](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Grankhul.html)
- Hobgoblins respect even a foe who proves capable, answer insults harshly, may parley once defeat is clear, and run a secret-police order that polices occupied communities. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand); [SRC](https://www.tribality.com/2020/12/29/using-monsters-beyond-their-stat-blocks-goblinoid-tactics/); [SRC](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/263-how-to-play-hobgoblins-like-perfect-soldiers)
- Bugbears display the heads of enemy leaders. [SRC](https://dungeonmister.com/lore/monsters/goblinoids-in-dnd/) (secondhand)
- Official D&D has almost nothing on how captives are kept. [SRC](https://dnd.kismetrose.com/DMSlaveryReasons.html) (search summary)

**Eryndal canon.**
- Tatsuya is held in a small tent at the east edge, cuffed by two guards, with `[YOU ARE NOW A PRISONER OF THE THRASH CLAN]` and movement restricted to camp until the chieftain releases him. A prisoner is not left in a tent with weapons. [CANON Chapter 6, ledger "Handoff mismatches RESOLVED"]
- Whoever brings word of Shiv stands as an ally. A prisoner can earn his way out by deed. [CANON Chapter 6]
- Sevish will not kill the helpless; Wyx despises cruelty. [CANON `codex/characters/sevish-thrash.md`, `codex/characters/wyx-thrash.md`]
- "Hah. Spoils." Sevish accepts that a kill's drops belong to the killer. Wyx hands out gear from her crate, so gear is rank-locked. [CANON Chapter 10]

**Proposals.**
- **Thrash keeps no slaves.** Captives work off their keep, are ransomed by deed, or are released at the chieftain's word. Feral clans keep slaves, "pets" and a Dust-kin tier to mind them. [PROPOSAL]
- **Spoils rule:** weapons and armor go to Wyx's crate, coin to the war tent chest, and nothing is taken from a body as a trophy except beast bone. [PROPOSAL]
- **Players as captives:** a captured adventurer is held, not killed. A character who logs off stays in the holding tent, as Tatsuya did. A player who dies simply wakes elsewhere, which makes murdering prisoners pointless and gives the clan a reason to keep them. [PROPOSAL, built on CANON Chapters 6 and 9]
