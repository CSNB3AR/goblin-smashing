# Tournaments & Events

**Framing:** HGO is not an esports title at the start of the story. Season Zero ended in a streamed spectacle, Crownfall (the Season One opener and first Siege) is announced with Hearthfire, and the Siege Pro League is an option from Season One (see `codex/pro-scene/esports-timeline-and-tatsuyas-long-arc.md`).

For the release sequence, payouts and currency, see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`. This file covers the formats.

## Competitive formats

- **Battle of the Border Fortress (Season Zero finale, in-world).** An open-world, king-of-the-hill fight in an unnamed walled border fortress city on the edge of the snowy plains, outside the Kingdom of Lunarest, not an arena. 12 Aogiri against 20 London Spire (the Knights of the London Spire), 300 points on the line with London Spire 10,500 and Aogiri 10,250 in season points, so Aogiri must win it. Ten uncontested minutes holding the zone wins it. The Knights play guerrilla warfare: hidden inside the walls, a kill zone at the choke point, Aogiri's leader poisoned and short of antidotes, reinforcements dying to 72-hour locks, and a leader who waits too long and dies, ending his banner. Full detail: `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`.

- **Season Zero (closed beta) finale format.** Twelve guilds qualify, four per prince's army. A skirmish win is worth 40 points. The finale week cuts the top eight two per day, the final four are set by the weekend, and Sunday is the Battle of the Border Fortress above. Hosted and broadcast from Singapore arenas and facilities (see `codex/locations/singapore-championship-venue.md`). Prize pool 40 million yen.
- **Crownfall (Season One opener) and the Siege format.** Siege is a battle royale: 5 guilds per session, 20 players each, a camp at spawn that upgrades to level 3 and becomes a fort, a guild flag, 60 lives in 3 waves, 3 hours, resources, creative forts, shillings and Ember cores for winners. Crownfall (December 25th, guild-only, open-beta characters, neutral zone) introduces it and opens the season for seasonal points. Garrison troops fill empty slots (see `codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`). See `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`.
- **Siege Pro League** (active from the start of Season One, mentioned by Kazuku as an option). The standing national and global circuit on the Siege battle-royale format. It is so watched that a pro scene forms. Seasonal points buy rare items, winners earn shillings and **Ember cores** (refined into Ember crystals that power the guild hall), troops are capped hard (about one or two per player), and the 60-life rule replaces the death lock. It grants no territory (that is Node Wars). Full rules and open items: `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`. The story stays not-esports: Tatsuya is on the open-world path.
- **Node Wars** (after Hearthfire). The main war content. See below.
- **Players bring their permanently-earned items and gear** into guild fights alongside their base class, the deck-building layer (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`). World-level items are nerfed for competitive balance but still matter.
- **Locks.** A flat **72-hour death lock** in raids, skirmishes, node wars and the open world. Siege Pro League sessions use the **60-life rule** instead; whether Crownfall uses the Siege 60-life rule or the 72-hour death lock is OPEN. Pass-out lock default 24 hours.
- **Character lock at Hearthfire.** Closed-beta characters freeze at their current level once Hearthfire launches. Owners keep playing them as-is or retire them for Legacy Points, which convert to Legacy Silver (locked until Hearthfire) and Legacy items. Open-beta characters are permanent.
- **1v1 Open Ladder:** separate from guild formats, anyone can enter, leaderboard resets each season, the top 100 earn pro tryouts, rare gear and media coverage. See `codex/arena/overview.md`.

## Node Wars, the main war content

After the world maps release, guild fights over **nodes** (towns, resource sites, forts) across the **five continents** are the war the story is about. Territory brings taxes, rules for the zone, infrastructure and NPC ties. Contested zones grant buffs and minor perks, and bigger guilds with existing numbers take them first. Owning a province or a kingdom is real income, which is why legitimate guilds and the darker real-money side of the game chase the same land (see `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`). Node Wars are the main war content, and Crownfall is the rehearsal for them. Guild halls, the 20-player side and the troop system carry into node war fights. Older notes that called this "GvG Wars" describe the same system.

**Funeral truce.** When the king dies around the start of Season One, his royal funeral pauses combat in the royal lands for its length (length and scope open, see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). Node Wars start after the funeral, once the princes move against each other and the map starts moving.

## Events, sponsors and history

- **Finale history.** The Season Zero finale, streamed as spectacle with sponsors such as the WattsUp Corporation, won by London Spire (40 million yen). Sometimes called the finals of the season-long regional-to-national qualifying structure, which feeds into a single flagship event per season.
- **Crownfall:** December 25th, 10 million yen pool, open-beta guilds only, Season One opener for seasonal points (see `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`). It replaces the old Winter Bash.
- **Season Zero bracket notes.** Kansai Black Lotus (Eren's guild) missed the top eight because Arya's small guild, Crimson Static (see `codex/pro-scene/guilds.md`), took the last qualifying spot by winning the Fire Festival guild event "The Second Dawn" (see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`). Dosuke AI's guild fell on Saturday of the finale week. None of this needs to land on the page in Chapter 1.
- **World Boss Raids / Server Firsts**, PvE guilds race to clear new content (see `codex/creatures/class-4.md`), first clear earns a cash prize, exclusive loot and a permanent mark on server history.
- **Streamer/Influencer events**, sponsored "all-star" or meme-build exhibition weeks, PR-driven.
- **National/City showcases**, country- or city-branded guilds backed by real sponsors and governments, top players can be drafted, traded or signed to multi-year contracts.
