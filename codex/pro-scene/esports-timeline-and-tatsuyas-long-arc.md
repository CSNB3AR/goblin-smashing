# Esports Timeline & Tatsuya's Long-Term Arc

Forward material, long-horizon. Locks the broad shape of Tatsuya's competitive arc so it doesn't get rushed or contradicted later, not detailed match-by-match plotting.

## Where the scene actually stands

- HGO's closed beta (Season Zero) ran roughly eight to nine months (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`).
- One championship has happened so far: the Season Zero Championship (Championship 1), won by London Spire (see `codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`).
- The competitive structure changes with the Hearthfire expansion: guild Siege play and the Siege Pro League for rare seasonal items, with Node Wars as the main war content (see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). Haya Storm's own end-of-Act-Two status is already locked as "positioned to compete in the first full season, not full pro status yet" (see `codex/pro-scene/haya-storm.md`).

## Tatsuya's arc, the long version

**Not winning a seasonal championship of the Siege Pro League until roughly season five**, a few years out from the story's present. His first full-season run gets him closer, genuinely competitive, but doesn't end in a win. This is deliberate pacing, not a placeholder, the point is that his climb is real and earned over multiple years, not a single-season power spike.

**A roster change follows that run.** One of Haya Storm's members from this stretch, an older player, chooses to step back from competing. The resulting roster change ends up being a net positive for the guild, not a loss the story treats as a setback.

**Long-term ceiling, flagged for later, not built yet:** the eventual goal is Tatsuya recognized as one of the greatest players to ever play, real "all-time great" status. Concretely, that means multiple seasonal championship wins, at least three, never back-to-back (he's not meant to dominate a single uncontested era, the climbs matter more than a streak). Not detailed further here, this is a horizon marker, not a plotted sequence of seasons.

## Flagged idea, being pushed forward: a sibling championship run, correctly sequenced

Confirmed by the author to actively develop, with one real correction to the original pitch: Taji doesn't parachute in as Tatsuya's teammate. He gets his own arc first. A stretch of Taji genuinely learning to play at a real level, then his own future where he ends up with teammates of his own, possibly winning a seasonal championship himself, on his own team, before any storyline about teaming up with Tatsuya specifically enters the picture. The eventual sibling team-up, if it happens, comes after Taji has already established himself as a real player in his own right, not as a favor or a legacy slot. Still a future arc, not detailed further here, but locked as the correct shape going forward: Taji's own development first, a possible convergence with Tatsuya much later. (Taji and Momo do not yet know about Dad's connection to the game, see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`.)
