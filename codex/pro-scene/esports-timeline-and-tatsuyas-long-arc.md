# Siege Pro League Arc and Tatsuya's Long-Term Arc

Forward material, long-horizon. Locks the broad shape of how Siege grows from a one-off event into a league, and Tatsuya's place in it, so it doesn't get rushed or contradicted later. The file name is kept for links. The old "esports timeline" framing is retired: **HGO is not an esports title at the start of the story and the Siege Pro League does not exist yet.**

## Where things actually stand

- HGO's closed beta (Season Zero) ran roughly eight to nine months (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`). It ended with the Battle of the Snowy Plains, a spectacle skirmish streamed for the closed-beta crowd, not a league final (see `codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`).
- Siege is announced as a **mode** inside the Hearthfire expansion, with **Crownfall** as a one-off Siege event, a ten million yen prize pool and a 72-hour 50 percent discount on drives. There is no league, no circuit and no points shop yet (see `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`).
- Small companies, the WattsUp Corporation among them, buy sponsorship exposure on HGO because the game's stock is high. That is why the beta finale plays on the screen in Gato's store. It is a sponsor relationship, not a sign of an esports scene.
- Haya Storm's end-of-Act-Two status is locked as "positioned to compete in the first full season" (see `codex/pro-scene/haya-storm.md`).

## How the Siege Pro League is earned (staged, with triggers)

The league has to be built on the page, not dropped in. Each stage needs its trigger before the next opens.

1. **Siege debuts as a mode (Crownfall).** Trigger: Hearthfire announcement and the Crownfall event. Crowds and viewers discover that a 20-per-side siege, with players bringing items they made and troops filling the queue, is genuinely fun to watch. A small underdog guild (Haya Storm) fills a full side with troops and reaches the semifinals.
2. **Sponsors and crowds follow.** Trigger: Crownfall viewership numbers and the physical stadium crowds. Companies like the WattsUp Corporation, drive makers and retailers ask HGO for more events. Players and guilds begin treating Siege as its own thing.
3. **HGO announces the league.** Trigger: Crownfall numbers plus early Season One demand. The announcement lays out the national and global circuit, the seasonal points that buy rare items, the Ember core economy, the troop cap (about one or two per player), full loot with no rebound and the only no-72-hour-lock rule in the game (see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`).
4. **The circuit forms.** Trigger: first full season. Guilds rebuild their rosters around the new rules, which is when Tatsuya's capped-troop problem and his reliance on connections and products (not raw numbers) become the real test.

Tatsuya does not chase any of this. His drivers stay money, Dad's note, Eren and the world. The league finds him, and each stage is something he reacts to through what he notices and needs, never a ladder he is climbing for its own sake.

## Tatsuya's arc, the long version

**Not winning a seasonal championship of the Siege Pro League until roughly season five**, a few years out from the story's present. His first full-season run gets him closer, genuinely competitive, but doesn't end in a win. This is deliberate pacing, not a placeholder, the point is that his climb is real and earned over multiple years, not a single-season power spike.

**A roster change follows that run.** One of Haya Storm's members from this stretch, an older player, chooses to step back from competing. The resulting change ends up being a net positive for the guild, not a loss the story treats as a setback.

**Long-term ceiling, flagged for later, not built yet:** the eventual goal is Tatsuya recognized as one of the greatest players to ever play, real "all-time great" status. Concretely, that means multiple seasonal championship wins, at least three, never back-to-back (he's not meant to dominate a single uncontested era, the climbs matter more than a streak). Not detailed further here, this is a horizon marker, not a plotted sequence of seasons. The league is one arc inside a larger story about Node Wars, Myrrhwood and the business he is building.

## Flagged idea, being pushed forward: a sibling championship run, correctly sequenced

Confirmed by the author to actively develop, with one real correction to the original pitch: Taji doesn't parachute in as Tatsuya's teammate. He gets his own arc first. Taji is the one in the family who loves the game, so he is the natural player. A stretch of Taji genuinely learning to play at a real level, then his own future where he ends up with teammates of his own, possibly winning a seasonal championship himself, on his own team, before any storyline about teaming up with Tatsuya specifically enters the picture. The eventual sibling team-up, if it happens, comes after Taji has already established himself as a real player in his own right, not as a favor or a legacy slot. Still a future arc, not detailed further here. (Taji and Momo do not yet know about Dad's connection to the game, see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`.)
