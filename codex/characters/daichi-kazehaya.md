# Daichi Kazehaya

**Role:** Tatsuya's father, deceased before the story begins.

## Facts

- Short, the shorter of Tatsuya's two parents. Tatsuya's height comes from Ohema, not him. What Daichi passed down instead was the analytical, technical mind, the tinkering instinct that keeps the Haya EB running and later gets a first-gen pod overridden.
- First-gen pod engineer and **the owner of the first-gen pods** through his company, Kazuhaya Tech (logo still visible on surviving pods).
- Has **history at Tochi Academy**: he helped build its tech infrastructure, and that standing is part of the family's name there. (Open: whether he was a student, an instructor or a donor-builder. Not decided.)
- Partner of Kazuku in building the third-gen pods. While they were in the middle of that build, before he died, Daichi **handed off some notes** to Kazuku. That stake **would have paid off Ohema's debt**, which is another reason his letter says to find the bear: Kazuku owes the family and holds what Daichi gave. He did **not** hand anything to the Tabuchis. Trusted Kazuku as a genuine business partner. The third-gen hardware is so advanced because the two were partners and Daichi passed some of his work on (see `codex/objects/pod-hardware.md`).
- Daichi and Kazuku weren't just a supplier and a buyer. Before Daichi's health declined, the two of them spent years shaping the actual idea of what HGO could be, not just the hardware to run it. Kazuku built and launched HGO after Daichi's death, but the vision underneath it, a full second world, not just a game, has Daichi's fingerprints on it as much as Kazuku's. This is the real weight behind "business partner," not just a schematics sale.
- **The childhood crash (years ago, separate from his death).** About eleven years ago, when Tatsuya was a child, a car accident left him the scar over his right brow and **paralyzed Daichi**. Its cause is not stated. Daichi spent the rest of his life in a wheelchair, working from a converted corner of the apartment, with an adapted car (his own hybrid **Haya Sedan**, which ran on gas, with hand controls; only the Haya EB scooter is fully electric). Tatsuya grew up with this image of him, late at night, wheelchair pulled up to a desk covered in pod components, and knows the crash did it. What he has not connected is how much of Daichi's work came out of it.
- **The crash is why he went all in on VR.** The paralysis is part of why the work became what it did: the Haya pod, the Haya EB, an adapted car. Daichi's own stated reason, something he said to Kazuku more than once and never quite said to his own family in so many words: he wanted a world where this world and that world wouldn't be so far apart, somewhere a body that couldn't move freely in this one wouldn't be the ceiling in the other. Tatsuya is chasing a version of that and is only starting to see it as his father's line.
- **Dad's manual and the letter.** Daichi handed Kazuku some notes while they were building the third-gen pods, but kept a working manual that holds what the Tabuchis need. Folded inside is a **letter** to Tatsuya (text as it stands on the page in `chapters/chapter-01.md`, drafted for the author to rework): *"Tatsuya, if you're reading this, then I'm gone, and I didn't manage to deal with the Tabuchis. I can't explain everything, not on paper. But what's in this manual is what they need, so don't give it to them. Not a page, not a drawing. They've been pushing me for a long time to sell the business out from under us, and I haven't, and I hope that by the time you read this they still haven't gotten hold of anything that matters to their plans. I'm sorry. I know I'm leaving this on you and Chiyo, and that isn't fair. But I know you can carry it. If you want the truth, talk to the bear. He's the only one who has it, and he'll tell you. Just be careful who is listening. I know this is a lot to put on you. Maybe you can do more with my schematics than I ever did, but I don't want to push you. If you meet the bear, a lot of what you're dealing with will probably sort itself out, and you'll understand why. I wish I could be there when you get to experience the world I helped create."* It is unsigned and marked only with a **bear paw print**, no symbol, no name. It warns and never proves, and it is why Tatsuya decides to go into HGO at all (see `chapters/chapter-01.md`). The Tabuchis have pushed Daichi hard to sell the business, and he had not. The schematics themselves never go in the game.
- Last words to Kazuku: *"If my son ever gets involved, please look after him."*
- **Killed about a year ago, months before HGO and the third-gen pods released**, in the accident caused by the sabotaged Haya Sedan (see the author-side truth below). Closed-casket funeral, never fully explained on the page. Was in debt to the Tabuchis but refused to hand over the schematics despite pressure, even when it might have cleared the debt.
- **Author-side truth (never confirmed on the page; Tatsuya holds only suspicion).** About a year ago the Tabuchis' hired yakuza sabotaged Daichi's Haya Sedan by pulling its plug-ins; it broke down and caused the **accident that killed him**, months before HGO and the third-gen pods released. It looked like his own product failing. The Tabuchis (who build cheap second-gen pods and have yakuza ties) used it to attack the business, retailers pulled back, and his income collapsed. **Every Haya product was pulled from the market, the EB included**, which is why money came in thin. The Tabuchis expected it to bankrupt the family while Ohema was in the hospital. Chiyo returning was not in their plans: she began paying the fees, and her money has gone into bills and Ohema. Kazuto hoped to take the family to court and win the business and the first-gen pod schematics, and it has not worked because Chiyo has her own money; she does not want to pay them and has hired lawyers (see `codex/characters/chiyo-nakamura.md`). Taji and Momo do not know any of this.
- His decline was a slow cascade, working more and more hours, disappearing from the family's daily life, before the family was simply told there had been a funeral.
- Tatsuya has never processed the closed casket. It sits as an open wound and an open question.

## The funeral and Kazuto's approach

The funeral itself was held and funded by the Tabuchis, which is how Kazuto got a foothold. At the funeral, Kazuto Tabuchi personally approached Tatsuya, not Chiyo, framed as an offer to help ("do this for your father, it'll cover..."), about the old schematics/computer files specifically. Tatsuya refused any contact, in part because Eren had already betrayed him by that point and he wasn't about to trust anything Tabuchi-shaped. This is the origin of the year of pressure that follows.

**Only Tatsuya knows this.** Chiyo assumes the pressure is ordinary debt collection, she was never at that conversation and doesn't know the files are the real target. Keep this as Tatsuya's private knowledge, surfaced sparingly and never as a dumped flashback, not something other characters reference. See `codex/subplots/tabuchi-schematics-and-family-debt.md`.

## Backstory block (author-stated, kept off the page for now)

- **Tatsuya's nature.** Very homebound. He loves his siblings and his family. He knew his mom was already ill.
- **HGO and Daichi.** Daichi was under an NDA because he was on the HGO development team, one of its creators or early patent holders (not a developer of the game itself), based on his first-gen pod schematics. This is found out much later.
- **Pod generations.** VR1 (the first VR pod, like Meta's, for convenience, business and daily life, not full dive, one or two games, though Daichi did produce games for it) has been out four to five years. VR2 has been out about two years; the Tabuchis invested in VR2, which is when games became patent-driven. New now: HGO and the third-gen pod (lighter, not a full-body rig).
- **Daichi's company and the manual.** Kazuhaya Tech (the author wrote "Haya Interply"; read as the company). His line was two vehicles (the Haya EB and the Haya Sedan) and a VR pod. The manual holds his early work and schematics and helps repair the EB and, if it is ever running, the Sedan. He had a lot invested in Tochi.
- **Tochi.** Tochi is the one place in Japan where the prime minister has no influence: family and backing mean nothing and you enter on merit. Tatsuya got in on merit with his dad's brain and could have gone in on athletics but chose neither. His parents have Tochi ties: Ohema worked there (before she got sick she was the national volleyball team's coach, went to nationals three years and won twice, and is an alumna); Chiyo is an alumna on the council with a stake; Daichi was an alumnus who worked there; the Tabuchis also have a stake. OPEN: the earlier wording that Tochi is "connected to all politics" is superseded; OPEN: "his dad finally got him in" versus merit only; OPEN: Ohema "former player" versus "national team coach"; OPEN: first-year versus "maybe second year".
- **The courtyard and Eren.** At Tochi Tatsuya talked to nobody. One day in the courtyard, on his tablet, he was watching HGO trailers, three or four months before release. He watched because Dad loved gaming, and the ads were everywhere; HGO was a mindful distraction. He did not know what his dad was working on. Eren walked by, saw it, and the friendship grew over a few months. Eren did not know Tatsuya was Daichi's son; Tatsuya did not know his dad knew Eren's dad. The kids were not involved until Eren's dad got involved and turned greedy. Later Tatsuya hates HGO, until he gets in the game himself.
- **The loan.** Daichi sold everything and his business money and royalties went to his wife's hospital bills. As a last resort he took a loan from a loan shark he did not realize was Tabuchi Enterprises, believing her last surgery would cure her; she relapsed, costing more. The Tabuchis pressed him for three to four months. Eren's dad then said that since he could not pay, he should give them the designs and patents of VR1 as collateral, because he knew VR3 was coming.
- **Why he left Tochi.** The betrayal and the mocking, not wanting to be at Tochi because of his family ("why am I here?"), "I can go my own route," needing to make money, worrying about his younger siblings and about Chiyo covering everything, knowing the Tabuchis want more than money now. Chiyo has money and wants him in school.
