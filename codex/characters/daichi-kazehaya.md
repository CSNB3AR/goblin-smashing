# Daichi Kazehaya

**Role:** Tatsuya's father, deceased before the story begins.

## Facts

- Short, the shorter of Tatsuya's two parents. Tatsuya's height comes from Ohema, not him. What Daichi passed down instead was the analytical, technical mind, the tinkering instinct that keeps the Haya EB running and later gets a first-gen pod overridden.
- First-gen pod engineer. Company: Kazuhaya Tech (logo still visible on surviving pods).
- Helped build tech infrastructure at Tochi Academy, part of the family's standing there.
- Sold the original first-gen pod schematics to Kazuku (**not** the Tabuchis) before his death, to pay for Ohema's emergency hospital bills. Trusted Kazuku as a genuine business partner.
- Daichi and Kazuku weren't just a supplier and a buyer. Before Daichi's health declined, the two of them spent years shaping the actual idea of what HGO could be, not just the hardware to run it. Kazuku built and launched HGO after Daichi's death, but the vision underneath it, a full second world, not just a game, has Daichi's fingerprints on it as much as Kazuku's. This is the real weight behind "business partner," not just a schematics sale.
- **The car crash.** When Tatsuya was a child, a car accident left him the scar over his right brow and **paralyzed Daichi**. The crash came from a breakdown of the **Haya Sedan**, Daichi's own car, a **hybrid that still ran on gas** (only the Haya EB scooter is fully electric). The Tabuchis used the breakdown as a way to attack the business, and people pulled the model from the market. Daichi spent the rest of his life in a wheelchair, working from a converted corner of the apartment, with an adapted car. Tatsuya grew up with this image of him, late at night, wheelchair pulled up to a desk covered in pod components, and knows the crash did it. What he has not connected is how much of Daichi's work came out of it.
- **The crash is why he went all in on VR.** The paralysis is part of why the work became what it did: the Haya pod, the Haya EB, an adapted car. Daichi's own stated reason, something he said to Kazuku more than once and never quite said to his own family in so many words: he wanted a world where this world and that world wouldn't be so far apart, somewhere a body that couldn't move freely in this one wouldn't be the ceiling in the other. Tatsuya is chasing a version of that and is only starting to see it as his father's line.
- **Dad's manual and the letter.** Daichi sold the original first-gen schematics to Kazuku early, but kept a working manual that holds what the Tabuchis need. Folded inside is a **letter** to Tatsuya (drafted text, flagged NEW for the author to rework): *"If you're reading this, then I'm gone, and I didn't manage to deal with the Tabuchis. I can't explain everything. What lies in my manual is what they need. Do not give it to them. They have pushed me hard to sell the business, and I haven't. I hope they haven't got hold of anything that matters to their plans by the time you read this. I'm sorry to leave this on you and Chiyo. I know you can handle it. A man who couldn't walk once built a place where he could. The world there doesn't know my name. Maybe you can change that. If you want the truth, speak to the bear. He will tell you. Just be careful."* It is unsigned and marked only with a **bear paw print**, no symbol, no name. It warns and never proves, and it is why Tatsuya decides to go into HGO at all (see `chapters/chapter-01.md`). The Tabuchis have pushed Daichi hard to sell the business, and he had not. The schematics themselves never go in the game.
- Last words to Kazuku: *"If my son ever gets involved, please look after him."*
- Died about a year ago under suspicious circumstances, closed-casket funeral, never fully explained. Was in debt to the Tabuchis but refused to hand over the schematics despite pressure, even when it might have cleared the debt.
- **Author-side truth, never confirmed on the page.** A year before he died, someone pulled plug-ins from the adapted car and it malfunctioned, which looked like his own product failing. Retailers pulled back, his income collapsed, and the Tabuchis (who build cheap second-gen pods and have yakuza ties) tightened their grip and **offered to take the first-gen schematics as collateral**. He refused. Tatsuya holds only suspicion, assembled from the demands that keep circling back to his father's paperwork. Taji and Momo do not know any of this.
- **Author-side truth, updated (never confirmed on the page).** The Tabuchis hired yakuza to sabotage Daichi's vehicle, expecting it to bankrupt the family while Ohema was in the hospital. Chiyo returning was not in their plans: she began paying the fees. Kazuto hoped to take the family to court and win the business and the first-gen pod schematics that way, and it has not worked because Chiyo has her own money. Chiyo does not want to pay them and has hired lawyers (see `codex/characters/chiyo-nakamura.md`). **Open:** whether the earlier "a year before he died, plug-ins pulled from the adapted car" malfunction is the same event as the Haya Sedan breakdown or a second one; left undecided. The date of the childhood crash is unchanged.
- His decline was a slow cascade, working more and more hours, disappearing from the family's daily life, before the family was simply told there had been a funeral.
- Tatsuya has never processed the closed casket. It sits as an open wound and an open question.

## The funeral and Kazuto's approach

The funeral itself was held and funded by the Tabuchis, which is how Kazuto got a foothold. At the funeral, Kazuto Tabuchi personally approached Tatsuya, not Chiyo, framed as an offer to help ("do this for your father, it'll cover..."), about the old schematics/computer files specifically. Tatsuya refused any contact, in part because Eren had already betrayed him by that point and he wasn't about to trust anything Tabuchi-shaped. This is the origin of the year of pressure that follows.

**Only Tatsuya knows this.** Chiyo assumes the pressure is ordinary debt collection, she was never at that conversation and doesn't know the files are the real target. Keep this as Tatsuya's private knowledge, surfaced sparingly and never as a dumped flashback, not something other characters reference. See `codex/subplots/tabuchi-schematics-and-family-debt.md`.
