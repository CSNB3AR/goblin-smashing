# Arya Sakura

**Role:** Leon's younger sister. Future ally/teammate. Surname **Sakura**, shared with Leon, easy to rename.

## Appearance

Red hair, street-biker aesthetic (per the old draft, carried forward), own HGO drive and account.

## Facts

- Enters the story slowly and deliberately. Their actual first contact, at the front desk when Tatsuya first comes looking for Leon, is brief and purely transactional, she points him toward Leon and the tech, nothing more, a beat of mutual noticing since he's around her age. That's it, not a conversation, not the start of anything yet.
- The real curiosity builds afterward, over multiple further visits. She notices patterns: Leon's business changing, an active VR presence around the arcade, being turned down herself a few times when she wanted to use the pods.
- That's what sparks her real interest. She researches Tatsuya independently and watches his gameplay footage before ever actually approaching him with intent.
- She's been playing competitively for years, she has no reason to casually team up with a stranger, so her eventual approach must be earned and specific, not casual.
- Sharp and guarded.

## Guild backstory (background, later chapters)

Her all-girls guild, **Crimson Static** (see `codex/pro-scene/guilds.md`), fractured after a real falling-out among the members, leaving Arya's own crew small heading into the Hearthfire expansion. Part of why she's genuinely available to ask to join Tatsuya's adventure, not just narrative convenience. Crimson Static reached the Season Zero finale through the **Fire Festival** guild event, winning it with the quest **"The Second Dawn"**: the guild saved Prince Dorrin from a setup by a player guild running a quest with Prince Maldren, using intel from other players (see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`). That took the last qualifying spot, the **eighth seed**, which is what cost Eren's Kansai Black Lotus its top-eight place. She's an established guild leader in her own right even before any of this lands on the page. She is an **information broker**, a highly skilled Spellforger, competitive and hot-spirited, and some players call her a traitor for leaking one guild's plan to an NPC prince. She is a second-year at her school (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`).

**The collapse, full sequence (surfaces Chapter 9).** Crimson Static went in as a three-leader guild (Arya plus two others) and was cut in the first days of the Season Zero finale week without taking a single skirmish, the 72-hour death locks bleeding its roster dry. **Arya never kicked anyone.** (Infighting, the death locks and Sana and Nao's departure are what sink the run.) She was the only one of the three who actually played it like real competition (best kill/point ratio on the team by a wide margin); the other two, **Sana Domoto** and **Nao Fujita**, were chasing viewership and stream moments over the actual matches, and after the loss, they turned on her instead. Sana ran the real damage: publicly blaming Arya's leadership and her relationship for the loss, claiming she demoralized the team. Nao was more a follower, peer-pressured along, never badmouthed Arya publicly herself, but left alongside Sana all the same. It worked: the guild bled from 80-90+ members down to roughly three who actually knew what happened and stayed.

A third, separate departure around the same time: **Jiro Amano**, an old Crimson Static member with real trade connections, NPCs and players both, does Arya one last favor before he leaves the guild himself, later extending to Tatsuya (see `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md` and `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md` for the PK_Wraith-loot payoff this sets up). He's not proud of the timing, he let Sana's rumor campaign carry him along even though he could have stayed, but he does the favor anyway before walking away. Distinct from the unnamed auction-house specialist Arya later recruits while actually rebuilding, keep the two separate, don't merge them.

Arya rebuilt under a new guild name with a couple of new recruits, one of them the unnamed auction-house specialist mentioned above, she leans on him for gear logistics. She's currently sitting on a fresh, low-level mage build (level 10-11), most of her discipline points still unspent, deliberately held back since the collapse rather than sunk into a build she can't yet commit to.

**The real pressure underneath it.** Her mother wants her in America for medical school, a real, live option, not a bluff. Arya doesn't want to go, wants to stay in Tokyo, near Leon, and build something real in HGO first. Her entire read on the current moment, the guild, the Crownfall run, all of it, is filtered through needing this to actually work before that decision gets made for her. **The real foundation underneath this, not yet stated on the page beyond what Chapter 10 Part One already implies through Leon:** this isn't just preference, it's a live custody situation, Leon won formal guardianship of her years ago on the strength of their late grandfather's legal backing, and without that money now, her parents pushing to bring her to America is the custody fight quietly threatening to reopen. See `codex/subplots/leon-arya-custody-battle.md` for the full mechanism.

**School:** attends a local Tokyo public school, not Tochi Academy, a different background from Tatsuya's.

**Crownfall's semifinal loss is a real, live stake for her specifically, not just a competitive disappointment.** Going in, she'd privately been ready to concede to her mother and leave for America if the tournament run didn't prove something real. It doesn't end in a win, the guild is eliminated in the semis, but reaching that far as an 8th-seed underdog is real enough that it doesn't close the door either, left genuinely open rather than resolved.

**Act Two: the Tochi scholarship.** On the strength of winning Tochi's representative slot (see `codex/pro-scene/tochi-representative-qualifier.md`), Tochi extends a real scholarship to her (alongside Pac_Pac, see `codex/characters/pac-pac.md`), enrolling her in the school itself, not just a sponsorship gesture. This is the real turn for her arc, and not only emotionally: it demonstrates she earned this on tournament merit, not charity, and it's the actual legal linchpin in the reopened custody fight (see `codex/subplots/leon-arya-custody-battle.md`), concrete, institutional proof that undercuts her parents' case. Draws real pushback, family and/or agents showing up to contest it, and Leon steps in on her behalf, flatly stating he'll bring in lawyers if it comes to that, a real, dramatized family-pressure conflict, not settled quickly or quietly. See `codex/pro-scene/haya-storm.md` for the team this scholarship keeps her on.

**Also Act Two: old guildmates resurface.** Sana Domoto and Nao Fujita come back into the picture now that she's a real, publicly visible player, stirring fresh guild-scene drama, callouts and rumor-mongering, consistent with exactly what already cost her the guild once (see the collapse sequence above). A real, personal-stakes thread distinct from the custody fight, running in parallel.

## Personality

High-energy, passionate, slightly chaotic. Plays aggressive and loud, gets easily tilted by lag or toxic teammates, then laughs it off. Refuses to read tutorials or guides, forces her way through everything by stubborn trial-and-error. Extremely expressive, her face and hands do half the talking. Fiercely protective of her people, defends them hard, carries lower-skilled friends without hesitation.

Her information-broker role is vital after Thrash Hill: she sells the legacy drops Tatsuya cannot use (through her auction contact Jiro Amano) and tracks the guild chats on his location. She joins his adventure first and his guild later, because Crownfall is guild-only and Tatsuya has to found one (see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). She is a Spellforger who tracks everything about the new game as it starts.

- **Her trading tip (author-stated, future scene).** She tells Tatsuya what she did in the beta: traded items she could not get off the auction board directly with other players, and came out ahead because of the insane tax. She says it plainly as her own history; he is the one who connects it to his own legacy drops and a business. Selling legacy items on the board is not viable for him right now because of PK_Wraith's group and the blacklist risk.

- **Her points edge (author-stated).** Crimson Static scaled high in seasonal points even though it was dysfunctional, which makes Arya a major asset to Tatsuya: she is an information broker and she knows how to scale points. The dysfunction: the guild was full of simps; its three girl-gamer leads collapsed after bad comms and nobody but Arya took it seriously; the comps were not serious, so serious guilds rolled them; in raids they got zoned out as alliances formed against them. This sits beside the collapse write-up above. The author's remark about her wider backstory was cut off and is not guessed.
