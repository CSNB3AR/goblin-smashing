# Call of the Bones Necklace

**Type:** Object, in-game. Piece 1 of 7 in Shiv's Warset. Tatsuya's signature goblin item.

## Facts

**World-tier** (purple) bound item, granted by Shiv during the "Call of the Bones" World Quest, the top of the rarity ladder (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`), reserved for World Quest rewards. Its card lists everything it grants in one place, so the item and its abilities always visibly correspond: an additional bonus to Fortune, negates the goblin Wisdom penalty, unlocks the Shaman's Veil spirit-magic path, and **unlocks The Bones Speak**, its own active combat ability. It's not a stat/ability grant alone: bonding evolves Tatsuya's race entry itself from Goblin to an advanced sub-race, **Shiv Goblin** (see `codex/lore/playable-races-and-character-creation.md`), the mechanical backbone behind the white-hair transformation and the camp's immediate, unearned elder treatment.

**Full ability grant, Chapter 7 (revised).** Beyond The Bones Speak, the bond also directly grants Tatsuya **Shiv-Touched Flare** (his existing Goblin Flare now cast from the palm rather than pulled from a pouch, doubles as an ally signal and minor two-tick fire damage, Wisdom-gated) and **Rune Priming** (see below), and his Whispers of Shiv passive additionally grants **Call of Command** (basic voice-directed squad commands/prompts: return, scout, call to arms). All three were originally slated for Ru in the first telling of this scene; a deliberate author revision moved them onto Tatsuya's own kit instead. Ru's blessing keeps only Toxic Breath as its mechanical gain (see `codex/characters/young-goblin-companion-candidate.md`).

**Bonding is consent-based.** Shiv asks outright before the bond forms, an explicit accept prompt for the advanced race, not an automatic or forced transformation. Confirmed Chapter 7 ("The First Disciple").

**Transformation, concrete detail (Chapter 7).** Bonding turns Tatsuya's hair pure white and shifts his skin tone lighter too, a shade off standard camp-goblin green, less saturated, reading as something in between rather than either race cleanly. The race evolution also carries a real level jump, Tatsuya goes from level 2 to level 7 in the moment the bond takes, ten Discipline points to spend on the spot (two per level, five levels), four into Wisdom and the remaining six into Fortune, continuing his crit-focused build. The Wisdom investment isn't what clears the tier-1 casting gate itself, see the Wisdom-18 threshold note below, it's on top of a bypass the bond already grants.

**Marking recognition:** goblins, at least older/elder ones, can identify a Shiv Goblin on sight purely from the necklace's visible marking, even if they never personally met Shiv themselves. See `codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`. **This reads as authority independent of, and potentially parallel to, Sevish's and Wyx's own gold-tag chieftain authority**, a literal vessel-of-Shiv read rather than something the clan's leadership grants. In practice, the actual on-page reaction the first time this happens (Chapter 7) is astonishment and confusion giving way to excitement, not automatic deference, camp goblins and even Sevish/Wyx themselves need a moment to process an elder-level marking appearing on someone who was a prisoner minutes earlier. Informally, goblins who witness a Shiv Goblin read the mark as roughly Hobgoblin-tier standing, even though it's a distinct evolution tag from an actual Hobgoblin (see `codex/characters/young-goblin-companion-candidate.md` for Ru's own, separate Hobgoblin evolution), a social read on power level, not a mechanical equivalence.

**Wisdom restriction removed, mechanically.** The necklace doesn't just improve his odds of clearing the tier-1 casting threshold, it bypasses the Wisdom-18 gate outright for him specifically (see `codex/magic/overview.md`), a one-off exception tied to the bond rather than something he earned by hitting the number. Investing further Wisdom past that still matters for how far he can grow within the path once it's open, access and power are two different gates.

**Death-penalty exemption.** The necklace is a bound item and cannot be dropped or traded under any circumstance, including the standard on-death item-drop roll (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`, Player death penalty). Same exemption applies to the Gambler's Earring (see `codex/objects/gamblers-earring.md`), both are race-roll or World Quest-origin items. Everything else Tatsuya carries or wears stays at normal drop risk on death.

**Visual reference (locked), Ancient Bone Flame Barrage:** a layered, fire-orange triangular rune circle, glowing runic text ringing the outer edge, a small glowing orb suspended at the circle's center.

**Affinity:** Dark (now Book of the Veil, see `codex/magic/book-of-the-veil.md`), tied directly to Shiv and the spirit-magic path the bond opens, not a separate choice.

Deepens the drive-color bond described in `codex/objects/hgo-access-drive.md` once bonded (his drive already reads emerald green from the rare-variant roll at creation, see `codex/lore/playable-races-and-character-creation.md`).

## The Bones Speak

A separate ability from Gambler's Gambit (the goblin racial passive from his rare-variant creation roll, see `codex/lore/playable-races-and-character-creation.md` and `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`), the two just share a gambling/Fortune theme, not an origin. Gambler's Gambit governs loot; The Bones Speak governs combat, and it belongs to the necklace alone.

**Design rule:** The Bones Speak never hands Tatsuya a borrowed or generic effect. It only ever amplifies or redirects abilities that are already his. One activation per in-game day, "the bones only speak once before dawn," and he chooses the mode at the moment he invokes it:

- **Amplify** (proactive) — he picks one of his own already-known abilities (Goblin Gas, Ancient Bone Flame Barrage, a Shaman's Veil effect, and so on) and supercharges it for this use. Fortune determines how strong the amplification lands: a clean high roll is a real power spike, a weak roll is a partial or fizzled boost, but it is always his own move, just bigger.
- **Reflect** (reactive, desperate) — when he's about to take a hit, he can gamble everything and try to turn the attack back on the attacker. Whether it lands clean or only partially connects, invoking it this way crashes his Energy down to 1 as the cost, a genuine last-resort button that leaves him on the brink either way. Fortune determines whether the reflect fully lands or only partially connects.

Both modes draw on the same single daily charge; he can't use both in one day. The necklace's power is about what Tatsuya already has, not what he's handed.

Goblin Gas, the Goblin Gas + fire = **Goblin Flare** combo, and Ancient Bone Flame Barrage (with its toxin-effect bonus) stay exactly as established in the old draft, unaffected by this rework, they belong to the goblin/shaman kit generally, not specifically to the necklace, and are exactly the kind of ability The Bones Speak's Amplify mode is built to juice.

**Ancient Bone Flame Barrage, mechanical depth (Chapter 6-7).** The already-locked visual reference (a layered, fire-orange triangular rune circle, glowing runic text ringing the outer edge, a small glowing orb suspended at the center) reflects the actual activation requirement: the spell needs three rune-points primed and placed before it can fire, a real setup cost, heavy on mana, and tactically risky, an obvious rune circle is something an alert enemy avoids stepping near if they recognize what it is. Currently capped well below its full potential by Tatsuya's own level, a genuinely tiered ultimate that scales toward a Tier 8 effect at max level (see `codex/magic/overview.md`), not something he can reliably pull off yet. This stays Tatsuya's own signature ability, not something a companion independently duplicates. **Rune Priming**, granted directly by the necklace bond in Chapter 7, is the ability that primes and places those three rune-points, Tatsuya's own, solo, not a two-person setup with Ru (an earlier telling of this scene had Ru priming for him; the author moved the ability onto Tatsuya's own kit instead, see `codex/characters/young-goblin-companion-candidate.md`).
