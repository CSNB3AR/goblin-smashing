# Pod / Helmet Hardware

**Type:** Object/tech, real-world. Two distinct hardware form factors, not just three tiers of the same thing.

## The three generations

- **First-gen pods (Kazuhaya Tech originals):** full-body immersion pods, the "metal coffin" style, built on Daichi's original schematics; his business owned the first-gen pods. Predate HGO. Third-gen architecture descends from first-gen DNA, which is why a manual override/hack can run HGO on one at all, but doing so causes real lag and degradation, hardware never built for the game it's now being asked to run. Leon has two of these pods, but only one is actually functional, the sole first-gen pod still running in the area, the other is a dead shell he hasn't repaired. **The degradation is ongoing, not a one-time cost:** running current the hardware was never designed to carry wears it down over real time. Sustaining an overridden pod long-term requires sourcing upgraded replacement components (the same family of parts used inside third-gen helmets) and doing hands-on manual maintenance, the same kind of upkeep Tatsuya already does on the Haya EB (see `codex/objects/haya-eb.md`). He recognizes this instinctively while performing the Chapter 2 override, reading it straight off the schematics, nobody else is going to keep this running for him. See `codex/subplots/technical-arc-leon-and-sakura-arcade.md`.

**End of Act One: the pod itself finally gives out.** Distinct from, and later than, Leon's external monitor rig, already resolved via a Tochi-lead parts fix in Chapter 8-9 (see `codex/lore/external-monitor-and-full-dive-safety.md`), this is the actual first-gen pod Tatsuya dives through, worn down by the same ongoing degradation stated above. It finally needs real maintenance right as Tatsuya, for the first time, actually has the sponsorship money to fix it properly instead of just patching by. The concrete trigger behind Act Two's already-locked "pod-building/parts logistics thread continues, the reason he came back in the first place" (`outline/act-two-haya-storm-rise.md`).
- **Second-gen pods (Tabuchi-backed):** also full-body "metal coffin" pods, made WITHOUT the true schematics (Daichi refused to sell them). Predate HGO entirely, originally built for other VR games before HGO existed. Only became HGO-compatible after the third-gen exclusivity window closed, and the lag is real: hardware that was never designed for this specific game, running it anyway. Funded, going back to the family's early days in VR hardware, by yakuza money laundered in through the Tabuchis, part of why the family is under real financial pressure of its own.
- **Third-gen hardware (Kazuku / Nexus Enterprises):** not a pod at all, a **helmet**. Launched together with HGO specifically, and deliberately, because it's broadcast and arena-friendly: a seated player in a helmet reads on camera and in a live stadium far better than someone lying motionless in a coffin-shaped pod. Still fully nerve-synced, real sensation, real damage feedback (magnified but capped, see the pass-out limit below), just without the bulk. Standard, official HGO hardware today. It is so advanced because Daichi and Kazuku were partners and Daichi passed some of his first-gen work on to him, which the second-gen pods (made without the true schematics) never had.

## Exclusivity timeline

- HGO launched exclusively on third-gen helmets only for the first 6 months, the only hardware built for the game from day one.
- After that window, second-gen pods were permitted to connect, but with real lag, hardware built for other VR titles pressed into service for a game it was never designed to run, real business pressure on the Tabuchis to solve their hardware gap or lose the market.
- Once third-gen drives and helmets became more affordable, internet cafes running second-gen pods could pay Nexus Enterprises a fee to exchange/upgrade to third-gen. Most major cafes took the deal, which pushed second-gen hardware out of most of the Japanese market, a real financial wound to the Tabuchis and part of what's driving their pressure on Tatsuya.

## Damage and the pass-out limit

Full sensation and real damage feedback across all hardware generations, magnified for drama but capped for safety. Taking enough damage in a fight causes a pass-out, which carries its own real-world penalty, a 24-hour lock-out by default (provisional).

**Resolved** (see `codex/lore/external-monitor-and-full-dive-safety.md`): pass-out and the 72-hour death lock (`codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`) are two distinct tiers, not the same mechanic described twice. Pass-out is a forced real-world extraction triggered by the external-monitor/failsafe system stepping in, a shorter safety-driven penalty. The 72-hour lock is the separate, harsher penalty for actual in-game character death.


## First-gen pod faults and repair (author-stated)

- **Overheating cause.** The first-gen pod at Sakura Arcade overheats because its **processor core** (like a GPU, a massive core) is at about 20 percent. Third-gen cores are expensive. Tochi's technology lab has them, which is a reason Tatsuya wants to go back.
- **Drive slot.** The slot built for the new HGO drives is missing; the drive is forced into an old slot that does not fit.
- **Other parts.** The cores are easy to get. Some wiring is needed and is cheap.
- **Hardwire.** He can hardwire and power it directly, as with the EB, but that burns the core's remaining life. He can only run it that way for a limited time before the core dies and must be replaced. He has to charge it from the wall for a while first, or he drains the core.
- **Second pod.** Dead because its core is already gone; a dead core cannot be charged.
- **No numbers set** beyond the 20 percent; lifespan and part names stay qualitative.

## Pod naming and the third-gen lookup (author-stated, Chapter Seven)

- **He has not found any third-gen processor yet.** Chapter Three's blackout search turned up nothing. In Chapter Seven, on his phone, he researches third-gen processor cores and every result leads back to **Nexus Enterprises** and its patent, the piece the third-gen pods and helmets are built on. Never sold openly (copying risk); only partners, and Tochi is the one school openly on that list.
- **"Gen" is a colloquial label.** People say first, second and third generation because each is the next version of a VR pod, even though they come from different companies with different schematics and patents: Kazehaya's (Kazuhaya Tech, first), the Tabuchis' (second), Nexus Enterprises and Kazuku's (third). Each could carry its own product name, which would keep the three apart on the page. OPEN: whether to name them; no names invented yet, and the page keeps the gen labels for now.
- **Fan (author-approved).** The pod's cooling fan whines as it winds down in the Chapter Three blackout and is still dragging in Chapter Seven.

## Core countdown and the two fixes (author-stated, Chapter Seven; supersedes "Tochi's technology lab has them" as a known fact)

- **Countdown.** The first-gen core read 20 percent in Chapter Three. Every hotwired session burns about one point; after the first login it reads 19, which gives him roughly 20 logins before it dies. He means to fix it well before that. The convincing point for Leon is capability: he understands Daichi's work well enough to keep it running.
- **Option one, temporary:** a second-gen core to get it running again, cheaper and not permanent. Second-gen cores carry faults (open: which, kept vague on the page), which is why people paid extra to go third-gen.
- **Option two, the real fix:** a third-gen core, which Nexus Enterprises patents and never sells. His only discoverable tie is the Nexus Enterprises sponsorship of Tochi Academy that the school announced about a year ago. That is a lead, not proof there is a core in the building. It is a discovery he has to make, so the page no longer says he saw the core logo in Tochi's lab or that the lab holds one. Instructors, not students, would hold any such access.
