# Playable Races & Character Creation

## The Rare Race Roll

At creation, every playable race has a chance to roll into a rare, upgraded variant right at the start, not just something earned through a later class promotion. An elf might roll High Elf, for instance, a genuine bonus tier with its own rare-class abilities, the equivalent of a mid-game knight-to-paladin promotion handed to a player on day one instead of earned over dozens of levels. It's a gacha-flavored bonus baked into the character screen, not something every player gets, and not something anyone can force.

**This roll is separate from, and unrelated to, whether a character can later be reset.** Every character gets the rare-variant roll at creation, no exceptions, Tatsuya included. Whether a drive supports resetting the character afterward is a completely different system, see `codex/objects/hgo-access-drive.md`, his drive doesn't support it because it's unsupported first-gen hardware, nothing to do with the creation-time roll.

Tatsuya's roll hit. Browsing race options on an overridden first-gen pod, curious despite himself, he taps Goblin on impulse, weakest race in the game, dull red on the selection wheel, just to see what happens. The arcade's power blips immediately after, and the reboot locks the selection in permanently, no confirmation screen, no do-over, no reset ever offered on this drive, that part of the accident is real. But the rare-variant roll still ran, same as it does for every character, and his came back rare: **Gambler's Gambit**, a Fortune bonus and a once-per-day chance after combat to roll for Rare-or-higher loot. The ability manifests physically the moment he's actually in the game world, as the **Gambler's Earring** (see `codex/objects/gamblers-earring.md`), equipped in Chapter 3 right after he drops into Bloodhurst, not something abstract living only in a menu. This is his real, concrete plan for actually generating income while he's got the game open, not just XP for its own sake, a repeatable, farmable shot at something sellable. The irony isn't that he got nothing, it's that the one piece of luck the system handed him landed on the one race everybody else throws away. (Much later, the Call of the Bones Necklace grants a separate ability of its own, Bone Surge, see `codex/objects/call-of-the-bones-necklace.md`, unrelated to Gambler's Gambit beyond a shared Fortune/gambling theme.)

## Playable races

The full list a player browses at creation: **Human, Elf, Orc, Beastfolk, Goblin, Dwarf, Frostborn, Drakekin** (rarer and harder to unlock, not tied to a starting zone, more of an endgame/achievement race). Each of the first six has a home zone in Eryndal (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`): Human/Lunarest, Frostborn and Dwarf/Valefrost, Beastfolk and Goblin/Myrrhwood, Orc/Sunmarch, Elf/Azure Expanse. No Undead race, deliberately cut, doesn't serve this story.

## The goblin glitch, mechanism

The override itself, Tatsuya getting an incompatible first-gen pod to run HGO software at all, is a real, deliberate technical act, using the same tinkering instinct that keeps the Haya EB alive (see `codex/objects/haya-eb.md`). The race selection, by contrast, is a real but impulsive choice, not a considered one: mid character-creation, he'd already decided on Elf, but paused on Goblin (weakest in the game, marked in dull red for its rock-bottom stats, not locked or unselectable) and tapped it anyway, dry curiosity more than intent, "just to see what would happen." The arcade's power blips right after, the pod's internal battery pack is dead and it's been running on direct wall power only, and the reboot locks the selection in permanently before he can back out or reconsider. **What makes this a genuine accident isn't the choice, it's the permanence.** A single impulsive tap becomes an irreversible, unresettable character, something that would have cost him nothing to undo under any normal circumstances. **Separately, the standard rare-variant roll every character gets still fires at the moment of creation**, unaffected by any of this, and his hits rare, see above.

See `codex/objects/hgo-access-drive.md` for the resulting drive-color mechanic.

## Shiv Goblin (advanced rare race, not selectable at creation)

**Not on the playable-races list above, not something anyone can pick or roll into at character creation.** Shiv Goblin is an advanced rare race reachable only in-game, by bonding with the Call of the Bones Necklace (see `codex/objects/call-of-the-bones-necklace.md`) during the "Call of the Bones" World Quest. Bonding doesn't just grant stat bonuses and unlock the Shaman's Veil path, it evolves Tatsuya's race entry itself, Goblin to Shiv Goblin, a real upgrade tier rather than a cosmetic-only change. The white-hair transformation is the visible marker of the evolution, not a separate effect. See `codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md` for how other goblins recognize the mark on sight.

## Lumi, the character-creation guide (Hearthfire-era canon)

**Lumi** is the cute system guide who walks players through character creation. She glitches when the goblin glitch hits Tatsuya (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`). Open-beta characters created after the Hearthfire announcement are permanent, and during Season Zero every race starts in Lunarest (see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`).

## Choosing a patron

At the start of the game a player also **chooses a patron to follow**. There are five patrons and each race leans toward one (dwarves follow a patron, goblins follow a patron). **Seranis** blesses demi-humans and is the goblins' patron (see `codex/lore/patrons-and-world-weapons.md`). In Tatsuya's case Lumi's walkthrough is interrupted by the power cut and the goblin glitch, so how his patron is applied is open.
