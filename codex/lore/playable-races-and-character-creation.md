# Playable Races & Character Creation

## The Rare Race Roll

At creation, every playable race has a chance to roll into a rare, upgraded variant right at the start, not just something earned through a later class promotion. An elf might roll High Elf, for instance, a genuine bonus tier with its own rare-class abilities, the equivalent of a mid-game knight-to-paladin promotion handed to a player on day one instead of earned over dozens of levels. It's a gacha-flavored bonus baked into the character screen, not something every player gets, and not something anyone can force.

This is the mechanic Tatsuya's goblin lock interacts with and subverts (see the goblin glitch mechanism below): browsing race options on an overridden first-gen pod, curious despite himself, he taps Goblin on impulse, weakest race in the game, dull red on the selection wheel, just to see what happens. The arcade's power blips immediately after, and the reboot locks the selection in permanently, no confirmation screen, no do-over, no reset ever offered on this drive. No rare roll ever happened, that system never even engaged. The irony isn't that he didn't choose it, he did, on impulse, the way anyone might poke at the worst option out of curiosity. The irony is that an ordinary, harmless impulse became permanent and irreversible through pure hardware accident, at the exact moment he had no way of knowing it would.

## Playable races

The full list a player browses at creation: **Human, Elf, Orc, Beastfolk, Goblin, Dwarf, Frostborn, Drakekin** (rarer and harder to unlock, not tied to a starting zone, more of an endgame/achievement race). Each of the first six has a home zone in Eryndal (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`): Human/Lunarest, Frostborn and Dwarf/Valefrost, Beastfolk and Goblin/Myrrhwood, Orc/Sunmarch, Elf/Azure Expanse. No Undead race, deliberately cut, doesn't serve this story.

## The goblin glitch, mechanism

The override itself, Tatsuya getting an incompatible first-gen pod to run HGO software at all, is a real, deliberate technical act, using the same tinkering instinct that keeps the Haya EB alive (see `codex/objects/haya-eb.md`). The race selection, by contrast, is a real but impulsive choice, not a considered one: mid character-creation, he'd already decided on Elf, but paused on Goblin (weakest in the game, marked in dull red for its rock-bottom stats, not locked or unselectable) and tapped it anyway, dry curiosity more than intent, "just to see what would happen." The arcade's power blips right after, the pod's internal battery pack is dead and it's been running on direct wall power only, and the reboot locks the selection in permanently before he can back out or reconsider. **What makes this a genuine accident isn't the choice, it's the permanence.** A single impulsive tap becomes an irreversible, unresettable character, something that would have cost him nothing to undo under any normal circumstances.
