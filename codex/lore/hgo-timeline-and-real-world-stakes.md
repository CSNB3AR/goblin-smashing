# HGO Timeline & Real-World Stakes

## Timeline

- HGO has been in a **closed beta (Season Zero)** of roughly eight to nine months (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`): about 5,000 players from roughly 870,000 pre-orders, one server, one continent (Lunarest, see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`).
- The beta ran on third-gen hardware, with second-gen access opening later and with lag.
- Guilds and sponsorships formed in Season Zero around the princes' armies and the mercenary contracts city lords offered.
- **"Tonight" (story present, December 10th, 2045, a Sunday, "Finale Sunday")** is the **last battle of Season Zero** (the Battle of the Snowy Plains), streamed from Singapore as spectacle, sponsored by small companies such as the WattsUp Corporation (HGO's stock is high, so brands buy exposure on the closed beta), and the Hearthfire expansion gets announced live during the broadcast. Kazuku's ad bundles Hearthfire, the Siege mode, the Crownfall Siege event with a 10 million yen prize pool, open beta tonight and a 72-hour 50 percent drive discount. There is **no Siege Pro League** in the announcement. Tatsuya avoids the broadcast because HGO reminds him of Eren. Fifteen days out from Crownfall (December 25th), six of which (roughly December 10th-16th) Chiyo and the twins spend away on their Kyoto trip, see `codex/subplots/tochi-academy-return.md`.
- **Hearthfire expansion launch date: January 1st**, a week after Crownfall (December 25th). The closed-beta character lock (see `codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`) triggers at this full launch, not at the announcement. Crownfall sits in the gap between announcement and launch as a Siege showcase for open-beta characters. Full sequence in `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`.
- **Thrash clan's Myrrhwood departure, real trigger locked Chapter 8-9.** The clan leaves Bloodhurst for Myrrhwood before the new year turns over, i.e. before the Hearthfire expansion launches, but the actual decision point is the Chapter 8-9 near-wipe (see `codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`), not the calendar alone. See `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md` for the deadline/rendezvous mechanic this sets up for Tatsuya specifically.
- **Hearthfire expansion, the actual patch, roughly 14 hours of full server downtime** (revised up from an earlier 10-12 hour estimate). Worth landing the real weight of this explicitly when it's on the page: this is the **first full server shutdown in HGO's life**. People have semi-lived in this game for the whole beta, in and out to eat and sleep but otherwise constantly connected, routine maintenance has never taken the whole server down before, this is a genuinely big deal, not routine housekeeping. Not narrated in exhaustive detail unless a scene calls for it, but locked for consistency: on return, a player outside any guild hall lands at their race's home region; a player inside a guild hall stays there unless that specific hall has its own unlocked travel access. This is why staying inside or outside a guild hall when the servers drop matters, a real, felt logistics decision, not flavor.
- **Patch notes drip out in the run-up to the shutdown, PvP in detail, PvE deliberately vague.** Kazuku releases the PvP-side patch notes with real specificity, King of the Hill map evolution, deck-building/world-item-transcendence lore, and the Siege mode's rules. The PvE-side patch notes are deliberately kept vague on the page, summarized in-story as "miles of stuff he doesn't want to read right now" rather than dumped on the reader, that's a deliberate choice, not a gap to fill in later.
- **No fast travel.** Real elapsed time to cross regions on foot or by mount, distinct from the arena hub's teleport-with-zone-lock system (see `codex/arena/overview.md`), which is a deliberate, narrow exception, not the general rule. Waystones are very expensive and only NPCs with a sigil can use waypoints (14-day recharge, no player holds one). After Crownfall each player gets one free teleport at the Lunarest waypoint to a home kingdom (see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). Reaching Myrrhwood specifically also requires real ship/ferry time, see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`. **Locked duration: four to five real/in-game days** for the actual ocean crossing, the concrete reason distant crew or allies take real time reacting to events elsewhere, if that lag is ever needed on the page.
- **Idle/offline characters go idle in place, they don't teleport to safety.** A character who logs off, or otherwise goes idle, stays exactly where they were, defenseless while idle. A true safe zone protects them regardless; a contested or red zone (open ocean, the Outskirts, anywhere hostile players or NPCs can reach) doesn't, they're genuinely vulnerable to capture or death while offline. This is the concrete, mechanical reason Tatsuya's needed guards during past log-offs in Bloodhurst (contested, not a true safe zone), and matters again for anyone going idle mid-ocean-crossing.

## Real-world stakes — keep this simple, do not over-explain

HGO generates enormous real money because in-game currency and items convert directly into real-world currency. Because of that money, the game has attracted real-world business interest, government interest, and criminal interest, everyone wants a piece of this new economic space, and no country wants to be left out of it. Criminal activity in the real world connects naturally to the open-world item-drop mechanic: steal valuable items via in-game death, launder or sell for real money. **PK_Wraith's crew is a concrete, in-fiction example of this**, not just in-game griefers, a real organization that's been gaining darker real-world pull alongside its in-game reputation (see `codex/characters/pk-wraith.md`).

**Most people chasing HGO's money are chasing the obvious path, prize pools and fame, that's genuinely where the visible money is, everyone's racing for a piece of it.** The deeper opportunity, actually bringing a real business into the game the way Kazuku's own "bring your business here" push invites, is something anyone smart will see. Hearthfire is "The New World" and its territory is open, so many people will try to make it big or find a business idea for the game, and few will pull it off (see `codex/characters/yuna-yunaile.md`'s family for one example already chasing it). It is real, live, open space, and Tatsuya is one entrepreneur among many in it, not the lone visionary. It ties forward to his own eventual trade-company ambitions (see `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).

**Why "bring your business here" is even a pitch worth making.** It is legitimate recruitment: it genuinely draws more people in because the game really does pay, and it is the main hook for anyone stuck in debt or needing a reset, since a game that pays can offer a way out. That is why Tatsuya joins at all (the money and Ohema's deadline first, the hints from his father second). At launch, HGO barely had a real player economy at all: NPC vendors sell mostly junk, so most player-to-player trade has been straight barter, item for item, not currency-driven. That gap is also why crafting guilds became genuinely rich rather than a niche hobby, not everyone lands a world-tier or even a rare drop, but a skilled crafter can produce a genuinely competitive unique item on demand, which made crafting the real, reliable alternative to relying on RNG drops (see `codex/lore/world-quests.md` for how world-tier items get nerfed but still matter in ranked play, exactly the kind of gear top players fight over, and exactly why crafted alternatives have real market value). A free MMO with no subscription needs a real transaction layer to survive at all, HGO's own lack of meaningful microtransactions at launch is a genuine structural gap, not a design virtue, and it's part of what the Hearthfire expansion's territory-ownership push (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`) is actually trying to fix.

**Open-world territory control is Node Wars**, the main war content after Hearthfire (see `codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`), and it works like a real node-war system (players familiar with Black Desert Online's node wars will recognize the shape): contested zones grant buffs and minor perks to whoever holds them, genuinely nice to have, not worth organizing your life around. Owning an actual province, or eventually a kingdom, is a different tier of stakes entirely, real income, not just a buff, which is exactly the kind of prize that pulls in both legitimate player-run guilds and the darker, real-money-laundering side of the game's economy (see above) chasing the same territory for very different reasons.

**Delivery mechanism, for Chapter 2's opening, not Chapter 1.** This surfaces as an ordinary morning news segment, framed as entertainment news, covering the Season Zero finale (the prize figure gets mentioned) alongside a darker undertone: criminal enterprises using the open in-game economy to launder money, nations that have tried and failed to make direct contact with HGO's ownership. See `outline/chapters-1-7-summary.md` for how this actually landed in the final Chapter 2 (old planning reference archived at `outline/archive/chapter-02.md`).

## Named apps and channels

- **Loop**: the social-media app where HGO engagement, clips, and reactions live, a Twitter-equivalent. Ties forward to Arya's later footage-posting beat, see `codex/subplots/aryas-loop-discovery-and-reputation.md`. **Chapter 4 detail:** the feed shows established players already switching to freshly-created post-reset characters and cashing in Legacy Points off their old ones (see the Hearthfire character-lock mechanic, `bible/game-system.md` §3), some guild leaders visibly hesitating since it means starting over at level 1, even with the new-character level cap sitting at 25 for Crownfall. This is what gives Tatsuya a real "I'm already ahead" feeling, his own character was never anything to retire in the first place.
- **HGO Companion App** — the official read-only info app: livestreams, VODs, news, a forum/Reddit-equivalent space. Not used for actual gameplay, informational only. **Streaming/partnership mechanic (locked, Chapter 5, numbers confirmed Chapter 7):** anyone can flip on a stream from their own account settings. Viewer and follower counts both update live, on the page, a streamer sees them climb in real time. The **affiliate tier** (the point subscriptions/sponsorship revenue actually unlocks) requires **500 followers**, but a streamer only finds out they've cleared it **after the session ends**, not live, no mid-stream "tier unlocked" popup. Below that threshold, going live is exposure, not income, which is the actual reason it matters to a player like Tatsuya this early: not a payout, just eyes nobody else currently has a reason to be looking with.
- **Official-channel hosting, established Chapter 7.** HGO's official channel automatically backs and hosts the stream of any player actively progressing a World Quest, standing platform practice, not something unique to Tatsuya. Redirects the channel's own viewership directly onto the hosted stream, a real platform-level spike distinct from ordinary organic growth. Happens to Tatsuya mid-Shiv-confrontation, his viewer count jumping from double digits into the thousands within the same scene, follower count climbing right alongside it, ending the chapter around 580, past the affiliate threshold without him realizing it yet. Tatsuya doesn't know the mechanic is standard practice for World Quests in the moment, guesses at it on the page ("must be because of the World Quest") without confirmation; **he confirms it himself Chapter 8, a quick search turning up that HGO auto-hosts any actively-progressing World Quest stream as standing, free platform promotion.**
- **Affiliate application and subscription mechanics, confirmed Chapter 8.** Crossing 500 followers unlocks the affiliate-application option on his HGO account, visible and clickable, but applying and actually earning from it are separate steps. **Retroactive rule:** a stream's viewer numbers never convert to subscription revenue after the fact, a streamer only earns subs from sessions run after they're already affiliate-approved, so Tatsuya's own Chapter 7 stream, despite the huge viewer spike, earned him nothing. Subscriptions run a flat **¥750/month**, grant small cosmetic VR perks (emotes players can trigger in chat-adjacent space), no expanded chat/typing privileges beyond what any viewer already has.
- **HGO TV** — Kazuku's official broadcast channel, the thing playing at WattsUp (see `codex/locations/wattsup.md`) and on Tatsuya's own TV at home. Match replays, Yuna's performances and caster appearances, and pro-scene news coverage (roster moves, Legacy Point cash-outs, format-shift fallout), all filtered on the page through Tatsuya's own insider recognition, never a narrator info-dump.

**One exception, background only:** the HGO Coin holds its value (10 shillings = 1 HGO Coin = 2 yen, see `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`) because the company behind the game has real holdings in industrial rigs and natural resources, and nobody knows who the owner is. It is mentioned in passing at most, never explained. Beyond that, **do not** invent specific real-world resource-acquisition mechanics (no doomsday market-collapse scenarios, no explicit geopolitical thriller machinery). Keep the real-world impact implied and atmospheric rather than mechanically explained. Criminals and police are background texture only. The story stays guild/economy-focused at its core, not a geopolitical thriller.

**Background mechanics (bible-only, for internal consistency, not for direct page use):** the laundering works through black-market merchants who convert illicit real-world cash into in-game value and back out again, functionally untraceable once it's passed through. HGO's internal privacy protections mean even outside enforcement (think a UN- or NATO-style unified body, never named that specifically on the page) can't easily reach in without agents operating inside the game world's own rules. This stays exactly as vague and atmospheric in the actual prose as the paragraph above already specifies, it exists here only so nothing contradicts itself later.
