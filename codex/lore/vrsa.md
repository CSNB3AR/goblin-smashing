# VRSA (Virtual Reality Safety Authority)

**Type:** Real-world regulatory body, background lore. Not a local inspector, a full international union, backed by national security authorities worldwide, legitimate cross-border jurisdiction over commercial VR hardware.

## Facts

Any business commercially operating VR hardware (arcades, cafes, anything charging the public for pod/helmet time) needs VRSA clearance before advertising or running it at scale. Exists for the same reason any real-world safety authority exists, full-dive hardware carries genuine physical risk (see the pass-out limit and hardware-generation lag issues, `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`), this isn't decorative bureaucracy.

Relevant to Leon's arc (see `codex/subplots/technical-arc-leon-and-sakura-arcade.md`): once Tatsuya fixes Sakura Arcade's second first-gen pod, Leon can't legally advertise or commercially run two working HGO pods until VRSA clears them. Keeps this whole thread grounded and mundane rather than a geopolitical-thriller device, consistent with the existing "keep real-world stakes atmospheric, not mechanically explained" rule in `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`.

## The VRSA Inspector vs. Tatsuya

**The Inspector is a regulator, not a repair technician, when it comes to the pods themselves.** He doesn't touch pod hardware or fix it. His core job is to grant (or withhold) legal clearance, functionally identical to a road closure: if VRSA hasn't signed off, a business cannot legally run that pod for paying customers, full stop, not a recommendation. Tatsuya is the only one who can actually *repair* first-gen pod hardware, that's a completely separate skill the Inspector doesn't have and doesn't need. The two roles should never be conflated on the page for the pods themselves, "the tech" is Tatsuya, never the Inspector.

**Carve-out: the external monitor station itself is VRSA's own equipment, not pod hardware.** Since the Inspector is the one who installs and certifies it (see below), servicing or repairing the monitor station is within his role, distinct from the pods. **Constraint, established Chapter 7:** he won't work on it while a diver is actively connected and dependent on it, a live safety-monitoring device doesn't get taken down for service mid-dive, that's the actual reason a real fix has to wait until the pod's powered down and the diver's out, not a hardware-access limitation.

**The clearance an Inspector grants at Sakura Arcade covers two separate things**, and they land at different times: (1) confirming the second, previously-dead pod is safe enough to bring into rotation at all, and (2) installing and certifying the external monitor station required before either pod can run anything online/multiplayer-capable (see `codex/lore/external-monitor-and-full-dive-safety.md`). A pod can be structurally sound and still not legally operable if the monitor isn't in place and certified.
