# Goblin Clan Culture & the Feral Stigma

## The stigma

An old, setting-wide prejudice: goblins (and demi-humans generally) have historically been branded "feral," an in-lore term for uncivilized, animalistic behavior, by the other races. Partially broken by Shiv (see `codex/characters/shiv.md`), the historical goblin sorcerer king, who proved goblins weren't inherently feral, which is why goblins and other demi-humans have any access to cities at all today. The stigma isn't gone, though, reputation and Karma/Alignment standing (see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`) still run low for demi-humans by default, and "feral" gets thrown around casually as an insult or a snap judgment, exactly what happens to Tatsuya in Chapter 3 when camp goblins clock him moving strangely (a real player-goblin among AI ones) and assume the worst.

## Myrrhwood's fractured clans

Without a unifying figure like Shiv, the various goblin clans across Myrrhwood (see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`) have fractured and turned on each other, exactly the behavior that reinforces the "feral" reputation from the outside. Compounding it, they're in ongoing conflict with the Beastfolk who share the region. The clans themselves increasingly believe they need a unifying figure to avoid destroying each other entirely, which is the real, in-world stakes behind the Thrash clan's push to reunify Myrrhwood under Shiv's legacy (see `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md`), not just a quest objective, a genuine cultural need the camp itself believes in.

## Village and city access, resolved

Only Thrash-clan goblins can enter villages and settlements at all. Every other goblin clan, having fractured from Shiv's original unifying philosophy after his death, reads as feral and is barred outright. This is why any new player who rolls or ends up on Goblin defaults into the Thrash clan specifically, not a feral one, and it's worth a forward note: once Tatsuya's own reputation starts making Goblin a genuinely popular race choice (see `codex/subplots/aryas-loop-discovery-and-reputation.md`), this access distinction becomes visible to the wider playerbase too, plausibly even drawing Taji himself toward rolling a Thrash goblin once he learns who Kaiseki actually is.

## Marking recognition

Bonding with the Call of the Bones Necklace evolves Tatsuya's race entry to Shiv Goblin (see `codex/objects/call-of-the-bones-necklace.md`), and that mark is culturally legible on sight: goblins, at least the older ones, can identify a Shiv Goblin purely from the necklace's visible marking, even if they personally never met Shiv. It's an ingrained piece of clan memory tied to the figure who broke the feral stigma in the first place, not something that has to be explained or proven, which is the actual in-world mechanism behind the camp's immediate, unearned elder treatment the moment the bond takes.
