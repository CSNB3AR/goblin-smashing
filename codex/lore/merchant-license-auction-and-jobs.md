# Merchant License, Auction House and Working Jobs

**Status:** Forward plot and system design, heavily Book Two. Book One only plants the pieces (see the timeline near the end). Ledger and codex only, nothing here is on the page yet. Built from the author's dictation plus existing canon. **Every number below is a proposal** unless a source file is named, because the ledger says no fee figures are set (see `outline/canon-ledger.md`, "Auction tax"). Anything in the "Open and conflicts" section needs an author decision.

## Where this sits in the story

- Node Wars and kingdom building are the real core of the game after Hearthfire (`codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`). This layer is the secondary money-making layer that feeds that war: it pays for halls, troops, gear and the Ember core trade, and it gives Tatsuya a way to earn that does not depend on winning a prize pool.
- It is the in-game payoff of Kazuku's "bring your business here" and of Tatsuya's origin motive: a trade channel that runs on written rules, not on whoever has the most reach (the PK_Wraith blacklist, see `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).
- Tatsuya is one entrepreneur among many here (Yuna's family, Nova Market, other guilds). He wins his place with connections and products, not by being first.

## Money and where the tax goes

- **Units:** 10 shillings = 1 HGO Coin = 4 yen. So 1 shilling = 0.4 yen and 1 yen = 2.5 shillings. A sale is three stages: shillings (in game), coins, then yen (bank cash-out). See `codex/lore/hearthfire-expansion-and-seasons.md`.
- **Scale check from canon:** a Minor Elixir was 21,000 shillings (2,100 coins, 8,400 yen), so a pair is 42,000 shillings (16,800 yen). Legacy items sit in the millions of shillings.
- **Every fee, cut, due, fine and wage levy in this document is a tax payment.** Each one splits two ways:
  - **City share, 60 percent.** Goes to the city treasury. In an NPC-held city the treasury pays guards, wardens and the wages in the Posts section below. In a city a player guild holds after a Node War, this share goes to that guild. This is how "holding nodes brings taxes" (Hearthfire doc) becomes real income.
  - **Realm Tithe, 40 percent.** Removed from player balances and not paid out again. The matching coin value stays with HGO. This is how HGO earns money from the market: the game wants its taxes, and a player who avoids them is taking money from the company.
- **Plain consequence:** the more a city trades, the more its holder earns, and the more HGO earns. That is why wardens exist and why the penalties below are real.

## What counts as selling

The license rule needs a clear line, because canon already has two legal ways around the board.

| Activity | License needed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Posting on the official auction board | No | The board's own fee and cut are the tax. Proposed figures: 4 percent listing fee (kept even if unsold) plus 8 percent cut on a sale, so 12 percent on a sold item. This is what Chapter 3 shows ("that tax has to eat most of it"). |
| Direct trade between two players (Arya's beta method, see `codex/characters/arya.md`) | No, up to a cap | Personal trade: up to 3 trades or 50,000 shillings (20,000 yen) in a 7 day period inside one city, and nothing displayed for sale. This keeps Arya's trick legal and explains why it works only in small amounts. |
| Anything past that cap, or any item displayed or held out for sale inside city limits | Yes | This is "selling" in the license sense: a stall, a cart, a table, a standing offer, a shop. |
| Selling to NPC vendors | No | NPC vendors pay little (canon: mostly junk), so this is not worth taxing. |
| Selling outside city limits (outskirts, camps, red zones, roads) | No | No license, and no warden, and no protection if the buyer cheats you or kills you. |

OPEN: where the Chapter 7 elixir sale happened. It shows no fee and no cut, so it fits a direct trade under the cap. Keep it that way unless the author says otherwise.

## Merchant license tiers

Licenses are bought at a city's Tollhouse (the office where the city lord's wardens keep the seal and the ledger). They are per city, last 30 real days, and are renewed for the lower renewal fee. Prices are in shillings with yen in brackets.

| Tier | Name | First fee | Renewal per 30 days | What it allows | Main limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | None | free | | Board posting, personal trade under the cap | No display, no stall |
| 1 | Peddler's Permit | 20,000 (8,000 yen) | 5,000 (2,000 yen) | Sell by hand or from a cart in one city's market square | Stock value up to 50,000 shillings. Common and Uncommon goods only. |
| 2 | Stall License | 120,000 (48,000 yen) | 30,000 (12,000 yen) | A fixed stall in one city, up to Rare goods, 3 Agent's Writs | Stock value up to 500,000 shillings, shared across holder and agents. Stall rent is separate. |
| 3 | Shop Charter | 800,000 (320,000 yen) | 200,000 (80,000 yen) | A shop in one city, up to 5 employees, 6 Writs, stored stock in a city warehouse, may file a Mark (see Brand section) | Stock cap 5,000,000 shillings. Legendary goods carry an extra duty (below). |
| 4 | Company Charter | 4,000,000 (1,600,000 yen) | 600,000 (240,000 yen) | A named trade company, up to 3 cities in one kingdom, 12 Writs in total, may hire players and NPCs as employees | One kingdom only. Needs Tier 3 held for 30 days first. |
| 5 | Auction House Charter | 15,000,000 (6,000,000 yen) plus a 5,000,000 (2,000,000 yen) bond | 1,000,000 (400,000 yen) | Run a player auction house in one city | See the Auction House section. Needs Tier 4 held for 60 days. |

- **Market Dues.** Every sale under a license pays 3 percent to the Tollhouse, lower than the 12 percent on the board. This is the whole reason to buy a license: a seller who moves volume pays less tax per sale, and the license fee is paid up front whether or not anything sells. It is also why Arya came out ahead in the beta: the board tax was so high that trading around it paid.
- **Rare Goods Duty.** Legendary items sold under any license pay an extra 4 percent. World-rarity items cannot be sold under a license at all, only on the board or by direct trade (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`).
- **Real company holders.** Kazuku's "bring your business here" lets a verified real company hold a Tier 3 or higher charter under its legal name. Real companies already pay for permission to trade inside a city's limits, so the price reads as normal to anyone from business, and as a wall to an ordinary player. A charter in a company's name can also be reached by that company's creditors (see the brand section).
- **Scale against Tatsuya's money.** A Stall License (48,000 yen) costs about three Minor Elixir pairs. A Company Charter (1.6 million yen) is roughly 16 percent of the Crownfall prize pool (10 million yen). The Auction House Charter plus bond (8 million yen together) is about two thirds of that pool and more than the roughly 4 million yen a top Season Zero contributor earned. So the Stall License is a Book Two opening move, and the Auction House is a Book Two late move.

## Agent's Writs (license copies)

This is the mechanic behind "a stamped copy for each goblin."

- A Writ is a stamped, named copy of the holder's license, issued by the same Tollhouse for one agent, one city, 30 real days.
- **Writ fee:** 20 percent of the license's renewal fee, per agent per month. Tier 2: 6,000 shillings (2,400 yen). Tier 3: 40,000 (16,000 yen). Tier 4: 120,000 (48,000 yen).
- **Shared cap.** The stock value cap belongs to the license, not to each agent. Ten agents do not carry ten times the stock. Writs add points of sale, not volume. This is what stops one license being copied into an unlimited number of sellers.
- **Liability.** The holder pays any fine an agent earns (up to 2 times the tax evaded). If the license lapses or is revoked, every Writ under it becomes void on the same hour.
- **A Writ is also a standing letter.** In a city where goblins carry bad fame (Lunarest, see `codex/lore/affiliation-and-reputation-system.md`), a warden who checks a goblin and sees a valid Writ reads "licensed agent of [holder]" instead of "feral." It does not lift the 20 percent floor with shopkeepers and it does not make a goblin welcome, but it removes the suspicion that would otherwise get a goblin stopped on sight.

## Enforcement and penalties

**How it is detected.**
- Every sale inside city limits is logged by the Tollhouse ledger. A seller with stock out (displayed or held for sale) and no valid seal at the right tier is flagged.
- Tax Wardens patrol markets. They are NPCs on the reactive system (`codex/lore/npc-tag-system.md`), so they remember faces and repeat offenders, and a named Collector (gold tag) sits in each major city. Wardens are not all-seeing: a warden can be walked around once, not twice.
- Enforcement exists in green zones (cities and settlements) only. Yellow and red zones have no wardens and no tax. That is where an unlicensed trade lives, and it is why the black market sits in outskirts and camps.

**Strikes (per account, per city, reset after 30 clean days).**

| Strike | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1, Summons | The sale goes through. The tax is taken from the proceeds plus a 25 percent penalty. The seller gets a card listing the license tiers. |
| 2, Seizure | Displayed goods are taken to the Tollhouse (recoverable within 7 days by paying 3 times the tax value). Fine of 2 times the tax due. Affiliation with that city's Tollhouse drops 10 points. |
| 3, Bar | No license of any tier from that city for 30 real days. Affiliation with the city drops 20. Karma drops 1. Wardens remove any stall. |
| 4 and after | Wanted for smuggling. Karma drops 1 per strike and a bounty is posted that NPC bounty hunters can take. Arrest only follows the existing Karma rule (about -15 to -20, see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`). Smuggling strikes alone rarely reach that, but a goblin starts near -5, so a goblin has less room than a human. |

- Selling Legendary goods without the Rare Goods Duty counts as a strike 2 on the first offence.
- A bribe to a warden is possible on the reactive system, but it is a crime of its own, costs Affiliation if it fails, and a Collector never takes one.

## The support tree and working-class jobs

**Gate.** At **level 10** a character opens the Support Tree. This is the same level where a character first has 20 stat points to spend and the garrison cap leaves its early limit of 1 (`codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`), so level 10 reads as the end of the opening stretch of the game. Arya (level 10 to 11) is already at the gate, so she can show the tab on the page.

**What the tree is.** A set of supplementary classes that sit beside the base classes (Spellforger, Ironbreaker and so on), do not replace them and give no stat points. Two kinds:
- **Utility supplements** with small passive perks (for example Tinker, Field Medic, Scout). Not jobs.
- **The working-class branch.** The player may hold **one working-class option at a time**. This is a **Post**: a job with an employer, paid in shillings.

**Posts.**

| Post | Typical employer | Pay per paid hour (shillings, yen) | Perk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand (mining, wood, field) | Farms, mines, lumber camps | 2,000 to 3,000 (800 to 1,200 yen) | Faster gathering |
| Courier or Dock Hand | Shipping companies, caravan houses | 2,500 to 3,500 (1,000 to 1,400 yen) | Carry weight |
| Apprentice (smithing, leather and bone, alchemy, cooking, enchanting) | City craft guilds, a master craftsman | 2,000 to 4,000 (800 to 1,600 yen) | Trainer bonus to Mastery |
| Clerk or Factor | Merchant houses, Tollhouses, banks | 3,000 to 5,000 (1,200 to 2,000 yen) | Reads item origin and stock caps |
| Caravan Guard | Caravan houses, city lords | 4,000 to 6,000 (1,600 to 2,400 yen) | Escort bonus. The one Post with real combat risk. |
| Overseer, Assayer, Journeyman | Master craftsmen, noble houses, the royal quartermaster | 8,000 to 12,000 (3,200 to 4,800 yen) | Needs Mastery 5 or higher and Trusted Affiliation with the employer |

**Employer types.** City craft guilds, merchant houses, caravan and shipping companies (canon: shipping companies are a business niche once boats cost shillings), noble houses, temples and cathedrals, inns and farms, dwarf mines in Valefrost, goblin clan quartermasters in Myrrhwood, and **player-run businesses** (a Shop Charter or higher can hire players as employees, which is how Tatsuya later staffs his workshop).

**How it works.** A shift is one real hour of real work in the game: delivering, sorting, hauling, forging to an order. The employer NPC checks the work and pays on completion. An AFK player is paid nothing.

**Limits that protect balance.**
- One Post at a time. Switching has a 7 real day wait.
- **4 paid hours per real day and 20 per week.** Entry pay at the cap is about 16,000 yen a week, master pay about 96,000 yen a week (before the levy). A Minor Elixir pair is worth about ten entry hours, so loot and crafted goods stay worth far more than wages.
- **Each city has a weekly payroll pool** that scales with its trade and its tax take. When the pool is empty, pay for that week drops. Wages come out of the same city treasury the taxes go into, so jobs and taxes balance each other.
- **Wage levy of 5 percent**, taken before the player is paid.
- No combat experience and no drops above Common from shifts. Mastery experience from shifts is limited to a 25 percent bonus while on a shift with a master trainer.
- Posts count against the crafting rule that one player can actively level two professions (`codex/crafting/overview.md`). An Apprentice Post trains one of those two slots, it does not add a third.
- Employers set their own entry rules. Many human employers in Lunarest refuse goblins and dwarves at the 20 percent floor. A goblin player's first Posts are with goblin clan quartermasters, Beastfolk houses in Myrrhwood and dwarf mines.

**Why it matters in the world.** Early in the closed beta a small version of this existed (Lunarest only, a handful of Posts, low pay), and people took real hours at it and told others on Loop. That is part of why HGO reached beyond gamers: people with no interest in raids could hold a job inside the game and cash the shillings out. Hearthfire widens the tree and adds the license system (the "economy and home" release). See the conflict note on the launch economy at the end.

## The auction house

**The official board** (Chapter 3). A remote, system-run listing board. Anyone can use it from anywhere, with the 4 percent listing fee and 8 percent cut proposed above. Its weakness is exactly what Tatsuya sees: the fee takes most of a reseller's margin.

**A player-run house (Tier 5 charter).**

*Who can run one.* A holder of a Company Charter (Tier 4) for 60 days or more, with:
- Trusted band Affiliation (50 or higher) with the city's lord or merchant guild (`codex/lore/affiliation-and-reputation-system.md`).
- A building: a leased plot or a city lease. City leases are very hard to win and need standing and NPC ties, not shillings alone (Hearthfire doc). So the first house is more likely in a town Tatsuya's own guild grows from a camp, in Myrrhwood, than in a big city.
- The 5,000,000 shilling bond and a minimum of 6 hired guards (NPC or player).
- A maximum of 2 player-run houses per city. One house per holder.

*How it works.*
- **Items are delivered in person.** A lot must be physically carried to the house and handed over (canon: nothing is pure abstraction, pickup requires a physical claim). It sits in the house vault until collected. This is the main difference from the remote board and the main reason a house needs security.
- **Fees are set by the house within charter limits:** listing fee 1 to 2 percent, house commission 3 to 5 percent, plus the fixed Tollhouse levy of 4 percent on every sale. A sold lot costs the seller 8 to 11 percent in total, always below the board's 12 percent. A house cannot go above the board, so sellers are never worse off than posting remotely.
- **What the house sells to sellers instead of price:** a vault, verified buyers, an appraiser, escrow (payment held until the lot is claimed), a streamed sale floor, and serious bidders in one room.
- **Break-even.** At about 5.5 percent house take (listing plus commission), the 1,000,000 shilling renewal (400,000 yen) needs roughly 7 million yen of sales a month. Millions-of-shillings Legacy items make that possible, which is why the house lives or dies on high-value consignment and not on cheap goods.
- **Auctioneer.** A house clerk NPC runs the floor on the reactive system, so a house with a good clerk and a good reputation draws better lots.

*What stops abuse.*
- **Shill bidding and wash trades:** the ledger links accounts that share a guild or a pod location. Linked bids are flagged and voided. The Tollhouse levy is charged on every sale even a fake one, so wash trading always costs money.
- **Self-dealing:** staff cannot bid. Lots the owner lists himself are tagged "House-listed" on the card, and house-listed lots may not exceed 25 percent of the lots in any week.
- **Large sales:** any sale above 5,000,000 shillings (2,000,000 yen) triggers a 24 hour payout hold and a ledger report to the Tollhouse. This also makes it harder for the real-world laundering the codex mentions to run through a house. It is a game rule, not a police rule, and stays background.
- **Theft and raids:** the bond pays sellers for any lot lost to a failure of security, up to its value. A house that loses lots loses its bond and its charter. Guilds cannot raid a house in a green zone without taking Karma and Affiliation damage and fighting the city's guards. In a yellow or red zone location, the house has only its own guards.
- **Reserve and time rules:** every lot has a reserve price and a fixed 24 to 72 hour window, so no lot can sit unsold at a hidden price.
- **Lapse:** if the renewal is missed, lots are returned to sellers, not seized.

**How Tatsuya's own house avoids oversupply.** He understands that putting too many of his own pieces on the market would push the price of everyone's gear down, which would drive high-end consignors away and leave the house without the lots that pay its renewal. His rules (proposals):
1. **Numbered runs.** Each Signature design is made in a fixed run (for example 12 or 20 pieces), numbered, then retired. A later variant is a new design, not a refill.
2. **Weekly cap.** His own pieces are at most 25 percent of lots in a week (the same house-listed rule), and no more than 5 of any one design in a month.
3. **Reserve at or above the 14 day median** for that tier of gear. He does not sell below his own recent price.
4. **One headline lot a week,** streamed at a fixed hour, so each piece is an event.
5. **Other players' real high-level items sit beside his.** His exclusive pieces are one part of the house. Other players' Legacy and high-tier items give buyers a reason to attend and keep the price of his work tied to a wider market.
6. **Price floor (optional, costs capital).** If a numbered piece appears on the board below 80 percent of its last house price, the house buys it back with a standing order.
7. **His supply is limited by crafting anyway.** Crafting failure destroys materials (`codex/crafting/overview.md`), and rare materials are scarce (`codex/materials/overview.md`), so he cannot overproduce even if he wanted to.

## The goblin trade group

**Who they are.** Thrash-clan goblins, hired by Tatsuya as agents. They are **not troops.** The garrison cap is small (1 below level 10, then growing by 5 per ten levels, see `codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`) and the troop system is for fighters. Agents are ordinary NPC employees on a wage, which also means they do not level (`codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`, non-troops do not level). His named squad (Grukk, Tikka, Muzzle, Drosh, see `codex/characters/goblin-squad-companions.md`) can act as group leads and escorts.

**Why goblins can enter cities at all.** Canon: only Thrash-clan goblins can enter villages and settlements, every other clan reads as feral and is barred (`codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`). So the group is drawn only from Thrash. Tatsuya is a Thrash elder and Shiv Goblin, so the clan lets him hire and send them. Their Writ makes the city side workable.

**Training.** Agents need a short course (about a week of real time and a cost of 5,000 shillings each, a proposal): appraisal, the Writ routine (show the seal, state the holder), price and haggling lines, how to decline a bad offer, when to run and where to report. Training gives them a "Schooled" tag that cuts their chance of making a mistake. It does not give levels. OPEN: who teaches. Canon says a character can teach only what they have mastered (`codex/lore/mastery-system.md`), and Tatsuya will not be master of trade for a long time. The practical answer is Jiro Amano, the Crimson Static trade contact (`codex/characters/arya.md`), or a hired merchant NPC.

**What they carry.**
- **Basic line.** Common and Uncommon goods Tatsuya buys and resells: repair kits, potions bought from other crafters, rope, simple bone and leather goods. This is the volume, paid through the 3 percent Market Dues. Margin comes from lower tax than the board and from buying where goods are cheap.
- **Signature line.** Exclusive pieces made by Tatsuya and his partner with rare materials and enchants. Rules: at most 2 Signature pieces per agent at a time, carried in a stamped case, never displayed on the stall. The goblin carries a sample card (a menu card, since the real item is hidden) and hands over the piece only to a named buyer or at an arranged meeting. The piece carries a Maker line (see Brand). This is the "black-market style": unlisted, by name, by word of mouth, delivered by hand. It is still licensed and the sale still pays its dues and any Rare Goods Duty.
- **Rare gear is not released to pay a debt.** It is rare on purpose. Signature pieces are numbered runs, held back, and handed out slowly through this one channel (the rule from the author). The same runs feed the auction house.

**Reputation effect.**
- Buyers learn that weapons from these goblins are made by a player, not generated by the game, and that a Maker line is on the card. A goblin carrying the Writ and the case becomes a known sight, which flips the feral stigma toward "that is the group that sells the good stuff." This is a visible, earned change in how humans treat goblins, which is the cultural payoff already set in `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`.
- Mechanically: the group earns a **Trade Standing** meter with each city's merchant guild, built from clean sales and Mark quality, and it adds to Fame (`codex/lore/affiliation-and-reputation-system.md`). Higher Trade Standing lifts the Writ cap and the shop discount from NPC suppliers.
- Agents have their own Affiliation with Tatsuya. Fair pay raises it. If it drops, an agent quits or sells information about the route.

**Risks.**
- **Seizure.** A lapsed Writ, a stock cap overrun, or a missing Duty means the strikes table applies to the holder, and all agents lose their Writs if the license is revoked.
- **Guild retaliation.** The PK_Wraith blacklist pattern (`codex/characters/pk-wraith.md`) can repeat: rival guilds boycott his sale prices, put bounties on agents, ambush them on red zone routes, or open cheaper stalls to undercut him. They cannot make a Tollhouse revoke a clean license, but a large guild with high Affiliation can make a warden harsher on him. He answers with escorts, changing routes, and the house's neutral rules.
- **NPC agents die for good.** White and yellow tags can die permanently. A dead agent is gone along with whatever the case held, which drops where the agent falls.
- **The goblin stigma.** Goblins start near -5 Karma and at the 20 percent floor with humans, so one bad meeting costs more for a goblin than for a human.
- **Counterfeits.** Other crafters will copy the look of a Signature piece. The Registry protects against this (below).
- **One agent's offence is the holder's.** A single agent's strike counts against the license and the holder pays.

## The Kazuhaya Tech brand and trademark

**What it is on the page.** Tatsuya brings his late father's company name, **Kazuhaya Tech**, into HGO. In the real world the name has a bad reputation (the Tabuchi case, the debt) and he cannot bring the business back there. On HGO's global stage he can, because the World Quest keeps him watched. Daichi left him the company and the rights in his will (Chapter 8, `codex/subplots/tabuchi-takedown-and-kazuhaya-brand.md`).

**In-game mechanism (proposal): the Hall of Marks.**
- A Tier 3 or higher license holder can file a **Mark** (a name and a device) with a city's Registry. Fee: 500,000 shillings (200,000 yen) for one kingdom, 100,000 shillings (40,000 yen) to renew each season.
- A filed Mark gives: (1) only the holder and listed workshop employees may sell under that name in the kingdom, and the Tollhouse blocks any other listing using it; (2) a **Maker line** on every item crafted under the Mark, naming maker, workshop and number; (3) a share of any fine an imitator pays (30 percent proposed).
- **Use it or lose it.** A Mark with no sale under it for 60 days lapses. This stops name squatting, and also limits what a rival could do by filing first.
- **Brand Rank 1 to 5.** Rises with number of pieces sold, few returns, and clean sales. Each rank lifts the reserve price buyers will accept and the Writ cap by 1. Small steps, so the brand is earned, not bought.
- **Real-company check.** A Mark that matches a real brand must be verified by HGO. Kazuhaya Tech's papers and the will are the proof. Chiyo has kept the company safe until Tatsuya inherits, so her consent is part of any filing (see the OPEN question below).

**The partner.** The author says a partner builds with him and they title the brand together. A dual-name Mark ("Kazuhaya Tech and [partner]") is possible only if the partner can hold a Mark, meaning a player or a company. An NPC cannot (see the dwarfling options). The partner is not named in canon. OPEN.

**Streaming is the marketing.** "The best way to sell something is to be seen using it." HGO's channel hosts an actively progressing World Quest stream (`codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`), so his gear is in front of thousands. Viewers can read the item card and Maker line in the overlay and then look up the name on the board or at his house. The only limit is Leon's agreement: he tags Sakura Arcade, with no ads every five minutes (`codex/subplots/leon-tatsuya-sponsorship-agreement.md`). Gear shown on a body is placement, not an ad read. The subscription tier and affiliate rules are unchanged (affiliate-approved sessions only, 750 yen per month).

**Workers.** A build can only go so far, so he employs people further along in a craft. Under a Shop Charter or higher he can hire players and NPCs as employees (Posts, above). Legendary schematics in the game help him build and brand his own line, and he also pursues the automaton and building route (see `codex/lore/crafting-and-alchemy.md`). The workshop is a registered part of the Mark so employees can lawfully work under it.

**Risks.**
- If the charter is in the company's legal name, creditors (the collections notice on the Kazuhaya Tech account, the Tabuchis' court actions) may be able to reach in-game revenue. If it is in Tatsuya's own name, the legal separation is cleaner, but the brand is no longer simply his father's company. OPEN.
- A rival can file a conflicting Mark first if Tatsuya waits, which only matters if the Registry's use-it-or-lose-it rule can be abused. Optional hook: the Tabuchi side tries this (see the timeline).
- If the brand's pieces fail or are overpriced, Brand Rank drops and the real-world name takes damage on a global stage.

## Summary

HGO taxes every trade: the official board takes 12 percent of a sale, and a merchant license (six tiers, from an 8,000 yen peddler's permit to a 6 million yen auction house charter) lets a seller trade inside a city at 3 percent dues, with every payment split 60 percent to the city and 40 percent to HGO. Wardens enforce it only in cities, with a strikes ladder from a fine to seizure to a bar. At level 10 a player opens a support tree and may hold one paid Post at a time, with hourly and weekly caps so wages stay far below loot. Tatsuya buys a stall license, copies it as named Writs for trained Thrash goblins, and sends them out with basic goods and a few numbered, Maker-marked Signature pieces. Later he opens a charter-backed auction house that never lists more than a quarter of its lots as his own and keeps his runs small. The Kazuhaya Tech name is filed as a Mark, kept alive by use, and sold by being worn on stream. The dwarfling smith is laid out as a troop, a player or a retainer, with the retainer already in canon. Book One only plants the pieces, and Book Two builds the business as a support for the war.
