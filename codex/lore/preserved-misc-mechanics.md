# Preserved Mechanics From the Old Draft

These carry forward unchanged from `bible/old-draft-chapter-bible.md`, flagged there as worth keeping.

- **Skill combo system** — Goblin Gas + fire = Goblin Flare; toxin effects boost Ancient Bone Flame Barrage's damage/radius. Reusable and mechanically satisfying, fits the deck-building pillar.
  - **Goblin Gas:** a fart-based radius attack, genuinely comedic, genuinely a radius, Tatsuya can hurt himself if he's standing in his own cloud when it matters. He doesn't know where it comes from the first time he uses it, that's part of the reveal in Chapter 3. Applies a nauseous debuff that stacks up to 3-4 times. Once he picks up the goblin gadget belt (see `codex/objects/goblin-gadget-belt.md`, materials required to unlock its abilities), he gets a real, tool-based alternative, thrown gas bombs instead of the bodily-function version.
  - **Goblin Flare:** presents as a distress/signal flare, shoot-in-the-air SOS, visible to allies on the mini-map within roughly a mile. Its tooltip states the signal function only, no damage listed anywhere on the card, that's deliberate: showing a damage stat would tell him it's a weapon before he ever tests it, killing the discovery moment. It's still visibly on fire the instant it's struck, a physical property of the object, not a stated ability effect.
  - **The combo, corrected:** Tatsuya does not know gas and fire combine into an explosion going in. In Chapter 3 he stacks four poison debuffs on two pursuers with Goblin Gas (whose own tooltip already says it "can be ignited for increased effect"), then throws the lit flare into the stunned pair's gas cloud to test that line, expecting an ignite effect, not an explosion. The resulting blast killing both is a genuine surprise, a reasoned test with an outsized result, not a plan paying off exactly as expected.
- **Karma/Alignment system** — a graduated negative scale, not a flat flip. Killing players outside sanctioned PvP, or mistreating NPCs and a region's people (see `codex/lore/player-owned-economy-and-living-world-npcs.md`), pushes Karma down, and how far down determines the real, felt consequence. Mildly negative (goblins default around **-5**, consistent with the already-locked feral stigma) reads as disliked but tolerated, nobody moves against you over it, just suspicion and a colder reception. Past a real threshold, roughly **-15 to -20**, NPCs and guards actively move to attack or arrest on sight, and the nameplate visibly flips white to red, "Blood Tinted," the visible marker for having crossed that deeper line. A strong, visible consequence system for open-world PvP, distinct from Affiliation (see `codex/lore/affiliation-and-reputation-system.md`), which is a separate, positive-scale, per-faction trust meter, not the same axis and not a substitute for it.
- **Legacy account freeze** — switching to a legacy account freezes the account entirely: no XP gain, no item drops, no progression gains of any kind. Players can still fight, but can't benefit from it. The real mechanical reason high-level players haven't rushed Tatsuya despite knowing his World Quest location: the math doesn't work, nothing comes out of a kill on a legacy build.
- **Scouting tent** — a temporary, player-placed idle shelter. A character parked in a scouting tent stays in place when the player logs off, rather than teleporting to a safe zone. Used to hold position near a contested area between sessions.
- **Zone karma classification** — three tiers. Red zones: open PvP, full loot drop on death, no karma penalty for killing. Yellow zones: PvP is possible but triggers negative karma; guards in green areas actively pursue and attack players whose karma has gone negative. Green zones: major cities and settlements, true safe haven under normal circumstances (workarounds exist but are risky and visible). Distinct from the individual karma/alignment axis (see the existing Karma/Alignment system entry above), which tracks a player's overall standing. Zone type determines the rules of engagement in a given area.
- **72-hour death lock**: dying carries a real three-day penalty, not just a respawn timer. A flat 72 hours in raids, skirmishes and node wars, changed from the older seven days. Siege Pro League sessions use the 60-life rule instead; whether Crownfall uses the Siege 60-life rule or the 72-hour death lock is OPEN. The pass-out lock is shorter, 24 hours by default (provisional). The Chapter 4 "[TIME LIMIT: 7 DAYS IN-GAME]" is a quest timer, not this lock. See `codex/objects/pod-hardware.md` for the open flag on how this relates to the pass-out limit.
- **Physical Arts** — moved to `codex/combat/`, see `codex/combat/physical-arts-overview.md` for the tier system and `codex/combat/way-of-the-fist.md`/`spear.md`/`sword.md`/`axe.md`/`bow-and-arrow.md` for the per-weapon technique trees, including Drosh's two named techniques.
- **Tactical Cartographer title** — earned for drawing up a battle plan, not just fighting. Rewards the brains-over-brawn playstyle the story leads with.
- **In-game time dilation** — time moves faster inside HGO than in the real world. Flagged as needing pacing care: any quest timer stated in-game (e.g., a three-day ultimatum) needs to be checked against how it lands against Tatsuya's real-world schedule before it's used in a chapter.
- **Meditation, a Common baseline ability** — widely available, not exclusive to any race or the Shiv bond specifically. Held while stationary, slowly restores SP and MP over time, doesn't touch HP, and grants nothing like Rested Travel's XP buff, small and unremarkable, the kind of ability most players have and few think about. Its real narrative use: it gives cover stories that lean on "I was meditating" something genuinely real to point to, a character caught apparently mid-Meditation reads exactly the same whether the truth behind it is mundane or, in Tatsuya's case, a lie covering for having logged out entirely.
- **Live streaming as an organic exposition tool** — chat reactions doing the work of flagging "this is unusual" on-screen, without narration or an announcer stating it outright. A deliberate craft technique, not just a mechanic, reuse it the same way.
  - **Viewers don't show up for free.** A stream starting doesn't organically pull an audience just because a World Quest happens to be active, no random chatter dropping in with nothing driving it. What actually draws eyes: a player's active quest tag is visible on their stream overlay whether they're hiding everything else or not, and it can't be faked or hidden selectively, so a streamer claiming a quest they don't actually have is instantly disprovable and a streamer who genuinely has one gets real, earned attention once people notice the tag. Chat activity should scale with something visibly happening on-screen (a dramatic effect, a transformation, a fight), not appear ahead of a reason to look.
  - **Stream privacy settings, established Chapter 7 ("The First Disciple").** A player can hide most of their HUD from viewers, minimap, location, anything that could dox a real-world position, while still choosing to leave their active quest tag and basic vitals (health, mana, stamina) visible. Tatsuya's own setup shows only the Call of the Bones tag and his vitals, sitting in the top-right periphery like a second HUD layer, dismissible with a thought and recallable the same way into a blue-box overlay, not permanently blocking his actual field of view.

**Death, Cathedrals and the lock (author-stated).** When a player dies they are sent back to a Cathedral, the resurrection spot in any major town or city, and take the 72-hour death lock. There are only three Cathedrals in the Kingdom of Lunarest outside the royal capital, because there are only three major towns there besides the capital. Tatsuya knows this from watching the game. **Status effects pop up in the player's HUD**, and in VR he has to look up to read them. The Challenging Howl is an **Immobilize** (not a root, which only pins the feet): a forceful, sound-based effect that locks the whole body.

- **The flare-and-gas combo, why it is novel and why it worked (author-stated, latest).** *Novelty:* a Goblin Flare only does damage (2) if you take the lid off and touch someone with it, and nobody will let you, so flares are treated as emergency supply. Nobody has thought to ignite gas from their own backside and throw a flare into it, which makes a small explosion through combustion. Only **players start with both** Goblin Gas and Goblin Flare. NPC goblins may have gas, or flare, or both (a gas unit, a gas-and-flare unit), but most never steer into the flare tree, and none have used the combination. The two Hobgoblins use advanced physical arts and Thrash clan techniques they hardly ever use: **Wyx never uses Goblin Gas** (she has Toxic Breath and won't use it either, it isn't ladylike, she keeps herself presentable) and **Sevish probably hasn't used gas or flare in a very long time** (he was built on war). So Wyx doesn't understand the logic; she only saw it, and reads it as the nearest thing to magic anyone in that group has seen, though nobody calls it magic because nothing was cast, which is why she calls him crafty. NPCs may try to copy it after seeing it (potentially). *Why it worked:* Tatsuya saw the gas had an ignite property and gambled. The enforcers were low level and his high Fortune outmatched their basic stats; the gas hit them straight in the face, a preemptive strike they did not see coming, so crits were close to guaranteed. Weak mobs plus high Fortune made the crits chain, and the stun chain stretched from the normal 2 seconds to about 8 seconds. Possible, but it does not always happen that way. Later he gets a better version (toxic breath), and with the Goblin Gadget Belt he will invent bombs. Sevish is not slow and does not ask "how": he only caught the end of the fight (the blast, the two men dying after his Howl stun-locked everyone), so he voices disbelief that this one caused the blast and killed two of his men, flat out.
