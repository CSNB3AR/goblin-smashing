# Hearthfire Expansion, Seasons & Competitive Structure

Single source of truth for the HGO release sequence, the competitive structure around it, and the currency and travel rules the expansion introduces. Replaces the old "Crystal League expansion" framing. Anything in the codex that says "Crystal League" means Hearthfire. See `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md` for the real-world calendar and `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md` for the world Season Zero was played in.

## Names, locked

- **Hearthfire** is the expansion: the economy and "home" release, the push to bring a real business into the game, the world maps, and the start of Season One. Launches January 1st. "Crownfall" is **not** the expansion.
- **Crownfall** is a Siege tournament, an open-beta showcase event held December 25th. It replaces the old Winter Bash entirely (same countdown, same 10 million yen prize pool). See `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`.
- **Season Zero** is the closed beta. Its finale is the **Season Zero Championship** (also called Championship 1), the broadcast the story opens on. There is no Winter Championship.
- There is no "Beta 2". The sequence is closed beta (Season Zero), then open beta (all players and businesses, with discounted access drives), then Hearthfire opens Season One.

## Release sequence

1. **Closed beta / Season Zero.** About 5,000 players out of roughly 870,000 pre-orders. One server, one continent (Lunarest, see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`), three princes, city lords hiring guilds as mercenaries. Runs roughly eight to nine months in real time (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`).
2. **Season Zero Championship.** Twelve guilds qualify, four attached to each prince's army. A skirmish win is worth 40 points. See "Season Zero finale" below. Prize pool 40 million yen.
3. **Closed-to-open gap.** Closed-beta veterans get a **24-hour cash-out window** for their Legacy Silver exchange (see "Currency"). Kazuku announces Hearthfire live during the championship broadcast.
4. **Open beta.** All players and businesses can enter. Access drives are discounted. Characters made here are permanent. Tatsuya's character is an open-beta character.
5. **Crownfall, December 25th.** Open-beta Siege tournament, 10 million yen pool.
6. **Hearthfire, January 1st, Season One.** Roughly 14 hours of full server downtime, the first full shutdown in HGO's life (landing rules in `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`). The five-continent world maps release.

## Character rules at Hearthfire

- **Closed-beta characters are frozen** at their current level once Hearthfire launches. Their owners can keep playing them as-is or retire them for Legacy Points (see "Currency").
- **Open-beta characters are permanent** and are not frozen.
- **The main story is frozen**, except for hidden quests. The princes are off-screen, held in restricted zones.
- **Tatsuya's goblin quest line expires at the expansion** if he has not progressed it (the Call of the Bones deadline, see `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md`).

## Siege (the arena mode)

A guild-scale arena mode.

- **Queue up to 20 players.** A guild only needs a small active core, since the troop system fills empty slots (see "Troops" below and the Whispers of Shiv material in `codex/characters/shiv.md`).
- **Ranking ladder** for guilds, separate from the individual ladder (`codex/pro-scene/ranking-ladder.md`).
- **Practice arena** unlocks only through a guild hall. The hall costs shillings, and the plot costs more.
- HGO hosts the Siege tournaments itself.
- Siege is a **side mode and showcase**, not the main story. It has two lives: the one-off Crownfall event, and afterward the **Siege Pro League**.

### Siege Pro League

A scheduled, ranked guild league running through each season. Its rewards are **rare seasonal items** (cosmetics, gear, crafting materials) plus seasonal points. It does not grant territory or world control. Pro guilds typically run about 50 members. Detail in `codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`.

### Troops

Guilds field NPC troops from their hall to fill the 20-player queue. Credit for troop kills and objective points routes to the guild member who commands them. Tatsuya's Whispers of Shiv troops count toward his total without him realizing it at first (see `codex/characters/shiv.md`).

## Node Wars (the main story after Hearthfire)

After the world maps release, the war the story is about is **Node Wars across the five continents**. Guilds fight over nodes (towns, resource sites, forts) on the real map, with kingdoms and city lords as the backdrop. Holding nodes brings territory, taxes, infrastructure and NPC ties. Contested zones grant buffs and minor perks, while a province or kingdom is a different tier of stakes entirely, real income (see `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`). Existing GvG War material (`codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`) is the base. Guild halls, the 20-player queue and the troop system are reused inside node war fights, but the Siege Pro League ladder is separate from them. Seasonal points come most from fights and wars, then world bosses, main quests, and crafting, infrastructure and NPC ties.

## Season Zero finale

- The finale week cuts the top eight guilds, **two per day**, with the final four set by the weekend. Dosuke AI's group is eliminated on Saturday. Cut guilds can still play but earn no points.
- **Sunday final: the Battle of the Snowy Plains.** 300 points, 12 Aogiri players (Japan) against 20 London Spire (UK), after attrition from the 72-hour death locks. Japan holds a winnable position and overplays it.
- The final is hosted at Singapore arenas and facilities (see `codex/locations/singapore-championship-venue.md`), convention-style, with attendee rare-drop draws.
- **Arya's small guild won the Fire Festival guild event**, "The Second Dawn": it saved Prince Dorrin from a setup by a player guild running a quest with Prince Maldren, using intel from other players. That took the eighth seed. Infighting, the 72-hour locks and the departure of Sana and Nao then sank them (full account in `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`).
- **Haruto Murayama** is Breakout Player of the Season. Dosuke AI is famous for a role award.

## Payouts and prizes

- **Season Zero:** 40 million yen. A top contributor's share of a winning guild is about a tenth, roughly 4 million yen, which is the figure that matters to the surgery fund (see `codex/subplots/tabuchi-schematics-and-family-debt.md`).
- **Crownfall:** 10 million yen pool. The winning guild's payout comes close to the surgery cost. The top three guilds earn seasonal points plus money.
- **Season One:** first, second and third pay out, plus HGO Coin for raids, bosses and the top 500.
- **Guild pay is contribution-based.** Guilds cap at 120. Inactive members receive a low base only.
- **Top 500:** a nameplate icon with tiers at 500, 100, 50 and 5, and separate role and race boards.

## Currency

- **Shillings** are the in-game currency. **10 shillings = 1 HGO Coin = 2 yen** (so 1 yen = 5 shillings). The coin is the cash-out token. Purchases inside the game need shillings, not coins. Prices on the page stay in yen.
- **Legacy Points** convert to **Legacy Silver** plus Legacy items. **Legacy Silver is locked until Hearthfire.** Only closed-beta veterans are affected by the lock, and they get a 24-hour cash-out window between closed and open beta. Earnings made in open beta cash out normally.
- **Why the coin holds:** the company behind HGO has real holdings in industrial rigs and natural resources. Nobody knows who the owner is. Criminals and police are background texture only (see `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`).

## Locks

- **Death lock: a flat 72 hours** everywhere (raids, skirmishes, sieges).
- **Pass-out lock: 24 hours** by default, shorter than the death lock. Not yet dramatized, treat as provisional (see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`).
- The Chapter 4 "[TIME LIMIT: 7 DAYS IN-GAME]" is a quest timer and is separate from the death lock.

## Travel and the world maps

- After Crownfall the world maps release. **Each player gets one free teleport** at the Lunarest waypoint to a home kingdom of their choice.
- **No fast travel.** Waystones are very expensive.
- Only **NPCs with a sigil** can use waypoints. A sigil drains and recharges over **14 days**, so its holder is stuck at the destination for 14 days. **No player has a sigil**, including Yuna Yunaile, who has restricted-area access but is still in Lunarest.
- Boats cost a lot of shillings, and shipping companies enter the game as a business niche.
- Kazuku's opening Hearthfire advertisement frames the five continents as favoring certain races.
- Race and continent: see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`.

## Open items

- Pass-out lock (24 hours) is a default, not a ruling.
- Whether Crownfall keeps the old level-25 new-region cap is unconfirmed.
