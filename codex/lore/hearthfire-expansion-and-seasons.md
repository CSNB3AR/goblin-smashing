# Hearthfire Expansion, Seasons & Competitive Structure

Single source of truth for the HGO release sequence, the competitive structure around it, and the currency and travel rules the expansion introduces. Replaces the old "Crystal League expansion" framing. Anything in the codex that says "Crystal League" means Hearthfire. See `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md` for the real-world calendar and `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md` for the world Season Zero was played in.

## Names, locked

- **Hearthfire** is the expansion: the economy and "home" release, the push to bring a real business into the game, the world maps, and the start of Season One. Launches January 1st. "Crownfall" is **not** the expansion.
- **Crownfall** is the **Season One opener for seasonal points** and the **introduction of the Siege format**, a guild-only event for open-beta characters held December 25th in a neutral zone, a prelude to Node Wars. It replaces the old Winter Bash entirely (same countdown, same 10 million yen prize pool). See `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md`.
- **Siege** is a battle royale format (5 guilds per session). Crownfall introduces it, and the **Siege Pro League** is the standing competitive option built on it, active from the start of Season One and mentioned by Kazuku in the Hearthfire ad. See "Siege and the Siege Pro League" below.
- **Season Zero** is the closed beta. Its finale is the **Season Zero finale** (the last battle of Season Zero), the streamed spectacle the story opens on. There is no Winter Championship.
- There is no "Beta 2". The sequence is closed beta (Season Zero), then open beta (all players and businesses, with discounted access drives), then Hearthfire opens Season One.

## Release sequence

1. **Closed beta / Season Zero.** About 5,000 players out of roughly 870,000 pre-orders. One server, one continent (Lunarest, see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`), three princes, city lords hiring guilds as mercenaries. Runs roughly eight to nine months in real time (see `codex/lore/timeline-and-beta.md`).
2. **Season Zero finale.** Twelve guilds qualify, four attached to each prince's army. A skirmish win is worth 40 points. See "Season Zero finale" below. Prize pool 40 million yen.
3. **Closed-to-open gap.** Closed-beta veterans get a **24-hour cash-out window** for their Legacy Silver exchange (see "Currency"). Kazuku announces Hearthfire live during the finale broadcast.
4. **Open beta.** All players and businesses can enter. The access drive **and the helmet rig** are discounted (50 percent for 72 hours), which also undercuts the Tabuchis' Gen 2 pods. Characters made here are permanent. Tatsuya's character is an open-beta character. This is a new game: closed-beta names (Dosuke AI, Eren and others) are established, everyone else starts equal, and nobody is established in the open world yet. Open-beta seasonal points roll into Season One (see "Seasonal points and Arc One").
5. **Crownfall, December 25th.** Season One opener for seasonal points, guild-only Siege event, 10 million yen pool.
6. **Hearthfire, January 1st, Season One.** Roughly 14 hours of full server downtime, the first full shutdown in HGO's life (landing rules in `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`). The five-continent world maps release.

## Character rules at Hearthfire

- **Closed-beta characters are frozen** at their current level once Hearthfire launches. Their owners can keep playing them as-is or retire them for Legacy Points (see "Currency").
- **Open-beta characters are permanent** and are not frozen.
- **The main story is frozen**, except for hidden quests. The princes are off-screen, held in restricted zones.
- **Tatsuya's goblin quest line expires at the expansion** if he has not progressed it (the Call of the Bones deadline, see `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md`).

## Why Crownfall

Crownfall is named for the king's fall. HGO's ownership knows King Aldemar Vaereth is dying (see `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`), so the Season One opener is called Crownfall. Nobody in the story knows this yet, and the name is a wink the reader catches later. The December 25th event runs in a neutral zone outside royal jurisdiction, so the coming death does not stop it.

**Naming note (OPEN):** the user said "the Crown Fall expansion." Hearthfire stays the expansion's name, and Crownfall is the arc and event where the crown falls. Whether the expansion itself should be renamed is undecided.

## The funeral and what follows

The king dies around the start of Season One, about when the Thrash Clan heads back to Myrrhwood. His funeral is a royal event. A truce holds in the royal lands and nobody can battle there while it lasts. When the funeral ends, the princes (Dorrin, Ilyan and Maldren) move against each other, the map starts moving, and Node Wars open. This is what breaks the expansion open.

- **Open:** the funeral's length, and whether the truce covers Lunarest only or the whole map.

## Siege and the Siege Pro League

### The Siege format (battle royale)

- **Session.** 5 guilds per session, 20 players per guild (comps sorted any way the guild likes). Match length **3 hours**.
- **Start.** Each guild begins at an **outskirts-like camp** at spawn. It must gather a set amount of resources to upgrade the camp to **level 3**, at which point it becomes a **fort**.
- **Flag and lives.** Each guild has a **guild flag** and **60 total lives** (3 waves of 20). The 60 lives replace the 72-hour death lock inside the Siege Pro League; whether Crownfall uses the Siege 60-life rule or the 72-hour death lock is OPEN.
- **Play.** Guilds fight for resources, strategize and survive in a battle-royale setting. Forts are built creatively with items and magic, which is what opens up warfare. It takes tactics.
- **Rewards.** The winning guild earns **shillings and Ember cores**.
- **Not Node Wars.** Node Wars last weeks and months. A Siege session is a three-hour contained match.
- **Troops.** Guilds fill empty slots with NPC troops from the hall (see "Troops").

### Crownfall (the introduction)

- Hosted by HGO on December 25th as the **Season One opener for seasonal points** and the first public Siege. **Guild-only**: individuals can earn seasonal points, but only guilds enter. Open-beta characters only.
- Set in a **neutral zone**. The zone may tie back to a legendary world quest. That tie is a rumor and a hint only, **undecided**.
- It is a taste of the larger conflict to come and prepares everyone for Node Wars (camp, resources, fort and warfare all carry over).
- **No troop cap at Crownfall.** The standing league caps troops (below).
- Heats, bracket size and regions: the old 32-guild bracket text in `codex/pro-scene/crownfall-siege-event.md` is **to be refit to 5-guild heats**. Full event notes and payouts are there.

### Siege Pro League

- **Active from the start of Season One.** Kazuku mentions it in the Hearthfire ad as an option for players who do not want the open-world route. There is an active pro system from the start, which gives players options.
- **Why it exists.** The Siege mode is so watched that a pro scene forms around it, and HGO runs a scheduled, ranked, national and global circuit. Pro guilds run about 50 members.
- **No territory.** It does not grant territory or world control (that is Node Wars).
- **Seasonal points shop.** Points per match, winning pays more, points buy rare items from a seasonal shop, with high end-of-season rewards.
- **Ember cores.** Matches pay **Ember cores**, the base material that, once refined, powers the guild hall. Borrowed in spirit from Albion's power cores. Cores use the normal rarity ladder (Common, Uncommon, Rare, and so on, see `bible/game-system.md`), run from Common up to Rare by default, and are tradeable.
- **Ember crystals and hall power.** Crystals are crafted, not dropped: enough Rare Ember cores make a **Legendary Ember crystal**, named by tier (for example "Legendary [name] Ember Crystal"). A crystal gives the guild hall power. A hall can still run operations unpowered, and power unlocks guild mounts and the other mechanics that develop the fortress. World rarity stays reserved for World Quest rewards.
- **Hall practice arena.** A guild-hall practice arena for Siege, unlocked only through a hall.
- **Full loot, no rebound.** Dying drops items as in the open world with no binding to the old owner. The 60 lives of the format replace the 72-hour death lock, so the league can be ground. The pass-out lock is unaffected.
- **Troop cap.** The standing league caps troops hard, about one or two per player (see `codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`).
- **Story stance.** The story is not an esports story. Tatsuya is on the open-world path and does not chase the league (see `codex/pro-scene/esports-timeline-and-tatsuyas-long-arc.md`).
- **Open:** whether "full loot, no rebound" and the troop cap still hold now that matches are three-hour, 60-life battle royales (adapted here, not confirmed). Which of the two (league option vs Crownfall-only) other docs should lead with is settled by the latest ruling: Crownfall introduces it, the league is an option from Season One.

### Troops

A guild fields NPC troops from its hall to fill the 20-player side. The garrison cap still scales with the commanding player's level. Credit for troop kills and objective points routes to the guild member who commands them. Tatsuya's Whispers of Shiv troops count toward his total without him realizing it at first (see `codex/characters/shiv.md`). Tatsuya is the first to fill guild slots with troops because he lacks the people. His enchantments multiply on troops and last longer on them than on players, since troops do not follow the player system (see `codex/lore/companion-troop-system.md`).

### Guild halls

Guild halls are a Hearthfire economy element and stay canon.

- **Cost.** A hall costs shillings, and the plot costs more.
- **Where halls sit.** Not always in a city. A hall inside a city requires the guild to own the city (a province, a landing or a lease), and city leases are very hard to get, so a lease is won with standing and NPC ties, not shillings alone. Most guilds therefore build on their **own plot**, and even that is usually a leased plot. In certain areas a guild can instead found a **camp** and grow it, Age of Empires style, into an **outskirts** and then a **town**. That takes enough players, resources and time, which is why big guilds with existing numbers move into the contested zones first and why a small guild such as Tatsuya's cannot be first.
- **Hall power** (Ember crystals, mounts) comes from the Siege Pro League economy above.
- **Myrrhwood.** Tatsuya's guild stays small for a long while. As he becomes the more dominant goblin, goblins want to fight beside him for the power it brings, and that is what fuels his early expansion there (see `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).


## Node Wars (the main story after Hearthfire)

After the world maps release, the war the story is about is **Node Wars across the five continents**. Guilds fight over nodes (towns, resource sites, forts) on the real map, with kingdoms and city lords as the backdrop. Holding nodes brings territory, taxes, infrastructure and NPC ties. Contested zones grant buffs and minor perks, and bigger guilds with existing numbers take them first. A province or kingdom is a different tier of stakes entirely, real income (see `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`). Existing GvG War material (`codex/pro-scene/tournaments-and-events.md`) is the base. Crownfall is the rehearsal for it, and the guild hall, the 20-player side and the troop system carry into node war fights. Node Wars last weeks and months, unlike a three-hour Siege session. Node Wars are the main war content and the spine of the story after Hearthfire. Seasonal points come most from fights and wars, then world bosses, main quests, and crafting, infrastructure and NPC ties.

## Season Zero finale

- The finale week cuts the top eight guilds, **two per day**, with the final four set by the weekend. Dosuke AI's group is eliminated on Saturday. Cut guilds can still play but earn no points.
- **Sunday final: the Battle of the Snowy Plains.** 300 points, 12 Aogiri players (Japan) against 20 London Spire (UK, called the Knights of the London Spire as an epithet, the guild name stays London Spire), after attrition from the 72-hour death locks. Season points going in: London Spire 10,500, Aogiri 10,250. The battle is worth 300, so Aogiri must win it to take the season and London Spire only has to hold. About two hours remain on the final window when Yuna recaps. Aogiri holds the zone but its leader never decides and the position collapses.
- **Setting.** An open-world fight, not an arena and not a teleport match. A small walled medieval city-state in the snowy plains (Arendelle-like: snow, a city hall, small houses, bunkers). The town is unnamed, name left open. Singapore is where Yuna and the crowd are, the fight is in-world.
- **Zone rules (king of the hill).** The zone reads CONTESTED while both guild banners stand in it. A guild that holds it alone for ten uncontested minutes becomes the zone leader and wins the battle. The same rule scales up once territories apply.
- **Why it matters (princes' factions).** The finalists fight under different princes (Dorrin, Ilyan, Maldren). The princes' story is the biggest world quest of the beta so far, not the only one, and Yuna says on air that nobody knows where it leads. Whichever guild walks out of the town puts its prince's momentum into the new year. Yuna gives no spoilers. OPEN: which prince London Spire backs and which Aogiri backs.
- **Tactical picture (guerrilla warfare).** The Knights of the London Spire hide **within the walls** and ambush anyone from Aogiri who comes in. They built a **kill zone at the choke point** (the gate and the street funnel) and strike from hidden positions. Aogiri's leader is stuck inside the town hall with 11 others (12 total) and their banner. The leader has been **poisoned** and needs **antidotes** that only reinforcements can bring. Every player who dies goes on the **72-hour death lock**, so the reinforcement pool only shrinks, and those who come back keep trying to push the choke into the prepared kill zone, wave after wave, never together. The plains have deep caverns, a flank route nobody uses. The right play was to cure the leader first or break out together, hold the antidote pass and time the waves. Instead the leader makes no decision, waits too long and lets his health drop until he **dies**. The **banner is bound to its leader**, so the flag ends, and the Knights hold the ten uncontested minutes.
- **Open:** what happens if the clock expires while the zone is still contested. The banner-bound-to-leader rule, the poison and the antidote supply are inferred and not confirmed.
- The broadcast and crowd are hosted at Singapore arenas and facilities (see `codex/locations/singapore-championship-venue.md`), convention-style, with attendee rare-drop draws. The battle itself is in-world.
- **Arya's small guild won the Fire Festival guild event**, "The Second Dawn": it saved Prince Dorrin from a setup by a player guild running a quest with Prince Maldren, using intel from other players. That took the eighth seed. Infighting, the 72-hour locks and the departure of Sana and Nao then sank them (full account in `codex/lore/lunarest-royal-court.md`).
- **Haruto Murayama** is Breakout Player of the Season. Dosuke AI is famous for a role award.

## Seasonal points and Arc One

- **Arc One:** Open Beta, then Crownfall (Season One opener for seasonal points, introduces Siege), then Hearthfire (Season One starts, expansion, map-wide). Mini sub-plot: the Battle of Thrash Hill.
- **The season restarts at open beta.** Kazuku explains at the start that open beta leads into Season One. Seasonal points earned in open beta (raids, world quests, guild quests and other activities) roll into Season One.
- **Individuals can earn seasonal points, but only guilds enter Crownfall.** Qualifying means having a guild with enough points, which is why Tatsuya founds a guild after he and Arya escape.
- **Tone.** This is a new game and nobody is established in the open world. Closed-beta names (Dosuke AI, Eren and others) made their names in the closed beta. Tatsuya is new, and after the global stream everybody is watching him.

## Payouts and prizes

- **Season Zero:** 40 million yen. A top contributor's share of a winning guild is about a tenth, roughly 4 million yen, which is the figure that matters to the surgery fund (see `codex/subplots/tabuchi-schematics-and-family-debt.md`).
- **Crownfall:** 10 million yen pool. The winning guild's payout comes close to the surgery cost. The top three guilds earn seasonal points plus money.
- **Season One:** first, second and third pay out, plus HGO Coin for raids, bosses and the top 500.
- **Guild pay is contribution-based.** Guilds cap at 120. Inactive members receive a low base only.
- **Top 500:** a nameplate icon with tiers at 500, 100, 50 and 5, and separate role and race boards.

## Currency

- **Shillings** are the in-game currency. **10 shillings = 1 HGO Coin = 2 yen** (so 1 yen = 5 shillings). The coin is the cash-out token. Purchases inside the game need shillings, not coins. Prices on the page stay in yen.
- **Legacy Points** convert to **Legacy Silver** plus Legacy items. **Legacy Silver is locked until Hearthfire.** Only closed-beta veterans are affected by the lock, and they get a 24-hour cash-out window between closed and open beta. Earnings made in open beta cash out normally.
- **Why the coin holds:** the company behind HGO has real holdings in industrial rigs and natural resources. Nobody knows who the owner is. Criminals and police are background texture only (see `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`).

## Locks

- **Death lock: a flat 72 hours** in raids, skirmishes, node wars and the open world. **Siege Pro League sessions use the 60-life rule instead**, so the lock does not apply inside the league; whether Crownfall uses the Siege 60-life rule or the 72-hour death lock is OPEN.
- **Pass-out lock: 24 hours** by default, shorter than the death lock. Not yet dramatized, treat as provisional (see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`).
- The Chapter 4 "[TIME LIMIT: 7 DAYS IN-GAME]" is a quest timer and is separate from the death lock.

## Travel and the world maps

- After Crownfall the world maps release. **Each player gets one free teleport** at the Lunarest waypoint to a home kingdom of their choice.
- **No fast travel.** Waystones are very expensive.
- Only **NPCs with a sigil** can use waypoints. A sigil drains and recharges over **14 days**, so its holder is stuck at the destination for 14 days. **No player has a sigil**, including Yuna Yunaile, who has restricted-area access but is still in Lunarest.
- Boats cost a lot of shillings, and shipping companies enter the game as a business niche.
- Kazuku's opening Hearthfire advertisement frames the five continents as favoring certain races.
- Race and continent: see `codex/locations/eryndal-world-overview.md`.

## Why this matters to the story

The systems above are the stage, not the story.
- **A way out.** Kazuku's "bring your business here" is legitimate recruitment: it brings people in because the game genuinely pays. It is also the main hook for anyone stuck in debt or needing a reset, since a game that pays can offer a way out, and open-beta characters start on fair ground. That is why Tatsuya joins: the money, Ohema's six-month deadline and the debt come first, and the hints from his father second (see `codex/characters/tatsuya-kazehaya.md`).
- **He is not the only one.** Hearthfire is "The New World" and its territory is open, so anyone smart will try to make it big or find a business idea for the game. Other guilds, merchants and entrepreneurs (Yuna's family is one precedent) are chasing the same space, so Tatsuya earns his place with connections and products, not by being first.
- **Connections run the systems.** City leases, hall growth and Myrrhwood's goblins all depend on relationships (Affiliation, NPC ties, trade contacts such as Jiro Amano, Arya the information broker, Nova Market). Ember cores and crystals, the Siege Pro League payout, are one real trade good among many (see above and `codex/subplots/goblin-trade-company-forward-plot.md`).
- **Tatsuya's goals stay the spine:** the surgery money, redeeming Dad's product, reaching Kazuku (reachable only inside HGO), beating Eren, and a footing in Myrrhwood.
