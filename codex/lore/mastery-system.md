# Mastery System

A third progression axis, distinct from the other two:

- **Discipline Points** (see `codex/lore/five-disciplines-stat-system.md`) — raw stat investment, spent points, a character's own build.
- **Affiliation** (see `codex/lore/affiliation-and-reputation-system.md`) — NPC trust/relationship, per-faction standing.
- **Mastery** — what a character is actually capable of doing, grown through use, not spent points.

## How it works

Mastery grows through repetition: actually using a given skill, spell school, Physical Arts style, or crafting profession over and over, an "you master what you actually do" model. It isn't purchased with Discipline Points or Crafting Points (see `codex/crafting/overview.md`), those unlock the tree and set the tier ceiling; Mastery is the separate, slower climb of how well a character actually executes within whatever tier they've unlocked. Two characters at the same tier in the same skill can sit at very different Mastery levels depending on how much they've actually used it.

**Scale:** Mastery runs from 1 to 10. Maximum is 10.

**XP:** 100 XP per level, 1,000 XP total to reach master level. XP is earned by actively using or training a skill — not by spending Discipline Points. A character who unlocks a skill and never uses it stays at Mastery 1 indefinitely. Training with another player or NPC who has already mastered the skill accelerates the climb.

**Skill descriptions:** Every unlocked skill has an in-game description entry. The base description covers what a skill does in its plainest form. It frequently undersells advanced use cases — Hushveil's base description reads as a fog utility spell and makes no mention of what the Silence upgrade turns it into in the right hands. The description tells you what it does; the player figures out what it can do.

**Mastery gates tier access, not just execution.** Discipline Points and Crafting Points unlock a tier's slot in a book's tree, that's the ceiling of what a character could invest in. Actually being able to cast or use a book's higher tiers requires real Mastery progress in that book as a whole, on top of having the points invested. Roughly 25% Mastery in a book unlocks access to about the first half of its tiers. A book's top tiers, six and eight specifically, stay locked out entirely below roughly 75% Mastery, even for a character who's already spent the points to nominally unlock them. This is why a freshly multiclassed or freshly respecced caster can have every tier "unlocked" on paper and still only be able to actually cast the early ones, the same reason Hushveil's Silence upgrade and other late-book spells stay out of reach for anyone who hasn't put in the real repetition.

## Teaching, the real payoff

Once a character reaches master-level in something, they unlock the ability to **teach** it to others, players included. This is a real, systemic mechanic, not unique to any one character. It underlies the crafting-profession system already locked in `codex/crafting/`, and sets up Tatsuya's own future arc training disciples in Shaman's Veil once he's mastered it himself, not immediately, a real, earned-over-time thread tied to his necklace's removability stake (see `codex/objects/call-of-the-bones-necklace.md`) and the Shiv/Wyx magic-restriction pattern he's currently the sole exception to.

## Crafting-manual comprehension

Mastery is also the system a character eventually operates within once they clear the joint Wisdom+Speed check that lets them absorb a crafting manual or technique in the first place (see `codex/lore/five-disciplines-stat-system.md`). Clearing that check gets the technique to "take"; Mastery is how good they get at it afterward.

## Cross-references

Keep Mastery distinct from Affiliation: Affiliation is who trusts you, Mastery is what you're actually capable of. Neither one substitutes for the other, and neither one is Discipline Points under a different name.
