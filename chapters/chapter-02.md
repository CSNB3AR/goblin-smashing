Chapter Two: Into the Game

Tatsuya stood in the middle of the empty apartment, phone still in his hand, the Nexus Enterprises text glowing against his thumb. The pickup notice sat there like a dare he'd already accepted without meaning to, seventy-two hours to collect, and the clock had started ticking the second he'd pressed purchase last night.

*"No going back now,"* he thought, and let the screen go dark.

The television was still paused on London Spire's trophy shot, confetti frozen mid-fall behind them. He grabbed the remote off the couch and flipped channels until HGO TV came up, the same feed that ran at WattsUp, Kazuku's official broadcast network. The bear wasn't on-screen this time. A panel of analysts sat around a glass table instead, all of them talking fast.

"...complete roster shakeup," one of them was saying, gesturing at something off-camera. "Teams built their entire identity around 3v3 flexibility, and now they've got three weeks to find specialists for roles hybrid builds used to cover. Dosuke AI's team is already shopping for a dedicated support, and they're not the only ones."

Tatsuya's hand went still on the remote. *"Dosuke's scrambling."* The thought landed harder than it should have. Dosuke AI's build had always reminded him of the old Warden concept he and Eren had worked out together on graph paper, back when they'd still been theorizing builds neither of them could afford to play. A jack-of-all-trades setup that could flex into damage, healing, or crowd control depending on what the team needed in the moment. Effective in 3v3 because versatility covered gaps. In 5v5, with room for real specialists, that same versatility turned into a liability.

*"Hate that I gave my first idea to that jackass,"* Tatsuya thought, Eren's face surfacing unbidden behind his eyes, grinning across a cafeteria table that didn't exist anymore. He shoved the memory down and kept watching.

Another analyst cut in, pulling up a graphic. "And it's not just the pros. Veteran players are cashing in Legacy Points en masse to rebuild fresh for the new season. The economy's about to flip."

Tatsuya muted it and set the remote down. He'd heard enough. The whole competitive ecosystem was in flux, which meant opportunity, but only if he could actually get in before the window closed.

He pulled his laptop off the kitchen counter and dropped onto the couch, opening the HGO Companion App he had downloaded on it. Market prices loaded first, item-set data sorted by zone and rarity tier. Blue gear running five figures in yen for a single piece. Legendary gear didn't even have listed prices, just auction-house estimates that made his stomach drop. He scrolled past all of it, looking for the baseline, the floor a new player could actually stand on.

Common gear was cheap. Uncommon gear was attainable. Rare gear started to hurt. The math made sense, it was a ladder, but it also meant the deck-building layer everyone kept talking about only mattered if you survived long enough to build a deck worth playing.

*"Start point matters more than the endpoint right now,"* he thought, pulling up the race-select data. *"Can't chase endgame gear if I pick wrong at character creation."*

The per-race stat table loaded, six numbers running down each row. Courage, Power, Wisdom, Speed, Fortune, Energy. He'd seen versions of this table before in forums and theorycrafting threads, but those had all been guesswork. This was official data, pulled straight from the app's database the day after the expansion announcement.

Elf caught his eye first. High Wisdom, solid Energy, average everywhere else. Magic-capable by default, which most races weren't. *"Spellforger path, maybe Windrider if I want ranged DPS,"* he thought, already building forward from that baseline in his head. *"Wisdom scales spell damage and learning speed. That's the gate I actually care about."*

He scrolled further, checking Dwarf, Beastfolk, Human, each one trading strengths in different directions. Dwarves traded Speed for raw Power and Courage. Beastfolk leaned Speed and Fortune. Humans split the difference on everything, the safe pick for someone who didn't know what they wanted yet.

*"Not me,"* Tatsuya thought, going back to Elf. *"I know exactly what I want."*

He pulled up a second tab, zone progression data, dungeon clears sorted by average party composition. Elves showed up disproportionately in high-level content, not because the race was overpowered, but because Wisdom let them pivot faster than other builds when the fight shifted under them. That was the edge. Not raw damage, not tankiness, not even utility. Adaptability. Being able to read a situation three moves ahead and respond before the rest of the team even saw the problem coming.

*"That's the game I actually know how to play,"* he thought, and closed the laptop.

Research had a ceiling. He could read market trends and stat tables until his eyes bled, but this was full-dive VR. No amount of reading substituted for actually being in the game, feeling how the controls responded, testing whether his instincts from years of watching translated into anything real when his hands were the ones on the skill.

He checked his phone. His bank balance sat at the top of the screen in clean, unforgiving numbers.

[¥47,320]

He backed out and opened Loop. His profile loaded, follower count sitting in the corner like it always did.

[9 followers]

Nine people he didn't know, following an account he barely posted from, watching a guy who'd never logged into the game they all cared about. *"That's gonna change,"* he thought, locking the screen.

He opened the booking app next, filtering by ward, then by availability. Third-gen cafes across Shibuya came up first, sleek storefronts with waiting lists that stretched into next month. He scrolled past them, already knowing what he'd find. Every single one was booked solid, Winter Bash hype driving demand past anything the infrastructure could handle.

Second-gen pods weren't much better. The Tabuchi-backed cafes had availability, technically, but the earliest slot was March, three months out, long past the point where any of this mattered.

*"This is what I was worried about,"* Tatsuya thought, thumb moving faster now, scrolling deeper into results he'd already written off.

A single listing caught his eye halfway down the third page. Sakura Arcade, a name he didn't recognize, marked with a small gray tag that read [Legacy Hardware - First-Gen Pods Available]. The description mentioned limited compatibility with newer titles, walk-ins accepted, and a single line at the bottom that made him stop scrolling entirely.

[Free after 5pm on weekdays.]

He clicked through to the full listing. Two pods, both first-generation Kazuhaya Tech, the same hardware his father had helped engineer before Nexus Enterprises bought the patent rights and moved on. The same pods that shouldn't be able to run HGO at all, according to every official source that mattered.

*"Shouldn't run at all,"* he thought, reading the compatibility warning twice. *"Unless you know exactly what's underneath the casing."* And he did, or close enough. Dad's notes went back further than any official spec sheet ever bothered to. *"Which means it's possible. Just needs someone who actually knows what they're doing."*

He checked the time. 3:47 PM.

Sakura Arcade's free window opened in just over an hour, and he still had to pick up the drive from Nexus Enterprises first. He stood, grabbed his jacket off the back of the couch, and headed for his room, pulling the door open to a space that still smelled faintly like old solder and circuitry.

Daichi's tech manual sat on the shelf above his desk, spine cracked from years of use, pages dog-eared in a dozen places where his father had marked something worth coming back to. Tatsuya pulled it down, weighing it in his hands for a second longer than necessary, then tucked it under his arm.

*"Not sure why I need this yet,"* he thought, heading for the door. *"Just a hunch."*

---

Nexus Enterprises' flagship store took up half a block in Ginza, glass and brushed steel rising three stories over a plaza with more foot traffic than Tatsuya had ever seen outside a WattsUp on tournament night. He'd been down here maybe twice in his life, both times as a kid, dragged along on some errand of Chiyo's, face pressed to a display window while she handled business he'd never been old enough to ask about. It didn't look like a place that sold anything so much as a place that let people look at what the future was supposed to be.

He pushed through the doors and the smell hit him first, something clean and faintly metallic, nothing like the ozone-and-rain mix that lived in his lungs most nights. The ground floor read more museum than store, third-gen helmets mounted on individual pedestals under warm spotlights, HGO's compass rose etched into the glass casing around each one, a wall-sized display looping match highlights on a screen taller than he was.

"...and that's game, London Spire takes the World Championship..." Yuna's voice, cutting through the ambient noise every time the reel restarted.

The pickup counter sat toward the back, past a bank of demo pods where a handful of kids waited their turn to try on a helmet for five free minutes. A line had formed anyway, everyone holding the same Nexus Enterprises confirmation text he had on his own phone. Tatsuya joined it and kept his head down, watching the guy ahead of him collect a small package, sleeve glossy with the Nexus Enterprises logo, the compass rose stamped in gold across the front. The same package got handed over three more times before Tatsuya reached the counter himself.

"Confirmation number," the attendant said, not looking up.

Tatsuya rattled it off from memory and watched the guy scan something on his tablet, then disappear into the back. He came out a moment later with the same sleeve, same logo, and slid it across the counter.

"Drive's pre-loaded," the attendant said. "Activation happens on first login. You've got seventy-two hours from purchase to register it, after that the license locks and you're stuck rebuying."

"Got it," Tatsuya said, taking the package and turning back toward the door before the guy could upsell him on something he couldn't afford.

Outside, the sun was already dropping behind the buildings, late-afternoon light going orange at the edges. He checked his phone. 4:22 PM. Sakura Arcade's free window opened in thirty-eight minutes, and he still had to get all the way back across town to Shibuya.

He pulled his helmet on, kicked the Haya EB to life, and opened it up down the nearest stretch of clear road, downtown thinning out into the streets he actually knew.

---

Sakura Arcade sat on a side street Tatsuya didn't recognize, tucked between a shuttered ramen shop and a convenience store that looked like it was barely hanging on. The building itself was older than most of the blocks around it, brick and wood paneling where everything else had gone glass and steel, warm pendant lights glowing through the front windows in a way that read more like a lounge than a gaming cafe.

He parked the Haya EB around the side and stepped through the front door into a space that smelled faintly like old carpet and warm electronics. Themed gaming chairs lined the walls in neat rows, each one facing a monitor, most of them dark. A couple of people were scattered across the floor, headphones on, lost in whatever they were playing, but the place read quiet in a way that wasn't peaceful, just empty.

Behind the counter, a guy in his mid-thirties looked up from a tablet, dark hair pulled back in a small knot, sleeves rolled to his elbows. He had the look of someone who'd been running on caffeine and optimism for longer than either one was built to last.

"Help you?" he asked, setting the tablet down.

"I'm here for a pod," Tatsuya said. "First-gen, if you've got availability."

The guy's expression shifted, not quite a wince, more like someone who'd had this conversation too many times in the past week and was already tired of it. "I do, but I'm gonna warn you up front, they're first-gen Kazuhaya Tech. They don't play well with newer titles. I've had to turn away a dozen HGO hopefuls since the expansion announcement hit, it's not gonna run the way you think it will."

"I know," Tatsuya said. "That's fine."

The guy studied him for a second, like he was trying to figure out whether Tatsuya actually knew or was just saying he did, then shrugged and pulled a clipboard off the counter. "Sign in. Name, time, pod number. You've got the back room to yourself, both pods are open."

Tatsuya took the pen and wrote his name across the top line in clean block letters. Tatsuya Kazehaya.

The guy glanced down at the clipboard as he took it back, and something in his posture changed, subtle, a flicker of recognition he didn't bother hiding. "Kazehaya," he repeated, quieter now, like he was testing the weight of it. "That name mean something to you, or am I making things up?"

Tatsuya's hand tightened on the strap of his bag. *"Here we go."* "Depends what you think it means," he said.

"Daichi Kazehaya," the guy said, setting the clipboard down carefully. "He helped build these pods, back when Kazuhaya Tech was still a thing. Worked with my dad on the prototypes before Nexus bought them out."

Tatsuya nodded once, slow, giving nothing away. "He was my father."

The guy's expression shifted again, warmer this time, something almost apologetic underneath. "Leon," he said, offering a hand across the counter. "My sister and I run this place. Your dad's work kept these things running longer than they had any right to. I'm sorry for your loss."

Tatsuya shook his hand and let go, throat tight in a way he wasn't going to spend time on right now. "Thanks."

Leon nodded toward the back hallway. "Pods are through there, second door on the left. Take your time."

---

The pod sat in the corner of a small, dim room, its curved shell reflecting the single overhead light in long, uneven streaks. Tatsuya stood in front of it for a moment, taking in the scratched casing, the worn edges where the seal had been opened and resealed too many times, the faded Kazuhaya Tech logo still visible near the base. This was first-generation hardware, older than HGO by years, built for a completely different kind of VR experience, slower, smaller, more limited in every way that mattered.

*"Dad helped build this,"* he thought, and felt the weight of that sit heavier than it should have.

He set his bag down, pulled out the Nexus Enterprises sleeve, and slid the access drive free. It was smaller than he'd expected, sleek black casing with a single port on one end and the HGO logo etched into the surface in fine gold lines. He turned it over in his hands once, then opened the pod's access panel and found the drive slot buried under a mess of older connectors and cables that hadn't been touched in years.

The manual sat open on the floor beside him, pages marked in his father's handwriting, notes scrawled in the margins where Daichi had worked through problems nobody else had figured out yet. Tatsuya scanned the diagrams, cross-referencing connection points, then pulled a small toolkit from his bag and started working.

The override wasn't elegant. It was a bypass, a workaround, forcing the pod to accept input it was never designed to handle by rerouting power through secondary channels and tricking the system into thinking the HGO drive was legacy-compatible hardware. He worked quickly, hands steady, following his father's notes like a map through a problem that shouldn't have had a solution.

*"This isn't a one-time fix,"* he thought, tracing a finger along a component he recognized from the Haya EB's own guts, the same family of part, just older. Running current this hardware was never built to carry was going to wear it down for real, piece by piece, the same way it would have worn down the scooter if he'd ever let it run dry on maintenance. He'd need real replacement parts eventually, the kind that showed up inside a third-gen helmet, not a first-gen pod, and he'd need to keep doing this by hand, same as always. *"Nobody's coming to fix this but me. Same as everything else."*

Twenty minutes later, the drive clicked into place, and the pod's internal display flickered to life.

Tatsuya closed the access panel, sealed it, and climbed into the pod itself, lying back against the padded interior as the shell hissed shut above him. The heads-up display lit up inside his visor, clean white text against black.

[KAZUHAYA TECH FIRST-GENERATION VRPOD]
[INITIALIZING...]
[DRIVE DETECTED: HGO ACCESS LICENSE]
[WARNING: HARDWARE INCOMPATIBILITY DETECTED]
[OVERRIDE ENABLED]
[PROCEEDING...]

The display went dark. Then the world did.

Sound came back first, a low hum building underneath everything, then light, bright enough that Tatsuya's eyes watered behind the visor before the brightness normalized. He was standing in a white void, no walls, no floor, no ceiling, just empty space that stretched in every direction without end.

Text appeared in front of him, floating at eye level.

[WELCOME TO ERYNDAL]
[CHARACTER CREATION WILL NOW BEGIN]

A wheel materialized in front of him, spinning slowly, each segment marked with a race name and a small icon. Human. Elf. Dwarf. Beastfolk. And at the bottom, locked behind a red padlock symbol, Goblin.

Tatsuya reached out, fingers brushing the Elf segment, but he hesitated, gaze drifting down to the locked option. *"Goblin,"* he thought, curious despite himself. *"Why's that even here if it's not playable?"*

He tapped it, just to see what would happen.

The wheel stopped spinning.

The lights went out.

Not just in the pod. Everywhere. The hum underneath everything cut off mid-note, and the void vanished, replaced by pure, weightless black. Tatsuya's breath caught, hands reaching instinctively for something solid that wasn't there, and then sound crackled back, distant, distorted, Leon's voice cutting through the dark from somewhere outside the pod.

"Hey, you alright in there?"

Tatsuya blinked, vision swimming as light flickered back into the edges of his visor, unstable, strobing in and out like the system was trying to reboot and failing halfway through. "Yeah," he called back, voice tight. "I'm fine."

"Backup generator just kicked in," Leon said, closer now, right outside the pod. "You running anything unusual?"

*"Do I tell him?"* Tatsuya thought. *"Does he even know what I did?"*

The pod's external monitor was dark, he'd noticed that on the way in, a dead screen Leon hadn't bothered fixing because nobody was watching solo sessions anyway. Which meant Leon couldn't see what Tatsuya was doing, couldn't see the character-creation screen, couldn't see the locked race he'd just touched.

"No," Tatsuya said, keeping his voice level. "Just character creation. Standard stuff."

"Alright," Leon said, footsteps retreating. "Let me know if it kicks again."

The light stabilized. The void came back, white and empty, and the character-creation wheel reappeared in front of him, spinning again like nothing had happened.

Except it wasn't spinning anymore.

It was locked.

The Goblin segment glowed green where it had been red a moment before, and text appeared beneath it, small and certain.

[RACE LOCKED: GOBLIN]
[PROCEEDING TO ATTRIBUTE ALLOCATION...]

Tatsuya stared at the words, breath caught somewhere between his ribs and his throat, and the only thought that made it all the way through was sharp and simple and undeniable.

*"What the hell just happened?"*
