# Chapter Six: VRSA Inspection

The ride back to the Nakamura Grand ran itself the same way the ride to the hospital had, muscle memory carrying him through turns he didn't register making, the city still out there somewhere beyond the visor but not landing the way it usually did. His phone buzzed twice in his jacket pocket before he made it three blocks, Chiyo's name lighting up the screen both times, and he let it sit there unanswered.

*"Not tonight. I'll deal with it when I'm not this pissed off."*

The frustration was still sitting hot in his chest when he parked the Haya EB around back, locked it, and took the side entrance up to the apartment. The lobby sat quiet below. He let himself in, the day finally settling into his shoulders.

Just the hum of the refrigerator and the sound of his own boots on the floor. He toed a stray pair of Taji's sandals back into line by the door, automatic, the kind of straightening up he did every time he got home first, and left his bag beside them. His phone went face-down on the kitchen counter without a glance, and he headed straight for the shower.

The water came out hot enough to sting, and he stood under it longer than he needed to, letting the heat work through the knot sitting between his shoulders, the anger starting to loosen into something closer to exhaustion.

He pressed his forehead against the tile, water running down his face, and made himself breathe through his nose once, slow, the way he did when there wasn't a clean answer waiting on the other side.

*"I wouldn't even be this mad if I hadn't had to find out from two nurses who thought I was already gone."* He let the water hit the back of his neck a second longer. *"Why didn't she just tell me. Did she actually think I couldn't handle it."*

The thought didn't make him less angry. Just made the anger sit somewhere different.

He shut the water off, dried off fast, and pulled on a pair of sweats. On his way through the front room he swung by the kitchen counter, scooped the phone up without breaking stride, and carried it back to his room before dropping onto the bed. Only then did he actually look at what she'd sent.

Two texts, both short, both hours old.

**[Chiyo: You doing okay? Haven't heard from you in a bit.]**

**[Chiyo: Let me know when you get home safe.]**

Nothing about Tochi in either one, same as the note she'd left on the counter that morning, already gone before he'd even gotten up. First real contact between them all day.

He typed a reply, cut it down twice, and sent the shortest version.

**[I've decided on going to Tochi. Found some stuff in Dad's old manual I want to get back into. Picked up a new part-time job too. I'm good.]**

Short, on purpose. He wasn't in the mood to explain the rest of it tonight, and he wasn't sure he wanted to yet anyway. If she wanted the whole story she could ask when she got back. Right now this was his.

The phone buzzed again before he'd even set it down.

**[Chiyo: Wait, really? That's amazing, Tatsu. What changed?]**

**[Chiyo: A new job, what about Gato? We can talk about the hours when I'm back. School still comes first though, right?]**

**[School's still first. I got it.]** One line, sent.

**[Chiyo: I love you. Get some sleep.]**

He didn't answer that one. Opened the banking app instead, one more thing to check before he let himself sleep.

**[BANK  BALANCE: ¥64,120]**

Not much. But it was his, earned off two elixirs and an ability he was already learning how to use right. Something almost like a grin pulled at the corner of his mouth before he caught it.

*"Whatever this turns into, it’s not going toward the Tabuchi’s. Mom comes first but I have to somehow get them off me. I doubt paying them off will be that simple.*

He scrolled down out of habit, the way he always checked his own numbers, and the second account caught him before he could stop it. Still listed under Daichi Kazehaya. Still his to see, next of kin, nobody had ever bothered pulling him off it.

**[KAZUHAYA TECH -** **Daichi Kazehaya]**

**[BALANCE: -¥3,014,200]**

**[COLLECTIONS NOTICE: TABUCHI HOLDINGS]**

He stared at his own balance first, then Dad's, then the name sitting under the collections notice, and Eren's face showed up uninvited, easy and entitled the way it always looked right before it said something meant to sting.

*"He's already taken enough. Now they want more on top of it."* His jaw set. *"I'll be damned if I hand them a single thing Dad wasn't willing to give them himself."*

But legally his hands were tied as long as the balance sat there owing, and that was the actual answer to a question he'd never bothered asking outright: *"Seven million."* He said it flat, testing the weight of it. *"Not impossible. Gonna be hard, but if there's anywhere I can actually do it, it's HGO."*

He set the phone down for real this time, lying back against the pillow, and let himself actually feel it, not the anger from earlier, something closer to the thing he'd been chasing since he was sixteen.

*"Debt comes first. The family doesn't need more pressure right now, least of all Chiyo. If everything plays out right I get Mom better. Maybe I can compete on top of it."* The fatigue was shifting in now, the good kind, the kind that came after a day that had actually moved somewhere.

His eyes closed on something almost like excitement sitting underneath the exhaustion.

• • •

Morning came too early, sunlight cutting through the blinds in a way that said he'd slept past his usual alarm. He checked his phone. 7:43 AM. December 11th.

*"Damn."* He sat up, a loc falling loose across his face that he pushed back automatically. *"Better move if I'm getting research in before Leon."*

He rolled out of bed, crossed to the closet he shared with the twins. His head nearly touched the ceiling frame when he stepped inside, which still caught him off guard sometimes.

*"Wild that I'm actually in HGO now."* He pulled a red hoodie off its hanger, grabbed clean joggers, his Humu 1's off the floor.

*"Not the class I wanted. But honestly? I think I might've landed something good by accident. Funny how that works."* He tugged the hoodie down. 

He got dressed, cleaned up his room, and dug the folded note out of his jacket pocket before putting it away.

Yunaile's handwriting at the top, clean block letters, the friend code underneath. He read the date at the bottom.

December 23rd. 689450YY.

*"Just a date. No name, nothing else."* He turned it over once. *"Either she's busy that day, or she wants me there."* He folded it back along the same crease and slid it back into his pocket.

He grabbed the laptop off the counter on his way to the kitchen stool and started typing before he'd even sat down fully.

**[Shiv | HGO lore]**

Almost nothing came back official. Three sparse lines from the game's own codex, two dead forum threads from launch week, both abandoned. He scrolled through them twice and gave up.

*"Alright, fine. I'm learning this one the hard way."*

He backed out and pulled up his character sheet, skill list on the left, thumb scrolling. Gambler's Gambit sat there with barely a paragraph under it, the World Quest tag pinned at the top of his log, both active, both about as explained as nothing. He closed a patch-notes post, new World Quest content confirmed rolling out, and sat with that a second.

*"Everyone out there already knows it's live. That's a target on me whether I want one or not."*

He switched to Loop, set his handle to TDRAG to match the game, and set a goblin icon as his profile image instead, something pulled straight off his own character sheet. No real photo, not while the goblin stayed separate from anything anyone could actually trace back to him.

After swiping through Loop for a bit he pulled up the Companion App next. Pro streams stacked down the front page, viewer counts running six figures on some of them, sponsor logos scrolling under the names that mattered.

*“There can be real money if I decide to stream the world quest but that comes with a huge target on my back.”*

He'd read enough threads to know what higher-level players did when they wanted something someone else had. World Quest items came back bound, forums were consistent on that much, but bound didn't mean safe. 

*"If someone decides I'm worth the trouble, I won't  have an answer for that yet."*

He almost closed the tab. Then the words Sevish said echoed in his head. 

*" Sevish did say I would be an Ally of Thrash, it holds as long as I don't blow the quest. Two powerful npcs plus a warband of goblins backing me means anyone coming after me needs an actual party. That's a commitment on their end too."*

Half the accounts that could actually field that kind of party were sitting frozen anyway, still deciding whether to cash out for Legacy Points before the reset makes the call for them. Fewer real threats walking around this exact week than there'd be in a month.

He pulled up the Tochi portal next. Entrance application sitting exactly where he'd left it weeks ago, untouched. He filled in what he could from memory, stopped at the submission window.

December 23rd.

Same date as the note in his pocket.

*"Already decided."* He looked at it one more second. *"I need Tochi more now than I did a week ago. If I'm expected to actually repair those pods I need proper training and they are the only ones I know that offer that level of teaching without being at university."*

He skimmed the race comparison page while he double-knotted his laces and caught something he'd glossed over before. Every other race had hard class locks sitting right there in print. Goblin's column was nearly empty. Low base stats, almost no restrictions.

*"Nobody's taken this seriously because the numbers scared them off on day one."* A short breath, something like a real grin. *“ And if the magic path opens up on top of it, which it looks like it actually will, that's a hybrid nobody has mapped out yet because nobody stayed long enough to find out."*

He checked the time. 8:47 AM. Sakura Arcade was thirty minutes out before placing his phone in his pocket.

*"I gotta move. Don't want to be stuck in camp when the Gambler's Gambit timer resets."*

He grabbed Daichi's manual off the desk, shoved it into his bag with his laptop, and headed for the door.

The ride gave him room to breathe, the Haya EB humming steady under him, the city still waking up around the edges. He pulled his phone at a red light, Loop already open on habit.

**[@Dosuke_AI: Has anyone else noticed that the rally outside Lunarest never popped this morning?]**

**[@PK_Wraith: Lunarest or nah. somebody confim..]**

**[@Xx_Wraithborn_xX:No doesn't look like they spawned yet]**

He killed the screen before the light changed. *"That means I'm ahead for now."*

Sakura Arcade looked different in the morning light, less like a place barely holding on and more like a place that just needed someone to actually push it. A woman was behind the counter when Tatsuya walked in, red hair, street-biker aesthetic, sharp eyes that landed on him the second he crossed the threshold and didn't move off.

Leon's sister had to be. The resemblance was there once you looked.

"How can I help you?" She said it directly.  

"“Looking for Leon,” Tatsuya said, keeping his voice level, hands loose at his sides. “He’s expecting me.”

"You're the guy fixing the pods." Not a question. She jerked her chin toward the back hallway, already turning like the conversation was done, or doing a good job acting like it was. "He's back with the VRSA  Inspector near them.  Go through."

"Appreciate it."

He held her look half a second longer than the exchange needed, then turned for the hallway.

Leon was crouched near the second pod’s access panel with a man in a branded VRSA polo, both of them bent over a tablet, deep in something technical, cables spread across the floor in a tangle that looked worse than it probably was.

"Morning.”Leon said, straightening up slightly, one hand still resting on the pod’s frame. “Inspector’s already here. Good timing.”

"Take your time," Tatsuya said, dropping his bag by the wall where it wouldn’t be in the way, the strap landing with a soft thud against the concrete.

Leon peeled off a few minutes later, the same energy from yesterday still sitting on him before returning back to Tatsuya's side. 

“Inspector’s confirming what I figured,” Leon said, rubbing the back of his neck, glasses slipping slightly down his nose before he pushed them back up. “Second pod’s a real project, not a lost cause.”

"Good. I've got leads on parts from Dad's manual. Can pull more if we need specifics."

"Exactly what I wanted to hear." Leon leaned back against the wall, arms loose, posture settling into something that read more relieved than he was probably willing to say out loud. "Been sitting on those pods for two years thinking they were basically scrap." He tipped his head toward the front of the arcade and dropped his voice to a whisper. "Sorry if Arya gave you a hard time out there. She's been asking questions since yesterday. I don't want her near this until we're cleared and running."

"Makes sense," Tatsuya said, and left it there.

They spent the next twenty minutes going through the parts list from Daichi's manual, cross-referencing against what Leon could source locally, flagging the stuff that would need Tochi's labs or a specialty supplier.

"This batch I can't get here," Leon said, tapping a line on the sheet. "Guy I know in Akihabara still stocks first-gen internals, but I've got to go pull them myself, nobody ships this stuff anymore. I'll head out once the Inspector's squared away here."

"I've got leads on funding it, too," Tatsuya said. "Not gonna let you front the whole bill on something that's supposed to make you money."

"We'll figure that part out." Leon didn't argue it further, which told Tatsuya more than an actual answer would have.

By the end Leon looked more settled than he had when Tatsuya walked in.

"Doable," Leon said. "Expensive, but doable. And if we pull it off, we're the only place in a five-ward radius with working first-gen HGO access." He clapped Tatsuya on the shoulder once, the kind that felt more like sealing something than saying goodbye. Then his face shifted, more careful. "Before you get in there, though. The Inspector's not done yet, so let me actually explain what he's doing back there, because it matters for you specifically."

"Go for it."

"He's not fixing anything. That's you, that's the only reason any of this is possible in the first place." Leon rubbed the back of his neck. "What he's doing is deciding whether VRSA's willing to sign off on us. Think of it like a road closure. Doesn't matter how good the road actually looks, if the sign says closed, you don't drive on it, or you're the one eating the consequences, not the city. "

"And the first pod? The one I already used?"

"He's putting in the external monitor. Both pods need it before either one can run anything commercially, period, that's the law, not an arcade rule."

"External monitor,” Tatsuya repeated slowly, turning the phrase over in his mouth, not liking the sound of it yet. “Means I need someone watching while I play.”

"That’s exactly what that means," Leon said. " Once you're in the game, your mind and your body are both gone. Your vitals will be on a chart, your gameplay will be displayed on the second monitor. It requires someone to be seated during all sessions in case something happens where the operator has to eject the person playing aka you”

*"“Makes sense. So basically whoever’s my operator is gonna see every move I make from here on out.”* Something about that settled uncomfortably in his chest, a weight he hadn’t anticipated sitting there.

“Third-gens do it with a VR agent instead of a person,” Leon went on, pushing his glasses up again, a nervous tic Tatsuya was starting to recognize. “Part of why they cost what they cost. Old pods don’t have that, so it’s gotta be an actual body in the chair for most sessions.”

"That’s pretty strict for just gaming right?."

“Tell that to VRSA,” Leon said, and a little of his usual dry humor came back, the corner of his mouth twitching. “I’ll do it myself, for what it’s worth. Keeps this whole thing quiet a while longer, and honestly” He paused, rubbed the back of his neck again, avoiding eye contact. “I also thought about the sponsorship idea, and I’ll be honest, my grandpa was against stuff like that, mainly because corporations always have funny business attached. I know it sounds crazy because I haven’t even seen you play this game yet, but because of what you’re willing to do for us in fixing these things, I’m willing to take a gamble on you too. So while you’re in there, consider me your eyes out here. I may not play, but Arya’s been talking to me about HGO for years, I can at least be a resource when you need something on this side.”

“That works for me,” Tatsuya said, and meant it. “Appreciate that. And to keep it clean and real with you my gambling, I got a real need. My mom’s sick, in a coma, and I need money fast. Real fast. I think HGO’s blessed me with something that can actually get that done.”

Leon didn’t say much after that, just nodded once, slow, and let the room breathe for a few seconds, the weight of what Tatsuya had just said sitting between them before Leon cleared his throat and changed the topic.

“Inspector’s got maybe another hour on the install,” Leon said, glancing back toward the booth. “You can get in now if you want, or wait till he’s fully done.”

“I’ll just wait it out,” Tatsuya said, dropping his bag back by the counter with a soft thud. “Rather go in once everything’s done than log in and out twice for no reason.”

“Suit yourself,” Leon said, tipping his head toward the front of the arcade.

Tatsuya took a stool near the front counter while the Inspector worked. Arya was cleaning down the front counter a few feet away, and the quiet between them lasted longer than he wanted it to.

“So you’re the one actually breaking that pod in first,” she said, not quite looking over, her hands still moving through the grips. “Leon’s been weird about it since yesterday. Won’t say why.” A glance, quick, sharp green eyes meeting his for half a second before going back to the counter. “Figured I’d just ask you instead.”

“I was told not to say anything right now,” Tatsuya said, keeping his voice easy, hands resting on his knees. “Ask Leon if you want the real answer.”

“Already did,” she said, and the corner of her mouth twitched, not quite a smile but close. “He changed the subject. Twice.”

“Then I guess you’re stuck waiting,” Tatsuya said.

She went back behind the counter, but her posture had shifted slightly, shoulders still tense, like she wasn’t done with the conversation yet. “Fine. Can’t hide it forever though. I do work here.”

He let it sit there, easy enough, and killed the rest of the wait scrolling through his phone, nothing loading that actually mattered, just something to do with his hands until the inspector called it done.

By the time the inspector finished, the operator’s booth had changed. A second monitor now sat angled beside the first pod, slim and modern compared to the older hardware around it, a soft green waveform glowing across its screen in steady pulses. Leon was crouched in front of it with the inspector, both of them running through something on a checklist, and Leon waved Tatsuya over without looking up.

“He’s signing off now.”Leon said, straightening up with a small grunt, one hand on his lower back.

The inspector clicked something on his tablet, nodded once in that flat, procedural way people did when they’d done the same task a hundred times, and peeled a small certification sticker onto the pod’s frame without any ceremony.

“You’re clear for sessions,” the inspector said, his tone as flat as his expression. “Standard operator protocol applies the whole time, no exceptions.”

“Got it,” Leon said. “Appreciate you fitting us in on short notice.”

The inspector packed up his gear, tablet and tools disappearing into a branded case, and headed for the front without another word.

Leon dropped into the chair at the operator’s booth, the monitor already syncing to the pod’s feed, green lines stabilizing across the screen.

“Alright,” Leon said, glancing over the readouts once, then back to Tatsuya. “Since we overrode this thing, we’re gonna need to take breaks. If we don’t, it’ll overheat, and that’s not safe for you to be in if it does.”

*“Right. Makes sense.”* Tatsuya climbed into the pod, the padded interior cool against his back, and pulled up his account settings on the small HUD that flickered to life in his vision. Based on what he’d read in the forums, you needed to preset streaming before entering and loading the game, not after.

He flipped it so he could go live as soon as got back in hgo.

**[STREAM STARTING IN 60 SECONDS – TDRAG]**

No names blinked into the chat window yet. Just him and an empty feed.

*“Alright. Let’s get it started.”*

The visor lowered, sealing him in, and the world outside went dark.

**[END CHAPTER SIX]**
