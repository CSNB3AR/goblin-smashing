# Chapter Two: Talk to the Bear

I didn't move. I kept both hands flat on the counter and breathed in through my nose and out slow, the way I always did when something was about to go wrong and there was no clean way to stop it. The black car idled outside the middle bay. The silver lotus on the jacket meant Kansai Black Lotus, Eren's guild, funded by Tabuchi money and built on a concept I'd helped him theorize back when we were still friends.

The passenger door opened a second later. Silver hair, a warmup jacket slung over one shoulder, still half in a practice kit that looked expensive even from across the lot. I knew the build before I could place the name.

Haruto. Kansai Black Lotus's best player, the guy they'd built the guild around, the beta's Breakout Player of the Season, the kind of player people actually respected instead of just hyping for content.

*"Great. Exactly what I needed tonight. Eren, and he brought a witness."*

Eren said something to Haruto that didn't carry through the window, gestured toward the charge bays like he was making a point, and the two of them started across the lot toward the front door, boots scuffing on wet asphalt, their breath fogging in the cold.

The bell over the door announced them before either one spoke, loud over the hum of the broadcast still running on the screen above my head.

Eren came in first, the way he came into every room, chin up and shoulders back, blond hair pushed off his forehead, the black jacket zipped to his throat. Haruto followed a step behind, silver hair damp from the cold, hands loose in the pockets of his warmup, and let the door swing shut with his heel.

Eren's eyes found my face under the store lights and stalled there, recognition landing a half-second late, then all at once, his expression shifting into something that wasn't quite a smile but was trying to be one.

"Well, well." He glanced sideways at Haruto like he needed a witness for this, his grin widening. "Guess who we've got here. Our dear friend Tatsu, working the night shift. Didn't know you'd taken up residence at the WattsUp."

My face didn't move. Some small muscle around my mouth flattened out and tightened, the closest thing to a reaction I was willing to give away for free.

*"Dear friend. Right. And I'm the king of Shibuya."*

"It's called a job, Eren. You should try one." I nodded toward the window and the black car parked beyond it, my voice level and professional, the same tone I used for every customer who walked through that door and didn't deserve anything extra. "Bay's open. Card or account."

Eren didn't answer that. He leaned into the counter instead, both hands flat on the surface I'd just cleaned, close enough that I caught his cologne cutting through the cold air he'd brought in with him, something sharp and expensive.

"You watching the broadcast?" Eren asked, tilting his chin toward the screen where Kazuku's bear was still grinning behind scrolling captions about the expansion launch. "Crazy night, right? We got bounced out of the top eight."

I rolled my eyes toward the ceiling and back. "Heard about that," I said, and left it there.

I didn't say what I was actually thinking, that a guild built on a stolen concept always hit a ceiling the concept alone couldn't push through, and that the only reason Eren's had gotten as far as it had was his dad's money buying him gear good enough to cover the gaps.

Behind Eren, Haruto had gone quiet, his eyes moving between the two of us like he was working out a math problem of his own.

Eren folded his arms across his chest and rocked back on his heels.

"My father wanted me to remind you the account's still open," Eren said, his voice dropping into that lower register he always used when he wanted to sound like a man.

I let my eyes slide up to the ceiling for a second. "I haven't forgotten," I said. "Tell your dad he can send an email next time. Save you the charge." I kept my hands exactly where they were, breathing even, my pulse steady in my throat. "Bay's open. Card or account."

Something in Eren's easy expression flickered, just for a second. It might have been irritation, or disappointment, or just the faint sense that this conversation wasn't going the way he'd pictured it.

"Card," Eren said, pulling it from his wallet and tapping it against the reader without looking away from my face, holding eye contact like it was a challenge I was supposed to rise to. "I hope your mother's doing better. I heard she's still at the hospital. That's rough, man. Really rough."

*"Fake as always. Say one more word about her."*

Heat climbed my neck. My grip on the counter's edge tightened at the knuckles, my jaw set so hard my teeth ached, and for one breath I wanted to reach across and grab his jacket. I let it go before the tension could spread anywhere visible. Nothing on my face moved.

"She's fine," I said, low and even, and I held his eyes. "Your card's going through. After that, the door's behind you."

Something in Eren's grin slipped, just for a second, and Haruto's eyes cut over to him. I watched the charge indicator climb on the screen in front of me, green light by green light, until the payment cleared and Eren finally stepped back from the counter.

Eren turned toward the door without another word, his jacket catching the overhead light, the Kansai Black Lotus emblem glinting silver across his back.

Haruto lingered a beat longer than he needed to, his weight still resting slightly against the counter.

"Guess I'll see you in Eryndal, then," Haruto said, quiet enough that the words didn't carry past the counter, just between the two of us.

"I don't even play that game," I said.

"Now's your chance." Haruto's eyes flicked to the door Eren had walked out of, then back to me. "I'd like to see what the brain behind that build looks like when it's actually playing it. Some people hit a ceiling with it. I don't think you would." He nodded once toward the TV, small and deliberate. "Beats wasting away behind that counter."

"That counter keeps the lights on at my place," I said. "I'm not knocking it."

I rolled my shoulders back and gave him a nod, nothing more spent on it than that. *"Haruto doesn't talk like Eren. That's what makes it stick."*

Haruto straightened up, pulled his jacket tighter against the cold, and followed Eren out into the night.

The door swung shut behind them with a soft chime, and the store went quiet again, nothing but the light bulbs buzzing overhead.

I stood there a moment longer than I needed to, watching through the window as the black car's taillights disappeared down the block, red glow fading into the neon of Shibuya at midnight. The hum of the charging cables outside was the only sound left in the place.

The courtyard at Tochi came back without me asking, the one spot where nobody bothered me, a tablet propped on my knee and an HGO trailer playing. Dad was already gone by then. He had always loved the idea of building a world inside VR, and he used to talk about it all the time. I didn't know why, but that game made me feel connected to him. Then Eren walked by, stopped, and said something about the trailer, and for months after that we argued about the game between classes until I thought I finally had a friend. I'd been wrong. Once he saw what my head could do, he made his offer. I couldn't get into the game yet. He could. So he flipped, ran my work on his father's money, and let the whole school watch him do it. It was the best thing I'd ever made, and now it was his. My throat went tight.

The light bulbs buzzed overhead and the courtyard was gone. Just the counter, the dark glass and my own hands.

*"Fuck. I just wish I'd never trusted that snaky bastard."*

Haruto's voice came back anyway. *"I'd love to see what the brain behind that build looks like when it's actually playing it."* I had never really answered him. I'd nodded and let him walk out on it, and now the words sat in my chest and wouldn't move. My jaw worked once. *"Why would he ask me that? Why now?"*

*"Sure, send Eren. He's too dumb to even think to ask why."* I raked a hand back through my locs, and a loose one slid across my face. As long as his father's money kept feeding the guild, he'd do whatever he was told.

*"But why keep pushing us for the money? Chiyo isn't broke, she's just not paying while the lawyers fight it. It has to be bigger than that."* I made myself look at the shelves, the neat rows of rice balls and phone chargers, the dark lot past the glass, and breathed until my pulse slowed. Every call circled back to Dad's paperwork, and I still didn't have an answer. I reached for the lights.

I looked up at the screen. The bear was gone. In its place, the discount banner ran on a loop, gold numbers ticking down a countdown that had just crossed under seventy-two hours.

**71:42:18**

**71:42:17**

I took one final breath and went to close up.

I moved through closing on autopilot. I killed the bays one at a time, the digital readouts blinking dark in sequence. Counted the drawer twice, same total both times, bagged it for Gato's morning deposit. Wiped down the counter one last pass, out of habit more than need, the rag coming up clean. Hit the lights, stepped out into the cold, the door locking itself behind me with a heavy clunk that echoed across the lot. The cold hit my face and my breath fogged in front of me.

Around back, the Haya EB moped was sitting exactly where I'd left it, old paint gone matte in patches under the yellow security light, the helmet hanging off the handlebar where I always left it when I knew nobody else would be back here. I pulled it on, tucked the loose loc back before it could catch in the strap, and swung a leg over the seat, the cold leather biting through my jeans.

The touchscreen between the handlebars flickered awake under my thumb the way it always did, half a second of hesitation like it didn't trust me yet. I waited it out, thumb pressed flat to the glass, until the dash finally lit green and the charge indicator climbed and stalled at two bars, not the full it should have read after a day on the charger.

I kicked the stand up, rolled it off the curb with my feet, and pulled out onto wet asphalt, letting Shibuya blur past in long smears of orange and white in the rain that had picked up again. The last trains had stopped running and the sidewalks were nearly bare. I opened it up on a long clear stretch, the hum of the electric motor climbing under me, rain needling cold against my cheeks where the helmet didn't quite cover. The hum stuttered once, a dip in the climb, then caught again.

Some track was playing low through the scooter's tiny speaker, one of Yuna's, something Momo had dropped into my queue two weeks ago without asking and I'd never bothered deleting.

The ride home ran out before I was ready for it. Two blocks short, the Haya EB slowed under my hand, the dash blinking dark for a full second and coming back dim, and then the motor quit altogether, the hum dropping out like someone had pulled a plug. I thumbed the throttle twice. Nothing. *"Come on, now. I'm already having a day."* I swung off into the rain and pushed it the rest of the way, boots splashing through the gutter, my arms burning on the handlebars, my breath rasping, rain needling my neck, my jaw tight as the familiar six-story building came up on my left, the one I'd looked at every night for two years without really seeing it anymore. A striped green awning caught the streetlight, faded a shade lighter than when we'd first moved in, and the glass doors of the lobby threw back the dead headlight of the scooter I was pushing before I steered it around the corner.

I parked around back on reflex, in the spot that wasn't technically mine but nobody else ever used, and came in through the side door the hotel staff used during the day.

She was still at the front desk. Alone.

I stopped in the doorway to the lobby longer than I meant to, just taking it in before she noticed me. Chiyo behind the counter in her blazer, the one she wore when she was working a shift instead of just managing, red hair falling loose over one shoulder in a way that meant she'd been standing there too long without a break. She was checking something on the reservation screen. The lobby smelled like floor polish and burnt coffee, and the screen gave a tired chirp every time she clicked.

A woman who led Japan's national volleyball team onto an Olympic court, still working a night shift that should have had two other people on it. I didn't have to ask why they weren't here.

She looked up and the tired dropped off her face on purpose, replaced with something warmer.

"You saw my message?" she asked, not looking away from me, her hands still moving across the keyboard. "About Tochi."

"Yeah," I said, my hands going into my jacket pockets, shoulders loose, posture casual in a way that took effort to hold.

"I need an answer soon, Tatsu." She said it plainly, no edge in her voice. "If you don't decide, I'm going to have to make the decision for you. You know that, right?"

I didn't answer that. I didn't have to. She'd meant it as a promise, not a threat, and I understood it exactly that way. She loved me too much to let me stay stuck, between a job that wasn't going anywhere and a school I'd walked away from for reasons she'd never pushed me to explain.

I gave her a nod that didn't commit to anything, the same nod I'd been giving her for months whenever this came up, and headed for the stairs, feeling her eyes shift back to the screen.

I took the steps two at a time, my boots landing heavy, the sound echoing in the stairwell.

*"So either I go back to Tochi with my tail tucked between my legs, or I do something first. Something she can't argue with. Something that proves I'm not just wasting time."*

Upstairs, the apartment was mostly dark. Momo's door was cracked open, a wedge of pink light spilling into the hallway, her voice humming along under her headphones to something with way too much reverb, a melody I recognized from the ride home. I caught the edge of her phone screen through the gap, @Yuna_N9 pulled up in her browser, a livestream replay looping in the corner, Momo mouthing the words without actually singing them out loud.

In the front room, Taji was still awake on the couch, wrapped in a blanket he'd clearly stolen from someone's bed, probably Momo's, based on the color, watching the same broadcast I'd just left behind at WattsUp. The London Spire trophy shot was paused on the screen, confetti frozen mid-fall, London Spire's captains in gold and white holding hardware that probably weighed more than it looked like it did.

"They actually did it," Taji said, not looking up, assuming the way he always did that I had no idea what he was talking about and needed the context explained.

"Yeah," I said, dropping onto the couch beside him and stealing a corner of the blanket back without asking. "It was on at work. Aogiri threw it at the end. Their leader sat on his hands and let the poison take him."

Taji turned to look at me, eyebrows raised, like I'd said something that didn't match the script he'd been expecting. "You actually watched it? Like, the whole fight?"

"Most of it," I said, leaning back into the cushions, one arm stretched along the back. "Corporate's got it on every screen in the building. It's not like I was busy tonight."

Taji cracked a small smile despite himself, the kind he tried not to give up too easily these days now that he was eleven and thought acting unimpressed made him look older. He shoved the rest of the blanket over without being asked, a peace offering that didn't need words attached to it.

I sat there in my own living room, the broadcast still paused on-screen, Momo's muffled humming coming through the wall, Taji's blanket warm against my legs, and Chiyo downstairs, still on shift. None of them had asked for this.

The Haya EB was still sitting dead around back, that long blank second when the dash went dark replaying in my head. I pushed myself up off the couch.

"Back in a minute," I said. Taji gave me a nod without looking away from the frozen trophy shot.

My room was the last door down the hall, the converted corner of the apartment my father had worked out of for most of my life, and I didn't bother with the overhead. The desk lamp was enough. The room smelled faintly of old solder and cold circuit boards, the way it had since long before the funeral, and two pale grooves still ran along the floorboards from the desk to the door where a wheelchair had worn them down.

Dad's tech manual sat on the shelf above the desk, spine cracked from years of use, pages dog-eared in a dozen places. *"The EB has to be fixable. Everything he built could be fixed."* I pulled the manual down and flipped to the section on the controller board, both hands flat on the desk, my thumb running down the margin notes in his slanted handwriting, looking for the line about the dash resetting itself under load.

Something slid out from between the pages and landed face-up on the desk. A single sheet, folded in thirds, soft at the creases like it had been sealed in there a long time. His handwriting again, but not the margin shorthand. Slower. Pressed harder into the paper.

**[Tatsuya,]**

**[If you're reading this, then I'm gone, and I didn't manage to deal with the Tabuchis. I can't explain everything, not on paper. But what's in this manual is what they need, so don't give it to them. Not a page, not a drawing. They've been pushing me for a long time to sell the business out from under us, and I haven't, and I hope that by the time you read this they still haven't gotten hold of anything that matters to their plans.]**

**[I'm sorry. I know I'm leaving this on you and Chiyo, and that isn't fair. But I know you can carry it.]**

**[If you want the truth, talk to the bear. He's the only one who has it, and he'll tell you. Just be careful who is listening.]**

**[I know this is a lot to put on you. Maybe you can do more with my schematics than I ever did, but I don't want to push you. If you meet the bear, a lot of what you're dealing with will probably sort itself out, and you'll understand why. I wish I could be there when you get to experience the world I helped create.]**

I read it once for the words and once for the date I couldn't find, because there wasn't one. The lamp buzzed faintly, the only sound in the room. My hands had gone flat on the desk again without me telling them to. At the bottom, where his name should have been, there was only a print in dark ink, smudged at one edge. I tilted the page toward the lamp and squinted until the shape came clear: a paw, four toes and a wide pad.

*"Talk to the bear."*

I said it out loud, low, to the empty room. *"What bear?"*

Nobody I knew had ever been called that. I ran through everyone who'd come up those stairs to see him, the parts suppliers, the repair guys, the neighbors who borrowed his tools, and none of them had a name that came close. *"A nickname, maybe. A shop. A bar called the Bear."* I tried it as a street, a station, a restaurant. Nothing fit. I tried it on Chiyo, and then on Mom, and dropped both before they finished forming. If either of them had known, they'd have told me a year ago.

I smacked the desk hard enough to rattle the lamp. *"Dad, why couldn't you just write a name?"*

Then I read the last line of that paragraph again. *Just be careful who is listening.* He hadn't left a name because a name could be read by the wrong person. Whatever the bear was, it had to be something only I would catch.

I rubbed the heel of my hand into one eye until the lamp went blurry. My phone lit up on the desk.

The sale reminder from Nexus. The bear on the screen was cartoon-round and grinning, one paw raised in a wave, sitting beside the gold countdown. **71:31:06.** Under it, in white letters, the line I'd heard on every screen for the last two weeks and tried not to hear once: *Bring your business here.*

I looked at the phone. I looked at the paw print on the page. I looked at the phone again.

*"No."*

*"The bear. Bear, bear, HGO. That damn mascot?"*

My stomach dropped and stayed there. *"Dad wouldn't hand me a cartoon. He'd never trust his last words to a cartoon."* But the bear wasn't a cartoon to anyone who'd met the man behind it, and nobody ever had. Nobody knew who Kazuku was. If anyone alive was careful about who was listening, it was the man who'd built himself a bear to hide behind.

*"The world I helped create."* I read it again with the game in my head instead of this room. The first-gen pod, the one Dad had spent his last years on, in this room, in a chair that wasn't built for the work. It had never been a side project. It had been a piece of this. My breath went out through my nose, slow, and a loose loc slid across my face that I didn't push back.

*"I could be wrong. I've been wrong about Dad before."* I'd spent a year telling myself the pieces only looked wrong because I was grieving. The calls that kept circling back to his paperwork. The Tabuchis, who wanted what was in this book more than they wanted their money. Standing there with the page still warm from my hands, I knew the only person in that apartment pretending was me.

I flipped to the back of the manual, to the first-gen pod schematic, the headset frame and the feed and the harness he'd drawn and redrawn until the paper wore thin. *"More than I did."* Nexus sold third-gen helmets now. I tried to picture that kind of power under an old first-gen pod, one that could actually run a game like HGO the way it should, and I could almost see where it would fit.

The thought dragged me straight to Tochi. Dad had learned everything he knew there, the circuits, the pods, all of it, and parts of that work were still sitting in the school's archives where I had never been allowed to look. I scratched along my jaw, and for the first time all night the school felt like more than a place I was running from.

I didn't have a plan. I had a bear, a game and a countdown, and the only door I could see to a man nobody had ever met was inside HGO. And it paid. Whatever else the game was, it paid, and Mom's bills hadn't stopped coming just because I'd found a letter.

I refolded the page along its old creases, slid it back in at the controller diagrams, and set the manual on the shelf exactly where it had been, spine out, because Chiyo and the twins weren't supposed to know there was anything in it to find. I killed the lamp and went back down the hall.

Taji hadn't moved. I dropped back onto the couch beside him, the blanket warm against my legs again.

I opened the app with my bank balance in it, the one that told me exactly how much room I had to move and how much I didn't.

Rent covered. Mom's hospital bill covered, barely, with Chiyo carrying most of the weight, groceries for one more week if we were tight about it. And underneath all of that, if I moved right now, there was enough left over for a drive.

My thumb swiped to the Nexus Enterprises purchase screen, the ad blinking next to a number that counted down the remaining time of the sale.

*"If I do this, I can't forget why I'm doing it."* Not for me. Not to prove something to Eren or show off to Haruto or get Loop clout or any of that. It was for them, and for the schematics Dad never got to finish. Haruto's question was still rattling around my head, and I hated that part of me wanted to answer it. My eyes flicked toward Taji, who was still staring at the frozen trophy shot like he was trying to memorize every detail.

*"Fine."* I pulled in a breath through my nose, held it longer than I needed to, and let it out slow. *"Doing it."*

*"Chiyo's going to kill me. Then she's going to bring me back and kill me again."* I pressed my thumb down on the purchase button before I could talk myself out of it, before the sensible part of my brain could start listing all the reasons this was a bad idea and I should wait and save more.

A small green confirmation bloomed on the screen a moment later, text appearing in clean white letters: **Purchase Complete. Your HGO Access Drive will be available for pickup within 24 hours.**

I locked my phone and set it face-down on the couch beside me, my hand coming up to rub the back of my neck. *"I can't waste a single day. Every hour I spend in Eryndal from here on has to count for something real."*

*"No noob bullshit. Find a way to turn this into money she can't argue with, and find a way to the bear."*

I didn't remember closing my eyes.

**[END CHAPTER TWO]**
