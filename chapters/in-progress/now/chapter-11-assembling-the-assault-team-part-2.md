# Chapter Eleven: Assembling the Assault Team, Part Two

I made it as far as the holding tent. I had barely sat down on the bedroll when Leon said, "Sit. Hold still," and the lamp smeared into one long white line, and then the world went out.

The lid hissed open on the ceiling of the pod room, soft lantern light after the dark of the visor. About three minutes had passed since the call. I sat up slow, and my joints complained the way they always did after a long dive. The inspector was already at the monitor bay, crouched in front of the open panel with his black suit pressed flat against the chair, his long black hair tied low at the neck, a bang falling across one lens of his glasses. His tools lay in a neat row on a cloth beside him.

Leon stood next to him, one hip against the desk, glasses slipping down his nose. He looked up when I moved.

"It hit the thermal limit," he said. "The override held. It's the stream that cooked it."

"Streaming roughly doubles the draw," the inspector said, flat, without looking up. "The unit was not built for that."

"Sorry about the pod," I said.

"Don't be." Leon pushed his glasses back up. "I've run this place a long time. I've watched a lot of streams on that screen." He nodded at the monitor in front of the inspector. "I never saw anything like what you just pulled."

"You could be my coach," I said, half a joke.

Leon didn't answer right away, still bent over the panel. Then, quieter, mostly to himself, "Coach. Yeah." A pause, like he was turning it over. "Yeah, I could see that."

"Give me the ten minutes," he said, louder. "Go stretch your legs. You've been under a long time."

I washed my face in the bathroom at the end of the upstairs hall, then leaned against the rail at the top of the stairs with my phone in both hands. The Romans I already knew: the legion's shields locked edge to edge, short swords working between them. For the rest I had a few minutes. The armies of China's Warring States had drilled their spear and shield lines until the front rank could take a charge, Japan's Sengoku armies had done the same with spear walls behind their shield screens, and the Vikings stood shoulder to shoulder with their shields overlapping in one solid row. Different centuries, same idea: a line that holds, and something sharp behind it. I kept my head down and my thumb moving, reading until I had the shape of it.

--------

"Hey, Nykri."

My head snapped up and met a pair of gray eyes before I'd finished the motion. She stood two steps down from the landing with one hand on the rail: white hair in a messy bun, ink running down both forearms to the wrist, a plain black tee, a half-smirk.

"How do you know that name?"

"Your handle's been on the lounge screen for hours." Arya came up one more step. "Leon has the stream running down there. I watched it, and I'd bet my last point that's you under that goblin skin."

"Okay. So you watched." I kept my thumb on the phone. "What do you want?"

"In." She said it plainly, without pushing closer. "Your quest, your camp, your fight. You're low on men. I watched Thrash drop to ten in an hour. You're up against four players, and you're putting all your trust in NPCs."

"They're not scripts."

"They follow patterns. They hold a line and they reset." She shrugged one shoulder. "I can think. I can read a fight."

"No."

"Come on. You really do need me."

"No." I pushed off the rail. "I don't know you."

The smirk went out of her face. For a moment she looked at the stairs, and when she looked up again her voice had dropped. "Look. I'm sorry I shrugged you off earlier. I've been going through a really rough time. I can be useful, and not just in a fight."

It was the kind of honest that made me stop. "Why are you so eager?"

"Because if I don't make it in this game, I have to leave my brother and everything here." She said it flat and quiet. "We probably have different reasons. But we're both relying on HGO to figure something out."

I looked at her for a long moment, at the ink on her arms and the tired line under her eyes. Down the hall the inspector's panel clicked shut.

"Yes," I said.

She let out a breath, and the half-smirk came back, thinner. "Then tell me what you need."
