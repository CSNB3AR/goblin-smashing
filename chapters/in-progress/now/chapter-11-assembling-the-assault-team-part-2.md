# Chapter Eleven: Assembling the Assault Team, Part Two

The lamp smeared into one long white line, the world went out, and the pod lid hissed open on the ceiling lights. Leon was already crouched at the side panel with the inspector, a flashlight clamped in his teeth, and he flapped one hand at me without turning around, which meant ten minutes, go.

I did the math on the way up the stairs. Thirty minutes on the game clock, and it had not stopped when I did. By the time I reached the bathroom at the end of the upstairs hall I had already decided I'd be back under the visor with whatever was left, and the plan I'd promised Sevish was still one sentence long. I sat on the closed lid with my phone and typed *how does a shield wall actually hold* into the search bar. The Romans I already knew, shields locked edge to edge with short swords working through the gaps. The armies of the Warring States had drilled their spear and shield ranks until the front could take a charge, and the Vikings had stood shoulder to shoulder with their shields overlapping in one solid row. Different centuries, same idea. *"A line that holds, and something sharp behind it."* I kept my head down and my thumb moving.

"Hey, Nykri."

My thumb froze mid-scroll. I looked up fast enough to feel it in my neck, and a pair of gray eyes was already waiting for me. She stood in the doorway with one shoulder against the frame, white hair in a messy bun, ink running down both forearms to the wrist, a half-smirk that didn't reach the rest of her face.

"How do you know that name?" I stayed on the lid, phone in my hand.

"I watched the stream." Arya tipped her head toward the floor below. "Gray skin, white hair, a goblin who talks to himself. Nobody else is running that, and Leon looked guilty enough downstairs to settle it."

"Okay. So you watched." I got up, because sitting down made me feel like I was the one being interviewed. "What do you want?"

"Would you create a guild with me?" She said it flat, hands loose at her sides, no run-up. "Not join your team. A guild, you and me founding it."

I stared at her. "You're serious."

"My last one had three leaders, and two of them cared more about the stream than the match." Her fingers moved while she talked, a quick flick toward the floor, a fist, an open palm. "I want one where the match is the point. You've got four raiders on your hill and a camp full of NPCs, and I can read a fight."

I stepped past her into the hall. *"Guild. With her. I don't even have a team."*

"Hey man, I'm talking."

"Heard you." I kept walking toward the stairs.

"Please, listen." She fell in a step behind me, close enough that I could hear her boots on the boards. "Why are you ignoring me?"

I stopped at the top of the stairs and looked back at her over my shoulder. "Pot calling the kettle black. You gave me that same energy when I first walked in."

She blinked, and then she laughed once, short and surprised, the smirk finally getting somewhere. "Okay. Fair."

"I'm good, though." I took the steps down two at a time, the way a person walks something off, and she followed.

At the bottom the pod room smelled of warm plastic and solder. Leon and the inspector were bent over the open panel with their backs to us, the inspector murmuring numbers in his flat voice while Leon said "Mm" at the right places. Arya kept her voice low and close behind my shoulder.

"I can seriously help you. A guild adds weight, and you don't have any right now."

"That's good to hear." I turned around and gave her a polite nod, the kind I'd give a cashier. "Not interested, thanks."

"I have information." She stepped around to where I could see her face. "About Thornwatch. I've got sources, nothing confirmed, only rumors. But it's going to be guild-based, Nykri. Whoever walks in there without one walks in as a free kill."

I didn't repeat a word of it. *"Guild-based. That's the second time somebody's said it."* If it was true, then a guild was either the best thing I could own or the fastest way to get every other guild in Tokyo pointing at me. I couldn't tell which, and I didn't answer on a guess.

"No," I said.

The smirk went out of her face all at once. Her hands stopped moving, and I realized that was the first time I'd seen them still.

"Please, just hear me out." She said it quietly, glancing once at Leon's back. "I wouldn't ask someone random to form a guild with me. I've been watching your stream. When I noticed my brother was actually helping you, I thought, okay, this is real. I'm sorry if I came off rude. I'm in a really bad spot."

I waited, because she wasn't done.

"I was texting my mom when you got here." Her thumb scraped the ink on her wrist, back and forth. "If I don't make it in this game, I have to leave my brother and everything here. We both rely on HGO to figure something out. I'm not asking you to like me. I'm asking you to let me be useful."

I scratched at my thin goatee, the habit I couldn't shake whether I was in the game or out of it. Four raiders in a red tent that drained anyone who crept close, and I'd be walking down a hill with two goblins I'd known for a day. *"I can't afford to die. And I don't know what's down there."* Arya had been through a real guild fight, and she'd read one better than any NPC I could point at. I looked at the ink on her wrist, at the way she was holding her own arm to keep from fidgeting, and I thought about how long she'd been carrying this by herself.

"Okay," I said. "Yes."

--------

Her whole body let out the breath. "Then what do you need?"

"What are you running?"

"Elf, Spellforger, level ten." She counted it off on her fingers. "Destruction, mostly. I put five points in the opening branch before the guild blew up and left the rest alone. I don't read guides, so most of my sheet is stubbornness."

"Is there a fog spell in the wind tree? Wind Mist."

Her eyes narrowed, the look of someone who already knew the answer and was waiting to hear the trick. "It doesn't deal damage. Fifteen seconds up, thirty seconds down, and it doesn't scale on anything you'd want to scale. I'm not spending fresh points on a spell that doesn't kill things."

"It blinds everyone inside. It mutes them too, so nothing that runs on stamina or mana works in there, and you see where everybody is while it's up." I leaned on the pod's edge with my arms folded. "Lightning conducts through it."

"Lightning also hits me, and my friends, and the guy holding the bucket." She shrugged one shoulder. "That's why nobody runs it."

"Take Protective Lightning. It's a passive, so your lightning doesn't hurt the people you're standing with." I pointed at her. "And did you read Chain Lightning all the way through?"

"Obviously."

"Did you read the part where it bounces off anything with mana? Allies included, and with the passive they're fine. You see them in the mist, so you use them as a relay when the target is out of range." I watched her face change. "I'm a goblin, but I have mana."

She was quiet, her tongue pressed against the inside of her cheek, her eyes somewhere past me. "Stormwall was my best field," she said slowly. "And I've never once used a teammate as part of a chain."

"I need the continuous version of the mist. The sporadic one gets you fifteen seconds, and that's not enough. The continuous one eats your mana and you can't move while you're holding it, but it stays up as long as you can pay."

"That costs another point." She said it the way people said the price of a bad meal.

"Fifteen unspent points since the guild went down." I kept my voice level. "I'd call that a fund."

For a second I thought she'd walk. Then she groaned and dragged a hand down her face. "Fine. Wind Mist, the continuous cast, and Protective Lightning. If you're wrong, you owe me every one of those points."

"There's no voice chat in the game," I said. "How do I know you're running dry?"

"I whistle." She said it like it was obvious, and she pursed her lips and gave me a sample, low and short. "Three slow ones when I'm getting low, spaced out so you don't mistake them for a bird. At ten mana I whistle three more, the same way, and that's your ten seconds."

"Ten seconds. Got it."

The inspector straightened behind us, panel closed, tablet tucked under his arm. "The unit is sound for now," he told Leon in that flat voice. "Do not run the stream at that draw again."

Leon looked at the clock on his monitor and winced. "Ten minutes was a lie. It's been twenty-five."

I did the math with my stomach. Five minutes left on the return clock. Arya already had her jacket on and was backing toward the door, spinning one finger by her ear. "J-Link. The big cafe down the line, third-generation pods, I book weeks ahead. See you on the other side, Nykri."

I shoved myself into the pod and the lid came down.

The tent was cold when the world came back, the lamp low and the canvas shifting in the wind. A bundle sat on the bedroll: leather bracers, a plain iron knife, and two coils of hemp rope somebody had thought to add. I strapped it on in the time it took to read the panel.

**[TIME LIMIT: 01:05:00]**
**[RETURN TO THE CENTER OF CAMP: 00:05:00]**

--------

Five minutes. I took the ridge path at a run, stakes and torches blurring past on both sides, and a pop-up slid across the top of my interface before I had covered thirty paces.

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Quick one while you run," Leon said, a little out of breath, like he'd been running too. "I've been reading Loop since you went under. People are tying the missing monthly goblin march to your world quest. Nobody's seen Thrash walk down to the walls of Lunarest this month, and a few streamers say they've been camped on that road for hours waiting for it."

I slowed half a step and caught a stake to keep my balance. *"The march didn't happen because I held them for a whole day."* Every month the camp rallied, charged the city walls, and got cut down, an easy early-level farm every newbie knew about. A party sitting on that road with nobody coming down it would have gone looking, and what they'd found was a camp on a hill. That was how four raiders had ended up in a red tent at the foot of mine.

"That explains why they're here," I said, breathing hard. "Thanks. Keep reading it."

"Copy," Leon said. "Stream's still off."

I reached the fire pit with a little over four minutes on the clock. They were all there. Sevish stood at the head of the circle with his axe grounded and his arms folded, Wyx at his left with her amber eyes already on me. Kesh, Ru and Zinka waited in front of them, and behind them ten goblins in the same club and round shield stood in a loose half circle, trying not to look afraid.

"You are on time," Wyx said, the half-smile thin.

I crouched and drew in the dirt with a stick, one line for the track, one for the cliffside, and a square on the road for the red tent. "The raiders sit here, on the shoulder of the road. They hold it, they rest, and they take whoever walks into range. Anything that creeps close to that tent gets weaker and slower, so nobody goes at it from the road." I split the drawing in two with the stick. "So we split. Three of us go down the back and come in behind them. Everyone else holds the hill, in case we fail."

"Who goes down the back?" Sevish's voice was low.

"Ru, Zinka and me." I pointed to the east edge, where the stakes ended and the ground broke into loose rock. "A regular goblin would take an hour to climb that, and I don't have an hour."

Ru stood very straight, one hand clamped around the other. Zinka only nodded once, the way she did everything, and then spoke, level and exact. "The east edge breaks into loose rock, then ledges, then trees. I have scouted it, and I have not climbed it. Someone must tie off first."

"We carry the rope," Ru said, stiff, trying to stand taller. "We-we can do that."

"Good." I turned back to the dirt. "Now the hill. This is the part that matters."

I drew the trail running down from the top, and fifteen paces below the crest I made two short rows of marks, three on each side. "Stakes, here. Three on the left of the trail and three on the right, set at an angle, points down the hill at whoever comes up. Between them, a gap as wide as the trail." *"Dash, space, dash."* "Then a second set, about seven paces above the first, built the same way."

"They will see a path," Wyx said, tilting her head. "And they will walk it."

"That's the point. It looks like a way up, and it's the only one. If they climb around the stakes they're slow targets on the rock. If they jump them, they're in the air with nowhere to go, and five bows are on them." The Romans never let a fight go where the enemy wanted it. They picked the ground and made the other side come through it. "Whoever gets that far runs a lane, and the lane ends at the wall."

"The wall," Sevish said.

"Six on clubs and shields at the top, locked edge to edge. The Vikings did it, and so did the Romans. Five of your best on bows behind them." I looked along the half circle at the five I'd picked. None of them had ever used a bow in a real fight, and I'd bet only one could hit a barn. Every goblin on that hill had lived through the night so far, and it nagged at me in a way I couldn't name yet. "They'll get better tonight if they live."

"Kesh shoot," Kesh said, nodding hard, one hand already on his short bow. "Not before Wyx step out. Kesh understand."

I turned to Wyx. "If they reach the first stakes, you go down to meet them. That move your brother used on me earlier, the one that pinned me to the dirt. Can you use it the same way?"

"Mine is not as strong as my brother's." She tipped her head, amused. "But I can do it. You want me to scream at your enemies?"

"They won't be able to ignore you." I met her eyes. "If they're weak, you take the fight yourself."

Her smile widened. "I do enjoy a fight."

"And if they push you back, you retreat to the second set, and the chief steps out and takes it while you recover." I looked at Sevish. "You use the same roar if you have to, so they turn on you."

His knuckles tightened on the axe haft, and he looked at the stakes, then at me. "Then I am the insurance."

"You're mine and the clan's. The second set is the last resort, and I don't think we get there." I stood and dropped the stick. "But it's there."

Wyx studied me for a long moment, and the smile came back. "Interesting."

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Heads up," Leon said in my ear, quick and low. "Arya called. She's in position and she's moving. I'm turning the stream back on, since chat's already asking where you went."

**[STREAM ONLINE]**

"Copy," I said under my breath. "Go."

--------

The rockslide dropped away under my boots in a slide of loose gravel and I locked a knot around a stump and went over the edge on the rope, Ru a body-length below me, Zinka already on the rock beside him. My palms burned from the first few meters. Below us the trees ran dark and close, and through the trunks I could see the red glow of the tent.

The first thirty feet were loose rock that slid under my boots, and the next stretch was ledges narrow enough that I climbed them with my belly flat to the stone. Zinka went first and tied off at every bend without a sound, and Ru brought up the back and caught my belt twice when my feet went out. My forearms were shaking by the time the rope ran out, and we dropped the last body-length into the dark of the trees.

Ru stopped with his back against a trunk, his chin lifted toward the camp above. "I hope my brethren are okay," he said quietly. "We have already lost so many."

"They'll be okay." I laid a hand on his shoulder, and felt it shake. "That's why we're here."

Zinka was already gone, a pale shadow slipping between the trees. Voices carried across the clearing, bored and loose. Somebody was laughing, and somebody else was complaining about what the elixirs cost.

I crept to the edge of the light. **[RIAN - LV 13 - HUMAN]** over the tall one with the shield on his back. **[TALO - LV 12 - DWARF]** beside him, arms like barrels. **[SPELLWEAVER_LYN - LV 14 - ELF]**, a staff across her knees. **[JOSS - LV 12 - BEASTFOLK]**, pale blue enchantments rippling off his hands. I could feel it already, a heaviness settling into my legs that had nothing to do with the climb, like wading into a river. *"Joss. Whatever those blue ripples are, they're what's pulling at us."* I held up two fingers to Ru and Zinka, then pointed left and right, and they slid away into the dark on either side of the clearing.

A whistle cut through the dark behind me, low and short. Arya stood twenty paces up the ridge in the trees, a staff in one hand, a coat that was too big for her. She held up two fingers. I held up two back, and my panel lit up in the corner of my eye.

**[ARISKY - LEVEL 10 - SPELLFORGER]**
**[ARISKY HAS JOINED YOUR PARTY]**

She planted her feet, and the world went gray.

**[STATUS EFFECT: WIND MIST]**

Fog rolled over the tent like something poured, and the voices stopped as if someone had thrown a switch. The heaviness went out of my legs. *"It cuts the mana. That pull runs on mana, and he can't pay for it now."* I ran.

I reached the tent mouth in the fog, and I was out of breath by the time I dropped to my knees in front of it, the dirt cold under my hands. I called up the rune on my panel and looked at it hard: a triangle drawn as one line of jagged claws, the corners left open so the points never touched. I drew it in the sand with the knife, pulling the first claw, then the second. The third closed a corner.

**[RUNE PRIMING: 10 MP]**

The sand flickered and went dark. I wiped it flat with my palm and started again, slower, the second line too even this time, the claws like teeth instead of talons. It sputtered and faded.

**[RUNE PRIMING: 10 MP]**

I checked my mana. Twelve left. Enough for one more.

*"Hands. Learn where the hands go."* I spread my fingers wide, anchored the heel of my hand, and breathed. From the ridge came three whistles, slow, spaced apart.

Behind the fog Rian was shouting something I couldn't hear. The wall of the tent bulged, and a shadow moved, and I kept my eyes on the sand. Three more whistles, the same slow spacing.

*"Ten seconds."*

I counted. The first claw came out sharp. The second came out sharp. I dragged the third line and the triangle held, the points open and wrong in exactly the right way. I let my breath go and pressed down.

**[RUNE PRIMING: 10 MP]**
**[BASE ARCANA RUNE: SET]**

The mist thinned, and the shouting came through all at once. Ru and Zinka had already put their mouths to the canvas on either side, green gas pouring in. Somebody coughed hard. The Elf and the Beastfolk went down inside the tent. The Human and the Dwarf burst out of the entrance together, swinging blind, and Talo's boot came down on the rune.

The ground lit. A ring of blue-white force slammed up around them and rooted both men where they stood.

I stepped back, held my palm out, and let the flare go.

**[SHIV-TOUCHED FLARE]**

The gas caught. The whole tent went up from the inside out, a roar I felt in my teeth, the heat across my face and the smell of singed lashes. For one moment everything rang. Then the fire fell back, and in the quiet the panels came down in a line.

**[COMBAT KILL: SPELLWEAVER_LYN]**
**[COMBAT KILL: JOSS]**
**[COMBAT KILL: TALO]**
**[COMBAT KILL: RIAN]**

My legs gave out. I sat down in the scorched dirt, mana at two, hands shaking, while the fire sank back and the drops rolled out of the ash one at a time.

**[LOOT: IRONHIDE CHESTPLATE]**
**[LOOT: VANGUARD'S GREAVES]**
**[LOOT: DOPPELGANGER'S BAND]**
**[LOOT: SHIVBONE STAFF - LEGENDARY]**

Arya stepped out of the trees with her own staff against her shoulder, breathing hard, and sank onto her haunches next to me. "You drew that on the ground," she said.

"Twice wrong first."

She laughed, short and shaky. "You wiped a full party in under a minute with a spell I was ready to throw away an hour ago."

I picked the Shivbone Staff out of the ash and held it out to her, handle first. Her eyes went wide, and her mouth opened before she caught it. "That's a legendary drop."

"I know what it's worth." I kept my arm out until her fingers closed around it. "You held that mist up on trust. Just keep speccing into what works."

She turned the staff over once in her hands, and the half-smirk came back, softer than before. Then the laughter stopped. A new banner hung across the corner of my vision.

**[PLAYER ENTERING ZONE: PK_WRAITH - ETA 00:08:00]**

"That wasn't one of the four," Arya said, and for the first time, she wasn't smirking at all.
