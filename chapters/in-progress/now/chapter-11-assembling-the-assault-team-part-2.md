# Chapter Eleven: Assembling the Assault Team, Part Two

The lamp smeared into one long white line, the world went out, and the pod lid hissed open on the ceiling lights. Leon already had the inspector halfway out the door, one hand on the man's elbow, steering him toward the stairs down to the lounge. "I'll be downstairs finishing the paperwork with VRSA," he said over his shoulder. "Ten minutes. Arya can start the pod back up when you're ready."

I did the math on the walk across the pavilion. Thirty minutes on the game clock, and it had not stopped when I did. By the time I reached the bathroom at the end of the upstairs hall I had already decided I'd be back under the visor with whatever was left, and the plan I'd promised Sevish was still one sentence long. I sat on the closed lid with my phone and typed *how does a shield wall actually hold* into the search bar. The Romans I already knew, shields locked edge to edge with short swords working through the gaps. The armies of the Warring States had drilled their spear and shield ranks until the front could take a charge, and the Vikings had stood shoulder to shoulder with their shields overlapping in one solid row. Different centuries, same idea. *"A line that holds, and something sharp behind it."* I kept my head down and my thumb moving.

"Hey, Nykri."

My thumb froze mid-scroll. I looked up fast enough to feel it in my neck, and a pair of gray eyes was already waiting for me. She stood in the doorway with one shoulder against the frame, white hair in a messy bun, ink running down both forearms to the wrist, a half-smirk that didn't reach the rest of her face.

"How do you know that name?" I stayed on the lid, phone in my hand.

"I watched the stream." Arya tipped her head toward the floor below. "A goblin with gray skin and white hair. Nobody else is running that, and Leon looked guilty enough downstairs to settle it."

She jerked her thumb at the stairs. "He's still down in the lounge with the inspector. He said you can get back on whenever you're ready, and I'm the one who's supposed to start the pod."

"Okay. So you watched." I got up, because sitting down made me feel like I was the one being interviewed. "What do you want?"

"Would you create a guild with me?" She said it flat, hands loose at her sides, no run-up. "Not join your team. A guild, you and me founding it."

I stared at her. "You're serious."

"My last one had three leaders, and two of them cared more about the stream than the match." Her fingers moved while she talked, a quick flick toward the floor, a fist, an open palm. "I want one where the match is the point. You've got four raiders on your hill and a camp full of NPCs, and I can read a fight."

I stepped past her into the hall. *"Guild. With her. I don't even have a team."*

"Hey man, I'm talking."

"Heard you." I kept walking toward the stairs.

"Please, listen." She fell in a step behind me, close enough that I could hear her boots on the boards. "Why are you ignoring me?"

I stopped at the top of the stairs and looked back at her over my shoulder. "Pot calling the kettle black. You gave me that same energy when I first walked in."

She blinked, and then she laughed once, short and surprised, the smirk finally getting somewhere. "Okay. Fair."

"I'm good, though." I started down the black staircase, the way a person walks something off, and stopped on the second step. Leon and the inspector were in the lounge at the bottom, and I didn't want to explain her to either of them. I turned around and went back up and into the pod room instead, and she followed.

The pod room smelled of warm plastic and solder. The two tall pods stood dark on the open floor under the amber lantern lamps, brick behind them and the mezzanine stair lost in the shadows, the lid of the working one still open. The operator desk sat at floor level right in front of them, lit by the monitor Leon had left running. Nobody else was there, and Arya still kept her voice low and close behind my shoulder, out of habit.

"I can seriously help you. A guild adds weight, and you don't have any right now."

"That's good to hear." I turned around and gave her a polite nod, the kind I'd give a cashier. "Not interested, thanks."

"I have information." She stepped around to where I could see her face. "About Thornwatch. I've got sources, nothing confirmed, only rumors. But it's going to be guild-based, Nykri. Whoever walks in there without one walks in as a free kill."

I didn't repeat a word of it. *"Guild-based. That's the second time somebody's said it."* If it was true, then a guild was either the best thing I could own or the fastest way to get every other guild in Tokyo pointing at me. I couldn't tell which, and I didn't answer on a guess.

"No," I said.

The smirk went out of her face all at once. Her hands stopped moving, and I realized that was the first time I'd seen them still.

"Please, just hear me out." She said it quietly, glancing once toward the stairs. "I wouldn't ask someone random to form a guild with me. I've been watching your stream. When I noticed my brother was actually helping you, I thought, okay, this is real. I'm sorry if I came off rude. I'm in a really bad spot."

I waited, because she wasn't done.

"I was texting my mom when you got here." Her thumb scraped the ink on her wrist, back and forth. "If I don't make it in this game, I have to leave my brother and everything here. We both rely on HGO to figure something out. I'm not asking you to like me. I'm asking you to let me be useful."

I scratched at my thin goatee, the habit I couldn't shake whether I was in the game or out of it. Four raiders in a red tent that drained anyone who crept close, and I'd be walking down a hill with two goblins I'd known for a day. *"I can't afford to die. And I don't know what's down there."* Arya had been through a real guild fight, and she'd read one better than any NPC I could point at. I looked at the ink on her wrist, at the way she was holding her own arm to keep from fidgeting, and I thought about how long she'd been carrying this by herself.

"Okay," I said. "Yes."

--------

Her whole body let out the breath. "Then what do you need?"

"What are you running?"

"Elf, Spellforger, level ten." She counted it off on her fingers. "Destruction, mostly. I put five points in the opening branch before the guild blew up and left the rest alone. I don't read guides, so most of my sheet is stubbornness."

"Can you cast Hushveil?"

Her eyes narrowed, the look of someone who already knew the answer and was waiting to hear the trick. "It doesn't deal damage. Fifteen seconds up, thirty seconds down, and it doesn't scale on anything you'd want to scale. I'm not spending fresh points on a spell that doesn't kill things."

"It blinds everyone inside, and it mutes them too, so nothing that runs on stamina or mana works in there. You see where everybody is while it's up." I leaned on the pod's edge with my arms folded. "Lightning conducts through it."

"Lightning also hits me, and my friends, and the guy holding the bucket." She shrugged one shoulder. "That's why nobody runs it."

"Take Protective Lightning. It's a passive, so your lightning doesn't hurt the people you're standing with." I pointed at her. "And did you read Chain Lightning all the way through?"

"Obviously."

"Did you read the part where it bounces off anything with mana? Allies included, and with the passive they're fine. You see them in the mist, so you use them as a relay when the target is out of range." I watched her face change. "I'm a goblin, but I have mana."

She was quiet, her tongue pressed against the inside of her cheek, her eyes somewhere past me. "My best spell is a fireball," she said slowly. "And I've never once used a teammate as part of a chain."

"I need the continuous cast, and I need stage two." I kept my arms folded. "The sporadic one gets you fifteen seconds, and that's not enough. The continuous one eats your mana and you can't move while you hold it, but it stays up as long as you can pay."

"Stage two is three points by itself." She said it the way people said the price of a bad meal, and then her eyes unfocused as she pulled the skill up and read what only she could see. "It adds deafen. Blind, silenced, and now deaf." Her mouth twisted. "That's a lot of points for one more debuff."

"If they can't hear us move, that's ideal for my plan." I kept my arms folded.

"What plan?" She dropped her hand and stared at me, hands finally still. "You want me to burn a fistful of points on a fog spell and you won't tell me the rest."

"I can't explain the whole thing. I have to get back, and I'm on a time limit." I glanced at the stairs, hoping the inspector would hurry Leon along. "You'll have to trust me on this one."

"I've been sitting on fifteen points since the guild went down." She said it flatly, to the ceiling, and then looked back at me.

For a second I thought she'd walk. Then she groaned and dragged a hand down her face. "Fine. Hushveil, stage two, the continuous cast, and Protective Lightning. If you're wrong, you owe me every one of those points, and I'll still want my guild."

"There's no voice chat in the game," I said. "How do I know you're running dry?"

"I whistle." She said it like it was obvious, and she pursed her lips and gave me a sample, low and short. "Three slow ones when I'm getting low, spaced out so you don't mistake them for a bird. At ten mana I whistle three more, the same way, and that's your ten seconds."

"Ten seconds. Got it."

Footsteps came up from the lounge, and Leon walked in with his glasses slipping down his nose and a signed readout folded in one hand. "Inspector's gone. He says don't run the stream at that draw again." He looked at the clock on his monitor and winced. "Ten minutes was a lie. It's been twenty-five."

I did the math with my stomach. Five minutes left on the return clock.

Leon looked from me to his sister, one eyebrow lifting. "What did I miss?"

"Nothing, brother." Arya already had her jacket on and was backing toward the door, her voice smooth, and she gave me a quick look. "Heading to J-Link to hop on for a bit. Met a new friend." She spun one finger by her ear. "The big cafe down the line, third-generation pods, I book weeks ahead. See you on the other side, Nykri."

Leon motioned me toward the pod, and I climbed in and dropped back against the cushion. "Don't stream the first ten minutes," I said, one hand on the visor. "We'll be planning, and I don't need anyone watching us do it. We don't know if these four answer to a guild, and someone could be in their ear, same as you're in mine."

Leon stopped with a knuckle against the monitor, then pushed his glasses up. "You're right. Good thinking. It stays dark until you say."

The lid came down.

The tent was cold when the world came back, the lamp low and the canvas shifting in the wind. A bundle sat on the bedroll: leather bracers, a plain iron knife, and two coils of hemp rope somebody had thought to add. I strapped it on while the panel opened.

**[TIME LIMIT: 01:05:00]**
**[RETURN TO THE CENTER OF CAMP: 00:05:00]**

Shiv's level jump had left four ability points sitting unspent under the clock, and I'd been too busy to touch them. I had maybe ninety seconds. *"Two things I can't go down that hill without."* The first was in the bow tree, the only branch I'd ever open, and only for one skill. I'd watched archers take it at the first tier and shrug, and I'd watched a mage stand inside a fog with it and see the whole fight.

**[FARSIGHT: LONG-RANGE VISION]**
**[POINTS SPENT: 2]**

The canvas seemed to slide away from me. I could see the torches along the east line, the stakes under them, the shape of a goblin's ear at a hundred paces, and I blinked until it settled. The second pair of points went into the earring. I pulled the Gambit up on the panel, found the line that had been grayed out since the night I put it on, and spent the last two.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT: DAMAGE MODIFIER UNLOCKED]**
**[ROLL OF 6: POWER SURGE]**
**[ROLL OF 1: ITEM BREAKS. DEBUFF: SLOWED, WEAKENED]**

I read the last line twice. *"A one doesn't just fail. It breaks whatever I rolled on and leaves me slow and weak in the middle of a fight."* I'd always known the earring was a gamble. This was the first time the price was a number I could see. I closed the panel and ran.

--------

Five minutes. I took the ridge path at a run, stakes and torches blurring past on both sides, and a pop-up slid across the top of my interface before I had covered thirty paces.

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Quick one while you run," Leon said, a little out of breath, like he'd been running too. "I've been reading Loop since you went under. People are tying the missing monthly goblin march to your world quest. Nobody's seen Thrash walk down to the walls of Lunarest this month, and a few streamers say they've been camped on that road for hours waiting for it."

I slowed half a step and caught a stake to keep my balance. *"The march didn't happen because I held them for a whole day."* Every month the camp rallied, charged the city walls, and got cut down, an easy early-level farm every newbie knew about. A party sitting on that road with nobody coming down it would have gone looking, and what they'd found was a camp on a hill. That was how four raiders had ended up in a red tent at the foot of mine.

"That explains why they're here," I said, breathing hard. "Thanks. Keep reading it."

"Copy," Leon said. "Stream's still off."

I reached the fire pit with a little over four minutes on the clock. They were all there. Sevish stood at the head of the circle with his axe grounded and his arms folded, Wyx at his left with her amber eyes already on me. Kesh, Ru and Zinka waited in front of them, and behind them ten goblins in the same club and round shield stood in a loose half circle, trying not to look afraid.

"You are on time," Wyx said, the half-smile thin.

I crouched and drew in the dirt with a stick, one line for the track, one for the cliffside, and a square on the road for the red tent. "The raiders sit here, on the shoulder of the road. They hold it, they rest, and they take whoever walks into range. Anything that creeps close to that tent gets weaker and slower, so nobody goes at it from the road." I split the drawing in two with the stick. "So we split. Three of us go down the back and come in behind them. Everyone else holds the hill, in case we fail."

"Who goes down the back?" Sevish's voice was low.

"Ru, Zinka and me." I pointed to the east edge, where the stakes ended and the ground broke into loose rock. "A regular goblin would take an hour to climb that, and I don't have an hour."

Ru stood very straight, one hand clamped around the other. Zinka only nodded once, the way she did everything, and then spoke, level and exact. "The east edge breaks into loose rock, then ledges, then trees. I have scouted it, and I have not climbed it. Someone must tie off first."

"We carry the rope," Ru said, stiff, trying to stand taller. "We-we can do that."

"Good." I turned back to the dirt. "Now the hill. This is the part that matters."

I drew the trail running down from the top, and fifteen paces below the crest I made two short rows of marks, three on each side. "Stakes, here. Three on the left of the trail and three on the right, set at an angle, points down the hill at whoever comes up. Between them, a gap as wide as the trail." *"Dash, space, dash."* "Then a second set, about seven paces above the first, built the same way."

"They will see a path," Wyx said, tilting her head. "And they will walk it."

"That's the point. It looks like a way up, and it's the only one. If they climb around the stakes, they're slow targets on the rock. If they jump them, they're in the air with nowhere to go." The Romans never let a fight go where the enemy wanted it, and neither did the Qin. They picked the ground and made the other side come through it. "Whoever gets that far runs a lane, and the lane ends at a wall of shields."

--------

"Six on clubs and shields at the top, locked edge to edge, the way the Vikings stood. Shield on the off hand, club in the other. They raise the shields together, and when it's time to swing they drop them together and swing in one motion. A club doesn't stab through the cracks, so the swing is all they have." I looked at Sevish. "Behind them, five of your best on bows. The bows don't shoot when they feel like it. They shoot when you call it, chief, one volley every fifteen seconds, from the moment they come in range. The raiders never get a breath to plan. If they've got an enchanter, he burns his mana on wards, and if they've got a mage, she burns hers answering."

Sevish grunted. "Fifteen."

I looked along the line at the five I'd picked. None of them had ever used a bow in a real fight, and I'd bet only one could hit a barn. Every goblin on that hill had lived through the night so far, and it nagged at me in a way I couldn't name yet. "They'll get better tonight if they live."

"Kesh shoot," Kesh said, nodding hard, one hand on his short bow. "When Sevish call. Kesh understand."

"They'll reach the first stakes as a group, because they will." I turned to Wyx. "That's when you go down to meet them. That move your brother used on me earlier, the one that pinned me to the dirt. Can you use it the same way?"

"Mine is not as strong as my brother's." She tipped her head, amused. "But I can do it. You want me to scream at your enemies?"

"They won't be able to ignore you. A big boss has finally shown up, and they'll want to test you." I met her eyes. "They'll fail, because they're being shot the whole time. If they're weak, take the fight yourself."

Her smile widened. "I do enjoy a fight."

"It holds about thirty seconds." I looked at Sevish again. "You count it in your head, chief, and when it's done you call your sister back. Then the wall goes up, shields raised, and steps forward while she falls behind it. The mage hits the boards with fire, the sword-and-shield man hits a goblin and the goblin hits him in the gut, and he backs off. The roar needs about forty-five seconds before she can use it again, so the wall holds fifteen. Then she walks out and does it again, and every one of them has to look at her."

"I would go out myself." His knuckles tightened on the axe haft. "Every time."

"Not every time." I kept my voice level. "If you go down, there's no one to lead Thrash."

He looked at the stakes, then at me, and a long breath went out of him. "That is true."

"The second set of stakes is the last resort. If they push Wyx back that far, you step out and take it while she recovers, and you use your own roar to pull them in." I dropped the stick. "I don't think we get there. But it's there."

Wyx studied me for a long moment, and the smile came back. "Interesting."

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Heads up," Leon said in my ear, quick and low. "Arya called. She's in position and she's moving. Do you want the stream?"

"Not yet." I looked at the three of them, Ru, Zinka, and a slope of dark rock that didn't care how much I'd planned. "When I go over the edge."

"Copy."

The rockslide dropped away under my boots in a slide of loose gravel. I locked a knot around a stump, tested it with my whole weight, and went over, Ru a body-length below me, Zinka already on the rock beside him. My palms burned from the first few meters. Below us the trees ran dark and close, and through the trunks I could see the red glow of the tent.

"Leon," I said. "Now."

**[STREAM ONLINE]**

From somewhere behind and above me on the ridge, someone whistled once, low and short.

**[END CHAPTER ELEVEN]**
