# Chapter Eleven: Assembling the Assault Team, Part Two

The lamp smeared into one long white line, the world went out, and the pod lid hissed open on the ceiling lights. The inspector was already at the side panel with his tablet, and Leon stood over him with one hand on the desk. "Give us ten minutes or so," Leon said, not looking up. "He has to reprogram a few things."

The bathroom at the far end of the pavilion was one of the open ones, the kind you'd find in a theater or any arcade, a row of stalls along one wall and a row of sinks under a long mirror, a tap dripping somewhere. Nobody else was in it. At the mirror I untied my locs, still dragging at the back of my neck from the visor strap, and twisted them up again, then took the last stall and sat down with my phone. I opened the world map in the HGO app first and dragged it until the camp sat in the middle of the screen, the hill, the road below it, the cliff behind. Then I pulled up a few diagrams beside it. The plan was only a rough shape in my head: a distraction up front, and a way to come at them from somewhere they weren't looking. I could name two people I'd take down the back with me. I ran the gas through it, the stuns, what happened if one of them resisted, and every time I reached the end the same hole stayed open. I needed something I didn't have yet, and I couldn't say what.

When I came out fifteen minutes later, Leon and the inspector were already going down the black staircase, the inspector's case on Leon's shoulder, and neither of them looked back. Across the pavilion, Arya sat at the operator desk with her feet up on an open drawer, white hair in a messy bun, ink running down both forearms to the wrist, her eyes on her own phone. I walked past the desk toward the pod without looking at her.

"Hey, Nykri."

My feet stopped for half a step. I kept going, and I didn't answer.

"Hey man, I'm talking." She set the phone down. "Why are you ignoring me?"

I stopped and turned. "Pot calling the kettle black. You gave me that same energy when I first walked in."

She blinked, and then she laughed once, short and surprised. "Okay. Fair."

"How do you know that name?" I asked.

"I watched the stream." Arya tipped her head toward the floor below. "A goblin with gray skin and white hair. Nobody else is running that, and Leon looked guilty enough to settle it." She nodded at the pod, its lid still open under the amber lantern lamps. "It didn't take long. He only had to program a few things and re-download a few things, so you can hop back in about ten minutes. Everything's rebooting right now. And your stream got big while you were out. People are talking about the world quest, London Spire and some of the other big guilds included."

The pod room smelled of warm plastic and solder. The two tall pods stood dark on the open floor, brick behind them and the mezzanine stair lost in the shadows, the operator desk at floor level in front of them with the monitor Leon had left running. Nobody else was there, and Arya stood up and came around the desk and stopped close.

She crossed her arms and looked me directly in the eyes, tilting her head back to do it. "Can you make a guild with me?"

I crossed my arms too, without deciding to, and I could feel my face doing something dumbfounded. We had barely spoken. Yes, I was working with her brother, and that was all.

"Why me?" I said.

"I feel like we'd make a great pairing." She said it easily, her weight on one hip. "And it looks like you need some extra help in the situation you're in. I can take some of the load, and you don't know what kind of enemies you're about to face." The half-smirk widened a little. "I could be impressed by how you moved through that world quest, or by how you even got HGO running on one of those ancient pods. I'm not going to ask which is crazier. That's a question for Leon. I have information, too. I've been playing this game a very long time."

It came out confident, a touch cocky, and it sounded so much like Eren that I could almost hear his voice under hers. She wasn't trying to be rude. I knew that. But "ancient" landed wrong, and my hand had tightened on the edge of the desk before I noticed.

"One of those ancient pods is my dad's." I kept my voice level. "We're in the process of fixing both."

Her mouth opened, and nothing came out of it.

"No thank you." I held up a hand, a little too polite. "I appreciate it."

I stepped around her toward the door. Behind me her footsteps stopped, and when I glanced back she wasn't looking at me. She was looking at the base of the working pod, where the old Kazuhaya Tech stamp sat under the cable bundles, and the color had gone out of her face.

"Wait." Her voice had changed. "Kazuhaya. That's Kazuhaya Tech."

I stopped in the doorway without turning around. "Yeah."

"I didn't know." It came out fast, almost over me. "Leon told me to be here when you came back, and I wasn't trying to insult it. I've had my head in my phone the whole time you've been around, and I thought you were just another player. My brother never shuts up about that company, and I just said that to your face." She pressed the heel of her hand against her forehead. "That was out of line. I'm sorry. It makes sense now, why he does business with you."

I turned around slowly and kept my arms folded. Her eyes were on the floor.

"I'll hire you," she said. "Name a number. I don't care what it is."

"I'm not for sale."

"Okay." She nodded, quick, taking it without arguing. "Okay. That's fair."

The pod room went quiet, the monitor humming. I scratched at my thin goatee and looked at her, really looked, at the ink on her wrist and the way she was holding her own elbow to keep her hands still. She'd gone from cocky to this in a minute, and none of it looked like an act.

"If I said yes," I said slowly, "why would it be me? You were top eight. You could pick anyone."

"Being a girl gamer is already tough." She said it without looking away. "Add a reputation that isn't even true, and getting people to join your guild is hard, and getting them to stay is harder. I'm not going to pressure you. You can say no. I saw the opportunity, and I figured I had nothing left to lose." Her thumb scraped the ink on her wrist. "I've been watching your stream for forty minutes. The way you talk to those NPCs, I haven't seen anything like it, and I've been playing a long time. A professional guild is the only way I stay here. I was texting my mom when you got here."

I didn't answer right away. *"Myrrhwood first, and Thornwatch at the end of all of it."* I wanted to be there when it happened, and I couldn't do it alone with a camp of goblins. Whatever came out of Thornwatch, I'd be taking on guilds, and I needed people who weren't only goblins. I let my arms drop.

"All right," I said. "If you're willing to help me, we can discuss it."

--------

Her whole body let out the breath. "Thank you. Seriously."

"Joining your guild doesn't mean I want to lead it." I kept my arms loose. "But I'm not going to sit there with nothing to say about it, either. We'd have to work out how it looks. I don't mind splitting the leadership."

"Co-leaders works." She nodded, quick. "It'd be great for recruitment, too, and I'm not trying to use you as bait. Bringing people in with a player in the guild who's tied to a world quest is huge." She hesitated, and her hand went back to her wrist. "It also decides what kind of crowd we end up with, and not all of that is good."

"Leon already mentioned some of that when he brought up the stream."

"So Leon's the one who got you to stream." Her eyebrows went up. "I figured you just turned it on."

"He pushed me." I shrugged. "I wasn't going to."

She grinned, a real one for the first time, and clapped her hands once. "Okay. Then what do you need?"

"What are you running?"

"Elf, Spellforger, level ten." She counted it off on her fingers, and something in my head clicked into place, the hole I hadn't been able to close in that bathroom stall. "Destruction, mostly. I put five points in the opening branch before the guild blew up and left the rest alone. I don't read guides, so most of my sheet is stubbornness."

"Can you cast Hushveil?"

Her eyes narrowed, the look of someone who already knew the answer and was waiting to hear the trick. "It doesn't deal damage. Fifteen seconds up, thirty seconds down, and it doesn't scale on anything you'd want to scale. I'm not spending fresh points on a spell that doesn't kill things."

"If you underestimate that spell, you'll never understand its true power." I leaned on the pod's edge with my arms folded.

"What do you mean by that?" She tilted her head, wary.

"Use it and find out."

She didn't answer, and I didn't push. "Take Protective Lightning too," I said.

"Nobody takes the lightning tree." She shrugged one shoulder. "It's weak, and it's expensive."

"Did you read Chain Lightning all the way through?"

"Obviously."

"Read it again." I pointed at her eyes.

She unfocused, reading what only she could see, and her eyebrows climbed. "It bounces off allies who have mana." She looked up at me. "And with the passive, it doesn't hurt them. You're a goblin with mana."

"Exactly."

She was quiet, her tongue pressed against the inside of her cheek. "My best spell is a fireball," she said slowly. "And I've never once used a teammate as part of a chain."

"I need the continuous cast, and I need stage two." I kept my arms folded.

"Stage two is three points by itself." She said it the way people said the price of a bad meal, and then her eyes unfocused as she pulled the skill up and read what only she could see. "It adds deafen. Blind, silenced, and now deaf." Her mouth twisted. "That's a lot of points for one more debuff."

"If they can't hear us move, that's ideal for my plan." I kept my arms folded.

"What plan?" She dropped her hand and stared at me, hands finally still. "You want me to burn a fistful of points on a fog spell and you won't tell me the rest."

"I can't explain the whole thing. I have to get back, and I'm on a time limit." I glanced at the stairs, hoping the inspector would hurry Leon along. "You'll have to trust me on this one."

"I've been sitting on fifteen points since the guild went down." She said it flatly, to the ceiling, and then looked back at me.

For a second I thought she'd walk. Then she groaned and dragged a hand down her face. "Fine. Hushveil, stage two, the continuous cast, and Protective Lightning. If you're wrong, you owe me every one of those points, and I'll still want my guild."

"There's no voice chat in the game," I said. "How do I know you're running dry?"

"I whistle." She said it like it was obvious, and she pursed her lips and gave me a sample, low and short. "Three slow ones when I'm getting low, spaced out so you don't mistake them for a bird. At ten mana I whistle three more, the same way, and that's your ten seconds."

"Ten seconds. Got it."

Footsteps came up from the lounge, and Leon walked in with his glasses slipping down his nose and a signed readout folded in one hand. "Inspector's gone. He says don't run the stream at that draw again." He looked at the clock on his monitor and winced. "The reboot ran long. It's been twenty-five minutes since you came out."

I did the math with my stomach. Five minutes left on the return clock.

Leon looked from me to his sister, one eyebrow lifting. "What did I miss?"

"Nothing, brother." Arya already had her jacket on and was backing toward the door, her voice smooth, and she gave me a quick look. "Heading to J-Link to hop on for a bit. Met a new friend." She spun one finger by her ear. "The big cafe down the line, third-generation pods, I book weeks ahead. See you on the other side, Nykri."

Leon motioned me toward the pod, and I climbed in and dropped back against the cushion. "Don't stream the first ten minutes," I said, one hand on the visor. "We'll be planning, and I don't need anyone watching us do it. We don't know if these four answer to a guild, and someone could be in their ear, same as you're in mine."

Leon stopped with a knuckle against the monitor, then pushed his glasses up. "You're right. Good thinking. It stays dark until you say."

The lid came down.

The tent was cold when the world came back, the lamp low and the canvas shifting in the wind. A bundle sat on the bedroll: leather bracers, a plain iron knife, and two coils of hemp rope somebody had thought to add. I strapped it on while the panel opened.

**[TIME LIMIT: 01:05:00]**
**[RETURN TO THE CENTER OF CAMP: 00:05:00]**

Shiv's level jump had left four points sitting unspent under the clock, and I'd been too busy to touch them. I had maybe ninety seconds. *"Wisdom first. Everything I own runs on it."* Two points went into Wisdom, one into Fortune for the earring, and one into Speed, because a goblin who couldn't move had no business on a rope.

**[WISDOM +2] [FORTUNE +1] [SPEED +1]**

The jump had also unlocked a new line on the earring, and I pulled the Gambit up to read it.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT: DAMAGE MODIFIER UNLOCKED]**
**[ROLL OF 6: POWER SURGE]**
**[ROLL OF 1: ITEM BREAKS. DEBUFF: SLOWED, WEAKENED]**

I read the last line twice. *"A one doesn't just fail. It breaks whatever I rolled on and leaves me slow and weak in the middle of a fight."* I'd always known the earring was a gamble. This was the first time the price was a number I could see.

One skill point was left. I opened the bow tree, the only branch I'd ever touch, and went to the single skill in it I wanted. I'd watched archers take it at the first tier and shrug, and I'd watched a mage stand inside a fog with it and see the whole fight.

**[EYES OF THE EAGLE: LONG-RANGE VISION]**

The canvas seemed to slide away from me. I could see the torches along the east line, the stakes under them, the shape of a goblin's ear at a hundred paces, and I blinked until it settled. I closed the panel and ran.

--------

Five minutes. I took the ridge path at a run, stakes and torches blurring past on both sides, and a pop-up slid across the top of my interface before I had covered thirty paces.

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Quick one while you run," Leon said, a little out of breath, like he'd been running too. "I've been reading Loop since you went under. People are tying the missing monthly goblin march to your world quest. Nobody's seen Thrash walk down to the walls of Lunarest this month, and a few streamers say they've been camped on that road for hours waiting for it."

I slowed half a step and caught a stake to keep my balance. *"The march didn't happen because I held them for a whole day."* Every month the camp rallied, charged the city walls, and got cut down, an easy early-level farm every newbie knew about. A party sitting on that road with nobody coming down it would have gone looking, and what they'd found was a camp on a hill. That was how four raiders had ended up in a red tent at the foot of mine.

"That explains why they're here," I said, breathing hard. "Thanks. Keep reading it."

"Copy," Leon said. "Stream's still off."

I reached the fire pit with a little over four minutes on the clock. They were all there. Sevish stood at the head of the circle with his axe grounded and his arms folded, Wyx at his left with her amber eyes already on me. Kesh, Ru and Zinka waited in front of them, and behind them nine goblins in the same club and round shield stood in a loose half circle, trying not to look afraid.

"You are on time," Wyx said, the half-smile thin.

I crouched and drew in the dirt with a stick, one line for the track, one for the cliffside, and a square on the road for the red tent. "The raiders sit here, on the shoulder of the road. They hold it, they rest, and they take whoever walks into range. Anything that creeps close to that tent gets weaker and slower, so nobody goes at it from the road." I split the drawing in two with the stick. "So we split. Three of us go down the back and come in behind them. Everyone else holds the hill, in case we fail."

"Who goes down the back?" Sevish's voice was low.

"Ru, Zinka and me." I pointed to the east edge, where the stakes ended and the ground broke into loose rock. "A regular goblin would take an hour to climb that, and I don't have an hour."

Ru stood very straight, one hand clamped around the other. Zinka only nodded once, the way she did everything, and then spoke, level and exact. "The east edge breaks into loose rock, then ledges, then trees. I have scouted it, and I have not climbed it. Someone must tie off first."

"We carry the rope," Ru said, stiff, trying to stand taller. "We-we can do that."

"Good." I turned back to the dirt. "Now the hill. This is the part that matters."

I drew the trail running down from the top, and twelve yards below the camp I made two short rows of marks, three on each side. "Stakes, here. Three on the left of the trail and three on the right, set at an angle, points down the hill at whoever comes up. Between them, a gap as wide as the trail." *"Dash, space, dash."* "Then a second set, six yards from the camp, built the same way."

"They will see a path," Wyx said, tilting her head. "And they will walk it."

"That's the point. It looks like a way up, and it's the only one. If they climb around the stakes, they're slow targets on the rock. If they jump them, they're in the air with nowhere to go." The Romans never let a fight go where the enemy wanted it, and neither did the Qin. They picked the ground and made the other side come through it. "Whoever gets that far runs a lane, and the lane ends at a wall of shields."

--------

"Five on clubs and shields at the top, locked edge to edge, the way the Vikings stood. Shield on the off hand, club in the other. They raise the shields together, and when it's time to swing they drop them together and swing in one motion. A club doesn't stab through the cracks, so the swing is all they have." I looked at Sevish. "Behind them, five rookies on bows, the best you've got. The bows don't shoot when they feel like it. They shoot when you call it, chief, one volley every fifteen seconds, from the moment they come in range. The raiders never get a breath to plan. If they've got an enchanter, he burns his mana on wards, and if they've got a mage, she burns hers answering."

Sevish grunted. "Fifteen."

I looked along the line at the five I'd picked. Four of them had never used a bow in a real fight. The fifth was Kesh, who had carried one on the scouting runs, and I'd bet he was the only one of the five who could hit a barn. Zinka could have outshot him with her eyes closed, but Zinka was coming with me. Every goblin on that hill had lived through the night so far, and it nagged at me in a way I couldn't name yet. "They'll get better tonight if they live."

"Kesh shoot first," Kesh said, nodding hard, one hand already on his short bow. "Others look at Kesh, shoot when Kesh shoot. When Sevish call. Kesh understand."

"They'll reach the first stakes as a group, because they will." I turned to Wyx. "That's when you go down to meet them. That move your brother used on me earlier, the one that pinned me to the dirt. Can you use it the same way?"

"Mine is not as strong as my brother's." She tipped her head, amused. "But I can do it. You want me to scream at your enemies?"

"They won't be able to ignore you. A big boss has finally shown up, and they'll want to test you." I met her eyes. "They'll fail, because they're being shot the whole time. If they're weak, take the fight yourself."

Her smile widened. "I do enjoy a fight."

"It holds about thirty seconds." I looked at Sevish again. "You count it in your head, chief, and when it's done you call your sister back. Then the wall goes up, shields raised, and steps forward while she falls behind it. The mage hits the boards with fire, the sword-and-shield man hits a goblin and the goblin hits him in the gut, and he backs off. The roar needs about forty-five seconds before she can use it again, so the wall holds fifteen. Then she walks out and does it again, and every one of them has to look at her."

"I would go out myself." His knuckles tightened on the axe haft. "Every time."

"Not every time." I kept my voice level. "If you go down, there's no one to lead Thrash."

He looked at the stakes, then at me, and a long breath went out of him. "That is true."

"The second set of stakes is the last resort. If they push Wyx back that far, you step out and take it while she recovers, and you use your own roar to pull them in." I dropped the stick. "I don't think we get there. But it's there."

Wyx studied me for a long moment, and the smile came back. "Interesting."

**[DIRECTOR IN BOOTH: VOICE CHANNEL]**

"Heads up," Leon said in my ear, quick and low. "Arya called. She's in position and she's moving. Do you want the stream?"

"Not yet." I looked at the three of them, Ru, Zinka, and a slope of dark rock that didn't care how much I'd planned. "When I go over the edge."

"Copy."

The rockslide dropped away under my boots in a slide of loose gravel. I locked a knot around a stump, tested it with my whole weight, and went over, Ru a body-length below me, Zinka already on the rock beside him. My palms burned from the first few meters. Below us the trees ran dark and close, and through the trunks I could see the red glow of the tent.

"Leon," I said. "Now."

**[STREAM ONLINE]**

From somewhere behind and above me on the ridge, someone whistled once, low and short.
