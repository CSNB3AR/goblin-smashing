Five dead on the east patrol, and the number wouldn't leave me alone.

Sevish didn't slow. He took the last ten paces to the war tent in long strides with Wyx half a step behind him, Kesh stumbling after them, and I let myself fall a step back with my eyes on nothing. A goblin hauling a bundle of stakes had to swerve around me. "Sorry," I said, and he was already gone.

A thought had run across me and it just wouldn't go away. Now that Thrash counted me as one of theirs, the alert in the corner of my vision opened past the defenders into the whole clan.

*"Fourteen of twenty-three, and that twenty-three is after the two enforcers I killed myself. So the party took nine in an hour and seven minutes."* I read it again and it came out the same. *"That's not a normal group. Nobody wanders into a camp by accident and does that. Ru stayed at my shoulder. The lanes between the tents stood empty, and what was left of the clan stood in a thin line along the stakes under the torches, with gaps in it. The sky over the cliff was clear and full of stars, and the waterfall hissed behind the tents.

Two things stuck as I walked. The stakes stopped a few paces short of the cliff on the east side, where the ground broke into loose rock and fell away into the dark. And every goblin on that line held a different shield, a barrel lid, a plank off the hay cart, a tent flap laced wet onto a pole frame, nothing made for the job, with a club in the other hand.

The flap fell shut behind us on canvas heat and old leather, and the noise of the camp went flat and far away.

The tent was round, with timber ribs curving up to the center pole and the lantern hanging from it. The back wall faced the cliff, and through it I could feel the cold coming off the rock.

Sevish went straight through to the far side of the table and planted both fists on the wood, the chest and barrels stacked tidy against the back wall behind him, his axe leaning in the chair at his right hand. I took the near side at the center, the flap at my back. Kesh dropped onto a barrel along the left wall and rubbed his knee. Ru stopped inside the flap on the right, one shoulder to the canvas, where a gap in the cloth gave him a strip of the camp to watch. He stood with his head bent under the cross rope above the entrance, still not used to his own height.

"I hope my brothers are all right," Ru said, low, eyes on the strip of light. "We have already lost so many."

*"Players get up at a Cathedral. I've never seen a goblin do it. If his brothers are dying, they're staying dead."* "We'll keep it from getting worse," I said quietly.

Ru gave one short nod and kept watching the camp.

"Wyx," Sevish said, without turning his head. "We have bled enough sending them down to the road in twos and threes. Pull everyone back inside the stakes and raise the inner barricade. Let them come uphill to us. If they do not come, we do not go."

"I would hear the scouts first, brother," Wyx said.

*"She should. She runs this clan beside her brother, and the scout's word is what the whole camp will move on. Somebody else has to set the line."* I looked at the big hobgoblin standing under the cross rope with his hands loose, waiting. *"Ru answers to me now."*

"Ru can do it," I said. "He's with me."

Sevish's eyes came around to me, heavy and patient, a look that asked a question without saying it. Wyx's brows rose, and for a second she looked at me instead of the door.

"Very well," Sevish said.

I turned to Ru. "Get the line in order. Raise the inner barricade behind the stakes, pull every goblin back inside, and make sure each one is armed and listening. If anyone won't listen, use a little force."

Ru straightened until the cross rope brushed his hair. "Understood," he said, and ducked out through the flap.

"That is not the first step you have taken tonight, Nykri," Wyx said quietly.

"Kesh," Sevish said. "A clean count."

"Kesh saw five," Kesh said between breaths.

"Kesh counted the tent pole twice," said a voice from the flap. "There are four, chief."

"Then Kesh's count is settled. Go and join Ru, Kesh," Sevish said. "He will need every hand."

Four inside. One stays at the flap and does not sleep. They pulled back five minutes ago and have not moved since. *"Five minutes of sitting still, and they keep stopping to drink. They're not winning this. They're hanging on."* I rubbed my thumb along the edge of the table. *"A party that stops pushing is either resting or waiting on help, and I can't tell which."*

The lid hissed open on the ceiling of the pod room, soft lantern light after the dark of the visor. I sat up slow, and my joints complained the way they always did after a long dive. The inspector was already at the monitor bay, crouched in front of the open panel with his black suit pressed flat against the chair, his long black hair tied low at the neck, a bang falling across one lens of his glasses. His tools lay in a neat row on a cloth beside him.

Leon stood next to him, one hip against the desk, glasses slipping down his nose. He looked up when I moved.

"It hit the thermal limit," he said. "The override held. It's the stream that cooked it."

"Streaming roughly doubles the draw," the inspector said, flat, without looking up. "The unit was not built for that."

"Sorry about the pod," I said.

"Don't be." Leon pushed his glasses back up. "I've run this place a long time. I've watched a lot of streams on that screen." He nodded at the monitor in front of the inspector. "I never saw anything like what you just pulled."

"You could be my coach," I said, half a joke.

Leon didn't answer right away, still bent over the panel. Then, quieter, mostly to himself, "Coach. Yeah." A pause, like he was turning it over. "Yeah, I could see that."

"Give me the ten minutes," he said, louder. "Go stretch your legs. You've been under a long time."

I took the spiral staircase down to the lounge, my knees stiff. It was quiet for the hour, the clock over the counter reading 8:54, one desktop glowing at the far wall. The only person in the room was behind the front counter, and she looked up before I reached the last step.

She had white hair in a messy bun, ink running down both forearms to the wrist, a plain black tee and sharp gray eyes. A half-smirk.

"Hey, Nykri."

She'd used the handle instead of my name. I stopped with one hand on the rail.

"Relax," Arya said, folding her arms on the counter. "Leon said you were on the quest. I watched the stream on the screen out here, and your camp's in trouble." She pushed off the counter. "So I'll skip the speech. I want in. Your quest, your camp, your fight. Whatever's coming for that camp, I've seen where the guild chat thinks it goes next, and I'd rather be on the inside when it does."

"No."

"I haven't said what I'm offering."

"Still no." I kept my voice even. "I don't know you. I'm a goblin in a hole and I'm not putting anybody else in it."

"Easy." The smirk went a little crooked. "I've heard goblins bite. I'm friendly, I promise."

"I've been told that," I said.

Something moved behind her eyes, and she looked at the counter instead of me. "My crew fell apart early in the season. The point is I've got a build and nowhere to run it, and you've got a quest the whole server wants."

"What's your build?" I said finally.

"Elf, Spellforger, level ten." She shrugged one shoulder. "Mostly field spells. Static Lash, Tempest Fan, Storm Shear, Stormwall, and a fog spell called Hushveil that I never use."

"What does the fog do?"

"Hides whoever's standing in it. It's about as wide as a small room, it lasts fifteen seconds, and then it needs thirty to cool down." She counted it off on her fingers. "It eats mana fast and it does zero damage. Nobody builds around it."

"Does the tree let you push it further?"

Her eyes went briefly unfocused as she pulled her own screen open. "There's a branch past it. Silence. Anyone caught in the mist can't cast and can't call out to their party." Her mouth twisted. "Five points deep, and I've never looked at it twice."
