Chapter Nine: Silence and Fire

"Where's Leon," Tatsuya said, still adjusting to the fact that it was Arya at the operator's desk and not Leon.

"Front desk." She didn't move from where she leaned against it, arms loose at her sides. "VRSA inspector again. Monitor overheated with the stream running on top of it. He told me to sit here while he's tied up."

"He told you that," Tatsuya said, his hand coming up to scrub over the back of his head. "Why would she bother lying about something this easy to check." He didn't have a clean answer, so he left it where it sat.

"I'm good," he said, and turned for the pod.

"Could be," Arya said, not raising her voice, her shoulder coming away from the desk. "Might be able to help with what you've got going on, if you're willing to hear it."

He didn't answer, his hand almost on the pod's handle.

"There are six guilds already positioned off your last two streams," she said, and her voice shifted, going more precise, like she'd decided to stop holding the real card back. He stopped.

She pushed off the desk and crossed the space at an easy, unhurried pace. "I'm in the regional guild leader chat. Fifteen guilds in rotation. At least six of them have been mapping your position off your own footage every time you go live, and four have already switched to legacy accounts." She tilted her head slightly, watching him work through it. "You know what that means."

"Higher level cap," Tatsuya said, his voice flat.

"Higher level cap," she agreed, her eyes staying on his. "And a seven-day death lock on a character with no reset option. I've got more than that, but I don't hand it over for free."

He looked at her for a long moment. "That's not help. That's leverage."

"It's both," Arya said, her weight settling back, steady, not pressing. "The information's still accurate either way."

He couldn't argue the second part. "What are you running," he said, turning to face her properly.

"Mage. Level ten, almost eleven." She stayed where she was. "Field-spell heavy. Haven't pushed the build much since my guild went under."

"Anything silence-adjacent," he said, his eyes moving briefly to the side, thinking through what she'd actually have access to.

"Nothing I've put points into." Her eyes narrowed slightly, watchful now. "Why."

"Hushveil," he said, watching her face.

The frown that settled in was skeptical, not curious, her gaze dropping away for a moment before coming back to his. "I have the base version. Never put points into it. Doesn't deal damage, doesn't scale, it's dead weight in any build that's actually trying to output something."

"Advanced version," Tatsuya said, his tone sharpening slightly. "Not base. Full package, silence and blind both."

"That's a real point cost." A sharper edge came into her voice. "Points I've been sitting on since reset. I don't even know if I use it again after tonight."

"Then you're not in," he said, flat, his eyes not moving from hers. "That's the price of admission. Not a negotiation."

She held his look for a moment, reading him the way she'd have read an opponent's opening move, something calculating behind it, working out whether he had a real reason or was just testing whether she'd push back.

"There's a café two streets over," she said, her voice settling into something steadier. "Tsukino's. I already had a pod time booked there anyway." She glanced once toward the hallway Leon had gone down, then back to him. "I can be under before you've even re-entered."

He sat with that for a second. A level ten walking into a full-party fight alongside someone who'd already turned her down twice wasn't a safe bet on her part. The thought landed somewhere close to the same place Ohema's transfer had, someone putting something real on the line without a guarantee it landed right.

"Fine," he said, and nodded once, brief. "Don't start without me."

"Wasn't planning on it," Arya said, and something in her posture eased, the held tension dropping out quietly, like she'd been braced for more resistance and hadn't needed it. She was already moving for the door when he spoke again.

"How are you getting there," Tatsuya said, his voice going quiet. "Alone, this late."

She paused and glanced back at him, her expression shifting slightly. "It's two blocks. I've made that walk before."

"Still," Tatsuya said. His gaze had already moved past her toward the front of the arcade, toward wherever Leon actually was.

Leon appeared at the end of the hallway a moment later, the inspector's voice still trailing faintly from the front desk, and took in the two of them with the look of someone who'd missed a full conversation and already knew it. "What'd I walk into."

"She's heading to the café on the corner," Tatsuya said, nodding once in Arya's direction. "Alone."

"I'll walk her," Leon said, already reaching for his jacket off the back of a chair, nothing deliberate behind it. "Two minutes there, two back. Inspector can watch the monitor that long."

"Thanks," Arya said, and there was nothing performed in it, just a plain, real kind of relief.

"Stay put till I'm back," Leon said, pointing loosely at Tatsuya on his way past. "Not diving with nobody watching the monitor."

Tatsuya waited, and it took longer than he expected for the door to open again, Leon dropping back into the monitor chair like the walk had eaten more of the evening than either of them planned for. He pulled his phone out one more time before climbing back in, and the time on the lock screen caught him off guard, further along than he'd expected. Whatever was about to happen down that road, Arya had already given herself the better part of two hours to actually make it matter.

He climbed in, settled against the padding, and let the lid close over him.

The world folded inside out the way it always did, and when it settled he was sitting exactly where he'd left his body, upright in the chair inside Sevish's war tent, like he'd never gotten up at all.

**[WELCOME BACK TO ERYNDAL, LUNAREST OUTSKIRTS]**

**[VIEWERS: 310]**

"Started over. Not even close to where it was. Have to earn that back same as everything else tonight."

The tent flap hung crooked on one of its ties, and past it the air outside had a thin haze to it that hadn't been there before, gray instead of the pale morning fog he remembered leaving. Somewhere off toward the eastern line, something was still burning low enough that he could smell it through the canvas.

Ru was already there, watching him blink back into focus, weight rocked back on his heels, his massive frame filling half the space beside the chair, a fresh cut scabbing over one forearm that hadn't been there before. "You went stiff. Didn't move. Didn't answer." His voice had an edge under it that wasn't aimed at Tatsuya, not exactly, more like it hadn't found anywhere to land yet.

"Yeah," Tatsuya said, standing, rolling the new weight of his shoulders, his eyes already tracking past Ru toward the tent flap and whatever was on the other side of it. "Had to handle something outside."

Sevish came through the flap a beat later, and the calm from earlier was gone from his face entirely, a fresh smear of soot along one forearm, his voice already sharp before he'd fully crossed the threshold. Rott and Nab stood behind him, both breathing harder than the walk from wherever they'd come from should have accounted for.

"You disappear for that long, and you come back to this." Sevish's voice carried an edge sharp enough to cut through the tent's low murmur, his scarred hand tight on the axe at his hip. "Second wave hit the eastern line while you were gone. We held it. Didn't hold it clean."

"Did you get a chance to speak with Shiv," Wyx asked, stepping in close behind her brother, her green eyes fixed on Tatsuya in a way that wasn't quite a question anymore, more a demand dressed up as one.

"Yeah," Tatsuya said, holding her look without letting anything show underneath it. "Meditated on it. Talked with him a while. Didn't realize how much time had passed until I came back to all this."

Something in Wyx's stance eased at that, not all the way, but enough that Tatsuya clocked it happening in real time. The edge in Sevish's voice eased too, a half second behind hers, his grip on the axe loosening.

"Good," Sevish said, and the frustration in his voice had somewhere else to go now, something closer to relief riding underneath it. "We're going to need whatever he's willing to give us. Especially now."

"How bad," Tatsuya said, his eyes moving from Sevish to Rott and Nab, both of them still catching their breath.

"Bad enough that I'm not sending another wave down that road blind," Sevish said, his voice dropping lower, more measured now, the fight already turning into a decision instead of a reaction. "Lost good goblins to it. Don't have the numbers to throw more at a tent full of raiders just to see what happens." He looked at Tatsuya directly, something weighing behind the scarred lines of his face. "Which is why it's still you. My party goes with you, not the warband. Rott, Nab, and Ru. Small enough to move quiet, strong enough to actually finish it, if you're as good as what I've already seen."

"That's the plan," Tatsuya said.

Ru cracked his knuckles, a sound like small stones grinding, and looked down the length of the war tent toward the flap and the hillside beyond it. "Feels different this time. Fighting something that fights back."

"Yeah," Tatsuya said, checking the strap on his chest piece, half his mind already down that road, half of it still on a cafe two blocks from Sakura Arcade. "It does."

Arya's voice came through a beat later, a little breathless, like she'd jogged the last stretch to wherever she'd ended up logging in. "I'm in. Where do you need me?"

"Treeline, my left," Tatsuya said. "Stay back until I give the word."

Sevish nodded once, toward the flap, toward the waiting party outside it. "Then let's go get it."

They moved through camp fast, Ru close at Tatsuya's shoulder, Rott and Nab already ahead. What had been a full warband earlier now moved in tighter, quieter clusters, fewer goblins in the open than there should have been, more of them working on damage than standing idle. The crowd still parted around him as he passed, but the murmurs that followed had a different weight to them now, less curiosity, more something closer to hope.

The eastern edge of camp sat at the top of the hill, where the ground fell away toward the road below. Tatsuya reached the treeline first and stopped.

A tent sat pitched off the road's shoulder, canvas walls dyed a deep, mismatched red, four corners staked into the dirt at hard angles. Not camp gear. Legacy-tier equipment, the kind that got carried over from a character that had already been through everything and cashed out.

Four nameplates flickered into resolution the longer he looked, distance narrowing enough for his HUD to lock onto them.

**[RIAN]** **[TALO]** **[JOSS]** **[SPELLWEAVER_LYN]**

Small text sat under each one, the same four words repeated.

**[GUILD: ANSWERS TO PK_WRAITH]**

"PK_Wraith. I've seen that name in Loop chatter for weeks. Never thought I'd be looking at his people through a treeline."

"You know them?" Ru asked, following his eyes down to the tent.

"Not personally," Tatsuya said. "Know the name they answer to, though."

Arya resolved into view a moment later, half-hidden behind a thick trunk to his left, staff already in hand, its head glowing faint blue.

"That's the tent," Tatsuya said, keeping his voice low. "Four of them inside, from what Kesh counted. We're not fighting them in the open. We're not giving them room to see what's coming."

Ru's tusks caught the light as his mouth split into something close to a grin. "So we take the tent instead."

"Four walls," Tatsuya said. "You, Rott, and Nab take three of them. I need the fourth side open, that's where the road runs, they won't expect anything from a direction they think is already covered." He looked to Arya. "That's where you come in. Hushveil, advanced version, full radius. I need it to cover the whole tent, not just the road."

Arya's frown deepened slightly, her staff's glow shifting. "It's a lot of mana for that big a radius. Tier six, at full size, it'll eat most of what I've got, and I can't hold it up forever once it's live. You need everyone in position before it runs dry."

"You've got it," Tatsuya said. "Once it lands, they can't see, can't hear, can't cast anything, can't cleanse anything. They're not going anywhere until I want them to."

He signaled toward Rott and Nab, both of them crouched near the treeline, waiting. They jogged over low and quiet, Rott still gripping the gas pouches at his belt.

"You two, with Ru," Tatsuya said. "Three walls, three of you, one each. Wait for the fog. Don't move until it's down. Once you're in, gas together, same moment, not one after another. Three of you hitting it at once stacks faster than any of you could manage alone."

The goblins nodded, already dropping into a crouch, working their way along the treeline toward the tent's blind side.

Tatsuya crouched near a cluster of rocks a few paces down the slope, close enough to the tent's back wall to reach it without breaking cover, and pulled up his ability list.

**[RUNE PRIMING – 3/3 AVAILABLE]**

"Three points. Same three walls the gas goes into. Has to hold together, or none of this works."

He moved along the tent's perimeter low and slow, staying just outside the canvas, and laid the first rune against the back wall, a faint pulse of orange light sinking into the dirt and fading, invisible to anyone who wasn't looking for it. The second went against the side wall nearest Ru's position. The third sat against the last open wall, close enough that he could hear muffled voices through the canvas, unbothered, unaware.

He backed off toward the treeline and looked to Arya.

"On my mark," he said. "Clean, no hesitation."

Arya's staff brightened, the spell already building at its head, the faint blue glow deepening into something thicker, heavier, ready.

Tatsuya looked at the tent one more time, at the shape of four people inside it who had no idea what was about to happen to them, and made the call.

"Now."

The fog rolled down off Arya's staff like something poured rather than cast, thick and white, swallowing the tent whole in seconds. The canvas walls vanished into it. The road vanished into it. A notification confirmed what he already felt in the sudden, total silence pressing down over the hillside.

**[HUSHVEIL (ADVANCED) – ACTIVE]**

**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: SILENCED, BLINDED, DEAFENED]**

"They can't see the walls anymore. Can't hear us coming. Can't cast anything. Can't cleanse anything that hits them inside it. Perfect."

"Go," Tatsuya said, and Ru moved first, his frame swallowed by the white a heartbeat later, Rott and Nab right behind him.

Tatsuya counted the silence in his own head, and then green haze started bleeding up through the white, thick and low, pouring in from three sides all at once.

**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: POISONED, MOVEMENT SLOWED, NAUSEOUS]**

Muffled sound started reaching him now, something between coughing and retching, trapped inside canvas walls that weren't going anywhere. Nobody came stumbling out into the road this time. There was nowhere for them to stumble to.

"Not like last time. They're not getting outside this one."

"They're not even trying to run," Arya said quietly, her voice tight with something between disbelief and awe. "They can't hear themselves choking."

Ru and the two goblins peeled back out of the fog and cleared the treeline, moving fast and low.

The tent went up from the inside out, flame catching the gas mid-breath and feeding on it, the light of it flaring bright enough to punch through the fog itself.
