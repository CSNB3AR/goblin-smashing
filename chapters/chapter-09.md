Chapter Nine: Silence and Fire

"Hey," he said, and everything he'd been carrying about Haruto and Eren dropped out of his head at once, replaced by the much more immediate problem of Arya standing there instead of Leon.

"Saw your stream," Arya said, no preamble, her sharp green eyes locked on his. "White hair, a title I've never seen on anyone, and now four guys camped on the only road out of wherever you are. I know roughly where that is. If they're still parked there when you go back under, it's only a matter of time before more people find that spot too."

"I've got it handled," Tatsuya said, his thumb still resting on the edge of his phone, the Haruto post still open on the screen behind his hand.

"You sure? Because from where I was watching, that didn't look handled." She tilted her head slightly, her red hair catching the overhead light. "I could help. I know your situation better than you think, and I've got a build that could actually matter here."

"I said I've got it," Tatsuya said, flatter this time, the words clipped short.

"No. Not doing this again. Last time I let someone in this close, it cost me everything."

Arya pushed off the desk behind her. "No pod free here anyway. There's a cafe two blocks over. I could be logged in and useful before you're even back under."

"I don't need it," Tatsuya said again, his voice steady.

Something in her expression cracked at that, the composed edge finally slipping, her shoulders dropping slightly.

"Please." The word came out different than everything before it, raw in a way nothing she'd said yet had been, her hand coming up to grip her opposite arm. "Let me be part of this. I swear I'd actually be useful."

Tatsuya didn't answer right away, and the silence pulled more out of her than his refusal had.

"My whole guild's gone," Arya said, quieter now, her eyes dropping to the floor between them. "We got into Worlds on a merit slot, borrowed off guilds that already had pro reps, and I actually thought we had a shot. We lost every single map instead. Two of my own leaders turned on me for it afterward, said I lost us the run, and it worked. Ninety members down to three who actually knew what happened and stayed anyway." Her voice had lost its practiced edge somewhere in the middle of that, gone plain and unguarded, her grip on her arm tightening. "And my mom's already got a school picked out in America. Medicine. I don't want that. I want to stay here, build something that's actually mine, be near Leon while I still can. I want to make something real happen in this game before she makes that decision for me instead." She wasn't looking at him anymore, looking somewhere past his shoulder instead, her voice thinning at the edges. "I need this to work."

Tatsuya felt something shift in his chest, the kind of thing he didn't have a clean word for. She was a level ten offering to walk into a fight against a full party for someone who'd already turned her down twice, and she had no idea he still only had Ru at his back. That wasn't a safe assist. That was a real risk on her end too, and it landed somewhere close to the same place Ohema's medical transfer had landed twenty minutes earlier, someone putting something real on the line and trusting him not to waste it.

"What's your build," he said.

Arya blinked, her eyes snapping back to his, her hand dropping from her arm. "Mage. Level ten, eleven. Haven't put much into it since the guild thing, wasn't sure what I was even building for."

"You know Hushveil?"

"Base version, yeah." She frowned slightly. "Silences anyone in the radius who isn't in your party, deafens them too, they can't hear anything outside their own head if they're not grouped up. Advanced version adds a full blindness effect, thick fog nobody outside the party can see through." Her frown deepened, her fingers tightening around her staff. "Sounds useful on paper but it's a waste of points. Doesn't deal damage, doesn't scale into anything real."

"That's exactly what I need," Tatsuya said, his eyes locked on hers. "Not the base. The upgrade. Full package, silence and blind both."

"That's not free," Arya said, her frown deepening. "That's points I've been sitting on since the reset. I don't even know if I'll use it again after this. Why does it even matter? It's not damage. It's not anything."

"It's the price of admission," Tatsuya said, his tone sharpening slightly. "I don't have time to walk you through why it matters. If you want in, that's what gets you in. Not optional."

Arya's eyes narrowed slightly, reading him the way she'd have read an opponent's opening move, something calculating flickering behind them. Whatever she saw in it, she didn't push for the explanation again.

She was quiet for a long moment, weighing it, and he let her sit in it instead of pushing.

"Fine," Arya said finally, her shoulders dropping. "Don't start without me."

"Wasn't planning on it," Tatsuya said.

She was already walking, phone out, thumb moving fast across the screen, her boots echoing against the tile as she headed for the door.

Leon straightened up from the monitor bay, the panel behind him closed again, tools already back in their cloth roll. "That's done. Should hold a lot longer this time." He glanced toward the hallway Arya had just disappeared down, then back to Tatsuya, something considering in his face. "She's really about to log in from a different building to help you fight four guys."

"Apparently," Tatsuya said, still watching the empty hallway.

"Alright." Leon pulled a stool over next to the monitor bay and dropped onto it, elbows on his knees. "Once you're back under, I'm staying on comms the whole time, not just watching a screen. You're gonna need someone actually talking to you out here, not just fixing hardware when it breaks. But I want the stream cut the second this fight's done, win or lose. Not risking your face and four strangers with real gear in the same clip a second longer than it has to be."

"Yeah, that's fair," Tatsuya said, already thinking through what that actually meant, a live voice in his ear instead of dead air between problems.

"Anything you need from out here while you're in there?" Leon asked, reaching for his phone almost before he'd finished the question. "I can't do much, but I can look stuff up. You'd be surprised what's actually out there if you know where to search."

Tatsuya thought about the character sheet still sitting behind his eyes, the title he still didn't understand, and figured there was no better time to ask than now, with someone actually free to chase it down. "Yeah. Look into 'First Descendant of Shiv' if you get a second. That's a title, not a class, and I've never heard of it existing before tonight. Could be brand new patch stuff nobody's cracked yet, or it could be old lore nobody bothered writing down. Either way I want to know before I'm standing in front of goblins who already seem to know what it means better than I do."

"On it," Leon said, already thumbing into a search bar, the light of it reflecting off his glasses. "Fair warning, half of what's out there on this stuff is somebody's guess dressed up like a fact. I'll tell you what I actually find, not what sounds good."

"That's still more than I've got right now," Tatsuya said, already turning back toward the pod.

He climbed in, settled against the padding, and let the lid close over him.

The world folded inside out the way it always did, and when it settled he was sitting exactly where he'd left his body, upright in the chair inside Sevish's war tent, like he'd never gotten up at all.

The tent flap hung crooked on one of its ties, and past it the air outside had a thin haze to it that hadn't been there before, gray instead of the pale morning fog he remembered leaving. Somewhere off toward the eastern line, something was still burning low enough that he could smell it through the canvas.

Ru was already there, watching him blink back into focus, weight rocked back on his heels, his massive frame filling half the space beside the chair, a fresh cut scabbing over one forearm that hadn't been there before. "You went stiff. Didn't move. Didn't answer." His voice had an edge under it that wasn't aimed at Tatsuya, not exactly, more like it hadn't found anywhere to land yet.

"Yeah," Tatsuya said, standing, rolling the new weight of his shoulders, his eyes already tracking past Ru toward the tent flap and whatever was on the other side of it. "Had to handle something outside."

Sevish came through the flap a beat later, and the calm from earlier was gone from his face entirely, a fresh smear of soot along one forearm, his voice already sharp before he'd fully crossed the threshold. "Yeah," Tatsuya said, holding her look without letting anything show underneath it. "Meditated on it. Talked with him a while. Didn't realize how much time had passed until I came back to all this."

Something in Wyx's stance eased at that, not all the way, but enough that Tatsuya clocked it happening in real time. The edge in Sevish's voice eased too, a half second behind hers, his grip on the axe loosening.

"Good," Sevish said, and the frustration in his voice had somewhere else to go now, something closer to relief riding underneath it. "We're going to need whatever he's willing to give us. Especially now."

"How bad," Tatsuya said, his eyes moving from Sevish to Rott and Nab, both of them still catching their breath.

"Bad enough that I'm not sending another wave down that road blind," Sevish said, his voice dropping lower, more measured now, the fight already turning into a decision instead of a reaction. "Lost good goblins to it. Don't have the numbers to throw more at a tent full of raiders just to see what happens." He looked at Tatsuya directly, something weighing behind the scarred lines of his face. "Which is why it's still you. My party goes with you, not the warband. Rott, Nab, and Ru. Small enough to move quiet, strong enough to actually finish it, if you're as good as what I've already seen."

"That's the plan," Tatsuya said.

Ru cracked his knuckles, a sound like small stones grinding, and looked down the length of the war tent toward the flap and the hillside beyond it. "Feels different this time. Fighting something that fights back."

"Yeah," Tatsuya said, checking the strap on his chest piece, half his mind already down that road, half of it still on a cafe two blocks from Sakura Arcade. "It does."

Arya's frown deepened slightly, her staff's glow shifting. "It's a lot of mana for that big a radius. Tier six, at full size, it'll eat most of what I've got, and I can't hold it up forever once it's live. You need everyone in position before it runs dry."
