Chapter Nine: Silence and Fire

He turned around slow, and there she was. Arya, leaning against the hallway wall, one shoulder against the tile, like she’d been standing there a while.

“Hey,” he said, keeping his voice easy, like his stomach hadn’t just dropped through the floor.

“Saw your stream,” Arya said, no preamble, her sharp green eyes locked on his. “I know roughly where you are. If those guys are still parked there when you go back under, it’s only a matter of time before more people find that spot too.”

“I’ve got it handled,” Tatsuya said.

“You sure? Because from where I was watching, that didn’t look handled.” She tilted her head slightly, her red hair catching the overhead light. “I could help. I know your situation better than you think, and I’ve got a build that could actually matter here.”

“I said I’ve got it,” Tatsuya said, flatter this time, the words clipped short.

“No. Not doing this again. Last time I let someone in this close, it cost me everything.”

Arya pushed off the wall behind her. “No pod free here anyway. There’s a cafe two blocks over, I could get logged in from there in twenty minutes. Just say the word.”

“I don’t need it,” Tatsuya said again, his voice steady.

Something in her expression cracked at that, the composed edge finally slipping, her shoulders dropping slightly.

“Please.” The word came out different than everything before it, raw in a way nothing she’d said yet had been, her hand coming up to grip her opposite arm. “Let me be part of this. I swear I’d actually be useful.”

Tatsuya didn’t answer right away, and the silence pulled more out of her than his refusal had.

“My whole guild’s gone,” Arya said, quieter now, her eyes dropping to the floor between them. “I kicked two members for good reason, they cared more about the cameras than the matches, and we lost every single map for it. They came back with a rumor campaign afterward, and it worked. Ninety members down to three who actually knew what happened and stayed anyway.” Her voice had lost its practiced edge somewhere in the middle of that, gone plain and unguarded, her grip on her arm tightening. “And my mom’s already got a school picked out in America. Medicine. I don’t want that. I want to stay here, build something that’s actually mine, be near Leon while I still can. I want to make something real happen in this game before she makes that decision for me instead.” She wasn’t looking at him anymore, looking somewhere past his shoulder instead, her voice thinning at the edges. “I need this to work.”

Tatsuya felt something shift in his chest, the kind of thing he didn’t have a clean word for. She was a level ten offering to walk into a fight against a full party for someone who’d already turned her down twice, and she had no idea he still only had Ru at his back. That wasn’t a safe assist. That was a real risk on her end too.

“What’s your build,” he said.

Arya blinked, her eyes snapping back to his, her hand dropping from her arm. “Mage. Level ten, eleven. Haven’t put much into it since the guild thing, wasn’t sure what I was even building for.”

“You know Hushveil?”

“Base version, yeah.” She frowned slightly. “Silences anyone in the radius who isn’t in your party, deafens them too, they can’t hear anything outside their own head if they’re not grouped up. Advanced version adds a full blindness effect, thick fog nobody outside the party can see through.” Her frown deepened, her fingers tightening around her staff. “Sounds useful on paper but it’s a waste of points. Doesn’t deal damage, doesn’t scale into anything real.”

“That’s exactly what I need,” Tatsuya said, his eyes locked on hers. “Not the base. The upgrade. Full package, silence and blind both.”

“She doesn’t see it because she’s pure DPS in her head. Damage doesn’t mean you only get to think in damage.”

“That’s not free,” Arya said, her frown deepening. “That’s points I’ve been sitting on since the reset. I don’t even know if I’ll use it again after this. Why does it even matter? It’s not damage. It’s not anything.”

“It’s the price of admission,” Tatsuya said, his tone sharpening slightly. “I don’t have time to walk you through why it matters. If you want in, that’s what gets you in. Not optional.”

Arya’s eyes narrowed slightly, reading him the way she’d have read an opponent’s opening move, something calculating flickering behind them. Whatever she saw in it, she didn’t push for the explanation again.

She was quiet for a long moment, weighing it, and he let her sit in it instead of pushing.

“Fine,” Arya said finally, her shoulders dropping. “Twenty minutes. Don’t start without me.”

“Wasn’t planning on it,” Tatsuya said.

She was already walking, phone out, texting the cafe or maybe just checking the time, her boots echoing against the tile as she moved down the hallway.

Leon’s voice found him again through the earpiece a moment later, cutting in clear.

“Hey, we’re done back here,” Leon said, his tone lighter now, the background noise quieter. “Whenever you’re ready.”

Tatsuya headed back to the pod.

The lid closed over him, and the world folded inside out the way it always did, and when it settled he was sitting exactly where he’d left his body, upright in the chair inside Sevish’s war tent, like he’d never gotten up at all.

**[WELCOME BACK TO ERYNDAL, LUNAREST OUTSKIRTS]**

**[VIEWERS: 310]**

“Started over. Not even close to where it was. Have to earn that back same as everything else tonight.”

Ru was already there, watching him blink back into focus, weight rocked back on his heels, his massive frame filling the space beside the chair. “You went stiff for a while. Didn’t move. Didn’t answer.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said, standing, rolling the new weight of his shoulders. “Had to handle something outside.”

Sevish stood near the tent’s entrance with a bundle of gear at his feet, Rott and Nab waiting a few paces off, restless. A crude chest piece and a set of bindings sat on top of the pile, better than the rags but nothing close to what he actually needed.

“Everything settled?” Sevish asked, one hand resting on the pommel at his hip, his scarred face unreadable.

“Getting there,” Tatsuya said, pulling the chest piece on over the rags, the leather stiff and unfamiliar against him, his clawed hands fumbling slightly with the straps. “One more piece coming. Give it twenty minutes, then we move.”

Sevish’s eyes flicked toward Ru, then back to Tatsuya, something unreadable passing behind them. “Another player.”

“Somebody who’s earning her spot,” Tatsuya said, his hands finally securing the last strap, the chest piece settling into place.

Wyx had drifted closer during the wait, watching the whole exchange with the same sharp attention she’d had since he’d first stepped out with white hair and a purple title over his head. “Twenty minutes,” she said, more statement than question, her head tilting slightly. “And then you take Bloodhurst’s own road back from whoever thinks they own it now.”

“That’s the plan,” Tatsuya said.

Ru cracked his knuckles, a sound like small stones grinding, and looked down the length of the war tent toward the flap and the hillside beyond it. “Feels different this time. Fighting something that fights back.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said, checking the strap on the chest piece one more time, half his mind already down that road, half of it still on a cafe two blocks from Sakura Arcade. “It does.”

The wait stretched longer than he expected, twenty minutes turning into twenty-five, and Tatsuya spent it checking his gear again, adjusting the bindings on his wrists, making sure the necklace sat right against his throat.

**[PARTY INVITATION: ARYA]**

He accepted it, and a second nameplate resolved beside his own in the party UI.

**[ARYA – LEVEL 11 – MAGE]**

**[ARYA HAS JOINED YOUR PARTY]**

**[PARTY SIZE: 3/5]**

Arya’s voice came through a beat later, a little breathless, like she’d jogged the last stretch to the pod. “I’m in. Where do you need me?”

“Treeline, my left,” Tatsuya said. “Stay back until I give the word.”

Sevish nodded once, toward the flap, toward the waiting party outside it. “Then let’s go get it.”

They moved through camp fast, Ru close at Tatsuya’s shoulder, Rott and Nab already ahead, holding position somewhere past the treeline. The crowd parted around them as they walked, goblins pulling back to make room, eyes tracking their path, murmurs rising and falling in their wake.

The eastern edge of camp sat at the top of the hill, where the ground fell away toward the road below. Tatsuya reached the treeline first and stopped.

A tent sat pitched off the road’s shoulder, canvas walls dyed a deep, mismatched red, four corners staked into the dirt at hard angles. Not camp gear. Legacy-tier equipment, the kind that got carried over from a character that had already been through everything and cashed out.

Four nameplates flickered into resolution the longer he looked, distance narrowing enough for his HUD to lock onto them.

**[RIAN]** **[TALO]** **[JOSS]** **[SPELLWEAVER_LYN]**

Small text sat under each one, the same four words repeated.

**[GUILD: ANSWERS TO PK_WRAITH]**

“PK_Wraith. I’ve seen that name in Loop chatter for weeks. Never thought I’d be looking at his people through a treeline.”

“You know them?” Ru asked, following his eyes down to the tent.

“Not personally,” Tatsuya said. “Know the name they answer to, though.”

Arya resolved into view a moment later, half-hidden behind a thick trunk to his left, staff already in hand, its head glowing faint blue.

“That’s the tent,” Tatsuya said, keeping his voice low. “Four of them inside, from what Kesh counted. We’re not fighting them in the open. We’re not giving them room to see what’s coming.”

Ru’s tusks caught the light as his mouth split into something close to a grin. “So we take the tent instead.”

“Four walls,” Tatsuya said. “You and the two Sevish sent ahead take three of them. I need the fourth side open, that’s where the road runs, they won’t expect anything from a direction they think is already covered.” He looked to Arya. “That’s where you come in. Hushveil, advanced version, full radius. I need it to cover the whole tent, not just the road.”

Arya’s frown deepened slightly, her staff’s glow shifting, but she didn’t argue it this time. “It’s a lot of mana for that big a radius. Tier six, at full size, it’ll eat most of what I’ve got.”

“You’ve got it,” Tatsuya said. “Once it lands, they can’t see, can’t hear, can’t cast anything, can’t cleanse anything. They’re not going anywhere until I want them to.”

He signaled toward Rott and Nab, both of them crouched near the treeline, waiting. They jogged over low and quiet, Rott still gripping the gas pouches at his belt.

“You two, with Ru,” Tatsuya said. “Three walls, three of you, one each. Wait for the fog. Don’t move until it’s down.”

The goblins nodded, already dropping into a crouch, working their way along the treeline toward the tent’s blind side.

Tatsuya crouched near a cluster of rocks a few paces down the slope, close enough to the tent’s back wall to reach it without breaking cover, and pulled up his ability list.

**[RUNE PRIMING – 3/3 AVAILABLE]**

“Three points. Same three walls the gas goes into. Has to hold together, or none of this works.”

He moved along the tent’s perimeter low and slow, staying just outside the canvas, and laid the first rune against the back wall, a faint pulse of orange light sinking into the dirt and fading, invisible to anyone who wasn’t looking for it. The second went against the side wall nearest Ru’s position. The third sat against the last open wall, close enough that he could hear muffled voices through the canvas, unbothered, unaware.

He backed off toward the treeline and looked to Arya.

“On my mark,” he said. “Clean, no hesitation.”

Arya’s staff brightened, the spell already building at its head, the faint blue glow deepening into something thicker, heavier, ready.

Tatsuya looked at the tent one more time, at the shape of four people inside it who had no idea what was about to happen to them, and made the call.

“Now.”

The fog rolled down off Arya’s staff like something poured rather than cast, thick and white, swallowing the tent whole in seconds. The canvas walls vanished into it. The road vanished into it. A notification confirmed what he already felt in the sudden, total silence pressing down over the hillside.

**[HUSHVEIL (ADVANCED) – ACTIVE]**

**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: SILENCED, BLINDED, DEAFENED]**

“They can’t see the walls anymore. Can’t hear us coming. Can’t cast anything. Can’t cleanse anything that hits them inside it. Perfect.”

“Go,” Tatsuya said, and Ru moved first, his frame swallowed by the white a heartbeat later, Rott and Nab right behind him.

Tatsuya counted the silence in his own head, ten seconds, twenty, and then green haze started bleeding up through the white, thick and low, pouring in from three sides at once.

**[GOBLIN GAS – ACTIVE]**

**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: POISONED, MOVEMENT SLOWED]**

Muffled sound started reaching him now, something between coughing and retching, trapped inside canvas walls that weren’t going anywhere. Nobody came stumbling out into the road this time. There was nowhere for them to stumble to.

“Not like last time. They’re not getting outside this one.”

“They’re not even trying to run,” Arya said quietly, her voice tight with something between disbelief and awe. “They can’t hear themselves choking.”

Ru and the two goblins peeled back out of the fog and cleared the treeline, moving fast and low.

Tatsuya triggered the first rune.

The tent went up from the inside out, flame catching the gas mid-breath and feeding on it, the light of it flaring bright enough to punch through the fog itself. The second rune fired a half second later, then the third, each explosion stacking on the last until the whole shape of the tent was a single sheet of orange fire collapsing in on itself.

**[ANCIENT BONE FLAME BARRAGE – TRIGGERED]**

**[COMBO BONUS: TOXIN AMPLIFICATION ACTIVE]**

**[DAMAGE: CRITICAL HIT x3]**

Four nameplates flickered and died in rapid succession, kill notifications stacking faster than he could read them.

**[RIAN – DEFEATED]**

**[TALO – DEFEATED]**

**[JOSS – DEFEATED]**

**[SPELLWEAVER_LYN – DEFEATED]**

The fog started clearing as the spell’s duration ran out, and when it lifted there was nothing left where the tent had stood except scorched canvas and four faint drop markers glowing in the dirt.

**[IRONHIDE CHESTPLATE – RARE]**

**[VANGUARD’S GREAVES – RARE]**

**[PK RAIDING CREST – RARE]**

“Legacy gear. They were sitting on real drops, and none of it’s bound. Not anymore. All three, sellable.”

A fourth marker sat apart from the other three, right where Spellweaver_Lyn had gone down, and what rose out of it wasn’t armor. A staff, gnarled dark wood wound in live green vines, a faceted pale-green crystal cradled at its head, soft motes of light drifting off it into the settling smoke.

**[SHIVBONE STAFF – LEGENDARY]**

He turned it once in his hands, weighing it, and thought of Arya standing not thirty feet from him, still holding onto a spell she’d almost thrown away an hour ago.

“This one’s not going on any market. She needs this more than the yen does.”

Then, separate from everything else, a second window blinked into existence beside the first, smaller, no rarity color he recognized, just plain text sitting in a border that read UNIQUE instead of any tier he’d seen before.

**[DOPPELGANGER’S BAND – UNIQUE]**

A plain iron ring, no visible enchantment, no description sitting under the name. Just the ring, and the UNIQUE tag, and a small note at the bottom of the window he had to squint to actually read.

**[GAMBLER’S GAMBIT BONUS PROC: INDEPENDENT ROLL]**

“That didn’t come off any of them. That’s a separate pull entirely.”

He stared at it a second, turning it over in his UI the way he’d have turned a real object over in his hand, looking for something that would tell him what it actually did. Nothing came back.

“Bound, probably. And I have no idea what it is. But it’s UNIQUE, which means there’s exactly one of these in the entire game, and it just landed in my inventory.”

The fog cleared fully, cold air rushing back in off the hillside, the smell of scorched canvas mixing with the lingering bite of gas as the green haze finally dissipated. Arya stepped up beside him at the treeline, her staff’s glow already dimming back to nothing as the spell ran dry.

“You just wiped a full party in under a minute,” she said, her voice climbing, disbelief cracking into something closer to a laugh. “With a fog spell I was ready to throw away. They couldn’t even cleanse. They were just stuck in it.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said, still watching the smoke curl up off the scorched dirt below. “That’s the idea.”

She went quiet for a second, scrolling through something only she could see, and when she looked back at him her expression had shifted, something recalibrating behind her eyes.

“You knew exactly what that spell was going to do,” Arya said, her tone flatter now, more precise. “Before I even cast it. You knew they couldn’t cleanse because you knew silence locks all skills. You set this whole thing up around that one mechanic.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said again, his eyes still on the scorched road below.

Another beat of quiet, and then she said it plainly, like she was confirming something she’d been working out since the fog went up.

“You’re not lucky. You actually planned this.”

He didn’t answer that one. Some things landed better when you let them sit.

A new notification stacked in beside the others.
