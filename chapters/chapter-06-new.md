# Chapter Seven: Consequences in Silence

The shell hissed open, and the bell was still ringing in my head. *"That wasn't just for me. That was a public announcement. The whole server just heard a world quest go live, with my name on it."*

The room came back around me one piece at a time. The smell first, cigarette smoke and dust and plastic, the same as when I'd gone under. Warm lantern lamps hung low over the brick pillars, and steel beams crossed a dark ceiling above the wood paneling on the far wall. The pod stood tall and dark around me, cable bundles looping down its sides and a blue glow in the seams, Kazuhaya Tech stamped small near the base.

My vision was blurry, the room smeared and dragging a half-beat behind my eyes every time I moved my head. My stomach swung like I'd stepped off a boat onto a roller coaster, one long lurch and then a drop. I swallowed twice. *"Okay. Rough, but I can work with rough."*

My own size hit me next. After the goblin, a little over six feet of me hung in a cradle built for a shorter man, shoulders jammed against the padded spine, arms heavy in the cuffs. My locs had worked half out of the tie while I was under, and they lay against the back of my neck and the headrest, weighing more than I remembered.

I popped the cuffs and stepped down off the platform and rolled my shoulders until something cracked. *"I'm stretching before I get in that thing next time. For real."*

My backpack sat zipped against the nearest brick pillar where I'd shoved it before I went back in, the toolbox packed inside.

The pod was another story. Heat rolled off the shell the way it had in the blackout, a thin thread of smoke leaking from a seam near the base. The cooling fan dragged in its housing with the same dying whine, and the two leads I'd bridged to the wall were warm to the touch. *"I'm surprised I even got through a session."* I dug the laptop out of the bag just long enough to plug into the service port, then packed it away. The core read nineteen percent. *"It was twenty when I first logged in. One session, one point."* If that held, I had maybe twenty logins before it died.

*"If I want long sessions, it needs a new core."* There were no Kazehaya cores left. Dad's company had gone down with him, every part pulled after the accident. I rested my palm on the warm casing. He built this thing, and I was a few bad sessions from losing it.

I eased the drive out of the slot beside the headrest, and shouldered the backpack off the pillar. Dad's manual was already inside with the letter. I pulled my phone out on the walk down the back hallway, floorboards creaking under my boots.

9:43 PM. Leon had said twenty minutes to closing, which left me seventeen.

I opened the HGO Companion App and held the drive against the back of the phone.

**[SCANNING...]**

**[DRIVE DETECTED]**

**[LOGGING IN...]**

My account loaded, the goblin face of my avatar staring back at me from a small circle at the top of the screen. *"That's not the face I picked. Not that I picked it."*

**[NYKRI - LEVEL 3 - GOBLIN]**

*"Nykri. Still strange, seeing it written down."*

A banner sat across the top of the home screen.

**[WORLD QUEST ACTIVATED: CALL OF THE BONES | ACTIVATED BY: NYKRI]**

My name sat right there on the banner. *"They told everyone who. Not where."* I swiped past it, and my thumb wasn't quite steady.

Near the bottom of the page, under a small header, my spells sat listed in plain text, Goblin Gas and Goblin Flare. My inventory sat above them.

**[GOBLIN GADGET BELT - COMMON (LOCKED)]**

**[GAMBLER'S EARRING - UNIQUE (EQUIPPED)]**

**[MINOR ELIXIR x2 - UNCOMMON]**

I tapped the elixirs, and the listing opened.

**[MINOR ELIXIR | VALUE: 21,000 SHILLINGS EACH]**

*"Twenty-one thousand shillings for a mana potion? What's that in yen?"* I tapped the number, and a rate card slid up.

**[10 SHILLINGS = 1 HGO COIN]**

**[1 HGO COIN = ¥4]**

I ran it in my head. Eight thousand four hundred yen each. I confirmed the listing, already bracing to wait. The vials vanished almost immediately.

*"That fast? Why?"*

**[BUY ORDER MATCHED: 2 ITEMS]**

*"Buy orders. Somebody had already ordered them."*

**[SOLD: 42,000 SHILLINGS]**

A second prompt rose over the first.

**[EXCHANGE SHILLINGS FOR HGO COINS?]**

I confirmed it, and the coins landed.

**[4,200 HGO COINS RECEIVED]**

One more prompt followed, and it was the one that mattered.

**[CASH OUT HGO COINS?]**

**[BANK ACCOUNT REQUIRED TO COMPLETE CASH OUT]**

I linked the account my WattsUp paychecks already hit, a few taps and a code texted to my phone, and the warning cleared.

**[CASH OUT COMPLETE: ¥16,800 DEPOSITED]**

**[BANK BALANCE: ¥64,120]**

I stood in the back hallway of Sakura Arcade with my chest warm and read the number a third time. *"First one. Definitely not the last."* I couldn't walk into the apartment and tell Chiyo I was just playing a game. She'd let me drop a year of school after Dad and Mom, and she'd let it ride because I was working under Gato, and I lived under her roof for next to nothing. As long as I was a kid, she had the last word. If I wasn't at WattsUp and I wasn't at Tochi, she'd ask what I was doing with my time, and the honest answer sounded like a boy wasting his life. I needed a job she could picture and money she could see.

I backed out of the app and opened Loop. My own profile loaded first. I changed my handle to Nykri to match the game, then picked the goblin profile picture from my HGO character profile and made sure it carried across. That was it.

Then I checked what was trending in gaming and entertainment. My name and the World Quest sat right there, glittering with reposts and posts about me. A few big heads were already talking about it, Dosuke_AI and Yuna_N9 among them.

*"I have to get back here early in the morning."* The feed kept refreshing under my thumb, my name sliding up it again and again.

I swapped out of Loop and opened my browser, then dug the address out of an old receipt I'd saved for the EB's parts. It was a marketplace Dad used to buy from, more eBay than shop, where people sold tech parts that weren't supposed to be sold. I hadn't been on it in years. Everywhere else I'd looked tonight had turned up nothing, so I searched it for processor cores. Nexus's were locked behind patents. The only ones listed were Kuroshio capsule cores, and the price stopped me cold.

**[KUROSHIO CAPSULE CORE | ¥860,000]**

*"For one core?"* There wasn't anything else to search, so I dropped the thought of buying one. More debt was the last thing I needed, with Mom's surgery already hanging over every yen. I leaned on the wall and worked at ways to make this different. I scratched my goatee. Dad hadn't copied anyone, so why should I? *"Build my own, off his schematics, made for his pod."* I couldn't learn that on a laptop in a back room, and I couldn't learn it working in Gato's shop either. The only places I knew that taught anything like it at a high level were Tochi or some other tightly run university, and Tochi was the one I had a way back into. Even then, lab access wasn't handed out. Most students never got in unless they sat on the student council, so I'd have to make some deals. It meant walking back into the place I'd left, with Eren still in it. I thumbed the screen dark before that thought got any louder.

I knew I had to tell Leon what was going on, but I had to figure out how to do it without it feeling like I'd already broken his trust. The clock said 9:52 PM, so I started toward the front. A draft from the street door stirred my locs as I threaded between the old desks and dark monitors, all six feet and change of me.

Leon was at the counter under the lantern lamps, tablet in one hand, rubbing the bridge of his nose under his glasses. The monitor on the front desk beside him cycled through its screensaver, humming.

"You look like you got hit by a truck." He set the tablet down and gave me a once-over. "Did everything go well in there?"

I leaned on the counter, my legs shaky from the pod. "Kinda. My first VR experience kinda did it."

"Everything okay?" He kept his eyes on my face.

I couldn't give him a shrug this time. "No."

Worry crossed his face, quick, before he smoothed it over. I had to be honest with him. I was the one who'd caused it.

"All right." He turned the tablet a quarter turn on the counter. "What's wrong with the pods?"

I straightened off the counter. "That's what I came to talk to you about."

"Okay, then." He folded his arms. "Go ahead."

I held his eyes. "I hotwired the pod tonight. I had to override the slot with my dad's manual to get the drive to run. I'm not saying that's what took the power out earlier. I don't know what did. But I'd be lying if I said it couldn't have been me."

Leon went very still, and for a second the only sound was the front desk monitor humming. Then he was moving, one hand already reaching toward the back hallway, his voice climbing a notch before he hauled it back down. "I told you that pod only plays retro games. I told you what happens if you push something newer through it. That's the only moneymaker I've got."

"It's not dead," I said, keeping my hands where he could see them.

"You started work on my machine without asking." His voice came out low and even again, which was worse than yelling, and his fingers pressed white against the edge of the counter. "Typically I'd throw out anyone who touched that. So what's stopping me from kicking you out right now? You must have a plan, or you wouldn't have walked up here. Or did you just come to tell me my stuff is broken?"

I stayed where I was. "I checked it over before I stepped out. The core is nearly dead, the drive slot's built for older drives, and the wiring's cheap. The core read twenty percent when I first logged in, and nineteen after one session."

"So the one session drained it?" He tilted his head, doing the math behind his glasses, and it wasn't making him happier.

"If I'm going off that, a point a session. About twenty logins, maybe fewer." I leaned in over the counter. "Dad left notes and schematics on the Kazehaya VR pods, so I know what I'm looking at. I'm not buying cores. I'm building one. From scratch it would've been brutal, but you've got two pods here to work from, and a core that can carry a newer title is doable. My Haya EB has lasted years, and all I've done is upgrade it. Dad's work doesn't die."

"Building it with what?" Leon rested a forearm on the counter.

"I don't have the lab or the parts. Not yet." I looked at the lamp over the counter, buzzing faintly, instead of at him.

Leon laughed once, short and humorless. "A lab and parts like that aren't cheap, and I'd know. Are you rich or something, kid?"

"No." I met his eyes again. "But Tochi has both."

"Tochi." He said it flat, with an edge. "What do you know about Tochi?"

"I was enrolled there." I took my wallet out and slid my old student ID across the counter. It was last year's, the corners gone soft, my face a little younger on it. "I haven't been in a while. After my dad died, I took a break."

Leon picked the card up and read it, then read it again. "The Tochi Academy." He said it like the name had weight in his hand. "Merit entry only. Every top student in the country is fighting for a seat." He slid it back. "And you were in."

"Yeah." I put it away.

"That makes sense, then." His shoulders came down a fraction. "I'm not going to judge anybody for missing school. My sister's the same."

He studied me. "HGO on one of my Kazehaya pods. That game chews through helmets built for it."

I gave a small shrug. "Barely."

"That's not barely." He let a breath out through his nose. "So going to Tochi is how you build the core."

"That's my lead." I kept my eyes on him.

"I see." Whatever anger he'd been holding ran out of places to go. When he spoke again his voice had dropped. "I'm going to be honest with you. They're the only thing that's drawn any money or attention here lately, even if it's not the attention most places want. If the one that works stops working, I've got nothing left but a retro arcade." He picked at a scuff on the counter with his thumbnail. "I don't love being told after the fact. But I'm not in a position to turn down help, so I'm gambling on this too."

"I've got a proposition for you." He straightened. "It's going to take partnerships and deals. Let me work out the details and the paperwork, and I'll bring in the VRSA inspector. If you're serious about getting those pods up, how long before profit starts coming in?"

"Depends." I thought of the class I'd have to get into and of what today's luck had paid. "On how much I make off today's luck, mostly. If I have the proper time, I think I can make it."

Leon took the unlit cigarette from behind his ear and rolled it between two fingers. "Today's luck." The monitor hummed. "Are you the one who triggered it?"

I didn't answer. The silence ran long enough for the lamp to buzz twice, and I didn't look away.

Leon lit the cigarette. He turned his head and let the smoke out toward the window, away from me, the way you do around somebody younger. "I see. Well, that explains why you walked in here and told me everything and risked all of it." He tapped ash into a saucer by the register. "A World Quest in your back pocket explains enough."

"Nykri," he added, trying the name out flat. I nodded once.

"I don't play the game. If it wasn't for my sister, I wouldn't know much about it." He turned the cigarette over in his fingers. "But I know most of the top guilds that ever got a World Quest saw something out of it. And there's money in recording what you do. That's content nobody will ever be able to repeat, and if it's recorded, it lasts forever."

"Come in at nine, nine-thirty when we open." "Open to close, with the booth monitor running and me watching it. I'll put you on the books, part-time, so your guardian has an answer. One of those pods is yours, and when the second one's up I'll pay you for it. It stays quiet until the clearance comes through."

*"A pod, a job I can point at, and the parts. Yeah. That's a deal."*

"I'll get it done," I said, and held his eyes until he nodded.

"I believe you." He walked me to the door. The sign flipped behind me and the lock clicked.

Outside, I stowed the backpack in the rear case, but before I pulled the helmet on, I opened my thread with Chiyo. Her last message was the one about Tochi, my thumbs-up still under it, answering nothing. I typed, deleted it, typed again.

**[Thinking about going back to Tochi.]**

I sent it before I could argue myself out of it. Then I pulled the helmet on, pressed my thumb to the touchscreen, and waited out the half-second scan until the dash lit green and the visor threw the route line across the street. The motor hummed steady under me. It held. *"Good. Hold for me one more night."*

I had one stop before home. The hospital came up on my left, white-lit and too clean. I parked, took the stairs two at a time, and caught the antiseptic smell before I hit the second floor landing.

Mom's room sat at the end of the hall, door half-open the way it always was. I pushed through and let it swing shut behind me.

She looked smaller than I remembered, the blanket lying flat across her where it should have lifted. Monitors beeped quiet and steady beside the bed, the IV drip slow and regular, her face turned toward the window.

I pulled the chair close and sat, elbows on my knees, hands loose between them.

"Hey, Mom." I kept my voice low. "I know it's been a minute."

The monitors kept beeping. I smoothed the edge of the blanket flat with two fingers. "I got into the game. HGO. Finally. Only took me saving up for a year and hacking an antique pod in someone's back room. I was going for the Elf Spellforger, the build I drew up, and the power went out mid-creation. Whole thing glitched, and now I'm permanently stuck as a goblin. Can't reset, can't trade out, can't do anything about it."

"But I made some money already." The monitor drew the same slow line across its screen. "Sixteen thousand eight hundred yen off two elixirs the earring rolled me."

"Crownfall's two weeks out. Ten million yen on the table. I've been in the game one time, a few hours total, and I'm already talking about the prize." I paused. "But everyone's starting fresh characters for the expansion. For once I'm not coming in behind everybody."

I stopped, my throat going tight.

"I'm doing this for you. For the surgery. For Momo and Taji. For Chiyo, so she doesn't have to keep covering everything herself." I looked at my hands, turned up and empty between my knees. "I know you'd tell me not to put that on myself. But it's already there. It's been there since Dad died."

"I'm gonna figure it out. I always do." I said it to the window as much as to her.

I stood and pressed my hand to her shoulder before I headed for the door.

I was halfway down the hall when I heard them. Two voices, low and careful, the kind of careful that meant they weren't meant to carry.

"...six months," one of them was saying. Ms. Sugiyama, the nurse who'd known me by name since Mom was first admitted. "Dr. Inoue was clear. Without surgery soon, the concern isn't just whether she survives. It's whether there's still a mind left to come back to."

I stopped walking.

"Chiyo asked us to keep it quiet," the second nurse said. "She didn't want the family worrying."

"I know," Ms. Sugiyama said. "But that boy deserves to know."

"Not our call," the second nurse said, already turning away.

Their footsteps faded down the opposite hall, and I stood alone in the corridor, hearing the words again in the same order.

*"Six months. And Chiyo knew. She knew and she didn't tell me."*

The anger came fast and clean, hotter than anything else I'd felt all day. Being kept out of the room like a kid who needed shielding while I was out here carrying half the weight myself.

She was trying to protect me. The thought didn't help.

I turned and walked, not toward the exit, just away from the voices, counting the ceiling lights as they passed because it gave my head something to hold. By the sixth one I'd lost count, and the corner came up fast.

The collision knocked the air out of me, a shoulder catching my chest, papers exploding outward in a soft white flurry. A small startled sound came from whoever I'd walked into, and my hands were already moving, catching an elbow, steadying someone before either of us actually went down.

"Sorry, I wasn't," I started, and then I actually looked at her.

Twin buns. Glasses that seemed specifically designed to make her forgettable and were failing completely at the job. She was already crouching to gather pages, hands quick and practiced, and when she looked up at me, something in her face did the exact same double-take mine just had.

Neither of us said anything for a second that ran a beat too long to be nothing.

"It's fine," she said, too fast, like she needed to get ahead of the silence. "I wasn't watching either."

I crouched across from her and started helping stack pages. Her eyes caught on the phone in my other hand, still lit on my HGO profile where I'd been fidgeting with it, the goblin face and my new name glowing in the corner.

"You play?" she asked, tilting her head, a real question.

"Just started." I turned the phone slightly without meaning to.

"Nykri." She read it slow, testing how it sat. Something flickered across her face, amused, like I'd handed her the funniest thing she'd heard all week. "I like it."

We finished stacking the last of the pages in a silence that had stopped being awkward somewhere in the last thirty seconds. I was close enough now to see the seam where her glasses didn't quite sit right on her face, like they weren't originally hers.

She stood first, I followed, the stack of papers changing hands without either of us fully letting go for a second.

"I have to go," she said, and somehow didn't sound entirely like she meant it. She pulled a pen from her jacket pocket, scribbled fast on the corner of one page, tore it free, folded it twice, and tucked it into my jacket pocket herself. Her hand rested against my chest for half a second longer than the motion needed. "My code. Add me sometime."

"Wait," I started.

"Good luck with the game," she said, taking the papers from me and turning, already three steps into the hallway before I'd finished the sentence. "Hope to see you soon."

I watched her go, twin buns bouncing once as she rounded the corner, gone before I thought to actually move after her. I stood there a second longer than made sense, the rest of the papers still in my arms.

I set the papers on the nearest chair and unfolded the note.

A friend code sat at the top, a string of letters and numbers in clean, careful handwriting. Beneath it, a date.

December 23rd. 689450YY

*"Two days before Crownfall."* I turned the paper over, checking the back in case something else was written there. Nothing.

I pocketed the note and looked down the empty hall where she'd gone, and the quiet didn't hold. Ms. Sugiyama's voice came back first, then the second nurse's. Six months. Chiyo knew. The two things sat next to each other, the note and the sentence, and neither one would stay separate from the other.

I pulled out my phone.

My thread with Chiyo was still open from outside the arcade. My own message sat at the bottom, *Thinking about going back to Tochi*, still unread on her end, and above it her last one about Tochi with my thumbs-up underneath.

She'd kept this from me because she thought it would break something. She was wrong. I scratched at my goatee once, hard. This was my call.

I slid the phone back into my pocket before continuing out of the building.

*"Tochi. I'm going."*

**[END CHAPTER SEVEN]**
