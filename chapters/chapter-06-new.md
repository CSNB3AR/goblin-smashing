# Chapter Seven: Consequences in Silence

The shell hissed open, and the bell was still ringing in my head. *"That wasn't just for me. That was a public announcement. The whole server just heard a world quest go live."*

Then the room came back around me one piece at a time. The smell first, cigarette smoke and dust and plastic, the same as when I'd gone under. Then the warm lantern lamps hung low over the brick pillars, the steel beams crossing a dark ceiling, the wood paneling on the far wall. The pod stood around me like a dark tower, cable bundles looping down its sides and a blue glow in the seams, Kazuhaya Tech stamped small near the base.

My vision was blurry. Not dark, just smeared, the room dragging a half-beat behind my eyes every time I moved my head. My stomach swung like I'd stepped off a boat onto a roller coaster, one long lurch and then a drop, and I swallowed twice and let it pass. *"Okay. Rough, but I can work with rough."*

Then my own size hit me. After the goblin I felt enormous. A little over six feet of me hung in a cradle built for a shorter man, shoulders jammed against the padded spine, knees nudging the front of the shell, arms heavy in the cuffs. My locs had worked half out of the tie while I was under, and they lay against the back of my neck and the headrest, weighing more than I remembered.

I hung there until the room stopped turning, the chat still scrolling behind my eyes and my pulse not coming down with the rest of me.

I popped the cuffs and stepped down off the platform, knees stiff from standing locked in there so long, and rolled my shoulders until something cracked. *"I'm stretching before I get in that thing next time. For real."*

My bag sat zipped against the nearest brick pillar where I'd shoved it before I went back in, the toolbox packed beside it. Nothing left out for Leon to trip over. That much the blackout had taught me.

The pod was another story. Heat poured off the shell, the fan dragging in its housing with a whine I hadn't heard going in, and the wires I'd bridged to the wall ran warm along the base. *"I'm surprised I even got through a session."* A bypass wasn't a repair. It was borrowed time on a core already running at twenty percent.

*"If I want long sessions, it needs a real fix, and I can't buy the part."* Third-gen cores weren't sold. If anyone could walk into a store for one, anyone could crack it open and copy the schematics, so they only went to partners. The only school openly partnered with Nexus Enterprises was Tochi, and I'd seen that core logo stamped on equipment in its technology lab. But the lab wasn't open to just anyone. Instructors got that access, not the kids. I'd have to climb: top grades, and a name in that school the way Mom's had been in volleyball and Dad's in the labs.

And if Sakura Arcade closed, it took my only pod with it. Every third-gen cafe in the area was eating this place alive.

I eased the drive out of the slot beside the headrest, then dragged the bag over, put Dad's manual back in with the letter still inside, and shouldered it. Some habits didn't need a reason. I picked up the toolbox and pulled my phone out on the walk down the back hallway, past the dark rooms and humming racks, floorboards creaking under my boots.

9:43 PM. Leon had said twenty minutes to closing, which left me seventeen. Nothing new from Chiyo on the lock screen, just the thread I'd left on a thumbs-up.

I opened the HGO Companion App, pinned the toolbox under my arm, and held the drive against the back of the phone.

**[SCANNING...]**

**[DRIVE DETECTED]**

**[LOGGING IN...]**

My account loaded, the goblin face of my avatar staring back at me from a small circle at the top of the screen. *"That's not the face I picked. Not that I picked it."*

**[NYKRI - LEVEL 3 - GOBLIN]**

*"Nykri. Still strange, seeing it written down."*

A banner sat across the top of the home screen before I could swipe on.

**[WORLD QUEST ACTIVE: CALL OF THE BONES]**

That was all it said. No zone, no name. *"They told everyone it started. They didn't say where, or who."* I swiped past it, and my thumb wasn't quite steady.

Near the bottom of the page, under a small header, my spells sat listed in plain text. Goblin Gas. Goblin Flare.

My inventory sat above that.

**[GOBLIN GADGET BELT - COMMON (LOCKED)]**

**[GAMBLER'S EARRING - UNIQUE (EQUIPPED)]**

**[MINOR ELIXIR x2 - UNCOMMON]**

I tapped the elixirs, and the listing opened.

**[MINOR ELIXIR | VALUE: 21,000 SHILLINGS EACH]**

Twenty-one thousand. Shillings, not yen. For a mana potion. Another line sat under it.

**[MARKET REFRESH: OPEN BETA | STANDING BUY ORDERS ACTIVE]**

*"That's why."* Open beta had wiped every shop and stash on the server back to the starting line, and every guild that had been sitting on a wish list since closed beta had buy orders stacked on the market, desperate to get their mages ready early. Next week the prices would settle. Tonight, somebody would pay almost anything.

I confirmed the listing, already bracing to wait. The vials vanished almost immediately.

**[BUY ORDER MATCHED: 2 ITEMS]**

**[SOLD: 42,000 SHILLINGS]**

That was stage one, and it only counted inside the game. Shillings were the money Eryndal ran on, and they didn't leave it. A second prompt rose over the first.

**[EXCHANGE SHILLINGS FOR HGO COINS?]**

**[10 SHILLINGS = 1 HGO COIN]**

I confirmed that too.

**[4,200 HGO COINS RECEIVED]**

Stage two. The coins were the bridge, the one thing the game would let out of its own walls. One more prompt, and it was the one that mattered.

**[CASH OUT HGO COINS?]**

**[1 HGO COIN = ¥4]**

**[BANK ACCOUNT REQUIRED TO COMPLETE CASH OUT]**

I linked the account my WattsUp paychecks already hit, a few taps and a code texted to my phone, and the warning cleared.

**[CASH OUT COMPLETE: ¥16,800 DEPOSITED]**

**[BANK BALANCE: ¥64,120]**

Shillings to coins to yen. Three steps, and nothing taken off the top at any of them. I read the number again and tilted the screen away from the hallway light, half expecting it to take the money back.

It didn't. I stood in the back hallway of Sakura Arcade letting that number do something to my chest that a few hours of getting chased around a goblin camp hadn't managed to. *"First one. Definitely not the last."* A game Chiyo couldn't see wasn't a job, and "I'm just playing" wouldn't survive one question from her. A number in the bank might. Mom's surgery, Momo and Taji, the Tabuchis circling, and for the first time something that could pay every week instead of once.

I backed out of the app and opened Loop on the same screen. My own profile loaded first, follower count still embarrassingly low in the corner. I changed my handle to Nykri to match the game and swapped my face for the goblin, pulled straight off my character sheet. Then I set up a fresh email just for business and filled in the bio I'd left blank for a year: For business or sponsorships, reach out here. That was all I put.

*"If I ever stream this, the name and the face have to already be there."* The Companion App had a Go Live toggle three taps from my character sheet, and the people at the top of the front page hadn't gotten there on gameplay alone. They'd been found. It wasn't a plan yet. It was a door I'd stopped leaving locked.

Then I backed out to the feed, and the feed was already loud.

**[@PK_Wraith: World Quest just went live. First one in months. Y'all better believe we're getting a piece of that.]**

**[@Dosuke_AI: interesting timing for a world quest? curious who actually triggered it]**

*"Dosuke's wondering who triggered it. Dosuke. Wondering about me."* I read his post twice, a cold prickle running up both arms, and then scrolled past a dozen reposts of the same thing. Every thread said it the same way. The announcement had gone out to the whole server and said nothing else, no zone and no name. People were comparing screenshots of the bell and arguing over who it could have been.

*"They don't know where. They don't know who."* My hands had gone cold, and then, slowly, steadier. That wouldn't last forever, but it bought me a few days. A different thread caught me, guild leaders arguing over whether to cash out their old characters yet for Hearthfire's Legacy Point payout.

*"Wild."* *"Not my problem. I'm already starting from the bottom."* I wanted to stay and dig, but the clock at the top of the screen said 9:49 PM, Leon was waiting up front, and I'd already kept him late. I killed the screen.

Leon was at the counter under the warm lantern lamps and the brick pillars, tablet in one hand, the other hand rubbing the bridge of his nose under his glasses. The chairs were already up on the desks along the wall. He set the tablet down when he saw me cross the floor.

"You look like you got hit by a truck." He almost smiled. "Sorry again about the booth thing. How'd it go?"

"Pod held," I said. My legs still weren't all the way mine, and I set the toolbox down and leaned on the counter. "No crashes. Ran longer than I thought it would."

"Good. That's what I wanted to hear, and also what worries me." Leon's smile faded. "Like I said in there, I can't see what you're in on that monitor, and nobody runs long sessions on those pods. If something went wrong, I wouldn't know until you didn't come out." He glanced toward the dark hallway where the second pod sat, then back. "So I want to be straight with you before you start getting comfortable coming back. Business is slow right now, and I can't keep handing out open access if it turns into a habit for both of us."

"How slow are we talking?"

Leon rubbed a hand over his face. "Nobody wants retro setups anymore. Every third-gen cafe in a five-block radius is eating us alive. Bookings, foot traffic, word of mouth, you name it."

"So you're getting cooked on every front." My thumb found the edge of the counter. Leon was being straight with me, and I was standing there on a pod I'd bent the rules on. "Alright. Since we're being real, I've got something you should hear too."

Leon's eyebrows went up. "Go ahead."

"I overrode one of the pods tonight." I kept my voice even, watching Leon's face. "Used my dad's manual. That's what caused the blackout earlier. It wasn't the hardware failing on its own."

Leon went very still. "Is the machine okay?"

"It's fine. Ran the whole session clean after that." I let that land a second before I kept going. "But here's the rest of it. The core in that pod is nearly dead. The bypass is holding it up, and I watched it run hot the entire time I was in there. Your second pod is a shell, not completely dead. If I can get both of them running properly with the right parts, you've got two working HGO pods in a city where every venue is booked out for months."

Leon studied me, something shifting behind his glasses. "You're actually telling me you got HGO running on hardware this old."

"That's why the session ran long. It wasn't the game. It was convincing the pod to accept the drive in the first place."

Leon started to say something, stopped, and shook his head slow instead.

"Here's the problem," I said. "A bypass only keeps it alive for so long. Doing it right needs third-gen cores, and nobody sells those. If they did, anyone could crack one open and copy it. I know where they are, though."

"Where?"

"Tochi."

Leon blinked. His face rearranged itself, quick and visible. "The Tochi Academy."

"Yeah. They're the one school openly partnered with Nexus Enterprises, and their technology lab has them."

"And they'd let a kid walk in and use it?"

"No. Instructors get that access, not students. I'd have to work my way up first."

"That explains a lot, actually."

"Wasn't trying to hide it," I said. "Just wasn't relevant until now."

"So the way you fix my pods is by going back to school."

"Or I stay out, take a job here, and keep patching it by hand until the core finally dies. Which won't take long." I made myself say the rest. "Tochi means a bed on campus and the game on weekends. I'd lose most of my time in the pod."

Leon leaned back against the counter, arms loose, and I watched him put it together. "Okay. Here's what I want to do. I'll get a tech in here tomorrow to look at the monitor and start moving toward proper VRSA clearance, because I can't have just anyone touching that second pod as-is. If you're serious about this, come back around nine, nine-thirty when we open."

"I can do that."

"And here's the deal, so neither of us has to guess later. I'll put you on the books here, part-time, floor hours and repairs. If your guardian asks what you're doing with your days, you work for me." He ticked it off on his fingers. "One of those pods is yours, permanently, whenever you need it. Consider it a sponsorship arrangement, however that usually works for players and their backers. In return, you get both pods running clean and keep them that way, and you get the parts from Tochi when the time comes. And it stays quiet. No posts, nobody hearing we've got working HGO pods here until the clearance is done."

*"A pod, a job I can point at, and the parts."* I turned it over once. *"He gets two working machines. I get a seat I don't have to beg for. Yeah. That's a deal."* A muscle in my cheek twitched as I tried to keep a grin off my face.

"One more thing," Leon said. "My parents have been pushing to get my sister back overseas for months. She won't go partly because of HGO and partly because she won't leave me holding this place when it's this close to going under. If we actually turn this around it would mean a lot to my family and my grandpa's wishes."

"I'll get it done," I said.

I meant it more than the two words let on.

I scratched along my goatee. The thought had felt like a loss a week ago. It didn't feel that way anymore.

"I believe you," Leon said, and flipped the sign on the door.

Outside, my breath misted in the cold. I stowed the toolbox and the bag in the rear case, but before I pulled the helmet on, I opened my thread with Chiyo. Her last message sat near the top, the one about Tochi, with my thumbs-up under it that hadn't answered anything. I typed, deleted it, typed again.

**[Thinking about going back to Tochi.]**

I sent it before I could argue myself out of it. Then I pulled the helmet on, pressed my thumb to the touchscreen, and waited out the half-second scan until the dash lit green and the visor threw the route line across the street. The motor hummed steady under me. It held. *"Good. Hold for me one more night."*

One thing before I went home. The hospital.

I pulled out into the street.

The ride to the hospital ran on muscle memory, the city blurring past in orange and white.

The hospital came up on my left, white-lit and too clean. I parked, took the stairs two at a time, and caught the antiseptic smell before I hit the second floor landing, like my body had already memorized the building.

Mom's room sat at the end of the hall, door half-open the way it always was. I pushed through and let it swing shut behind me.

She looked smaller than I remembered, the blanket lying flat across her where it should have lifted. Monitors beeped quiet and steady beside the bed, the IV drip slow and regular, her face turned toward the window.

I pulled the chair close and sat, elbows on my knees, hands loose between them.

"Hey, Mom." I kept my voice low. "I know it's been a minute."

The monitors kept beeping. Footsteps passed in the hall outside, a nurse saying something I couldn't catch.

"I got into the game," I said. "HGO. Finally. Only took me saving up for a year and hacking an antique pod in someone's back room." I let out a short sound, almost a laugh. "I was going for the Elf Spellforger, the build I drew up, and the power went out mid-creation. Whole thing glitched, and now I'm permanently stuck as a goblin. Can't reset, can't trade out, can't do anything about it."

I leaned back and pushed the loose loc behind my ear where it kept falling.

"But I made some money already. Sixteen thousand eight hundred yen off two elixirs the earring rolled me."

The words came easier here than they did anywhere else.

"Crownfall's two weeks out. Ten million yen on the table. I know how that sounds. I've been in the game one time, a few hours total, and I'm already talking about the prize." I paused. "But everyone's starting fresh characters for the expansion. For once I'm not coming in behind everybody."

I stopped, my throat going tight. I let the quiet sit a second before pushing through it.

"I'm doing this for you. For the surgery. For Momo and Taji. For Chiyo, so she doesn't have to keep covering everything herself." I looked at my hands, turned up and empty between my knees. "I know you'd tell me not to put that on myself. But it's already there. It's been there since Dad died."

The monitors kept their rhythm. The room stayed quiet.

"I'm gonna figure it out. I always do."

I sat there a little longer, then stood and pressed my hand to her shoulder before I headed for the door.

I was halfway down the hall when I heard them. Two voices, low and careful, the kind of careful that meant they weren't meant to carry.

"...six months," one of them was saying. Ms. Sugiyama, the nurse who'd known me by name since Mom was first admitted. "Dr. Inoue was clear. Without surgery soon, the concern isn't just whether she survives. It's whether there's still a mind left to come back to."

I stopped walking.

"Chiyo asked us to keep it quiet," the second nurse said. "She didn't want the family worrying."

"I know," Ms. Sugiyama said. "But that boy deserves to know."

"Not our call."

Their footsteps faded down the opposite hall, and I stood alone in the corridor, hearing the words again in the same order.

*"Six months. And Chiyo knew. She knew and she didn't tell me."*

The anger came fast and clean, hotter than anything else I'd felt all day. Being kept out of the room like a kid who needed shielding while I was out here carrying half the weight myself.

She was trying to protect me. The thought didn't help.

I turned and walked, not toward the exit, just away from the voices, counting the ceiling lights as they passed because it gave my head something to hold. By the sixth one I'd lost count, and the corner came up fast.

The collision knocked the air out of me, a shoulder catching my chest, papers exploding outward in a soft white flurry. A small startled sound came from whoever I'd walked into, and my hands were already moving, catching an elbow, steadying someone before either of us actually went down.

"Sorry, I wasn't," I started, and then I actually looked at her.

Twin buns. Glasses that seemed specifically designed to make her forgettable and were failing completely at the job. She was already crouching to gather pages, hands quick and practiced, and when she looked up at me, something in her face did the exact same double-take mine just had.

Neither of us said anything for a second that ran a beat too long to be nothing.

"It's fine," she said, too fast, like she needed to get ahead of the silence. "I wasn't watching either."

I crouched across from her and started helping stack pages. Her eyes caught on the phone in my other hand, still lit on my HGO profile where I'd been fidgeting with it, the goblin face and my new name glowing in the corner.

"You play?" she asked, tilting her head, a real question.

"Just started." I turned the phone slightly without meaning to.

"Nykri." She read it slow, testing how it sat. Something flickered across her face, amused, like I'd handed her the funniest thing she'd heard all week. "I like it."

We finished stacking the last of the pages in a silence that had stopped being awkward somewhere in the last thirty seconds. I was close enough now to see the seam where her glasses didn't quite sit right on her face, like they weren't originally hers.

She stood first, I followed, the stack of papers changing hands without either of us fully letting go for a second.

"I have to go," she said, and somehow didn't sound entirely like she meant it. She pulled a pen from her jacket pocket, scribbled fast on the corner of one page, tore it free, folded it twice, and tucked it into my jacket pocket herself. Her hand rested against my chest for half a second longer than the motion needed. "My code. Add me sometime."

"Wait," I started.

"Good luck with the game," she said, taking the papers from me and turning, already three steps into the hallway before I'd finished the sentence. "Hope to see you soon."

I watched her go, twin buns bouncing once as she rounded the corner, gone before I thought to actually move after her. I stood there a second longer than made sense, the rest of the papers still in my arms, some part of me already running the last thirty seconds back to confirm it had actually happened.

I set the papers on the nearest chair and unfolded the note.

A friend code sat at the top, a string of letters and numbers in clean, careful handwriting. Beneath it, a date.

December 23rd. 689450YY

*"Two days before Crownfall."* I turned the paper over, checking the back in case something else was written there. Nothing.

I pocketed the note and looked down the empty hall where she'd gone, and the quiet didn't hold. Ms. Sugiyama's voice came back first, then the second nurse's. Six months. Chiyo knew. The two things sat next to each other, the note and the sentence, and neither one would stay separate from the other.

I pulled out my phone.

My thread with Chiyo was still open from outside the arcade. My own message sat at the bottom, *Thinking about going back to Tochi*, still unread on her end, and above it her last one about Tochi with my thumbs-up underneath.

She'd kept this from me because she thought it would break something. She was wrong. I scratched at my goatee once, hard. This was my call.

I slid the phone back into my pocket before continuing out of the building.

*"Tochi. I'm going."*

**[END CHAPTER SEVEN]**
