# Chapter Seven: Consequences in Silence

The shell hissed open, and the bell was still ringing in my head. *"That wasn't just for me. That was a public announcement. The whole server just heard a world quest go live, with my name on it."*

Then the room came back around me one piece at a time. The smell first, cigarette smoke and dust and plastic, the same as when I'd gone under. Then the warm lantern lamps hung low over the brick pillars, the steel beams crossing a dark ceiling, the wood paneling on the far wall. The pod stood around me like a dark tower, cable bundles looping down its sides and a blue glow in the seams, Kazuhaya Tech stamped small near the base.

My vision was blurry, the room smeared and dragging a half-beat behind my eyes every time I moved my head. My stomach swung like I'd stepped off a boat onto a roller coaster, one long lurch and then a drop, and I swallowed twice and let it pass. *"Okay. Rough, but I can work with rough."*

Then my own size hit me. After the goblin I felt enormous. A little over six feet of me hung in a cradle built for a shorter man, shoulders jammed against the padded spine, arms heavy in the cuffs. My locs had worked half out of the tie while I was under, and they lay against the back of my neck and the headrest, weighing more than I remembered.

I hung there until the room stopped turning, my pulse not coming down with the rest of me.

I popped the cuffs and stepped down off the platform and rolled my shoulders until something cracked. *"I'm stretching before I get in that thing next time. For real."*

My bag sat zipped against the nearest brick pillar where I'd shoved it before I went back in, the toolbox packed beside it. Nothing left out.

The pod was another story. Heat rolled off the shell the way it had in the blackout, the cooling fan dragging in its housing with the same dying whine, and the two leads I'd bridged to the wall were warm to the touch. *"I'm surprised I even got through a session."* I dug the laptop out of the bag just long enough to plug into the service port, then packed it away. The core read nineteen percent. *"It was twenty when I first logged in. One session, one point."* If that held, I had maybe twenty logins before it died. I'd fix it long before that.

*"If I want long sessions, it needs a new core."* There were no first-gen cores left. Dad's company had gone down with him, every part pulled after the accident. I rested my palm on the warm casing. He built this thing, and I was a few bad sessions from losing it.

I eased the drive out of the slot beside the headrest, then dragged the bag over, put Dad's manual back in with the letter still inside, and shouldered it. I picked up the toolbox and pulled my phone out on the walk down the back hallway, floorboards creaking under my boots.

9:43 PM. Leon had said twenty minutes to closing, which left me seventeen. Nothing new from Chiyo on the lock screen, just the thread I'd left on a thumbs-up.

I opened the HGO Companion App, pinned the toolbox under my arm, and held the drive against the back of the phone.

**[SCANNING...]**

**[DRIVE DETECTED]**

**[LOGGING IN...]**

My account loaded, the goblin face of my avatar staring back at me from a small circle at the top of the screen. *"That's not the face I picked. Not that I picked it."*

**[NYKRI - LEVEL 3 - GOBLIN]**

*"Nykri. Still strange, seeing it written down."*

A banner sat across the top of the home screen before I could swipe on.

**[WORLD QUEST ACTIVATED: CALL OF THE BONES | ACTIVATED BY: NYKRI]**

My name sat right there on the banner. *"They told everyone who. Not where."* I swiped past it, and my thumb wasn't quite steady.

Near the bottom of the page, under a small header, my spells sat listed in plain text. Goblin Gas. Goblin Flare.

My inventory sat above that.

**[GOBLIN GADGET BELT - COMMON (LOCKED)]**

**[GAMBLER'S EARRING - UNIQUE (EQUIPPED)]**

**[MINOR ELIXIR x2 - UNCOMMON]**

I tapped the elixirs, and the listing opened.

**[MINOR ELIXIR | VALUE: 21,000 SHILLINGS EACH]**

*"Twenty-one thousand shillings for a mana potion? What's that in yen?"* I tapped the number, and a rate card slid up.

**[10 SHILLINGS = 1 HGO COIN]**

**[1 HGO COIN = ¥4]**

I ran it in my head. Eight thousand four hundred yen each. I confirmed the listing, already bracing to wait. The vials vanished almost immediately.

*"That fast? Why?"*

**[BUY ORDER MATCHED: 2 ITEMS]**

*"Buy orders. Somebody had already ordered them."*

**[SOLD: 42,000 SHILLINGS]**

A second prompt rose over the first.

**[EXCHANGE SHILLINGS FOR HGO COINS?]**

I confirmed that too.

**[4,200 HGO COINS RECEIVED]**

One more prompt, and it was the one that mattered.

**[CASH OUT HGO COINS?]**

**[BANK ACCOUNT REQUIRED TO COMPLETE CASH OUT]**

I linked the account my WattsUp paychecks already hit, a few taps and a code texted to my phone, and the warning cleared.

**[CASH OUT COMPLETE: ¥16,800 DEPOSITED]**

**[BANK BALANCE: ¥64,120]**

I stood in the back hallway of Sakura Arcade letting that number do something to my chest that a few hours of getting chased around a goblin camp hadn't managed to. *"First one. Definitely not the last."* A game Chiyo couldn't see wasn't a job, and "I'm just playing" wouldn't survive one question from her. A number in the bank might. Mom's surgery, Momo and Taji, the Tabuchis circling, and for the first time something that could pay every week instead of once.

I backed out of the app and opened Loop on the same screen. My own profile loaded first, follower count still embarrassingly low in the corner. I changed my handle to Nykri to match the game and swapped my face for the goblin, pulled straight off my character sheet. Then I set up a fresh email just for business and filled in the bio I'd left blank for a year: For business or sponsorships, reach out here. That was all I put.

My phone buzzed before I'd finished saving it. Follow alerts, tags, messages from strangers. Everyone who typed Nykri into Loop was landing on me, and a minute ago there'd been nothing here to land on.

I opened the feed. The big hitters already had it loud.

**[@Dosuke_AI: World Quest just popped. London Spire again? End of the beta season, would make sense]**

**[@Yuna_N9: A World Quest has been ACTIVATED, everybody! And it's not London Spire. It's a player called Nykri. Who is Nykri?!]**

**[@PK_Wraith: Who the hell is Nykri? Anybody know who holds that quest, and where? Asking for a few friends.]**

**[@KansaiBlackLotus: Quest holder is Nykri, no guild listed. Anyone with info, send it our way.]**

Eren's guild. I scrolled past it fast.

The whole server knew a World Quest had popped, and every one of them wanted whoever held it. They'd be hunting me by morning. *"Damn it."* My pulse kicked. The game was brand new, the quest clock was running, and I had to be off that pod by ten. Leaving now, with my name on every feed, was the worst timing I could have picked, and I was missing it all standing in a hallway. *"I have to get here early tomorrow. I need longer sessions, and Leon has to agree to them."*

But a name everyone was asking about was also the best leverage I'd ever get. I knew what the top players had pulled out of this game, the streams, the sponsors, the payouts. *"Bait pulls both ways. I just have to be the one holding the line."*

But eyes didn't pay on their own. Gambit hit once a day, and every gamble had a buy-in. The day I crit failed, something would break or blow up on me, and I wasn't building rent on a dice roll. I needed a profile with my loot on display, a face people knew, the email already in the bio. A real stream could wait for the weeks around Crownfall, planned and staged right. The Companion App's front page ran sponsored streams in the six figures. For now I'd farm every login and balance it against the quest clock, and build the brand quietly.

Before I killed the screen I typed *Nexus helmet processor core* into the search bar. In the dark earlier it had given me nothing. Patents and press, every trail pointing back at Nexus Enterprises, and nobody selling one. If anyone could buy one, anyone could crack it open and copy the schematics.

The only thing that tied Nexus Enterprises to anywhere I could reach was a headline from a year ago, Tochi Academy announcing an active sponsorship with them. It said nothing about a processor, or whether one sat in that building. Even then, instructors would hold anything in that lab, not the kids. I'd have to climb: top grades, and a name the way Mom's had been in volleyball and Dad's in the labs.

Copying Nexus wasn't the answer. Dad hadn't copied anyone. *"Why not build my own core, one made for his pod?"* A Kuroshio core could show me how one worked, if I could stomach black market prices, but I'd build for the Kazuhaya pod, not mimic theirs. I couldn't learn that on a laptop in a back room.

The clock said 9:52 PM. Leon was waiting up front, and I'd already kept him late. I killed the screen.

Leon was at the counter under the lantern lamps, tablet in one hand, rubbing the bridge of his nose under his glasses.

"You look like you got hit by a truck. Sorry again about the booth thing. How'd it go?"

"Pod held," I said. I set the toolbox down and leaned on the counter. "No crashes."

"Good. That's what I wanted to hear, and also what worries me." Leon's smile faded. "I can't see what you're in on that monitor, and nobody runs long sessions on those pods. So I want to be straight with you before you start getting comfortable coming back. Business is slow right now, and I can't keep handing out open access if it turns into a habit for both of us."

"How slow are we talking?"

Leon sighed. "Nobody wants retro setups anymore. Every helmet cafe in a five-block radius is eating us alive."

"So you're getting cooked on every front." "Alright. Since we're being real, I've got something you should hear too."

Leon's eyebrows went up. "Go ahead."

"I got HGO running on one of your pods tonight." I kept my voice even, watching Leon's face. "The drive wouldn't seat, so I overrode it with my dad's manual. I'm not saying that's what took the power out earlier. I don't know what did. But I'd be lying if I said it couldn't have been me."

Leon went very still. "Is the machine okay?"

"It's fine. But here's the rest of it. The core in that pod is nearly dead. Your second pod is a shell, not completely dead. If I can get both of them running properly with the right parts, you've got two working HGO pods in a city where every venue is booked out for months."

Leon studied me. "You're actually telling me you got HGO running on hardware this old."

"Barely," I said. "Here's the problem. That core read twenty percent when I first logged in. After one session it's at nineteen."

"So the one session drained it?"

"If I'm going off that, a point a session, which gives me about twenty logins. Maybe fewer. One session isn't much to go on, but I'd fix it long before then."

Leon's jaw worked. "And you can actually fix it?"

"I know my dad's work well enough to keep it running. A Kuroshio core would get it going for now, but I can't buy one the normal way, and on the black market the price is insane. Tabuchi money sits behind Kuroshio too. I'm not putting that in my dad's machine. A Nexus core is a long road away, and nobody sells those. So I build one. A core made for these pods. It would fix your second pod too."

"So where does that leave you?"

"Tochi."

Leon blinked. "The Tochi Academy."

"I can't learn it here. They have the lab, and Nexus sponsors them, so I might even see what a real core is made of. It might lead nowhere, but it's the only lead I've got."

"And they'd let a kid into that lab?"

"No. Instructors get that kind of access. I'd have to work my way up first."

"That explains a lot, actually."

"So the way you fix my pods is by going back to school."

"Or I stay out, take a job here, and keep patching it by hand until the core finally dies. Which won't take long. Tochi means a bed on campus and the game on weekends. I'd lose most of my time in the pod."

Leon leaned back against the counter, arms loose, and I watched him put it together. "Okay. Here's what I want to do. I'll get a tech in here tomorrow to look at the monitor and start moving toward proper VRSA clearance, because I can't have just anyone touching that second pod as-is. If you're serious about this, come back around nine, nine-thirty when we open."

"I can do that. I'll need longer sessions than tonight, though. Open to close, if the pod holds."

"Open to close, with that monitor running and me here," Leon said. "Fine."

"And here's the deal, so neither of us has to guess later. I'll put you on the books here, part-time, floor hours and repairs. If your guardian asks what you're doing with your days, you work for me. One of those pods is yours, permanently, whenever you need it. Consider it a sponsorship arrangement, however that usually works for players and their backers. In return, you get both pods running clean and keep them that way, and you build the core, and learn how at Tochi. And it stays quiet. No posts, nobody hearing we've got working HGO pods here until the clearance is done."

*"A pod, a job I can point at, and the parts. Yeah. That's a deal."*

"One more thing," Leon said. "My parents have been pushing to get my sister back overseas for months. She won't go partly because of HGO and partly because she won't leave me holding this place when it's this close to going under. If we actually turn this around it would mean a lot to my family and my grandpa's wishes."

"I'll get it done," I said.

I scratched along my goatee. The thought had felt like a loss a week ago. It didn't feel that way anymore.

"I believe you," Leon said, and flipped the sign on the door.

Outside, my breath misted in the cold. The hours were painted on the glass of the door. Opens at nine. *"Eleven hours, and by then the whole server's looking for me. Fuck. I'm here when he unlocks it."* I stowed the toolbox and the bag in the rear case, but before I pulled the helmet on, I opened my thread with Chiyo. Her last message sat near the top, the one about Tochi, with my thumbs-up under it that hadn't answered anything. I typed, deleted it, typed again.

**[Thinking about going back to Tochi.]**

I sent it before I could argue myself out of it. Then I pulled the helmet on, pressed my thumb to the touchscreen, and waited out the half-second scan until the dash lit green and the visor threw the route line across the street. The motor hummed steady under me. It held. *"Good. Hold for me one more night."*

One thing before I went home. The hospital.

I pulled out into the street.

The ride to the hospital ran on muscle memory, the city blurring past in orange and white.

The hospital came up on my left, white-lit and too clean. I parked, took the stairs two at a time, and caught the antiseptic smell before I hit the second floor landing, like my body had already memorized the building.

Mom's room sat at the end of the hall, door half-open the way it always was. I pushed through and let it swing shut behind me.

She looked smaller than I remembered, the blanket lying flat across her where it should have lifted. Monitors beeped quiet and steady beside the bed, the IV drip slow and regular, her face turned toward the window.

I pulled the chair close and sat, elbows on my knees, hands loose between them.

"Hey, Mom." I kept my voice low. "I know it's been a minute."

The monitors kept beeping.

"I got into the game," I said. "HGO. Finally. Only took me saving up for a year and hacking an antique pod in someone's back room." "I was going for the Elf Spellforger, the build I drew up, and the power went out mid-creation. Whole thing glitched, and now I'm permanently stuck as a goblin. Can't reset, can't trade out, can't do anything about it."

"But I made some money already. Sixteen thousand eight hundred yen off two elixirs the earring rolled me."

"Crownfall's two weeks out. Ten million yen on the table. I've been in the game one time, a few hours total, and I'm already talking about the prize." I paused. "But everyone's starting fresh characters for the expansion. For once I'm not coming in behind everybody."

I stopped, my throat going tight.

"I'm doing this for you. For the surgery. For Momo and Taji. For Chiyo, so she doesn't have to keep covering everything herself." I looked at my hands, turned up and empty between my knees. "I know you'd tell me not to put that on myself. But it's already there. It's been there since Dad died."

"I'm gonna figure it out. I always do."

I stood and pressed my hand to her shoulder before I headed for the door.

I was halfway down the hall when I heard them. Two voices, low and careful, the kind of careful that meant they weren't meant to carry.

"...six months," one of them was saying. Ms. Sugiyama, the nurse who'd known me by name since Mom was first admitted. "Dr. Inoue was clear. Without surgery soon, the concern isn't just whether she survives. It's whether there's still a mind left to come back to."

I stopped walking.

"Chiyo asked us to keep it quiet," the second nurse said. "She didn't want the family worrying."

"I know," Ms. Sugiyama said. "But that boy deserves to know."

"Not our call."

Their footsteps faded down the opposite hall, and I stood alone in the corridor, hearing the words again in the same order.

*"Six months. And Chiyo knew. She knew and she didn't tell me."*

The anger came fast and clean, hotter than anything else I'd felt all day. Being kept out of the room like a kid who needed shielding while I was out here carrying half the weight myself.

She was trying to protect me. The thought didn't help.

I turned and walked, not toward the exit, just away from the voices, counting the ceiling lights as they passed because it gave my head something to hold. By the sixth one I'd lost count, and the corner came up fast.

The collision knocked the air out of me, a shoulder catching my chest, papers exploding outward in a soft white flurry. A small startled sound came from whoever I'd walked into, and my hands were already moving, catching an elbow, steadying someone before either of us actually went down.

"Sorry, I wasn't," I started, and then I actually looked at her.

Twin buns. Glasses that seemed specifically designed to make her forgettable and were failing completely at the job. She was already crouching to gather pages, hands quick and practiced, and when she looked up at me, something in her face did the exact same double-take mine just had.

Neither of us said anything for a second that ran a beat too long to be nothing.

"It's fine," she said, too fast, like she needed to get ahead of the silence. "I wasn't watching either."

I crouched across from her and started helping stack pages. Her eyes caught on the phone in my other hand, still lit on my HGO profile where I'd been fidgeting with it, the goblin face and my new name glowing in the corner.

"You play?" she asked, tilting her head, a real question.

"Just started." I turned the phone slightly without meaning to.

"Nykri." She read it slow, testing how it sat. Something flickered across her face, amused, like I'd handed her the funniest thing she'd heard all week. "I like it."

We finished stacking the last of the pages in a silence that had stopped being awkward somewhere in the last thirty seconds. I was close enough now to see the seam where her glasses didn't quite sit right on her face, like they weren't originally hers.

She stood first, I followed, the stack of papers changing hands without either of us fully letting go for a second.

"I have to go," she said, and somehow didn't sound entirely like she meant it. She pulled a pen from her jacket pocket, scribbled fast on the corner of one page, tore it free, folded it twice, and tucked it into my jacket pocket herself. Her hand rested against my chest for half a second longer than the motion needed. "My code. Add me sometime."

"Wait," I started.

"Good luck with the game," she said, taking the papers from me and turning, already three steps into the hallway before I'd finished the sentence. "Hope to see you soon."

I watched her go, twin buns bouncing once as she rounded the corner, gone before I thought to actually move after her. I stood there a second longer than made sense, the rest of the papers still in my arms.

I set the papers on the nearest chair and unfolded the note.

A friend code sat at the top, a string of letters and numbers in clean, careful handwriting. Beneath it, a date.

December 23rd. 689450YY

*"Two days before Crownfall."* I turned the paper over, checking the back in case something else was written there. Nothing.

I pocketed the note and looked down the empty hall where she'd gone, and the quiet didn't hold. Ms. Sugiyama's voice came back first, then the second nurse's. Six months. Chiyo knew. The two things sat next to each other, the note and the sentence, and neither one would stay separate from the other.

I pulled out my phone.

My thread with Chiyo was still open from outside the arcade. My own message sat at the bottom, *Thinking about going back to Tochi*, still unread on her end, and above it her last one about Tochi with my thumbs-up underneath.

She'd kept this from me because she thought it would break something. She was wrong. I scratched at my goatee once, hard. This was my call.

I slid the phone back into my pocket before continuing out of the building.

*"Tochi. I'm going."*

**[END CHAPTER SEVEN]**
