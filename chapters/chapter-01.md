# Chapter One: No Resets in Shibuya

The WattsUp station smelled like ozone and cold rain, the kind of smell that got into a person's jacket and stayed there through three washes. Tatsuya Kazehaya leaned against the counter, watching a couple's tiny electric hatchback pull away from bay three, and rubbed some warmth back into his hands. Eleven at night, and Shibuya was still throwing light at the sky, but the block WattsUp sat on was quiet enough that he could hear the charging cables humming in their cradles, a low electric drone under everything else.

Four bays. Two open. One customer left, an old man topping off a delivery scooter who hadn't said a word in twenty minutes, which suited Tatsuya fine.

He checked the register total in his head before he checked it on the screen. Slow night. Slow month, if he was honest, though honesty about money was a habit he'd trained himself into the way other people trained themselves out of biting their nails.

His phone buzzed against the counter. Chiyo.

*Tabuchi called again. Told them the usual. Also, Tochi's application window opens back up Jan 4. Just so you know it exists.*

He read it twice. The first time for the words, the second time for what she wasn't saying, that the calls from the Tabuchi side of things weren't slowing down no matter how many times Chiyo told them the usual, whatever the usual currently was. He typed back a thumbs up, because a thumbs up cost nothing and didn't invite more.

The screen mounted over the register was running the Winter Championship broadcast on mute, captions crawling along the bottom in white block text. He'd had it on all shift, more out of habit than interest, except that wasn't quite true either. He'd been watching the bottom-right corner of the screen since seven, where the score sat like a heartbeat he couldn't stop checking.

London Spire versus Aogiri, game five, and Aogiri's back line was collapsing the way a back line collapses when the shot-calling gets a half-second slow. Tatsuya reached over and thumbed the volume up two clicks, not enough for the old man at bay three to notice, just enough that the crowd noise came through as a real sound instead of a shape on a graph.

*They're rotating wrong,* he thought, watching Aogiri's healer commit to a lane a beat too early. *That's not a mistake you make at this level unless someone above them told them to.* He'd have held that healer back one more count, forced London Spire to show their hand on the point first. It wasn't his call to make. It had never once been his call to make, and some nights that fact sat in his chest like a swallowed stone, and other nights, like tonight, he just watched and did the math anyway, because the math was the only part of it that was still his.

The delivery scooter finished charging with a soft double chime. The old man nodded at him, wordless as ever, and rolled out into the wet street.

Tatsuya was alone with the hum of the cables and the muted roar of a stadium eight thousand kilometers away when London Spire closed it out. Forty million yen and a trophy shaped like a stylized shield, and the camera cut to the winning captain screaming something the captions rendered as *WE'RE HOME*, and Tatsuya felt the old familiar itch behind his sternum, the one that had nothing to do with the money and everything to do with watching people play a game he understood better than most of them and never once getting to touch a controller that mattered.

The broadcast didn't cut to credits. It cut instead to a soundstage draped in silver, and the enormous cartoon bear ambled out from behind a curtain to scattered, delighted applause.

Kazuku. Nobody's real face, just the mascot, round-bellied and grinning, HGO's logo stitched across a little vest that always looked freshly pressed no matter how many times he wore it on camera.

*Congratulations to London Spire, and to every team that fought for that stage tonight,* the captions read, the bear's arms spread wide like he meant to hug the whole stadium. *And now, since you've all been so patient with me...*

A shimmer ran across the screen, gold particles resolving into a shape Tatsuya didn't recognize yet, five landmasses where there had only ever been one.

*Eryndal is growing. The Crystal League expansion arrives this spring. Five new continents. Player-owned cities. A whole new world waiting for whoever's brave enough to claim it first.*

Tatsuya's hand had gone flat on the counter without him deciding to put it there. Five continents. He did the math on that too, out of habit, guild wars over unclaimed land, economies nobody had modeled yet, and felt the itch behind his sternum turn into something closer to hunger.

The bear wasn't done. *And because I want every one of you standing at the starting line when that world opens,* the captions continued, the bear producing a comically oversized drive from behind his back, gold light spilling off it, *for the next seventy two hours only, every HGO access drive is thirty percent off. New players, lapsed players, anyone who's been sitting on the fence. Come find out why they call it Hero's Glory.*

The number sat there in the corner of the screen, and Tatsuya read it twice, same as he'd read Chiyo's text, once for the number and once for what it meant.

Thirty percent off put a drive within actual reach of what was sitting in his account. Not comfortably. Not with room to spare. But within reach, for the first time since the whole world had gone and moved on without him.

*This might be it,* he thought, and the thought scared him a little, the way any thought that mattered always did. *This might be the only door that opens.*

The bell over the door rang, and he looked up expecting nobody, and instead found Eren Tabuchi standing in the entrance in a black team jacket with KANSAI BLACK LOTUS stitched across the chest in silver thread, like the letters themselves were expensive.

Tatsuya felt his face do nothing at all. That was the trick. Do nothing, say nothing you couldn't afford, and let the math run underneath where nobody could see it.

"Didn't know you were still working nights," Eren said, glancing around the station like it was smaller than he remembered, which it probably was, to him. Behind him, a black car idled at the curb, and the driver stayed in his seat, patient as furniture.

"Bay two's open," Tatsuya said, nodding toward it, keeping his voice level. "Card or account."

Eren didn't move toward the bay. He drifted closer to the counter instead, close enough that Tatsuya caught the cologne under the cold night air, something expensive that didn't belong in a place that smelled like ozone and rain. "You watching the broadcast?" Eren asked, tilting his chin at the screen, at the bear still grinning behind fresh captions. "Kansai got bounced two days before the final. Rough tournament."

"Heard," Tatsuya said. He didn't say what he thought about it, that a team built on a stolen build was always going to hit a ceiling the build couldn't push through alone, because saying that cost more than a thumbs up.

At the far end of the counter, the man who'd come in a few minutes earlier to buy a canned coffee, gray Kansai scarf still looped around his neck from some earlier match he'd caught on his phone, went very quiet and very still, the way people go quiet when they realize they're standing inside somebody else's argument.

"My father wanted me to remind you the account's still open," Eren said, and his voice dropped into that lower register he used when he wanted to sound like a man instead of a rich kid in his father's shadow. "In case you'd forgotten."

"I haven't forgotten," Tatsuya said. He kept his hands flat on the counter, weight even, breathing even. "Bay two. Card or account."

The easy look on Eren's face flickered, just for a second, the closest thing to a reaction Tatsuya was going to get. For that second, the old version of this, two kids theorizing builds on graph paper in a cafeteria that didn't exist for either of them anymore, surfaced somewhere behind his eyes and then was gone, replaced by a small, satisfied smile that had nothing warm in it.

"Card," Eren said, and tapped it against the reader without looking away from him. "Tell your mother I hope she's doing better."

He said it soft enough that it could have been kindness, if Tatsuya hadn't known him for eleven years and known exactly what kindness from a Tabuchi cost the person receiving it. Tatsuya didn't answer that one at all. He just watched the charge indicator climb, green light by green light, until Eren finally turned and walked back out to the idling car, the bell over the door singing once behind him.

The man with the scarf peeled himself off the counter and straightened, like he'd only just remembered how to stand normally. "You good?" he asked, tentative, the kind of question a stranger asks when he already suspects the answer is complicated.

"Every night's the same night," Tatsuya said, and managed something close to a smile, the kind that didn't reach anywhere real. The man didn't push, just nodded and went back to his coffee, and Tatsuya let the quiet settle back over the station.

He looked at the screen. The bear was gone. In its place, the discount banner ran on a loop, gold numbers ticking down a countdown clock that had just crossed under seventy two hours.

He pulled his phone out again and opened the account he barely used anymore, the one that still had his real balance sitting in it instead of the fiction he kept in his head to get through a shift without flinching. He did the math three times, because three times was how many times he trusted a number that mattered. Rent covered. Ohema's bill covered, barely, with Chiyo's help doing most of the actual lifting. Groceries for one more week, tight but there.

And underneath all of it, if he moved now, if he didn't think about it any longer than it took to think it, enough left over for a drive.

*Everyone else got a running start,* he thought, thumb hovering over the purchase screen, the cursor blinking against the last of his cushion like it was daring him. *Eren got his the day it launched. I get mine two years late, on a countdown clock, with thirty percent off because the bear needs bodies in his new world.*

*Fine.*

*Late's still in.*

He pressed his thumb down before he could talk himself out of it, and somewhere on a server farm on the other side of the city, a transaction cleared, and a small green confirmation bloomed on his screen like it was nothing at all instead of the biggest thing he'd done in two years.

Outside, the Haya EB was still parked where he'd left it at the start of his shift, and the rain had started up again, fine and cold under the streetlights. Tatsuya killed the register, locked the bays, and stood there a moment longer than he needed to, phone still warm in his hand.

*Now I just have to find somewhere to actually play it.*
