# Chapter One: No Resets in Shibuya

The overhead screen at WattsUp had exactly one setting after dark: HGO, whatever HGO happened to be showing. Gato had explained it to him once, back when Tatsuya was new enough to ask why a charge station needed a screen at all. Nexus Enterprises paid the block's rent on half its signage in exchange for airtime, a partner station, same as half of Shibuya. Tatsuya hadn't asked twice. There wasn't a version of that conversation that ended with him getting to turn it off.

So the Winter Championship played over his shoulder whether he wanted it there or not, and some nights, most nights, that was the whole joke of his life in one sentence: the only screen he couldn't walk away from was the one aimed straight at everything he used to want.

He caught his own reflection in the dark half of the front window between customers, locs pulled back off his face with a frayed elastic, the scar over his right brow doing its usual thing under the fluorescents, catching light at an angle that made it look fresh even eleven years out. He didn't linger on it. Working nights taught you not to.

Eleven o'clock, and the block outside was still throwing neon at a sky that couldn't decide if it planned to rain. Four bays out front, two occupied, cables humming low and electric in their cradles. The rest of the noise was traffic, and under that, the broadcast crowd, muffled through the glass but constant, a stadium eight thousand kilometers away breathing through a speaker bolted to WattsUp's ceiling.

His phone buzzed against the counter. Chiyo.

*Tabuchi called again. Same folder of your dad's old files as always, like that's ever once been about the money. Told them the usual. Also, Tochi's application window opens back up Jan 4, just so you know it exists.*

He read it twice, once for the words and once for the part that itched at him longer, the same folder of old files, two years running, like the debt was somehow beside the point every single time. He didn't know what was in that folder that mattered more than the money owed on it. He typed back a thumbs up, because a thumbs up cost nothing and didn't invite more, and put it out of his head the way he'd trained himself to put things out of his head.

On the screen, London Spire and Aogiri were deep into game five, and Aogiri's back line was folding the way a back line folds when the shot-calling runs a half-second behind the fight. Tatsuya watched without meaning to, the small, ugly satisfaction of being right about something nobody had asked him.

*They're rotating wrong,* he thought, watching Aogiri's healer commit to a lane a beat too early. *That's not a mistake you make at this level unless someone above them told them to.* He'd have held that healer back one more count, forced London Spire to show their hand on the point first. It wasn't his call. It had never once been his call, and some nights that fact sat in his chest like a swallowed stone, heavy and specific, and other nights, like tonight, he just watched and did the math anyway, because the math was the only part of any of this that was still his.

London Spire closed it out at eleven-forty. Forty million yen and a trophy shaped like a stylized shield, and the winning captain screamed something the captions rendered as WE'RE HOME while confetti fell on a stage on the other side of the planet. Tatsuya felt the old itch settle in behind his sternum, the one that had nothing to do with the money and everything to do with understanding a game better than most of the people getting paid to play it, and never once logging in to prove it.

The broadcast didn't cut to credits. It cut to a soundstage draped in silver, and the enormous cartoon bear ambled out from behind a curtain to scattered, delighted applause.

Kazuku. Nobody's real face, round-bellied and grinning, HGO's logo stitched across a vest that always looked freshly pressed no matter how many times he wore it on camera.

*Congratulations to London Spire, and to every team that fought for that stage tonight,* the captions read, the bear's arms spread wide like he meant to hug the whole stadium. *And now, since you've all been so patient with me...*

Gold light bloomed across the screen and resolved into five landmasses where there had only ever been one.

*Eryndal is growing. The Crystal League expansion arrives this spring. Five new continents. Player-owned cities. A whole new world for whoever's brave enough to claim it first.*

Tatsuya's hand had gone flat on the counter without him deciding to put it there. Five continents. Guild wars over land nobody had priced yet, economies nobody had built a model for. The itch behind his sternum sharpened into something closer to hunger.

The bear wasn't finished. *And because I want every one of you standing at the starting line when that world opens,* the captions continued, a comically oversized drive appearing in the bear's paws, gold light spilling off it, *for the next seventy two hours only, every HGO access drive is thirty percent off. New players, lapsed players, anyone who's been sitting on the fence. Come find out why they call it Hero's Glory.*

Tatsuya read the number twice, same as he'd read Chiyo's text. Thirty percent off put a drive inside actual reach of what was sitting in his account. Not comfortably. Within reach.

*This might be it,* he thought, and the thought scared him a little, the way any thought that mattered always did. *The one door that's actually open.*

Headlights swept across the front window, and a black car eased up to the curb outside bay two. Tatsuya's stomach dropped before his brain caught up to why, the particular make of car, the particular unhurried way it parked like it owned the block.

Eren climbed out first, black team jacket zipped to the throat, KANSAI BLACK LOTUS stitched across the chest in silver thread that caught the streetlight like it was trying to be noticed. The passenger door opened a second later and Haruto Murayama unfolded out of it, still in half his practice kit, warmup jacket over the shoulder pads, a face Tatsuya had watched on three different broadcasts this season alone. Rookie of the Year. Kansai's tank. The one player on that roster who actually earned his spot.

Tatsuya's face didn't do much. Some small muscle around his mouth flattened out, the closest thing to a reaction he was willing to spend. *Do nothing you can't afford. Let the math run where nobody can see it.*

"Didn't know you were still working nights," Eren said, glancing around the station like it was smaller than he remembered, which it probably was, to him. Behind him, the driver stayed in his seat, patient as furniture.

"Bay two's open," Tatsuya said, nodding toward it, voice level. "Card or account."

Eren didn't move toward the bay. He drifted closer to the counter instead, close enough that Tatsuya caught the cologne under the cold night air, something that didn't belong anywhere near a place that smelled like ozone and rain. "You watching the broadcast?" Eren asked, tilting his chin at the screen, at the bear still grinning behind fresh captions. "We got bounced two days before the final. Rough tournament."

"Heard," Tatsuya said, and left it there. He didn't say what he actually thought, that a team built on a stolen build always hit a ceiling the build alone couldn't push through, because saying that out loud cost more than a thumbs up ever did.

Behind Eren, Haruto had gone quiet, eyes moving between the two of them with the specific stillness of a guy doing math of his own.

"My father wanted me to remind you the account's still open," Eren said, voice dropping into that lower register he used when he wanted to sound like a man instead of a rich kid standing in his father's shadow. "In case you'd forgotten."

"I haven't forgotten," Tatsuya said. He kept his hands flat on the counter, weight even, breathing even. "Bay two. Card or account."

Something in Eren's easy expression flickered, just for a second, and for that second the old version of this, two kids theorizing builds on graph paper in a cafeteria that didn't exist for either of them anymore, surfaced somewhere behind his eyes and was gone, replaced by a small, satisfied smile with nothing warm in it.

"Card," Eren said, tapping it against the reader without looking away from him. "Tell your mother I hope she's doing better."

He said it soft enough that it could have passed for kindness, if Tatsuya hadn't known him eleven years and known exactly what kindness from a Tabuchi cost the person receiving it. Tatsuya didn't answer that one at all. He watched the charge indicator climb, green light by green light, until Eren finally turned back toward the car.

Haruto lingered a beat longer than he needed to, gaze flicking once to the drive-shaped outline of Tatsuya's phone still face-up on the counter, the discount banner glowing on the lock screen where he hadn't quite put it away. Something crossed his face that wasn't quite recognition and wasn't quite respect, closer to a question he wasn't going to ask out loud tonight. He gave the smallest possible nod, more to himself than to Tatsuya, and followed Eren out.

The bell over the door sang once and went still.

Tatsuya stood there a moment in the quiet the car left behind, hum of the cables the only sound left in the place. He looked at the screen. The bear was gone. In its place, the discount banner ran on a loop, gold numbers ticking down a countdown that had just crossed under seventy two hours.

He pulled up the account he barely used anymore, the one with his real balance in it instead of the fiction he kept in his head to get through a shift without flinching. He ran the math three times, because three times was how many times he trusted a number that mattered. Rent covered. Ohema's bill covered, barely, with Chiyo doing most of the actual lifting. Groceries for one more week, tight but there.

And underneath all of it, if he moved now, if he didn't think about it any longer than it took to think it, enough left over for a drive.

*Everyone else got a running start,* he thought, thumb hovering over the purchase screen, cursor blinking against the last of his cushion like it was daring him. *Eren got his the day it launched. I get mine two years late, on a countdown clock, thirty percent off because the bear needs bodies in his new world.*

*Fine.*

*Late's still in.*

He pressed his thumb down before he could talk himself out of it. Somewhere on a server farm across the city a transaction cleared, and a small green confirmation bloomed on the screen like it was nothing at all instead of the biggest thing he'd done in two years.

Outside, the rain had started up again, fine and cold under the streetlights. Tatsuya killed the register, locked the bays, and swung a leg over the Haya EB where it sat parked out back, old paint gone matte in patches, the touchscreen between the handlebars flickering awake under his thumb the way it always did, half a second of hesitation before it trusted him.

He pulled out onto wet asphalt and let Shibuya blur past in long smears of color, rain needling cold against his jaw, the hum of the engine climbing under him as he opened it up down an empty stretch. Some track was playing low through the scooter's speaker, something he'd added to a queue weeks ago and forgotten, a girl's voice riding over synth that built and built and never quite broke, and he didn't know the name of it, didn't think to check, just let it run under the wind and the wet street and the countdown still ticking somewhere in his pocket.

*Seventy two hours,* he thought, taking the turn toward home with the city lights streaking gold across the visor. *Now I just have to find somewhere to actually stand in it.*
