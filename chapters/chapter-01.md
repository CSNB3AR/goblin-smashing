# Chapter One: The Countdown Starts

Only me left on shift at WattsUp this late. I worked a rag along the counter in slow, even strokes over the same stretch of laminate, the rag dragging with a faint squeak, my shoulder stiff from the angle.

The counter had been clean for an hour already. Outside, the canopy lights hummed over three empty charge bays, the cables hanging slack from their housings, the tall pylon sign out front cycling through ad after ad against a sky gone fully dark. Past the old gas pumps standing dark at the far end of the lot and the string of little flags along the canopy's edge, Shibuya kept doing what Shibuya did at midnight, every sign lit, the crossings still crowded, a thump of bass carrying faintly across the lot.

An electric sedan had pulled into the middle bay a little after eleven, a man in a rumpled suit behind the wheel thumbing his phone while it took its charge with a soft double chime. He never came inside. Hardly anyone did anymore, with the bays taking cards and the app doing the rest. I couldn't remember the last time anyone had pulled up to one of those pumps, hoses coiled and dusty, and the motor cars were fading out of Japan the way retro games had once VR took over, and WattsUp had been quick to rip out the signs and call itself a charging chain. The last person I'd known to keep feeding one was Dad, whose hybrid still drank gas, hand controls worn smooth under his palms, and I pushed that thought back where it lived and went back to the counter. I squinted up at the fluorescent light bulbs buzzing overhead and rolled my neck until something popped.

The register drawer ticked in the quiet, a number I could have recited without looking. Gato was a good boss and the job was steady, and steady covered rent and groceries and not much else. I tried not to do the other math.

I caught myself in the dark half of the window between the counter and the door and looked longer than I meant to. Locs pulled back off my face with an elastic gone soft from reuse, a few loose ones falling free at my temple no matter how many times I tucked them back. The scar over my right brow, thin and pale, catching the fluorescent light at an angle that made it look almost fresh even eleven years out from the car accident that put it there. A Black face under the store lighting, my mother's dark brown skin gone sallow, the tired showing mostly around my eyes. I had my mother's height, six foot three of long legs and long arms, and my father's hands, steady and precise, the kind that fixed things instead of breaking them, and some nights working nights like this that felt like its own kind of inheritance I wasn't sure I'd earned yet.

My phone buzzed against the counter, the vibration loud enough to cut through the hum of the store. Chiyo's name lit up the screen.

**[Tabuchi called again. Told them the usual. The lawyers are handling it, so don't worry if anything turns up at the hotel, I've got it. Also, Tochi's submission window closes on the 23rd and the entrance exams are Jan 4, I'm gonna need a response soon, Tatsu.]**

I read it twice, once for the words and once for what she hadn't bothered spelling out. Chiyo called it ordinary debt collection, same as always, and mentioned the lawyers like it was nothing. I'd stopped being sure it was ordinary a while ago. The demands kept circling back to my father's old paperwork specifically, in a way that never fully squared with "just pay us."

*"Dad wouldn't want her fighting this battle for him."* My chest tightened. *"This wasn't hers to carry. Never was."*

I typed back a thumbs-up emoji, because a thumbs-up cost nothing and didn't invite follow-up questions I didn't have answers for yet, and flipped my phone face-down on the counter.

The screen mounted over the register ran what it always ran, HGO, and I hadn't picked it. All day it had sat at the edge of my eye, a bright rectangle I'd trained myself not to look at, the same logo, the same clips, the same voices selling the same thing. The WattsUp Corporation had bought a sponsorship slot on the game, the same as a dozen other small companies trying to get their names onto one of the biggest titles out there while it was still locked in closed beta, and the company's stock had climbed with the game every week since. So corporate had every screen in every store looping it. Gato had stopped complaining about it a month ago. Most nights that meant some replay or guild footage playing quietly behind me while I worked, and I kept my back to it. I'd gotten good at that. Tonight corporate had turned the volume up on the whole chain for the last battle of the closed beta, the one they'd been pushing all week, and it was getting harder to talk myself out of listening.

Then Yuna's bright, familiar voice rose over the loop, riding the crowd noise, calling the fight, loud enough that I quit pretending I wasn't listening. My rag stopped mid-stroke.

"Aogiri is pinned inside that fortress, folks, and the Knights of the London Spire are not letting up!" Her voice pitched up, riding the crowd roar underneath. "For anyone just tuning in, I'm Yuna, and this is the last battle of Season Zero, live from Singapore! The Knights sit on 10,500 season points, Aogiri trails by 250, and the fight for Thornwatch is worth 300. Thornwatch is the newest zone in the beta, a walled city on the edge of the snowy plains, outside the Kingdom of Lunarest, and the gate to the port city beyond it. With the season this close to the end, Aogiri has to win this one, and the Knights only have to hold. And with two hours left on the clock, a seventy-two-hour death lock means anyone who falls is gone for good. Three days of them have ground this down to twenty Knights against twelve Aogiri!"

"And that's what makes Hero's Glory Online different from anything else out there!" Yuna went on, the crowd roaring back up under her. "It's a sandbox VRMMORPG, so a lot of it is built by the players. The NPCs are alive, the quests and raids never stop, and this is still only the closed beta, so there is so much more to come. You can take this game down a lot of different routes, even a unique one like mine!"

"And look at who they're marching for!" Yuna said, the crowd swelling under her. "London Spire carries Prince Dorrin's banner, Aogiri carries Prince Maldren's, and Prince Ilyan sits this one out in the royal city. The king is sick, the princes are on the move, and this is the first time their guilds have clashed head to head over a city, right at the edge of the map where those trailers teased more lands! It's the biggest world quest anyone has cracked open so far, nobody knows where it ends, and tonight it decides the season! Thank you, HGO, for having me host this one, and thank you to every fan who has followed my music this far. New song coming soon!"

Yuna. The name surfaced automatically, tied to Momo's whole personality lately, her voice blasting through a closed bedroom door most nights, singing along to tracks Yuna had performed in some virtual space in HGO. I didn't have the energy to be impressed tonight, but some habits didn't need energy to run, and my eyes tracked back to the screen before I'd decided to look.

The broadcast cut to a wide shot of a border fortress city sunk in the snow, thick walls and squat towers around gray stone houses and a city hall with a banner snapping on its steps, small enough to cross in ten minutes on foot. Smoke hung low over the roofs. Along the bottom of the frame the overlay read **ZONE: CONTESTED**, and the WattsUp lightning bolt sat on the sponsor bar right there between two logos I didn't know, and I almost laughed. I squinted against the store lights glaring off the glass and dug the heel of my hand into one eye, like that would make me stop looking. It didn't.

"And look at how the Knights are playing it," Yuna said, her voice dropping low and quick, the way it did when she was about to say something good. "They've cloaked their whole line in enchantments and gone to ground inside those walls in small cells, each one covering the gate from a different angle, hit and fade, hit and fade. Guerrilla warfare. That's why twenty against twelve is the real number, folks, because nobody coming in from outside changes it. Every Aogiri reinforcement who runs that gate is a kill, and every kill is six more for London. Behind the walls, Aogiri's twelve, a guild leader, two officers and nine hands, have dug into the city hall as their fort, and nobody's burning that down. But they're bleeding out. And in Eryndal the magic isn't for show, so don't let the glowing wards and fancy gear fool you into thinking it's cosmetic."

My thumb dragged along the counter's edge. *"Hit and fade. At least somebody over there knows what they're doing."*

Past the fortress the plains rolled out white toward the one pass that led back to Lunarest, and that was where the fight was really being won, and not in the open. The Knights of the London Spire had pulled back into the walls themselves, twenty of them tucked into shuttered houses and the shadow of the gatehouse, and they weren't holding a line at all. They were waiting.

Every Aogiri tag that came through the gate walked into a street the Knights had already built a kill zone for. The cam kept cutting to it. A lone Spellforger in midnight blue stepped off the road onto packed snow, and bolts and fire came out of three windows at once and folded him before his boots cleared the gatehouse. That was one more body out for seventy-two hours. The cam pulled back and I could see the whole shape of it, every window a firing slot, every alley a cut-off, the street a funnel with nowhere to run but forward.

That was the whole fight, and it was working. Aogiri's leader was stuck inside with eleven others, pinned in the city hall and a little house beside it with their banner on the hall steps and his name pinned on the overlay as its guardian, and he wasn't well. The overlay flagged it in small green letters beside his name, **POISONED**, a poison already ticking, eating a little health every few seconds, and nothing inside the walls cleared it. Every reinforcement who died at the gate went on the seventy-two-hour lock, and with two hours left that meant out of the fight for good, and still they kept throwing themselves at the same gate with the same kill zone waiting, one at a time. London's own leader was guarding their flag too, but from a tent set back from the gate, with the one way in sealed. Nobody had to charge in and finish the twelve. The Knights only had to keep the gate bleeding and let the poison do the work.

I hated how clean the read came. It was the tactics. It had always been the tactics, the reason I'd loved this game in the first place, who had the ground and who had the clock and where a fight actually broke, back when Eren and I spent whole nights drawing builds on graph paper and arguing over how a fight should be played. That was exactly why I kept my back to it now. It was still in there, and I hated that it was still in there. I dragged a hand back through my locs, a loose one slipping free again, and made myself breathe.

*"And they were playing like boneheads."* The leader had one real choice and the poison was eating it. Aogiri's Shadowveils were the only class that could slip an antidote down the narrow way into the fortress, and every one of them had died early in the fight, so nobody was coming in. That left him two plays. He could hand the flag to someone inside and go meet a runner with the antidote outside the walls, or he could break out with the eleven while a wave hit the gate from the other side at the same moment, so the Knights had to choose a front. Neither was clean. The overlay showed the flag's guardian by name, so the second he handed it off London would see it, know he was moving, and swarm whoever held it, and both his officers were DPS who wouldn't last under that. But sitting on the steps was the only play that couldn't win. The reinforcements outside were worse. Every one that died was out for the rest of the fight, and they kept feeding the gate one by one instead of massing and going in together. The gate was London's single point of failure too, one input, one output, no second line, the kind of thing my father would have glanced at and asked where the redundancy was, and the Knights were thin there. Every minute the leader sat on the steps was a minute the poison took from his health bar, and the flag was his to guard. If he went, it went.

Aogiri's leader didn't move. He sat on the hall steps with his shield across his knees and watched his own health tick down, one tick, then another, calling out to no one, waiting for a wave that kept dying at the gate before it ever reached him. The cam lingered on his face, a big man with his jaw set like he meant to outlast the poison on stubbornness alone. My hands went flat on the counter.

"Pick something," I said out loud, low, to no one, already knowing how this ended, and I hated that I'd said it. I turned my back on the screen and went back to wiping the same spot I'd already cleaned twice tonight. "Break out or run. Do something."

Behind me, sound cracked out of the speaker, sharp electronic hits stacking fast over each other, a double-kill chime that made the crowd explode, and I winced as the volume spiked. Then the roar changed. Something had gone down on the hall steps.

"His health bar's gone! Aogiri's leader is down, and the banner goes with him!" Yuna's voice cut through the noise, half-shouting. "The zone belongs to the Knights alone, and the ten-minute clock is running!"

I didn't turn around for any of it. I wiped the same spot a third time and a fourth, listening to the crowd climb as the minutes bled off, a long dull groan rolling through it every time another Aogiri tag tried the gate and didn't make it. There was nobody left to contest the zone. Then came one low, resonant tone, the kind that meant something had just ended for good, and I already knew what it sounded like when a hold timer ran out.

"That's game!" Yuna's voice cut through the eruption. "London Spire takes Season Zero, forty million yen and the trophy, in front of a sold-out house here in Singapore and however many millions of you are watching from home!"

I let myself turn back around just in time to catch the confetti falling, gold and white streaming down over the stage. *"Tough, losing like that."* I felt nothing about it.

*"Forty million."* The thought went exactly where it always went. *"Even a tenth of that pays for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly covering gaps she thinks I don't notice. No more Tabuchi calls."*

The broadcast didn't cut to credits. It cut to a soundstage draped in silver and blue, and the enormous cartoon bear ambled out from behind a curtain to real, delighted applause, both in the arena and, faintly, from the little speaker Gato kept behind the counter for his news when he worked the front.

Kazuku. Nobody knew his real face. Just the bear, round-bellied and grinning, HGO's compass-rose logo stitched across a vest that always looked freshly pressed no matter how many times he wore it on camera. He stood on a stage animated through holographic projection tech Singapore had been pioneering for years, his avatar as real to the crowd there as if he'd walked out in person.

"Congratulations to London Spire," the bear said, arms spread wide like he meant to hug the whole arena, his voice warm and genuine in a way that always made me suspicious. "And to every guild that fought for that stage tonight. You've reminded the world why we do this."

He paused, let the applause roll through and fade, then his expression shifted into something brighter.

"And now, since you've all been so patient with me."

Gold light bloomed across the screen behind him, a map pattern I recognized a half-second before my brain caught up. Not one continent anymore but five. Five separate landmasses where there had only ever been one, each one detailed enough that I could see mountain ranges and coastlines rendered in fine, deliberate strokes.

"Eryndal is growing," Kazuku said, and the arena noise swelled again, louder this time, people on their feet. "The Hearthfire expansion arrives January 1st. Five new continents, each one favoring the people born to it. Kingdoms and cities you'll build and defend yourselves, in a world where the people already living there notice exactly who you are and what you've done. Earn their trust, or don't. And before the real wars come, we're opening a new way to fight. Siege. Five guilds, one neutral zone, every guild starting with nothing but a camp and a flag. Build it, hold it, outlast the rest. And for the players who would rather fight for sport than for land, there's a league waiting for you when the season starts."

My weight shifted forward slightly, like I could lean into the screen and see the map more clearly if I just got close enough. Then I caught it and stepped back, and found the counter's edge with my hand again.

"And to christen it," the bear went on, "we'll be hosting the Crownfall Siege on Christmas Day, in a neutral zone nobody owns. Twenty of your guild on the field, open to every new character, with ten million yen on the table." The number bloomed in gold behind him, **¥10,000,000**, and the arena roared for it. He reached behind his back and produced an oversized access drive, the kind of theatrical prop that only worked because he was ten feet tall and made of CG, gold light spilling off it. "Open beta starts tonight, and Crownfall opens Season One. Everything you earn from the moment you log in counts toward the season. Points are yours alone, but Crownfall is a guild event, so bring your guild. And whether you're stepping into Eryndal for the first time, or you've been circling the idea of setting up shop in the new economy, bring your business here. There's never been a cheaper way in. For the next seventy-two hours only, every HGO access drive and every rig is fifty percent off. And about your existing avatar, every closed-beta character locks at its level once the expansion launches. Legacy Points waiting to be spent or cashed out on your terms, and once you're on a new avatar the old one is retired."

The number appeared on-screen beneath him, clean white text against the dark: **50% OFF - 72 HOURS ONLY**.

I read it twice, the same way I'd read Chiyo's text, making sure the number was real and not something my exhausted brain had invented to torment me.

*"Half off. For three days."* I did the math automatically, the same way I did every math problem that involved money I didn't have. If I bought it right now, there was enough. Barely. And ten million yen on the table for one night, from the one game on earth I'd sworn I'd never touch.

I shook it off. *"Not tonight, and not this game. Tonight is a shift, tomorrow is another one, and a sale is only a sale. I've watched people burn their savings on less."*

My thumb found the edge of the counter and pressed there, hard enough that my nail went white, a loose loc slipping across my face that I didn't bother pushing back. My mind kept running the number anyway, a stubborn little machine that never knew when to quit, and I made it stop.

*"It's not like I'm making bank here."* Gato was a good guy, but this job had a ceiling and I'd hit it six months ago. Chiyo was covering more than she should, Mom's bills weren't getting smaller, and the Tabuchis weren't going to stop calling just because we kept ignoring them. If I didn't do something, Chiyo was going to make the Tochi decision for me, and I'd be walking back into that school with my tail between my legs. I scrubbed a hand down my face, my palm dragging over eyes gone dry and gritty under the store lights.

I wasn't going to gamble the little I had on a game I couldn't even stand to hear. I picked the rag back up and told myself that was the end of it.

Red-gold headlights swept across the front window and my stomach dropped half a second before I understood why. A black car eased up to the curb outside the charge bays, slow and certain.

Through the windshield I could just make out a figure in dark clothes, hands steady on the wheel as the car powered down.

The driver's door swung open first, and blonde hair caught the streetlight before anything else registered. Black team jacket zipped to the throat, a silver lotus crossed with two staffs stitched into the chest, the emblem catching the light.

*"Eren."*

**[END CHAPTER ONE]**
