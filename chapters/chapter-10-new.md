# Chapter Ten: Silence and Fire, Part One

The first thing I noticed when I logged back in wasn't Ru. It was the dark.

The tent had gone deep while I was out, the single oil lamp guttered low, shadows covering the corners it used to just touch. I blinked against the dim canvas ceiling, the ache of full re-entry sharper than it should've been for how short the gap actually was. *"Ten minutes. That's all I was gone."* It didn't feel like ten minutes in here.

The smell hit before my eyes finished adjusting. Smoke, not the usual rotting-camp kind, but something sharper underneath, the bite of burned material rather than cook fire. My body turned toward the tent entrance before the thought caught up, and I stopped it. *"Gear first. Then whatever's out there."*

Two items resettled on me as the game registered my full presence. The Gambler's Earring found my right ear again, the carved dice pressing against my lobe in a way I'd half-forgotten across the session out. The Call of the Bones necklace landed a moment behind it, warmth returning low against my sternum, the bone pendant swinging once and going still. The leather chest plate was already there from before the log-out, buckles sitting loose. I found the straps and cinched them down, the worn leather pulling snug against my ribs on the second try.

**[COMMON GOBLIN LEATHER CHEST]**
**[+1 ENERGY] [COMMON]**

I rolled one shoulder, testing the fit. All three items together carried real weight on this frame, but nothing pinged encumbrance. I adjusted the belt, and the whole kit settled. This place had not waited on me.

The blue UI materialized without being called, expanding outward from a point in the tent's dim air. A 3D model of my character rendered fully in place, lit from the inside out, turning the canvas around me a dim electric blue. The title rendered gold where the rest of the display ran blue-white, set apart from everything else. I hadn't known it would do that.

**[CHARACTER: TDRAG]**
**[RACE: SHIV GOBLIN]**
**[LEVEL: 7]**
**[TITLE: THE FIRST DESCENDANT OF SHIV]**

**[HP: 65/65] [SP: 32/32] [MP: 32/32]**

**[STATS]**
**[COURAGE: 4] [POWER: 4] [WISDOM: 8 (13)]** +5 Call of the Bones (Necklace)
**[SPEED: 9] [FORTUNE: 15 (18)]** +3 Gambler's Earring
**[ENERGY: 4]**

Below the primary stats, a second layer I hadn't seen before, the secondary lines that primary discipline investment actually produced:

**[PHYSICAL AP: 22] [MAGIC AP: 52]**
**[RANGED AP: 31] [ACCURACY: 33]**
**[CRIT RATE: 36%] [CRIT MULTIPLIER: 2.1x]**
**[PHYSICAL EVASION: 27] [MAGIC EVASION: 26]**
**[PHYSICAL CC RESIST: 8%] [MENTAL RESIST: 8%]**

I'd seen columns like this before. Taji had them running on his phone at dinner half the time, arguing with the screen about stat weightings while Momo scrolled past Yuna's latest HGO clip on the other side of the table, and I'd tuned it out every time. The shape was the same, but I didn't know what the numbers meant for a level-seven goblin running a kit I'd never planned to build. *"Taji could read this in his sleep. I can't read it awake."* Hearing him argue over somebody else's build and reading my own were two completely different things.

"Leon." I said it while still looking at the display. "Secondary stats just showed up on my sheet. I've seen this layout on Taji's phone but I don't know what I'm actually reading for my build."

A sound came through on the line, brief and mechanical, then the voice right behind it.

**[OPERATOR'S DESK — LEON]**
**[EXTERNAL COMMS ACTIVE]**

"Yeah, what's up?" Easy, unhurried.

I read off the lines one at a time, labels and numbers, and added nothing to them.

Keys on Leon's end, the sound of someone opening a new tab. "Physical AP." A pause, longer than the others, reading as he went. "Power and Speed combined, weighted toward Power." More keys. A longer pause. "I think. Hold on, let me double-check that." The keys went faster, then stopped. "Yeah. Power-weighted. Your twenty-two is low. Power at four is why. Not moving without a discipline point in it."

I looked down at my hands, the claws small and dark against the blue light, and curled them into my palms.

"Magic AP," I said.

"That one I actually know." The dry quality in Leon's voice shifted into something that had real weight behind it. "Arya ran full caster for her whole guild. Three hours before their first skirmish going over her skill trees. I didn't ask to know this stuff, but I ended up knowing it anyway. Magic AP is wisdom-driven. It's the caster number, the one everything scaled off of in her kit." Keys. "Fifty-two with the necklace bonus. That's real output. That's your actual game plan, if I had to guess."

A faint warmth pulsed behind my sternum where the necklace sat. *"Fifty-two. Finally, a number I can work with."*

"Crit Rate," I said.

"Fortune-based." Less certain now, the easy authority pulling back into something more careful. "I saw it in some of her notes, but she wasn't running a crit build so I didn't dig into it. Thirty-six with the earring sounds right, but don't hold me to the exact formula." More keys, a different quality to them, something he was clearly pulling up for the first time. "Evasion I'm less sure on. Especially for goblin specifically. Give me a second." The wait stretched longer than the others. "Physical Evasion's Speed. I'm pretty sure. Magic Evasion comes from Wisdom. Twenty-seven and twenty-six at your level." A pause. "I think that's functional. Not going to absorb hits, but you've got room to work with. Maybe."

My gaze settled on the last line. I already had a bad feeling about it.

"CC Resist," I said.

"Yeah." Leon's voice didn't change register, the same dry quality he used for things that were true and uncomfortable. "Physical CC Resist is Power and Courage combined. Both of yours are at four. Eight percent is about what that math gets you. I'm pretty confident on that one because Arya complained about the same number on her first build."

"So if anything gets in close and lands a root." The answer was already there. I said it aloud anyway, working through it the way I worked through anything I'd only ever seen other people deal with.

"You're going to feel the full duration," Leon said. "That I'm sure about."

I braced a hand on the tent's center post, the wood cold under my palm. *"Glass frame. A real magic ceiling, physical output going nowhere without points I don't have, evasion as the only answer to anyone who closes the distance."* Any root that landed would hold for its full length.

I ran the picture once and let it settle. It wasn't the strong, balanced build I'd gone in wanting. Everything I'd seen of this game was balanced, always covering weaknesses, always built to survive the moments in between, and this build didn't have an in between. It was all ceiling and no floor. *"Squishy as hell."* But if I could stay out of reach and keep the magic output going, that was the build, and the evasion was the entire defense plan.

"Pretty much," Leon said when I said it aloud. "Goblins aren't designed to eat hits. At least, that's what the wiki says. I've never actually seen one in a real fight to compare against."

"Neither have I. Nobody runs goblin. Which means nobody knows what this looks like when it actually works, either."

Below the stats, on the same display, a second layer resolved into view: a full outline of myself rendered in the same holographic light, each piece of gear sitting exactly where it actually sat on my body. The necklace glowed steady at my collar. A single earring caught light at one ear. The chest plate showed solid, real weight to it. A belt sat cinched at my waist, unlit, worn but empty. Everything else, the second accessory slot, both legs, my boots, my gauntlets, my helmet, both weapon slots at my sides, sat outlined in dim, unlit gray. Eleven slots and I had four of them filled, and none of the four were a weapon.

I'd argued builds with Eren on our tablets for months like they were an abstraction. It had never once been my own hand. Theory and practice weren't the same conversation, and right now theory was all I had.

*"It's a deck. Four cards down, the rest still somewhere in the pile."* What was down right now wasn't a fighting hand, it was a support hand. If I went in swinging, I was doing maybe six damage with my fists before something with actual armor put me down, or that was how I thought it worked, since I'd never punched anything in this game. My claw went to my chin to scratch the goatee that should have been there and found smooth green skin instead.

I reached out and tapped the necklace itself. A small note unfolded beside it: World-tier, organized guild events such as Crownfall apply a scaling reduction for balance. I'd half-heard that somewhere before, some aside on the WattsUp screen. Yuna's voice, the same voice Momo played on repeat through the apartment walls, explaining how even the strongest items got brought down to earth once a guild fight was organized. Something about the cadence of it snagged on a memory I couldn't place and let go again before I could chase it. Real power, just not unlimited power everywhere it went.

I looked at my ability bar. The gas icon sat beside the flare, the same two icons from my first real kill, back when I'd dropped the gas and lit it on instinct without knowing what either ability actually did. That order had snagged somewhere at the back of my head since. The gas stacked something on targets. The flare ignited. I didn't know what they added up to together, just that the combination hadn't let go of me. I wasn't going to force it into something before it was ready. Eren used to do that, commit to a combo he didn't fully understand and eat the consequences.

A strand of my new hair fell across my eyes and I shoved it back with the flat of my hand. I dismissed the display, and the blue light folded back into nothing, leaving the tent the way it had been.

I dropped my hand from the post. "Two other things. If you haven't caught it on the monitors, the camp took a hit while I was out. I don't have the full picture yet, but it's not good. And when you get a chance, whatever you can find on the Book of Veil."

"Got it." Leon's tone shifted, the easy quality tightening up around something more focused. "I'll send notes on the Veil when I have something solid. Camp damage I can see from here."

"Bet." I glanced toward the tent flap, toward the gray haze visible through the gap. "One more thing. Can you check if HGO's dropped any patch notes or updates? A lot of what's happening with this garrison system, this World Quest, it all feels like new space. Someone told me we might be in a rare window." I didn't name her. "I want to know if the update is still rolling out."

"Give me a sec." Keys on Leon's end, fast and deliberate, then a pause. "Okay, looking at it. The Hearthfire expansion is dripping small updates throughout the game because of how big it is, but they're not dropping full patch notes until after the seventy-two-hour sale window closes." Another pause. "Which is about twenty-six hours from now, based on when the finale wrapped."

One of my pointed ears twitched at the rapid clatter of keys, a habit this body had picked up without asking.

"Twenty-six hours." I let that land. The rare-space read was real. The person who'd told me that knew exactly what she was looking at. "That confirms it. Thanks."

"Yeah." Leon's chair shifted. "Also, heads-up on the stream. Once you're close to actually engaging down there, I'll flip it live. We've been careful about the pod running hot, so I'm holding until you're actually moving." A pause, something more personal under it. "Got some info about the parts situation that we should talk about face-to-face when you're out."

"That works," I said. "Let me know the second it goes live so I'm not caught off guard. And after any engagement, kill the feed right after. I don't want to be immediately targeted if these people have friends watching."

"Will do." Leon's voice settled back into working mode. "Chat restrictions too? Followers only, message delay?"

"All of it. Every restriction."

"Copy."

The UI minimized, Leon's name staying visible in the corner of the HUD. I turned to the party screen without deciding to.

**[PARTY MEMBERS: 2/5]**
**[T_DRAG — LEVEL 7 — SHIV GOBLIN]**
**[RU — LEVEL 5 — HOBGOBLIN] [MOBILE]**

The MOBILE tag sat in small gray text beside Ru's name. I read it twice to be sure I was reading it right. *"Alive."* Relief hit hard enough that I almost laughed out loud in an empty tent. Wherever Ru was, he was alive, and the frustration could wait.

*"If he's mobile and not here, something's up."* Only one way to find out. I crossed to the entrance and pushed the canvas aside.

---

Eryndal's moon sat full over the eastern ridge, hard and white against the dark. The night came all the way in at once, no gradual adjustment. My goblin eyes caught the dark fast, yellow-tinted, pulling detail from shadow before a human's would have found it. The cold hit with it, immediate, cutting straight through the leather chest plate and settling against my skin, a reminder that this body ran colder than my real one and always had.

Leon's voice came in low through the HUD corner. "Yeah. This place looks like hell."

I stepped fully through the flap, boots finding packed dirt, and stopped. I scanned left first, then right.

The burn marks were on three panels of tarp along the northern line, visible even in the dark, the scorched edges curling inward where something had hit hot. *"Aimed at the boundary. Not a cook fire that got away from somebody. Someone pointed this at us on purpose."* My stomach sank as I read them again. *"That's on me. I told them ten minutes."*

"You're fine." A brief pause on Leon's end, something adjusting in it. "I watched them come up at least twice on the monitor. They turned back both times. Beyond that I don't have clean reads on what happened down there."

The full width of the warband that had been cycling watch rotation earlier had compressed into clusters of three and four. Goblins worked repairs rather than standing posts, the momentum from before replaced by something that sat lower and moved slower. Not broken, just recalibrated toward what they'd already absorbed. I didn't run. Running drew attention and questions I couldn't answer standing still.

Two goblins I recognized were patching the eastern breach with material that hadn't been cut to fit, rough planks lashed with cord pulled from somewhere else. Another pair had moved the cook fire back from its usual central spot, away from the line. Everything I saw as I walked made the same point, over and over. The camp had pulled in on itself while I was gone. The space that fifty goblins had occupied now held fifteen, and the fifteen moved like people who'd watched the other thirty-five stop moving.

The crowd parted around me the same way it always did. The sound following behind had changed, though. Less curiosity, more waiting. The quiet of people who'd already been scared once and were hoping the second time would go differently.

A younger goblin near the supply line reached out like he wanted to touch my sleeve, fingers stretching toward the hem of the chest plate, and then pulled his hand back before making contact.

*"They think Shiv's name means I already know what I'm doing."* I kept pace, kept my eyes forward, and said nothing about the fact that I'd never fought another player before in my life, not in this game, not once. The gap between thinking and doing had never been wider than it was right now, walking through a camp that expected me to close it.

---

Sevish's tent sat near the center of camp, larger than the others, canvas reinforced at the corners with stakes driven deep. I reached the flap and stopped outside it, hearing voices inside before I said anything.

"It's T-Drag." I kept my voice even. "Coming in."

The voices stopped. Silence held for a second, then Sevish's voice came back, rough and controlled. "Enter."

I pushed the flap aside and stepped through.

The interior was dim, a single lamp hanging from the center pole throwing uneven light across the space. Sevish stood near the back with one hand resting on the haft of his battle axe, the blade planted butt-first in the packed dirt floor. Fresh soot smeared dark across one forearm. His mouth was set in a line that said he was holding his voice exactly where he wanted it rather than where it naturally wanted to go.

Wyx stood beside him, arms crossed over her chest, her green eyes moving between me and her brother with the attention of someone who'd already run through several versions of this scene in her head and wanted to see how it actually played out. Ru leaned against the far wall near the entrance, his frame taking up more space than the angle of the room wanted to allow for. His right arm was pressed against his ribs at an angle that said something had gotten in there and hadn't been fully handled. Rott and Nab sat near the entrance, still, watching. Everyone in the tent was waiting on the same thing.

*"Careful. Sevish has authority over everyone under Thrash, Ru included, until I outrank him. That's not a wall I can afford to knock down before I understand it."* I kept my weight settled evenly on both feet and my breathing even.

"Good." Sevish's voice cut through the tent's air before the canvas had fully settled behind me. His eyes locked on me, the heat in his face the kind that had been sitting a while. "I hope your little nap was worth it." He drove the axe butt into the floor once, a solid thud. "Half my camp is gone and those bastards down that hill are mocking me. You want to explain what took so long."

My heart was going hard, and I kept that out of my voice. I held his eyes, didn't drop my chin, didn't shift my weight. "Whose fault is that, though." It wasn't a question. I kept my voice level, not raised, not backing off it. "You're the leader of this camp. I'm a grunt who spoke with Shiv. Those are different things. What gives me the call to dictate your operations unless you're telling me you're not a competent leader."

I let that sit there.

The tent went still enough that I could hear the wind finding the canvas outside. Sevish held his expression for a long count. Then something shifted in it, something that looked almost like approval but came with its own edge. He brought his teeth together in a slow smile, shoulders rolling back slightly.

"Competent." He said the word the way someone tried the weight of a piece of metal they were thinking about buying. "No. I am not a coward. We push them. We show them the power of this camp."

I held the look. "And what has pushing them gotten you?"

The smile held. Nobody moved. The quiet ran out another two seconds before Sevish broke it.

"No matter what you say," Sevish said, the words steady and even, carrying the satisfaction of a man who didn't back down from his own convictions even when he was owning the results of them, "I would not have done it another way. Those bastards retreated before you got here." He lifted one hand from the axe haft and held it flat, a conclusion in it. "If we strike now, we can finish them."

Leon's voice came through low in my ear, amused and warm. "Oh, you really said that to his face, huh."

"Not now."

Wyx stepped forward half a step, the tight cross of her arms loosening. She was watching me with something in her expression that wasn't the camp-discipline attention she usually brought to strangers in her brother's space. There was more interest in it than that, more precision, as though she'd already decided something and was waiting to see if the next answer confirmed it. "Did you get a chance to speak with Shiv?"

"Yeah," I said, meeting her look without flinching and keeping my tone easy. "Meditated on it. Talked with him for a while. Didn't realize how much time had passed until I came back to all of this."

I watched the shift happen. Wyx's shoulders dropped half an inch, the tension in her face easing by degrees. Sevish's grip on the axe haft loosened just behind hers, the posture of a man setting down something he'd been carrying for longer than was comfortable.

A small notification flickered at the edge of my vision, there one second before fading.

**[AFFILIATION: THRASH CLAN — 12% → 15%]**

Below it, smaller and quieter, a second one:

**[AFFILIATION: WYX THRASH — 0% → 4%]**

I caught the second one. Wyx's eyes had settled on me the moment I'd said Shiv's name, and they hadn't fully left since, carrying something that didn't read like standard camp-leader assessment. I filed it away and kept my eyes on Sevish.

A voice came through comms, pitched low. "Hey." Leon, careful in a way he didn't usually bother with. "Is that NPC tracking you different right now, or am I reading the feed wrong?"

I didn't answer. I kept my face toward Sevish.

"Good," Sevish said, and the frustration in his voice had found somewhere else to go now, something closer to relief underneath the rest of it. He rolled one shoulder, loosening it, his axe hand settling more naturally. "We're going to need whatever he's willing to give us. Especially now." His eyes settled on me directly, something weighing behind the scarred lines of his face. "My men will follow you down. Use them as you see fit. They follow your command."

A brief notification pushed up from the lower edge of my HUD:

**[FIELD COMMAND: ACTIVE — TATSUYA | 3 THRASH CLAN AUXILIARIES ASSIGNED]**

*"Four of us. I've never led anyone in a fight. Never been the one calling it."* My claws flexed once at my sides. Then I looked back at Sevish. "You have a scouting report?"

Sevish jerked his chin toward Rott. "Kesh went down close enough to count. Four of them inside the tent. They pulled back about five minutes ago. Haven't moved since." His voice dropped. "Last push, they made it to the camp gates before they turned back. That's where the real damage happened. They were farming their way up the hill until they saw what was waiting for them at the top."

"Gold tags," I said. "They saw you and Wyx and ran the math."

"Briefly engaged," Sevish said, without apology. "They saw the gap between our levels and made a reasonable call." His mouth curved, the ghost of satisfaction in it. "They still took the damage in."

"Which means now they're resetting. The question is how long that takes."

I did the math out loud, for Sevish as much as for myself. I'd heard recovery timers argued over before, Taji pausing clips at breakfast to fight about pot timing with his phone propped against a cereal bowl, and I'd tuned it out every time. Applying it to a live situation with my own neck on the line was different. "Three or four pushes, and the last one just happened. Standard potions at their level cut recovery down to roughly forty minutes from empty. Top-shelf stock, twenty-five to thirty." I paused, checking myself, not wanting to state something as fact that I'd only ever heard secondhand. "They're not running unlimited premium at level seven or eight. At least, I don't think they can afford to. We've got a window. Not a long one."

"Leon," I said, pitching it outward. "Can you confirm that? Recovery rates at this tier. Am I in the right range?"

Keys on Leon's end. "Give me a second." A pause. "Yeah, you're close. Wiki says standard pots at that level do about fifteen HP over ten seconds. Higher-tier ones are faster but expensive. If they've been hit multiple times and they're using vendor stock, forty minutes from empty sounds about right. Could be less if they're carrying anything better."

I shifted my weight, the packed dirt gritting under my boots, and ran my tongue across my sharp little teeth while I counted. *"Twenty minutes at the outside. Maybe less if they're not running premium. We can't wait them out."*

I looked toward the tent flap. "We need to be in position before it closes."

Sevish's teeth showed, something fierce and satisfied in it. "Then let's not waste it."

Wyx's gaze stayed on me longer than it needed to, her attention landing in a way that was specific to me rather than to the room. I noticed and didn't acknowledge it.

---

I stepped back from the group until the voices inside the tent dropped to a low murmur behind me. I opened comms.

"Leon. I need Arya's friend code."

A pause. The familiar note of caution rode underneath it, the reflex Leon reached for whenever anything touched the arcade arrangement. "Wait. You're bringing her into this? Like, actually into the game with you?"

"It's not about the pods," I said, keeping my voice flat and patient. "She came to me. She wasn't asking about the hardware." I looked back at the dark gap in the eastern line where goblins used to stand watch, the bare stakes and the nothing behind them. "She asked to join my adventure. Crownfall first."

The pause that followed was different from the first one. Something in it shifted, something that took a moment to sort itself into words.

"Wait." Leon's voice came back quieter. "After the finals? She told me she was done playing. Said it exactly like that, like she'd thought it through." Nothing came through for a moment but the faint ambient sound of a room on the other end. "I didn't know she'd changed her mind."

That landed somewhere in my chest that I didn't have a name for. I looked at the low watch fires still burning down the eastern stretch, orange embers going gray under ash nobody had touched.

"She said that to you directly."

"Yeah." A pause. "Keep this between us." Leon's voice dropped further, not a whisper but close. "Her parents overseas have been pushing for her to come back for a while now. Without granddad, there's no legal backing if it becomes a real fight, and that kind of fight doesn't come cheap. The guild drama ate through what she'd built up. The guild route hasn't had the traction she needed since everything that happened with her old guild. She's been grinding every angle she can find."

I turned the pieces over once. Arya watching guild chats from J-Link. Tracking my location. Offering to partner on nothing but the game itself as the bet. *"Not curiosity, not goodwill. The game is the only lever she has that moves anything real."*

"So she's gambling," I said. Not a question.

"Yeah," Leon said. "She is."

I looked back at the watch fires. The coal underneath had gone mostly dark. I checked the next sentence before I said it, the way I checked the register at WattsUp when the numbers came up short and I had to decide how to say it without making it worse.

"Since you're being straight with me." I kept my voice level. "My mom's been in a coma. The hospital's given her about six months without surgery, and Chiyo's been trying to keep that timeline from me. I caught it anyway." I let that settle, then kept going. "The debt collectors that have been after my dad's estate, it goes past a standard collections notice. They've been at it too long and they know too much. I don't have anything solid, but the pattern doesn't sit right." I stopped walking. "Going back to Tochi is on the table. But I can't just walk back through the door and have nothing change. I need this game to actually pay. Not cover one bill. Pay."

The line went quiet for several seconds. Outside, the camp made its small sounds, the creak of patched boards, something being dragged across packed dirt a few lanes over, the wind finding the canvas of Sevish's tent and pressing against it.

Leon came back with the dry quality reduced, the wit stripped back to just the voice. "So we're all in the same boat."

"Yeah." I said it simply, no performance in it. "Kind of fitting, though. Being in a class full of gamblers."

Neither of us said anything sentimental. We were just two people who'd traded the real number and now both knew what it was.

"Alright." Leon's tone shifted, the efficient quality returning. "Give me a second. She was showing online at J-Link last time I checked. If she hasn't logged in yet she will fast." A pause, muffled voices on Leon's end, quick and indistinct. Then: "Sending it now. And hey," dry again, already moving past it, "same channel or private line? System's asking."

I didn't hesitate. "Same channel. I'm not managing two separate secrets with two people who work the same front desk, that'll drive me insane, and she's going to find out everything eventually anyway."

"Copy that. Honestly relieved you said that, keeping two threads straight was gonna be a headache I didn't need either."

---

The party notification blinked at the edge of my vision.

**[PARTY INVITATION SENT: ARISKY]**

A half-second delay. Then:

**[ARISKY — LEVEL 11 — SPELLFORGER]**
**[ARISKY HAS JOINED YOUR PARTY]**
**[PARTY SIZE: 3/5]**

A second notification materialized directly below:

**[ARISKY IS REQUESTING TO MERGE OPERATOR AND PARTY CHANNELS]**
**[ACCEPT] [DECLINE]**

I tapped accept without hesitating. The channel header shifted to a single combined feed, one line for all three of us.

"Better," Arya said through the channel, her voice carrying the satisfied note of someone whose preference had just been accommodated. "I hate managing two windows."

"Same," Leon said from his end, something wry in it.

Something in me settled when her name sat beside mine on the party list, and I didn't examine why. I opened the text window while the channel was still settling.

[T_Drag: eastern edge. treeline, your left side. thirty minutes out?]

[AriSky: give or take. already moving]

[AriSky: heads up. i was watching the guild feeds from j-link while you were offline. two of tokyo's biggest guilds have leadership already talking about moving on your location]

[T_Drag: how urgent]

[AriSky: not immediately. most guilds aren't moving til morning, people have jobs. ganking crews do overnight runs but this doesn't read like that from the message structure]

[AriSky: war banner can't stay planted much longer though]

I squinted at the pale text glowing in the dark, the cold air stinging my eyes. *"Two of Tokyo's biggest guilds. Morning. That's not a lot of night."*

I closed the text window and crossed back to the tent where Sevish was still running through something with Wyx at the back wall.

"Thirty minutes," I said from the flap. "I need Rott and Nab ready to move."

Sevish gave me a single nod. That was enough.

---

We moved through camp fast.

Ru fell in at my shoulder the moment I cleared the tent, his heavy footfalls settling into an even rhythm against the packed earth. The right arm was still pressed against his ribs, but his stride had steadied from what it had been when he'd first appeared, and his weight sat more evenly through each step. Rott and Nab went in front, their smaller frames moving low between the tent rows, the depleted gas pouches at their belts still bouncing despite everything.

The crowd parted the same way it always did. The murmur behind us had changed in content without changing in tone, less wondering and more decided, the sound of people who'd picked a side and were waiting to find out if they'd picked right.

A younger goblin near the last supply row reached out like he wanted to touch my sleeve and pulled his hand back before making contact, the same motion as before, fingers stopping just short and then dropping back.

"Two times now," I said, low enough that only Ru would catch it. "Same kid, same motion."

Ru's tusks shifted. "Camp talks," he said. "When T-Drag walks out of Sevish's tent alive and unhurried, camp talks."

"Great. No pressure."

The eastern edge of camp came into full view when the last row of tents gave way to open ground, the hill falling away sharp toward the road below. I reached the rim first, my boots finding frost-stiff grass that crackled under my weight, the cold coming up through the soles in a way the tents had blocked completely.

Below the slope, set back from the road's shoulder at an angle that said someone had thought about sight lines when they planted it, a tent sat pitched in the deepening gray. Canvas dyed a deep, mismatched red. Corners staked at hard, even angles, the spacing too precise for field improvisation, the stitching too clean. Legacy-tier equipment, the kind that traveled with a character who'd already finished one full life in the game and wasn't starting from scratch.

We force them into the open on the fourth side, the road side, the one they think is already covered because the road's behind them." I let the shape of it sit a moment. "Those nameplates down there. ANSWERS TO PK_WRAITH. That tag." Her voice settled into something careful and deliberate. "I've been monitoring every major guild chat in Tokyo for the last three weeks. That handle has been running a thread in all of them, specifically tracking this World Quest."

The frost-stiff ground didn't move. The red tent sat exactly where it had been, the four nameplates unchanged. *"So they didn't stumble onto us. They were looking."* The cold in my chest had nothing to do with the night air.

# Chapter Ten: Silence and Fire, Part Two

The timer on Arya's nameplate read seven minutes when I moved.

I'd spent the last eighteen working the perimeter, staying low, my boots finding purchase on frozen ground without a sound, every step deliberate. The cold bit through my gloves as my fingers worked, frost crystals melting against my palms as I pressed each rune into place. The first went against the back wall. A faint pulse of orange light sank into the dirt where the canvas met earth, invisible to anyone who wasn't actively looking for it, sitting there for one full second before it faded, the warmth of it flaring once against my palm and going dormant.

The second went against the side wall nearest Ru's position, placed low where the fabric touched ground, the packed dirt giving slightly under my fingers. The third went against the last open wall, close enough now that I could hear muffled voices through the canvas. Someone was laughing at something. Metal clinked on metal. Nobody in there had any idea what was creeping up around them, and that was the last moment they'd have where they didn't.

I backed off toward the treeline, knees protesting as I rose, joints popping in the cold, and pulled up my ability list.

**[RUNE PRIMING: 3/3 AVAILABLE]**

*"Three points. Same three walls the gas goes into. It has to hold together, or none of this works."*

Arya resolved out of the dark a moment later, half hidden behind a thick trunk to my left, her staff already in hand with its head glowing a faint blue. She gave me a quick nod, sharp and professional, her fingers drumming once against the shaft before going still. The fighter in her sat right under the surface now. The easy lean from the call was gone, replaced by weight set evenly on both feet, the staff angled slightly forward, her eyes already working angles.

"That's the tent," I said, keeping my voice low, my eyes on the red canvas below and the shadows moving behind it. "Four inside, from what Kesh counted. The plan changes a little now that you're here. I'm not forcing them out the road side anymore. I want them to stay exactly where they are."

Arya's frown deepened, the glow on her staff brightening for half a second and then dimming back down. "You want Hushveil."

"The silence upgrade, full radius." I turned to meet her eyes across the gap, moonlight catching in her red hair. "Cover the whole tent, not just the road."

She bit her lower lip, her eyes unfocusing as she ran the numbers. "That's a lot of mana for that big a radius. Tier three at full size will eat most of what I've got." Her knuckles went white on the staff. "And it only lasts a few seconds once it's live. Everyone has to be in position before it drops."

"You've got it," I said, my voice steady. "Once it lands, they can't see, can't cast, can't cleanse anything. They're not going anywhere for as long as it holds."

I signaled toward the treeline. Rott and Nab were already crouched in the shadows twenty feet away, their eyes catching the firelight from camp behind them, and they jogged over low and quiet with the pouches at their belts barely shifting. Ru came in behind them, moving carefully around the arm he still kept pressed to his ribs.

"Three walls, three of you, one each," I said, crouching to their level and meeting their eyes one at a time. "Wait for the fog. Don't move until it's down, and stay clear of Arya's side of it, because the fog doesn't pick who it hits." I tapped the pouches at Rott's belt, the leather worn smooth under my clawed finger. "Once you're in, gas together. Same moment, not one after another. Three of you hitting it at once stacks faster than any of you could manage alone."

Rott nodded and scratched at his head with one clawed hand, his fingers catching in the matted hair, his eyes darting between me and the tent. "Rott understands. Gas together. Stack fast."

Nab grunted his agreement and went back to checking his own pouches, his fingers running over each one in turn, his lips moving as he counted.

"Ru," I said. "You hold the wall and nothing else. Anyone who breaks, they break into you."

"Ru understands." His tusks caught the moonlight as his mouth split into something close to a grin, his claws flexing once with a soft click.

The goblins dropped into a crouch and worked their way along the treeline toward the tent's blind side, barely rustling the underbrush, shadows moving through shadows.

A small indicator already sat in the corner of my vision from the moment Leon had flipped the feed.

**[STREAM: ACTIVE]**
**[VIEWERS: 310]**
**[CHAT: FOLLOWERS ONLY, 10-MINUTE COOLDOWN ACTIVE]**

Started over. Not even close to where it was before the pod cooked. I'd have to earn that back same as everything else tonight.

I looked across the dark to Arya, meeting her eyes through the trees. "On my mark. Clean, no hesitation."

Her staff brightened at once, the blue glow deepening into something thicker and heavier, ready to drop. Her feet planted wide, her whole body tensing around the shaft.

I looked at the tent one more time, at the shapes of four people inside it who had no idea what was coming. They were probably dividing loot or arguing about the next target, or doing whatever raiders did when they thought they were safe, their shadows thrown long against the canvas by the lamplight.

"Now."

The silence that came with it pressed down over the whole hillside.

**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: SILENCED, BLINDED]**

**[GOBLIN GAS AND TOXIC BREATH: ACTIVE, THREE SOURCES]**
**[POISON STACK: 4x, ACCELERATED]**
**[ENEMIES WITHIN RADIUS: POISONED, MOVEMENT SLOWED, NAUSEOUS]**

*"Full stack already."* Three of them hitting it together did in seconds what usually took a couple of rounds. I hadn't even known it could move that fast, and a grin tried to happen on my face before I caught it.

Sound started reaching me through the fog, something between coughing and retching, desperate and wet, trapped inside canvas walls that weren't going anywhere. Voices rose in panic that nobody could hear. Nobody came stumbling out into the road. There was nowhere for them to stumble to. Not like last time. They weren't getting outside this one.

"They're not even trying to run," Arya said quietly beside me, her voice tight with something between disbelief and awe, the glow on her staff flickering as the staff trembled in her grip. "They can't even call for help."

Ru and the two goblins peeled back out of the fog seconds later, clearing the treeline fast and low. Ru's chest heaved as he drew in clean air, his breath pluming white from his mouth, with Rott and Nab right behind him, gasping.

The chat window exploded.

I triggered the first rune.

With a fog spell I was ready to throw away an hour ago. They couldn't even cleanse. They were just stuck in it."

You knew they couldn't cleanse because you knew silence locks their casting." Her voice picked up speed. "You set this whole thing up around that one mechanic."

I dismissed the window and looked back toward camp, toward the dark ridge where Sevish and the others were waiting. Arya fell into step beside me, the Shivbone Staff resting easy in her hand, and for the first time since I'd logged in the camp didn't feel like it was waiting for me to fail.

"Yeah," I said. "Welcome to the team."

*"Crownfall is guild-only. Two people on a ridge isn't a guild, and seasonal points don't care how clean one fight was. I need a guild of my own, and soon."* I rubbed my thumb across my cold claws and let the thought settle.
