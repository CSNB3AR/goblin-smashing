# Chapter Six

The roar had hit before I'd had a chance to enjoy the money I'd just made. Now my chest was tight and the air itself seemed to be pressing down, squeezing me into a crouch, and no matter how hard I told my body to move, it wouldn't. Only my head would turn. A status icon had popped into the top of my vision, and I had to look up to read it.

**[STATUS EFFECT: IMMOBILIZED - 6s]**

I'd watched roots and stuns land for six months on the screen over the WattsUp register and on Dosuke's streams, and on paper this was no different. What made it special was that it was the first time one had hit me like this, my body reacting as if it were real. That was probably why HGO ranked among the best virtual MMORPGs of its time, and why it was so cutting edge, with that much detail packed in. It topped the sales charts even in the closed beta. I could finally see why everyone wanted their hands on it.

The icon ticked to five. Over the kid's head the timer read twelve, double mine. I tried to inspect Sevish and got a panel of question marks, then Wyx and got the same.

The icon ticked to three. *"One way in, one way out, straight through two I can't even inspect and twenty goblins. I'm outmatched, and the smart move is probably not getting my ass sent back to the Cathedral."* The lock that came after was the real cost. *"Seventy-two hours is a long wait. The Gambit got me lucky early money, but luck runs out, and three days locked out sends me crawling back to Gato with nothing to show Chiyo. Think."*

**[STATUS EFFECT EXPIRED: IMMOBILIZED]**

The weight left all at once and my legs shook and prickled as I came up out of the crouch, slowly, with my hands open and away from my belt so nobody mistook it for a move. Out of habit my hand went to my chin, rubbing for the goatee I only had back home, and found smooth green skin. Sevish stood an arm's length in front of me, a wall of a goblin with my head barely reaching his chest, and I had to look up to hold his eyes. Wyx stood five or six feet behind him, a head shorter than her brother and still taller than me, the big tent twenty feet back where the two of them had come from. She gave me nothing, no smile, just a serious look to see what I'd say. The kid was still stiff against the crate ten feet to my left, the bowl clutched to his chest.

"Two of your guys were picking on a kid over some rotten stew," I said, keeping my voice even. "I took care of them. I'd have handled it peacefully, but they wanted violence, so I answered. I'm not losing sleep over it." I folded my arms and held his stare.

Sevish said nothing, the axe loose in his fist. The immobilize was still prickling out of my arms, and somewhere past the tents the crickets ticked on, the only thing in camp not holding its breath. I kept going before the quiet could set. "It was two on one, the odds were stacked against the kid, and nobody else wanted to step up. Everybody just stood there and watched." I unfolded my arms and spread my hands a little. "What kind of allies throw punches over a bowl of stew? Don't we have bigger fish to fry?"

A murmur ran through the five or six goblins hanging back by the stew pot and died, and it wasn't the idle camp noise from before. It had an edge on it that made my palms sweat. I let my eyes drift over them. One goblin took a step back. The one nearest the pot stared at me like I'd walked up and slapped the chieftain. Beside the crate the kid made a thin, scared sound, his pointed ears pinned flat, still shaking off the stun. Wyx's brow lifted a fraction and the corner of her mouth went with it, a small curve of a smile in the drifting smoke.

"He has a quick tongue on him," she said, pitched low, just loud enough to carry. "I like him."

I blinked, my head tilting a degree. *"Huh. That's not how NPCs talk in the clips. I wasn't expecting that."* A small notification flickered at the edge of my vision.

**[AFFILIATION: WYX THRASH - 45% → 48%]**

*"Affiliation. I've only ever seen it in the clips, never from the inside. I'm curious how it moves for a goblin."* My eyes lingered on the number.

Sevish's eyes went from Wyx to me and back, and he rolled one shoulder, shifting his weight like a leader reading the room before he spoke into it. His tag flickered at the edges, not turning yellow or red, just flickering like a notification trying to fire and being held back. Then a low, rough laugh broke out of him, short and a little delighted, his heavy brows lifting and his whole chest shaking with it as he planted the butt of the axe in the dirt. "Hah. Of course you do."

The grin thinned. "If it happened as you say, we have no quarrel. The clan's rule is plain: we do not fight each other. And you are a stranger in my camp. A goblin who wants a clan comes to its leader first, and you never came to me. A stray causing trouble under my watch, I need to know about it. So why should I trust you? Who says it went as you tell it? I came out to a blast and two of mine dead by your hand. By the look of that ground, it was a fierce fight." His eyes went over the watchers, then to the crate. "Those two will not rise again. The dead stay dead. If any of you saw it, speak."

He'd read the fight off the scorched ground. I glanced at the black patch, then back to him.

I looked back over the watchers, hunting for one face that would meet mine, anyone who'd been near the stew pot when it started. Nobody answered. The kid's mouth worked, but all that came out was a thin breath, and the watchers only muttered, low and shapeless, none of it aimed at Sevish. The dice on my earring ticked against my jaw when I swallowed.

The silence stretched, and I cut into it before it could set. "If you don't believe me, that's on you," I said, and made myself hold his stare. "But it happened how I said it did."

Every word I'd told him was true. What I'd left out sat at the back of my throat, that the fight probably never happens if I hadn't said what I said to those two. *"If that comes out, who knows what he does with it. Hopefully nobody brings it up."* My thumb pressed into my palm.

The camp went as silent as a mouse in a room with a cat. My ears flicked at every shift of the crowd, and a cold drop of sweat slid down my spine.

Then Wyx came forward from behind him, calm and unhurried, a loose sway to her stride, one hand resting on the skull at her hip, and stopped at Sevish's shoulder. "Brother," she said, "I saw all of it."

My eyes followed her. She was the only one in the whole camp who'd stepped out for me.

*"She's siding with me."* My eyes stayed on her while I wondered why. Then it clicked. On the broadcast at work, Kazuku had said the people in this world noticed exactly who you were and what you'd done, and that you could earn their trust or not. I'd heard it at work and filed it as marketing. This was what he meant. How I spoke and what I did was how they'd treat me, so I couldn't move any differently than I did in the real world. I had to be smart.

"Is that so?" Sevish turned to her, the amusement still sitting in the corner of his mouth. He loved a good fight, and it showed. "I caught only the end of it. You tell me this one made that blast and killed two of my men, flat out?"

Wyx rolled one shoulder, her weight settling onto a single hip, black hair sliding off her shoulder like she knew exactly who was looking. "He is a crafty one," she said, dry warmth in her voice. "He drew them into his own fumes and set the ground alight. One hell of an explosion, I say. A goblin so crafty should be of use to us, brother, and of great use, since we have already failed at our task more than once." My head snapped toward her, and I made myself keep my eyes on her face. A snort went through the watchers, my ears swiveling toward it, and the kid's shaking eased a little.

My hands loosened at my sides. I was nervous, but under it something bright kept fizzing in my chest. A couple hours ago I'd been planning on an elf, and now I was trading words with two people who weighed every one of mine. *"Stay level. Listen, collect questions. Once I log out, I'll look up everything on this quest and this class."* I kept my face still and my breathing slow.

Sevish looked at his sister for a long moment and didn't argue.

"The matter." He chewed on it, and the grin came back slow. "Hah. Perhaps so." He pointed the axe at me. "You killed two of my men. This camp has already lost too many, and I cannot overlook that. It leaves you in my debt. But a goblin who can kill two enforcers has power, and power I understand. I may find a use for you. There is a task I have been turning over."

A purple notification bloomed at the edge of my vision.

**[WORLD QUEST UPDATED: CALL OF THE BONES]**

**[STAGE 1 OBJECTIVE COMPLETE: DEFEND THE YOUNG GOBLIN FROM THE THRASH ENFORCERS]**

**[NEW OBJECTIVE: GATHER INFORMATION ON SHIV]**

My pulse kicked. As far as I knew nobody else had touched this quest, and if word got out, this camp would be a farming zone overnight.

"Not here," Sevish said. He jerked his chin at the crate where the kid still stood stiff against the wood. "Inside, the both of you."

Three goblins in real armor stepped out of the dark, fitted leather and metal plates, weapons drawn and not yet raised. White tags floated above their heads, and my eyes went to the plate and buckles. *"Real gear. These aren't like the two I just dropped."*

"You come with us," one of them said, voice flat and professional. Another peeled off toward the crate. "And you, runt."

They didn't bind or touch us, just fell in on either side and started walking, close enough that running wasn't an option. Goblins stopped to watch us pass, my bare soles aching on the packed dirt. Wyx fell in a few paces behind. I kept my eyes forward and my stride even.

We stopped at the big tent, bone totems carved into the posts on either side of the flap. A guard pulled it open and gestured us inside.

I ducked through, the kid right behind me, into cooler air and the light of a single oil lamp hanging from the center pole. Sevish dropped into the chair behind a crude table of stacked crates, the axe leaning within reach, and when Wyx took up a position at his shoulder, the tent got more formal.

Sevish's eyes went from me to the kid and back, taking his time. The kid pressed so close to my side I could feel him shaking.

"Name," Sevish said, voice deep and rough, a voice that carried without getting loud. "I know every goblin who has walked through this camp, and I have never seen your face. Where do you hail from?" He tipped his head. "Are you new to these parts?"

I took a breath and weighed what he could actually follow. Pods and character creation were out, and the true answer in his terms was short.

"T-Drag," I said, keeping my hands where he could see them. "I woke up by your fire pit tonight. I don't know how I got there."

"Woke up," Sevish repeated, turning the words over. "T-Drag." He glanced at Wyx.

She said nothing. Her amber eyes hadn't left me since the stew, and the corner of her mouth moved, not quite a smile. A small notification flickered at the edge of my vision and faded.

**[AFFILIATION: WYX THRASH - 48% → 52%]**

Sevish stood from his chair, slow and deliberate, the axe sliding into the sling across his back, and walked around the table. He stopped a few feet away, close enough that I had to tilt my head back to keep eye contact, and crossed his arms over his broad chest.

"I cannot let you walk out of here without knowing more," Sevish said, voice carrying absolute certainty. He paused, letting that sink in, then continued. "You have shown more in one fight than most of this camp has shown in weeks. Only my sister has shown as much." A nod toward Wyx, who was still watching me with that sharp, evaluating gaze. "So here is what I offer."

"Myrrhwood is a bleak place, and worse each year. Shiv has been gone near half a century, and the clans have never found their footing since. That is why Wyx and I gathered our people and came this far, to find what Shiv hid before he vanished and carry it home, so we may survive what is coming. Thrash gave its land to another clan to make the journey. Every goblin in this camp left something behind. Bring the clans together as they once were, and no other race shoves us about again."

"The old tales say every clan answered to Shiv once," Wyx said, her voice softening, her eyes going somewhere far past the canvas wall. "My grandfather's generation knew it. I mean to see it with my own eyes."

*"Shiv. The name from the quest. Whoever he was, they gave up their land over tales of him."*

I shifted my weight onto the other foot, the cold of the packed dirt coming up through my bare soles, and kept my eyes on his face.

"We have tried the rally three times," Sevish continued, his tone matter-of-fact, not defensive. "Three times we failed. We could not get past the city guard and the adventurers who rally to them."

"Three that I can count," Wyx said quietly, her eyes on the map. "It feels like more." Sevish's mouth flattened, and he let it go.

My eyes flicked between them. *"Three times. Three that she can count. They both say it like the number matters."*

"We tried the Grand Library by force," Sevish said, gesturing toward the map on the table. "It does not work. But what you did back there, slipping through instead of meeting them head-on, using the ground, that is not something anyone here can do. It may be what we need."

My long fingers curled against my thighs.

"The library," I repeated, making sure I understood correctly. "You want me to get into the Grand Library of Lunarest."

"The restricted floors, specifically," Sevish clarified, uncrossing his arms and letting one hand drift up to the haft of the axe on his back. "The public floors hold nothing we seek. Find anything on Shiv up there, records, journals, anything that tells us what he knew or where he went, and bring it back here."

I reached for my chin again, the same habit whenever I was weighing something, and found only a ridge of smooth green skin. "And if I do this," I said slowly, working through the implications as I spoke, "what does that actually mean?"

"Finish this, and you stand under Thrash's protection. You are ours, and we are yours. Whoever comes for you comes for the clan."

*"Real, long-term backup. That's not nothing."* A bead of sweat rolled down my temple.

"And if I don't?" I asked, needing to hear the other side spelled out just as clearly.

Sevish's tag flickered again, and so did Wyx's.

"Then you stay here," Sevish said, and there was no heat in his voice, no threat, just a plain statement of fact. "I do not let one who can kill two of my men walk off into the world before I know which side he stands on. And understand this. If you take the task and do not return, we leave. If we cross paths after that, you are an enemy. That is all."

The small sharp teeth of this new mouth caught against my lip. *"So I don't really get to say no. I only get to pick when."*

Wyx spoke for the first time since we'd entered the tent, her voice dry and carrying a hint of warmth that didn't quite match the sharpness in her eyes. "Gently, brother. You will frighten off the first promising thing we have found in months." She laid two fingers on his forearm, light as a touch could be, and I watched the tension in that thick arm ease a degree. She turned her amber eyes on me. "He stood up for a runt over a bowl of stew. A goblin like that returns." A beat. "And he amuses me, at least. That is worth something."

Sevish grunted, weighed it, and gave a short nod. "She is usually right," he said to me, flat and plain. "Do not make her wrong."

Before I could answer, the clock in the corner of my vision caught me.

9:41 PM.

I winced. *"Damn."*

Leon's voice crackled through the pod's external speaker a second later, distant and tinny, cutting in from somewhere outside the world I was standing in. "Hey, heads-up, we're closing in about thirty minutes. Just wanted to give you a warning."

I blinked, a goblin chieftain's tent on one side and Leon's voice reaching in from the other.

"I'll be off shortly," I said out loud, the words aimed at Leon.

"No worries, man," Leon's voice came back through the speaker, relaxed and unbothered. "You've been in there a while. Hope the pod didn't give you any more issues after that initial hiccup."

"Nah, it's been good," I said, keeping my voice casual while my heart hammered from everything happening inside the game.

Sevish and Wyx were both looking at me strangely now, and my ears went hot.

*"Right. They can't hear Leon. All they see is me talking to empty air. Great first impression, the new guy talks to himself."*

"Whom do you speak to?" Wyx asked, her head tilting a degree.

"A voice." I shrugged. "It comes and goes." It was close enough to true.

"Spirits do that," she said, and let it go, though her eyes stayed on me a beat longer than the answer needed.

"I need a few days," I said out loud, squaring my shoulders at Sevish this time. "Three days to think about this and figure out my next move."

"You may have them, T-Drag," Sevish said, and his tone made it clear that wasn't a negotiation, it was a decision he'd already made. "But you stay in camp while you think."

Two guards stepped forward from just inside the entrance and flanked me without being told to, smooth and practiced.

**[YOU ARE NOW A PRISONER OF THE THRASH CLAN]**

**[MOVEMENT RESTRICTED TO CAMP BOUNDARIES]**

**[DURATION: UNTIL RELEASED BY CHIEFTAIN SEVISH]**

**[WORLD QUEST UPDATED: CALL OF THE BONES]**

**[STAGE 2 UNLOCKED]**

**[OBJECTIVE: INFILTRATE THE GRAND LIBRARY OF LUNAREST – RESTRICTED FLOORS]**

**[REWARD: UNKNOWN]**

**[TIME LIMIT: 7 DAYS IN-GAME]**

The guards walked me back through camp, past the black scorch mark where the enforcers had gone down, a few goblins already scuffing dirt over it. The stew pot was down to coals, one last goblin scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon.

They stopped at a tent near the eastern edge of camp, away from the central fires. One of the guards pulled the flap open. "Inside."

I ducked through the entrance, my shorter frame making it easier than it would have been in my real body, and the noise from outside dropped off almost at once. The tent was small, barely tall enough to sit upright in, lit by a single oil lamp that threw uneven shadows every time the flame guttered. A thin patched bedroll took up most of the floor.

I sat and the straw stuffing crackled under me. I pulled one foot up and found the sole raw and gritty. *"Shoes. First thing I buy."*

This was where I'd be staying until I gave them an answer. *"Three days. I asked for them."* I let out a slow breath.

I pulled the two green vials out of my inventory. They materialized in my palm, and I turned them over in my green hands. The number stamped beneath each one was easy to read: ¥8,400.

Sixteen thousand eight hundred yen from one roll, in my first hour, and the Gambit only fired once a day. I set the vials on the bedroll and grinned at nothing, a fizzy, stupid grin I couldn't have wiped off if I'd tried. *"Not a bad first login."*

It left me with more questions than answers. I touched the dice at my ear. *"Fortune feeds crits and drops, I know that much. What does this die do as it climbs?"* I knew this game cold, down to the Spellforger build I'd drawn up with Eren. A goblin body I'd never planned for was the blank, and I hadn't had one minute to look anything up. For once I'd get to be the one working out the rules.

*"Chiyo's going to ask where I'm spending my days."* I picked at a loose thread in the bedroll. If I could call it a job at Leon's, she might let it ride. If not, it was Tochi, a bed on campus and the game on weekends only. Mom's surgery sat under all of it.

I slid the vials back into my inventory. First things first: get offline, cash these in, and start digging. Whatever I found stayed with me until I knew what I was holding.

In the corner of my vision the chat window I hadn't looked at since spawning suddenly lit up, messages scrolling past too fast to read. Then a bell rang, loud enough that it should have echoed through the camp, though nobody else reacted.

**[SYSTEM ANNOUNCEMENT: WORLD QUEST | CALL OF THE BONES HAS BEEN ACTIVATED]**

My eyes shot up to the chat window and I watched it erupt, questions and theories and arguments breaking out faster than I could read.

*"Damn. That's not good."* I reached for the logout button and my vision of the world went dark.

**[END CHAPTER SIX]**
