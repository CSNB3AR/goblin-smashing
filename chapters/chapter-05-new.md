# Chapter Six

The roar had hit before I'd had a chance to enjoy the money I'd just made. Now my chest was tight and the air itself seemed to be pressing down, squeezing me into a crouch, and no matter how hard I told my body to move, it wouldn't. Only my head would turn. A status icon had popped into the top of my vision, and I had to look up to read it.

**[STATUS EFFECT: IMMOBILIZED - 6s]**

*"HGO's got a dozen ways to lock somebody down. Roots, quicksand, worse. I watched them all on streams, and no clip said it would feel like this."* It was the first time the game had flat out overruled my own body. It was weird, and it wasn't a screen effect I could shrug off. My body believed it.

The icon ticked to five. Over the kid's head the timer read twelve, double mine. I tried to inspect Sevish and got a panel of question marks, then Wyx and got the same.

The icon ticked to three. *"One way in, one way out, straight through two gold tags and twenty goblins. I'm outmatched, and the smart move is probably not getting my ass sent back to the Cathedral."* The lock that came after was the real cost. *"Seventy-two hours with ¥16,800 of vials I can't put on the board. The Gambit works, so I know I can make money this way, and I'm not dying before I prove it to Chiyo. Think."*

**[STATUS EFFECT EXPIRED: IMMOBILIZED]**

The weight left all at once and my legs shook and prickled as I came up out of the crouch, slowly, with my hands open and away from my belt so nobody mistook it for a move. My hand went to my chin to scratch the goatee this face didn't have, found only smooth green skin, and dropped. I looked at Wyx first, no smile, just a serious look to see what she'd give me, and she gave me nothing. She'd watched the whole thing. One wrong detail from me and she'd correct it, or someone would. Then I looked back at the kid, still stiff against the crate with the bowl clutched to his chest, and last at Sevish. Up close he was a wall, and I had to look up to hold his eyes.

"Two of your guys were picking on a kid over some rotten stew," I said, keeping my voice even. "I took care of them. I'd have handled it peacefully, but they wanted violence, so I answered. I'm not losing sleep over it."

Sevish said nothing, the axe loose in his fist. The immobilize was still prickling out of my arms, and somewhere past the tents the crickets ticked on, the only thing in camp not holding its breath. I kept going before the quiet could set. "It was two on one, the odds were stacked against the kid, and nobody else wanted to step up. Everybody just stood there and watched." I spread my hands a little. "What kind of allies throw punches over a bowl of stew? Don't we have bigger fish to fry?"

A murmur ran through the watchers and died, and it wasn't the idle camp noise from before. It had an edge on it that made my palms sweat. One goblin took a step back. Another stared at me like I'd walked up and slapped the chieftain, and beside the crate the kid made a thin, scared sound, his pointed ears pinned flat, still shaking off the stun. Wyx's brow lifted a fraction and the corner of her mouth went with it, and when the wind shifted the smoke and lifted her black hair off one shoulder, that small curve of a smile was the only thing I could see.

"He has a quick tongue on him," she said, pitched low, just loud enough to carry. "I like him."

Sevish went completely still. Both gold tags flickered at the edges, not turning yellow or red, just flickering like a notification trying to fire and being held back. Then a low, rough laugh broke out of him, short and a little delighted, his heavy brows lifting and his whole chest shaking with it as he planted the butt of the axe in the dirt. "Hah. Of course you do."

The grin thinned. "If it happened as you say, we have no quarrel. Thrash keeps one law above the rest. No goblin lifts a hand against his own. But why should I trust you? Who says it went as you tell it? I came out to a blast and two of mine dead by your hand. By the look of that ground, it was a fierce fight." His eyes went over the watchers, then to the crate. "Those two will not rise again. The dead stay dead. Who saw it?"

Nobody answered. The kid's mouth worked, but all that came out was a thin breath. The watchers only muttered, low and shapeless, none of it aimed at Sevish. *"Damn it. The kid's the one who'd speak up for me, and he can barely stand. Nobody else is going to say a word."*

The seconds crawled, and a cold drop of sweat slid down my spine. *"They don't get back up, and he wants a reason to trust me. I don't have one. I'm going to die right here."*

Then Wyx stepped forward, calm and unhurried, the firelight sliding along the red markings on her arm, one hand resting on the skull at her hip. "Brother," she said, "I saw all of it."

*"She was there. Of course she was. I never figured she'd say anything."*

"Is that so?" Sevish turned to her, and the amusement went out of his face and left attention behind. I figured whatever she said next would carry more weight than anything I could. "You tell me he felled two of ours that easily?"

"If they were fool enough to run headlong into his fumes and choke on them," Wyx said, dry warmth in her voice, "what use were they to us, brother?" A snort went through the watchers, and the kid's shaking eased a little.

She let it settle, and her voice warmed the way it did when she spoke of Shiv, her gaze lifting past the firelight as if the name carried weight. "And if we are in earnest about Shiv's cause, ask yourself whether a goblin who speaks so might serve it. He may help us with the matter that has stopped us."

*"They weigh me, they weigh each other, they care who's telling the truth. Nobody here is reading from a script."* My hands tightened at my sides, the only part of me I trusted to move.

Sevish looked at his sister for a long moment and didn't argue. From how still he held, I figured he rarely did.

"The matter." He chewed on it, and the grin came back slow. "Hah. Perhaps so." He pointed the axe at me. "You killed two of my men, and that leaves you in my debt. A goblin who can fell two enforcers has power, and power I understand. I may find a use for you. There is a task I have been turning over."

A purple notification bloomed at the edge of my vision, the color pulling my eye at once.

**[WORLD QUEST UPDATED: CALL OF THE BONES]**

**[STAGE 1 OBJECTIVE COMPLETE: DEFEND THE YOUNG GOBLIN FROM THE THRASH ENFORCERS]**

*"Stage one's done. Did I really just advance it?"*

"Not here," Sevish said. He jerked his chin at the crate where the kid still stood stiff against the wood. "Inside, the both of you."

Three goblins in real armor stepped out of the dark, not the rags everyone else wore but fitted leather and metal plates, weapons drawn and not yet raised. White tags floated above their heads, bright and clear. *"Real gear. These aren't like the two I just dropped."*

"You come with us," one of them said, voice flat and professional. Another peeled off toward the crate. "And you, runt."

They didn't bind either of us or even touch us, just fell in on either side and started walking, a flanking position that made it obvious running wasn't an option. Goblins throughout the camp stopped what they were doing to watch us pass, and every step on the packed dirt reminded my bare soles what the sprint had cost them. The wind off the waterfall had turned damp, with rain in it. Wyx fell in a few paces behind us. I kept my eyes forward and my stride even.

We stopped at the big tent. Bone totems were carved into the posts on either side of the flap, their patterns throwing strange shadows across the canvas in the firelight. One of the guards pulled the flap open and gestured us inside.

I ducked through, the kid right behind me, and the temperature dropped immediately. The air inside was cooler, darker, lit only by a single oil lamp hanging from the center pole that threw uneven, flickering light up the canvas walls. Sevish dropped into the chair behind a crude table made from stacked crates and a plank of wood, the two-handed battle axe leaning against its side within easy reach, and when Wyx stepped in behind us and took up a position at his shoulder, the air shifted and the tent got more formal.

Sevish's eyes went from me to the kid and back, taking his time before he spoke. The kid pressed so close to my side I could feel him shaking. The silence stretched, broken only by the crackle of the oil lamp and the distant sounds of camp life continuing outside.

"Name," Sevish said, voice deep and rough, a voice that carried without getting loud. "I know every goblin who has walked through this camp, and I have never seen your face. Where do you hail from?" He tipped his head. "Are you new to these parts?"

*"New here. If this is the goblin spawn, and nobody ran goblin in the beta, then I'm the first stranger this camp has ever had."* I weighed what he could actually follow. Pods and character creation were out, and the true answer in his terms was short.

"T-Drag," I said. "I woke up by your fire pit tonight. I don't know how I got there."

"Woke up," Sevish repeated, turning the words over. "T-Drag." He glanced at Wyx.

She said nothing. Her amber eyes hadn't left me since the stew, and the corner of her mouth moved, not quite a smile. A small notification flickered at the edge of my vision and faded.

**[AFFILIATION: WYX THRASH - 45% → 52%]**

*"Forty-five to fifty-two. I don't know the rules on that yet, but it moved on a first meeting, and it started a lot higher than I'd have guessed."*

Sevish stood up from his chair, slow and deliberate, the battle axe sliding into the sling across his back, and walked around the table with heavy steps that made the packed dirt floor creak. He stopped a few feet away, close enough that I had to tilt my head back to keep eye contact, and crossed his arms over his broad chest.

"I cannot let you walk out of here without knowing more," Sevish said, voice carrying absolute certainty. He paused, letting that sink in, then continued. "You have shown more in one fight than most of this camp has shown in weeks. Save my sister." A nod toward Wyx, who was still watching me with that sharp, evaluating gaze. "So here is what I offer."

He laid it out plain and rough, no sugarcoating, just the facts.

"Myrrhwood is a bleak place, and worse each year. Shiv has been gone near half a century, and the clans have never found their footing since. That is why Wyx and I gathered our people and came this far, to find what Shiv hid before he vanished and carry it home, so we may survive what is coming. Thrash gave its land to another clan to make the journey. Every goblin in this camp left something behind. Bring the clans together as they once were, and no other race shoves us about again."

"The old tales say every clan answered to Shiv once," Wyx said, and her voice softened the way it did whenever she spoke his name, her eyes going somewhere far past the canvas wall. "My grandfather's generation knew it. I mean to see it with my own eyes."

*"Shiv. Never heard of him. Whoever he was, they gave up their land over tales of him."*

I shifted my weight onto the other foot, the cold of the packed dirt coming up through my bare soles, and kept my eyes on his face.

"We have tried the rally three times," Sevish continued, his tone matter-of-fact, not defensive. "Three times we failed. We could not get past the city guard and their allies."

"Three that I can count," Wyx said quietly, her eyes on the map. "It feels like more." Sevish's mouth flattened, and he let it go.

*"Three times. Three that she can count. They both say it like the number matters."*

"We tried the Grand Library by force," Sevish said, gesturing toward the map on the table. "It does not work. But what you did back there, slipping through instead of meeting them head-on, using the ground, that is not something anyone here can do. It may be what we need."

My long fingers curled against my thighs, the nails pressing small dents into the rag over my knees.

"The library," I repeated, making sure I understood correctly. "You want me to get into the Grand Library of Lunarest."

"The restricted floors, specifically," Sevish clarified, uncrossing his arms and letting one hand drift up to the haft of the battle axe slung across his back, a casual motion that still carried weight. "The public floors hold nothing we seek. Find anything on Shiv up there, records, journals, anything that tells us what he knew or where he went, and bring it back here."

My hand went up to scratch my chin the way it did when I was weighing something, hunting for a goatee this face didn't have, and found only a ridge of smooth green skin. "And if I do this," I said slowly, working through the implications as I spoke, "what does that actually mean?"

"Finish this, and you stand under Thrash's protection. You are ours, and we are yours. Whoever comes for you comes for the clan."

*"Real, long-term backup. That's not nothing"* A bead of sweat rolled down my temple, slow and ticklish, and I let it go instead of wiping it.

"And if I don't?" I asked, needing to hear the other side spelled out just as clearly.

Sevish's gold tag flickered again, and so did Wyx's.

*"Stupid question. I already know the answer."*

"Then you stay here," Sevish said, and there was no heat in his voice, no threat, just a plain statement of fact. "I do not let one who can fell two of my enforcers walk off into the world before I know which side he stands on. And understand this. If you take the task and do not return, we leave. If we cross paths after that, you are an enemy. That is all."

The small sharp teeth of this new mouth caught against my lip. *"So I don't really get to say no. I only get to pick when."*

Wyx spoke for the first time since we'd entered the tent, her voice dry and carrying a hint of warmth that didn't quite match the sharpness in her eyes. "Gently, brother. You will frighten off the first promising thing we have found in months." She laid two fingers on his forearm, light as a touch could be, and I watched the tension in that thick arm ease a degree. She turned her amber eyes on me. "He stood up for a runt over a bowl of stew. A goblin like that returns." A beat. "And he amuses me, at least. That is worth something."

Sevish grunted, weighed it, and gave a short nod. "She is usually right," he said to me, flat and plain. "Do not make her wrong."

Before I could answer, the clock in the corner of my vision caught me, small and quiet and impossible to ignore once I'd looked.

9:41 PM.

*"Damn."*

Leon's voice crackled through the pod's external speaker a second later, distant and tinny, cutting in from somewhere outside the world I was standing in. "Hey, heads-up, we're closing in about thirty minutes. Just wanted to give you a warning."

I blinked, trying to hold both places in my head at once, a goblin chieftain's tent on one side and Leon's voice reaching in from the other.

"I'll be off shortly," I said out loud, the words aimed at Leon.

"No worries, man," Leon's voice came back through the speaker, relaxed and unbothered. "You've been in there a while. Hope the pod didn't give you any more issues after that initial hiccup."

"Nah, it's been good," I said, keeping my voice casual while my heart hammered from everything happening inside the game.

Sevish and Wyx were both looking at me strangely now, their expressions sliding from serious evaluation to mild confusion.

*"Right. They can't hear Leon. All they see is me talking to empty air. Great first impression, the new guy talks to himself."*

"Whom do you speak to?" Wyx asked, her head tilting a degree.

"A voice. It comes and goes." It was close enough to true.

"Spirits do that," she said, and let it go, though her eyes stayed on me a beat longer than the answer needed.

"I need a few days," I said out loud, aiming the words at Sevish this time. "Three days to think about this and figure out my next move."

"You may have them, T-Drag," Sevish said, and his tone made it clear that wasn't a negotiation, it was a decision he'd already made. "But you stay in camp while you think."

Two guards stepped forward from where they'd been standing just inside the tent entrance, moving into flanking positions on either side of me without being told to, their movements smooth and practiced.

**[MOVEMENT RESTRICTED TO CAMP BOUNDARIES]**

**[DURATION: UNTIL RELEASED BY CHIEFTAIN SEVISH]**

**[WORLD QUEST UPDATED: CALL OF THE BONES]**

**[OBJECTIVE: INFILTRATE THE GRAND LIBRARY OF LUNAREST – RESTRICTED FLOORS]**

**[REWARD: UNKNOWN]**

**[TIME LIMIT: 7 DAYS IN-GAME]**

I squinted at the wall of text like narrowing my eyes would make it read any better, the glow of it sitting hot behind my forehead.

The guards walked me back out of the tent and through the camp, past the black scorch mark where the enforcers had gone down, a few goblins already scuffing dirt over it, past the arm-wrestling match that had fully broken up now, people dispersing back to their fires and their own business, past the older goblin who'd gone back to mending that same torn shirt by the light of a small lamp, needle moving in the same patient rhythm. The stew pot was down to nothing but coals now, one last goblin scraping the very bottom of it with a wooden spoon, the sound of wood on metal carrying across the quieter parts of camp.

They stopped at a tent near the eastern edge of the camp, away from the central fires but close enough that I could hear the low murmur of conversation and the occasional burst of laughter from goblins still awake. One of the guards pulled the tent flap open, canvas rustling. "Inside."

I ducked through the entrance, my shorter frame making it easier than it would have been in my real body, and the noise from outside dropped off almost at once, muffled by the thick canvas. The tent was small, barely tall enough for me to sit upright in without my head brushing the peaked ceiling, lit by a single oil lamp hanging from the center pole that threw uneven shadows up the walls every time the flame guttered. A bedroll took up most of the floor, thin and patched in multiple places but relatively clean, and a wooden bowl sat beside it filled with water that smelled faintly metallic when I leaned closer.

I sat down on the bedroll and the straw stuffing crackled loudly under my weight, sharp in the sudden quiet. I pulled one foot up and found the sole raw and gritty with dirt. *"Shoes. First thing I buy."* The fabric under my hands was rough, woven from something coarser than cotton, and the tent wall, when I reached out to touch it, was thick and heavy, oiled or waxed against rain and wind. The ground under the bedroll was hard-packed dirt, uneven in places, and the cold seeped up through the thin padding, through my bare feet and the rags I was wearing.

This was where I'd be staying until I gave them an answer. *"Three days. I asked for them."*

Outside the canvas walls, the camp kept moving around me, life going on whether I was part of it or not. Footsteps passed close to my tent, boots scuffing on the packed earth. Farther out, past the immediate camp sounds, a dog barked once and went quiet, answered a moment later by another bark from a different direction. Wind moved through the tree line beyond the camp boundary, branches creaking, and underneath that came the distant sound of something larger moving through the underbrush, probably some kind of wildlife I couldn't see from here.

I pulled the two green vials out of my inventory, and they materialized in my palm, and I turned them over in my green hands, watching the lamplight catch in the glass and make the liquid glow faintly. The number stamped beneath each one was clear and easy to read: ¥8,400.

Plus whatever else I could farm with Gambler's Gambit once per day. I set the vials down carefully on the ground beside the bedroll so they wouldn't roll away. *"Not a bad first login."* I scrubbed a hand over the back of my neck, the skin there rougher than I was used to, and grinned at nothing for a second.

Once I was back online I'd just accept his mission and get things moving. *"A clan at my back and a quest nobody else has. That's a start."* The weight came in behind it anyway: Chiyo in Kyoto, Tochi sitting there as an option I'd been avoiding for months, Mom in the hospital and the surgery I still couldn't pay for.

The camp kept breathing around me outside the canvas, fire and low voices and the smell of woodsmoke still thick in the air even here.

In the corner of my vision, barely noticeable until it moved, the chat window I hadn't looked at since spawning suddenly lit up, messages scrolling past faster than I could read individual lines. Then a bell rang, clear and loud enough that it should have echoed through the camp, though nobody else reacted to it.

**[SYSTEM ANNOUNCEMENT: WORLD QUEST | CALL OF THE BONES HAS BEEN ACTIVATED]**

My eyes shot up to the chat window and I watched it erupt. Messages flooded in so fast the text was nearly a blur, player names I didn't recognize all reacting at once, questions and theories and arguments breaking out in real time.

*"Damn. That's not good."* I reached for the logout button and my vision of the world went dark.
