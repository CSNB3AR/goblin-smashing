# Chapter Eight: VRSA Inspection

One word was enough to make Chiyo text me twice before I'd made it three blocks. My phone buzzed in my jacket pocket, her name lighting the screen both times, and I let it ride. The trip back to the Nakamura Grand ran itself the way the ride to the hospital had, muscle memory carrying me through turns I didn't register making, the streetlights sliding past the visor.

*"Not tonight. I'll deal with it when I'm not this pissed off."*

The frustration was still sitting hot in my chest when I parked the Haya EB around back, locked it, and came in through the staff door. The elevator was across the lobby.

The lobby was small and warm at that hour, pale stone underfoot, plants in the corners, one lamp burning over the front desk. Someone was behind it for the first time in weeks, her back to the door, reaching up to a shelf, ink running down both forearms and white hair twisted into a messy bun.

I didn't say a word to her. She was busy, and I walked past before she could turn around and say welcome to the Nakamura Grand. If she had, I'd have had to answer, and tonight whatever came out of my mouth would have been snapped at somebody who hadn't done anything to me. I hit the elevator button with my knuckle and watched the numbers instead of the desk.

I let myself into the apartment and stood in the dark entry a second, listening to nothing.

Just the hum of the refrigerator and the sound of my own boots on the floor. I toed a stray pair of Taji's sandals back into line by the door, automatic, the kind of straightening up I did every time I got home first, and left my bag beside them. My phone went face-down on the kitchen counter without a glance, and I headed straight for the shower.

The water came out hot enough to sting, and I stood under it longer than I needed to, letting the heat work through the knot sitting between my shoulders, the anger starting to loosen into something closer to exhaustion.

I pressed my forehead against the tile, water running down my face, and made myself breathe through my nose once, slow, the way I did when there wasn't a clean answer waiting on the other side.

I wouldn't even be this mad if I hadn't had to find out from two nurses who thought I was already gone. I let the water hit the back of my neck a second longer. *"Why didn't she just tell me? Did she actually think I couldn't handle it?"*

The thought didn't make me less angry. It just made the anger sit somewhere different.

I shut the water off, dried off fast, and pulled on a pair of sweats. On my way through the front room I scooped the phone off the kitchen counter and grabbed my bag from beside Taji's sandals without breaking stride. My feet took me down the hall to the last door, the room that used to be Dad's. Since the funeral I'd slept on the couch next to Taji and stayed out of here as much as I could. It was the only place in the apartment where nobody would ask me anything.

I left the overhead off. The desk lamp was enough. The room still smelled of old solder and cold circuit boards, the same as the night the letter slid out of the manual, and the two pale grooves the wheelchair had worn still ran along the floorboards from the desk to the door. The shelf above the desk sat empty. I took the manual out of the bag and set it on the desk under the lamp, its cracked spine catching the light, the letter still folded somewhere in the controller diagrams where I'd tucked it.

I dropped onto the bed I hardly ever slept in and sat there with the phone dark in my hand, the lamp buzzing faintly. *"You carried all of it by yourself too. The Tabuchis, the bear, whatever you couldn't put on paper."* He'd still found a way to leave me something where I'd look. My shoulders came down a notch, and my breathing slowed without my asking it to. For the first time since the funeral, the room felt a little like mine, and I thought I might actually sleep in here tonight.

Only then did I actually look at what she'd sent.

Two texts, both short, sent a minute apart.

**[Chiyo: You doing okay? Haven't heard from you in a bit.]**

**[Chiyo: Let me know when you get home safe.]**

Nothing new about Tochi in either one. She'd already asked what changed, and all I'd sent back was one word, same as the note she'd left on the counter that morning, already gone before I'd even gotten up.

I typed a reply, cut it down twice, and sent the shortest version.

**[Home. I'm good.]**

Short, on purpose. Right now this was mine.

The phone buzzed again before I'd even set it down.

**[Chiyo: I love you. Get some sleep.]**

I didn't answer that one. It sat on the screen longer than I wanted, and I closed the thread without typing anything back. The banking app was one more thing to check before I let myself sleep.

**[BANK BALANCE: ¥64,120]**

Not much. But it was mine. The corner of my mouth lifted before I caught it.

Whatever this turned into, it wasn't going toward the Tabuchis. Mom came first, but I had to somehow get them off me. I doubted paying them off would be that simple.

I scrolled down out of habit, the way I always checked my own numbers, and the second account caught me before I could stop it. Still listed under Daichi Kazehaya, and somehow still mine to see. Nobody had ever bothered pulling me off it.

**[KAZUHAYA TECH - Daichi Kazehaya]**

**[BALANCE: -¥3,014,200]**

**[COLLECTIONS NOTICE: TABUCHI HOLDINGS]**

I stared at my own balance first, then Dad's, then the name sitting under the collections notice, and Eren's face showed up uninvited, easy and entitled the way it always looked right before it said something meant to sting.

They'd already taken enough. Now they wanted more on top of it. My jaw set. *"I'll be damned if I hand them a single thing Dad wasn't willing to give them himself."*

*"Why is this still in front of me?"* The account sat under my own login like it belonged there. I scratched at my goatee and turned that over, and came up with nothing. Whatever the answer was, I wasn't getting it tonight.

I set the phone down for real this time, lying back against the pillow. If there was anywhere I could fix this, it was HGO. I let myself actually feel that, not the anger from earlier, something closer to the thing I'd been chasing since I was sixteen.

Debt came first. The family didn't need more pressure right now, least of all Chiyo. If everything played out right I got Mom better. The Christmas Day prize was the only number in this game big enough to matter, and the clock on it was already running. The fatigue was shifting in now, the good kind.

My eyes closed on a low hum of excitement underneath the exhaustion.

• • •

Morning came with sunlight cutting through the blinds and the alarm still a few minutes off. I checked my phone. 7:31 AM. December 12th.

I sat up, a loc falling loose across my face that I pushed back automatically. For once I wasn't behind on anything. *"Good. Research before Leon."*

I took a quick shower to shake the last of the night off, then crossed to the closet I shared with the twins. My head nearly touched the ceiling frame when I stepped inside, which still caught me off guard sometimes.

*"Wild that I'm actually in HGO now."* I pulled a red hoodie off its hanger, grabbed clean joggers, my Humu 1's off the floor.

Not the race I wanted. I'd wanted the Elf, the Spellforger build I'd drawn out myself, and the game had handed me a goblin. *"But honestly? I might have landed something good by accident."* Funny how that worked. I tugged the hoodie down.

Before I touched the laptop I dealt with the room. I wiped the dust off the shelf where the manual used to sit, squared the desk under the window, and gathered the loose papers from the corner into the bottom drawer. A room I could actually work in, if I ever got the time.

The drawer wouldn't close. A manila folder had slid crooked inside it, the corner gone soft like it had been handled a lot, and the tab said **[Kazuhaya Tech]** in Dad's slanted handwriting. I pulled it out and sat down in the desk chair.

The will was on top. Nobody had hidden it from me. It had been sitting in an unlocked drawer the whole time, and I'd never had the chance to read it, or maybe the nerve. I read it now.

**[I leave Kazuhaya Tech, its name and its rights, to my son, Tatsuya Kazehaya. I ask Chiyo Nakamura to keep the company safe until he is ready to take it on.]**

I read it twice. *"That's why the account's on my login. It's mine. It was always going to be mine."* I still owned Dad's company, which meant I could still use his name. *"Bring your business here."* Kazuku's line from the broadcast came back to me, and this time it landed differently. *"I have one."*

The rest fell into place one piece at a time. The Tabuchis kept coming because the company was still open, and as long as it was open and owing, they had a legal claim on it. Chiyo had lawyers on it and could fight in court all she wanted. The only way she wins is if I knock that debt down, because once we owe them nothing, everything they do after that looks like plain harassment. Right now my name sat on the debt, and that was the only reason they could keep knocking.

*"Three million to the Tabuchis. About four for Mom's surgery. Seven million."* I said it out loud and scratched at my goatee while I ran the number again. It wasn't impossible. And at least now I knew what I was swinging at.

I put the folder back in the drawer and shut it.

I grabbed the laptop off the counter on my way to the kitchen stool and started typing before I'd even sat down fully.

**[Shiv | HGO lore]**

Almost nothing came back official. Three sparse lines from the game's own codex, two dead forum threads from launch week, both abandoned. I scrolled through them twice and turned the brightness down against the dim kitchen, and gave up.

*"Alright, fine. I'm learning this one the hard way."*

I backed out and pulled up my character sheet, skill list on the left, thumb scrolling. Gambler's Gambit sat there with barely a paragraph under it, the World Quest tag pinned at the top of my log, both active, both about as explained as nothing. I closed a patch-notes post, new World Quest content confirmed rolling out, and sat with that a second.

Everyone out there already knew it was live. That was a target on me whether I wanted one or not. I pulled the hoodie sleeves down over my knuckles.

I switched to Loop, the goblin still sitting on my profile from last night.

After swiping through Loop for a bit I pulled up the Companion App next. Streams stacked down the front page, viewer counts running six figures on some of them, chat scrolling too fast to read on the biggest ones.

There could be real money if I decided to stream the World Quest, but that came with a huge target on my back.

I'd read enough threads to know what higher-level players did when they wanted something someone else had. World Quest items came back bound, the forums were consistent on that much, but bound didn't mean safe.

If someone decided I was worth the trouble, I wouldn't have an answer for that yet.

I almost closed the tab. Then the words Sevish said echoed in my head.

Sevish did say I would be an ally of Thrash, and it held as long as I didn't blow the quest. Two powerful NPCs plus a warband of goblins backing me meant anyone coming after me needed an actual party.

Half the accounts that could actually field that kind of party were sitting idle anyway, still deciding whether to cash out for Legacy Points before the reset made the call for them. I stretched my arms over my head until my back popped.

I pulled up the Tochi portal next. The entrance application was sitting exactly where I'd left it weeks ago, untouched. I filled in what I could from memory and stopped at the submission window. December 23rd.

Already decided. I scratched at my goatee and looked at it one more second. I needed Tochi more now than I had a week ago.

I skimmed the race comparison page while I double-knotted my laces and caught something I'd glossed over before. Every other race had hard class locks sitting right there in print. Goblin's column was nearly empty. Low base stats, almost no restrictions.

Nobody had taken this seriously because the numbers scared them off on day one. This time the grin stayed, and I let it. And if the magic path opened up on top of it, which it looked like it actually would, that was a build nobody had mapped out yet, my own caster idea on a body nobody else would touch.

I checked the time. 8:47 AM. Sakura Arcade was thirty minutes out. I put my phone in my pocket.

*"I have to move. I don't want to be stuck in camp when the Gambler's Gambit timer resets."*

I grabbed Dad's manual off the desk, shoved it into my bag with my laptop, and headed for the door.

The ride gave me room to breathe, the Haya EB humming steady under me, the route line steady across the visor, the city still waking up around the edges. I pulled my phone at a red light, Loop already open on habit.

**[@Dosuke_AI: Has anyone else noticed that the rally outside Lunarest never popped this morning?]**

**[@PK_Wraith: Lunarest or nah. somebody confirm..]**

**[@Xx_Wraithborn_xX: No doesn't look like they spawned yet]**

Dosuke noticed first. Of course he did. I killed the screen before the light changed. *"That means I'm ahead for now."*

• • •

Sakura Arcade looked different in the morning light, less like a place barely holding on and more like a place that just needed someone to actually push it. A woman was behind the counter when I walked in, white hair knotted in a messy bun, ink running down both arms to the wrist, strong and full-figured under a plain black tee, with a scowl and sharp grey eyes that landed on me the second I crossed the threshold and didn't move off.

*"That has to be Leon's sister."* Nothing in her face matched his, but the way she sized me up did. Her hair tugged at something, like I'd seen it recently, and I had no time to chase it.

"How can I help you?" She said it directly.

"Looking for Leon," I said, keeping my voice level, hands loose at my sides. "He's expecting me."

"You're the guy fixing the pods." Not a question. She jerked her chin toward the back hallway, already turning like the conversation was done, or doing a good job acting like it was. "He's back with the VRSA inspector near them. Go through."

"Appreciate it."

I held her look half a second longer than the exchange needed, then turned for the hallway.

Leon was crouched near the second pod's access panel with a man in a branded VRSA polo, both of them bent over a tablet, deep in something technical, cables spread across the floor in a tangle that looked worse than it probably was.

"Morning," Leon said, straightening up slightly, one hand still resting on the pod's frame. "Inspector's already here. Good timing."

"Take your time," I said, dropping my bag by the wall where it wouldn't be in the way, the strap landing with a soft thud against the concrete.

Leon peeled off a few minutes later, still carrying yesterday's tension in his shoulders, and came back to my side.

"Inspector's confirming what I figured," Leon said, rubbing the back of his neck, his glasses slipping slightly down his nose before he pushed them back up. "Second pod's a real project, not a lost cause."

"Good. I've got leads on parts from Dad's manual. Can pull more if we need specifics."

"Exactly what I wanted to hear." Leon leaned back against the wall, arms loose. "Two years I've been sitting on those pods thinking they were scrap." He tipped his head toward the front of the arcade and dropped his voice. "Hope Arya didn't bite. She's been asking questions since yesterday, and I'd rather she stayed out of this until we're cleared and running."

"Makes sense," I said, and left it there.

We spent the next twenty minutes going through the parts list from Dad's manual, cross-referencing against what Leon could source locally, flagging the stuff that would need Tochi's labs or a specialty supplier.

"This batch I can't get here," Leon said, tapping a line on the sheet. "Guy I know in Akihabara still stocks Kazehaya pod parts, the drive slot housing and the wiring harness, but I have to go pull them myself. Nobody ships this stuff anymore. I'll head out once the inspector's squared away."

"I can put money toward the parts," I said. "You shouldn't be fronting all of it on something that's supposed to make you money."

"We'll figure that part out." I tapped a knuckle against the cool frame of the pod and went back down the list, my eyes starting to ache from the small print.

By the end Leon looked more settled than he had when I walked in.

"Doable," Leon said. "Expensive, but doable. And if we pull it off, we're the only place in a five-ward radius with a working Kazehaya pod." He tapped the sheet once with a knuckle, which from Leon counted as celebrating. Then his face went careful. "Before you get in there, you should know what the inspector's doing back there. It affects you."

"Go for it."

"He's not fixing anything. That's you, that's the only reason any of this is possible in the first place." Leon rubbed the back of his neck. "What he's doing is deciding whether VRSA's willing to sign off on us. Think of it like a road closure. Doesn't matter how good the road actually looks, if the sign says closed, you don't drive on it, or you're the one eating the consequences, not the city."

"And the first pod? The one I already used?"

"He's putting in the external monitor. Both pods need it before either one can run anything commercially, period, that's the law, not an arcade rule."

"External monitor," I repeated slowly, turning the phrase over in my mouth, not liking the sound of it yet. "Means I need someone watching while I play."

"That's exactly what that means," Leon said. "Once you're in the game, your mind and your body are both gone. Your vitals will be on a chart, and your gameplay will be on the second monitor. Someone has to be seated there for every session, in case the operator has to eject whoever's playing. Which is you."

*"Makes sense. So Leon sees every move I make in there from here on out."* That settled badly, and I looked at the dead second monitor instead, the fluorescent tubes overhead throwing a flat white glare across its black glass.

"Nexus helmets do it with an AI watcher instead of a person," Leon went on, pushing his glasses up again, a nervous tic I was starting to recognize. "Part of why they cost what they cost. The pods don't have that, so it's got to be an actual body in the chair."

"Strict, for a game."

"Tell that to VRSA." The corner of Leon's mouth twitched, there and gone. "I'll sit the booth myself. Keeps this quiet a while longer." He rubbed the back of his neck, eyes on the floor. "While you're in there, I'm your eyes out here. I don't play, but Arya's been talking my ear off about HGO since before the beta, so I can at least tell you when something looks wrong on this side."

I didn't have anything quick to say back. I pushed a loc behind my ear and let that land.

"That works for me," I said, and meant it. "You'll be the one watching, so you should know what's behind this. My mom's in a coma, and I need money fast. I think HGO gave me something that can get it."

Leon didn't say much after that, just nodded once, slow, and let the quiet stand for a few seconds before he cleared his throat and changed the topic.

"Inspector's got maybe another hour on the install," Leon said, glancing back toward the booth. "You can get in now if you want, or wait till he's fully done."

"I'll just wait it out," I said, dropping my bag back by the counter with a soft thud. "Rather go in once everything's done than log in and out twice for no reason."

"Suit yourself," Leon said, tipping his head toward the front of the arcade.

I took a stool near the front counter while the inspector worked. Arya was cleaning down the front counter a few feet away, and the quiet between us lasted longer than I wanted it to.

"So you're the one actually breaking that pod in first," she said, not quite looking over, her hands still moving through the grips. "Leon's been weird about it since yesterday. Won't say why." A glance, quick, sharp grey eyes meeting mine for half a second before going back to the counter. "Figured I'd just ask you instead."

"I was told not to say anything right now," I said, keeping my voice easy, hands resting on my knees. "Ask Leon if you want the real answer."

"Already did," she said, and the corner of her mouth twitched, not quite a smile but close. "He changed the subject. Twice."

"Then I guess you're stuck waiting," I said.

She went back behind the counter, but her posture had shifted slightly, shoulders still tense, like she wasn't done with the conversation yet. "Fine. Can't hide it forever though. I do work here."

I let it sit there, easy enough, and killed the rest of the wait scrolling through my phone, nothing loading that actually mattered, just something to do with my hands until the inspector called it done.

By the time the inspector finished, the operator's booth had changed. A second monitor now sat on the operator's desk up on the podium, slim and modern compared to the older hardware around it, a soft green waveform glowing across its screen in steady pulses. Leon stood over it with the inspector, both of them running through something on a checklist, and Leon waved me over without looking up.

"He's signing off now," Leon said, straightening up with a small grunt, one hand on his lower back.

The inspector clicked something on his tablet, nodded once in that flat, procedural way people did when they'd done the same task a hundred times, and peeled a small certification sticker onto the pod's frame without any ceremony.

"You're clear for sessions," the inspector said, his tone as flat as his expression. "Standard operator protocol applies the whole time, no exceptions."

"Got it," Leon said. "Appreciate you fitting us in on short notice."

The inspector packed up his gear, tablet and tools disappearing into a branded case, and headed for the front without another word.

Leon dropped into the chair at the operator's booth, the monitor already syncing to the pod's feed, green lines stabilizing across the screen.

"Alright," Leon said, glancing over the readouts once, then back to me. "Core's reading nineteen, and you said it burns about a point a dive. Since this thing was never built for HGO, we're gonna need to take breaks. If we don't, it'll overheat, and that's not safe for you to be in if it does."

*"Right. Makes sense."* I climbed into the pod, the padded interior cool against my back, and pulled up my account settings on the small HUD that flickered to life in my vision. Based on what I'd read in the forums, you needed to preset streaming before entering and loading the game, not after.

I flipped it so I could go live as soon as I got back in HGO.

**[STREAM STARTING IN 60 SECONDS – NYKRI]**

No names blinked into the chat window yet. Just me and an empty feed, and I couldn't tell if that was a relief or the opposite.

*"Alright. Let's get it started."*

Nineteen now. Eighteen when I came back. The visor lowered, sealing me in, and the world outside went dark.
