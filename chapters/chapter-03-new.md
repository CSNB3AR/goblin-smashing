# Chapter Three: Into the Game

I woke up wrong, sluggish and heavy, on the couch where I'd slept most nights since the funeral. Traffic noise leaked up from the street, a scooter whining past, a shutter rattling somewhere, and under it the fridge humming in the kitchen. Sunlight instead of the dim pre-dawn gray, warm on my eyelids in a way that said it was way past morning.

My phone was still in my hand, the screen dark and cool against my palm. I thumbed it awake: **3:04 PM**.

*"No. No, no, no."* I was off the couch before I was fully awake, the blanket sliding to the floor, my heart already going. *"The morning shift. I told Gato I'd cover it."* Extra hours, extra money, and I'd slept straight through it. *"Gato is going to kill me."*

A message from him sat at the top of the screen, sent before seven.

**[Opened up myself. Figured you'd be dead on your feet after the night shift. Don't sweat it, kid. Sleep. - Gato]**

I read it twice. *"Damn. He ain't tripping."* The panic drained out of me and I dropped onto the arm of the couch. A day off, six days with Chiyo gone, and a game waiting. But I couldn't grind that and keep picking up shifts. Sooner or later I'd have to call Gato and step back.

The television was still paused on the soldiers marching through the gate. Momo's door stood open, her room empty, the bed made lopsided the way she did it on her way out. Taji's side of the couch was cold.

A note sat on the kitchen counter, folded once, my name across the front in Chiyo's handwriting.

**[Left early with the twins for the Kyoto trip, didn't want to wake you after the night you had. We'll be back in six days. A temp takes the front desk this afternoon. Please eat something real, not just convenience store rice balls. Chiyo]**

I smoothed the fold. *"The trip. Right."* It had been on the fridge calendar for two weeks and I'd still lost track. I opened the fridge, found the container of rice and grilled fish she'd left, and ate it standing at the counter, cold. Six days with nobody home but me.

My phone buzzed. A text from **Nexus Enterprises**.

**[Your HGO Access Drive order has been processed and is ready for pickup. Please collect within 24 hours of this notification. Pickup Code: 4472-KAZE]**

The sale clock had a fifth of its seventy-two hours gone. *"Okay. I need to go pick this up. Today."* I killed the television before I could look at it any longer. Corporate could run HGO on every screen at the store, but not in my living room.

I showered fast, hot water on my neck until the smell of the store was gone, then wrung out my locs and tied them up high, a few strands left loose along my face. In my room I pulled on my black and red Urawa Red Diamonds jersey, the club Dad always followed, with black joggers, my hoop in and the chain swinging, jacket over my arm.

Out in the living room I dropped onto the couch, hair still damp, and opened the HGO Companion App on my phone. WattsUp made every one of us put it on our phones in case a customer asked about the game, and I'd told myself that was the only reason. It wasn't. I hated how much of it I knew.

The feed opened on a headline. **[Season Zero final: London Spire, Guild Leader Edmund Vane, 10,800. Aogiri, Guild Leader Lin Haoyu, 10,250. Stillwater, Guild Leader Dosuke AI, fourth. Kansai Black Lotus, ninth.]** *"Fourth. Good for him."* Dosuke was the one player I'd actually sat and watched, and his build looked far too much like the one Eren and I used to draw on our tablets. I'd never said that to anybody. *"Eren's guild got ninth. Bastard. Probably carried by Haruto."* If I was going to make anything in there, I'd have to be bigger than that, and it was going to be harder with every new player pouring in. I scratched at my goatee and tapped over to the market.

The auction board was packed. Unique legacy items from the closed beta sat at the top with millions on them, prices you read twice. Under them, new listings stacked up with every refresh, fresh gear and raw materials from players who'd started over a few hours ago, everybody back on the same line. A posting fee and a cut on every sale. *"That tax eats most of it before the seller sees a yen."*

*"Everything's new. Nobody's ahead."* The closed beta was over and the gates were open, and a whole wave of players was pouring in. I was part of that wave. *"I need to ride the new-game momentum. Get in early, get lucky, land something I can sell."* Crownfall's ten million was the only official prize pool, but the way this game was set up, you could make money anywhere. It wasn't only players coming in, either. Businesses were, because of the market. I pulled up the coin quote, **[HGO COIN: ¥2.00 | STEADY ALL SEASON]**, and it hadn't moved in months. *"This isn't play money."* I needed a balanced build, one that could scale across everything the game had, not just the first hour.

The race table sat on the same page. I opened the Elf row without really deciding to. Spellforger, Wisdom stacked high, the build I'd spent months drawing out with Eren and never once played. Dosuke ran a Spellforger of his own, a unique one. Eren's had hidden mechanics, mist and a few others, that you had to unlock and that he never touched. Haruto had looked straight through him last night. *"I'd just like to see who the brain behind that build looks like when it's actually playing it."* Eren had the build. He didn't know how to run it. And it was a closed-beta build. After a balance pass it might not even be playable, so I'd treat all of it as experimental, a new game: more players, new stuff, probably a new race, fixes.

I caught myself grinning at the screen and checked the empty room to make sure nobody had seen. Half excitement, half nerves. Dad's game, the world he said he helped create, and at least I had an idea what I'd build instead of walking in blind. *"If I run into that son of a bitch in there, I can't be the one who gets outmatched."* It wasn't revenge. The last thing I wanted was to hand Eren any kind of recognition. Haruto's face crossed my mind with it.

*"Six days."* Chiyo would be back by then, and I wanted something of value to show her. *"I've never played a minute of it, only watched."* I couldn't let how I used to feel about this game decide how I went in. Clear head. My goal was to start making a positive income, for Mom, the twins, Chiyo. I was doing it for them. I was perched on the edge of the cushion, and I made myself lean back.

My bank balance sat as a widget at the top of the screen.

**[¥47,320]**

Every month I paid Chiyo what she asked, groceries and a small bill. It was never the real number, and we both knew it. It still felt good to put it in her hand.

I opened Loop, the big social app where everything happened, gaming included. I followed the HGO account, Yuna, a few major accounts like Dosuke AI and a few guilds, to keep up with updates. I couldn't stay dormant and keep up with the speed of this.

Then the cafe booking app, to see if I could get in somewhere, since I didn't own a rig. Second-gen still meant full-body rigs. The third-gen helmets that came out with the beta ran HGO and the other VR games, full dive, a lot easier to keep at home, and they cost like a flagship phone, close to ninety thousand yen, which I didn't have. The sale and the open beta had every cafe in the city booked solid, third-gen waiting lists stretching into next year. Second-gen cafes ran pods built by developers the Tabuchis had put money into. The developers hadn't done anything to me, and I still wasn't putting a yen into anything the Tabuchis stood behind. The earliest opening was March anyway. *"Three months. Chiyo's not going to wait three months. She'll have me back at Tochi."* It was why I'd never gone deep into other VR. Every mainstream game ran on second-gen.

I went down every list and every source. Nothing. Then, at the bottom of the third page, a gray tag: **[Legacy Hardware - First-Gen Pods Available]**. Sakura Arcade. Limited compatibility with newer titles. **[Free after 5 PM on weekdays.]**

First-gen. *"Dad."* That was all I thought for a second. I'd never seen his work out in the world. It could be an old place with a listing nobody had taken down, and the listing itself said it couldn't do much. *"This might not work at all."* But had anyone even tried? It was a gamble, and it was my only lead. I'd blow the whole thing up trying before I sat around for three months. I rubbed two fingers along my goatee while the worry settled in.

3:47 PM. The window opened in just over an hour, I still had to pick up the drive, and the EB had died on me last night. I laced my boots and went down the hall to my room. It was Dad's room first, the corner he'd worked out of, with the bed I hardly slept in, the smell of old solder and cold circuit boards hitting me at the door. His toolbox sat under the desk and his manual on the shelf above it. Chiyo had boxed most of his gear and carried it down to storage a few months back so the room could be mine again. *"I should clean this place up. Start working in here again."* It felt right standing in it, and I was a little surprised how okay I was with that. The closed casket came back to me the way it did every time I stood here. *"How did Dad really die?"*

I wasn't leaving the manual on that shelf anymore. I pulled it down, the letter still hidden in the controller diagrams where I'd tucked it, and flipped to the electrical section until a note in his slanted handwriting stopped my thumb. **Aux cell sags under load. Jump at the marked terminals, then swap or recondition.** I put the manual, his toolbox and my laptop in my bag and took it all downstairs.

The front desk was empty, the bell untouched, nobody in yet. The service door to the basement storage was behind it. I took Chiyo's key off the hook and unlocked the cage. Boxes lined the shelves, labeled in Dad's blocky hand. Chiyo had moved most of his old gear down here a few months back. I dug until I found one marked EB: a jump pack, cables, a spare 12-volt cell, a coil of wire, a handful of connectors. *"Everything he built could be fixed."*

The EB sat where I'd left it around back, black paint still clean, dash dead. I propped it on its stand and plugged my laptop into the service port before I touched anything. The big pack read full. The 12-volt that ran the dash and the controller read six and a half and sagged the second I asked it for anything. *"That's why the dash died on me."* The EB was fully electric, the big pack ran the motor, but Dad never built anything without a 12-volt system, same as the Sedan, so any mechanic with a jump pack and cables could wake it. A traction pack can't be jumped. The 12-volt can. You didn't guess, you measured and let the machine show you where it hurt. It was the reason Tochi had let me in on merit, and the only part of that place I'd never had to defend. I clamped the jump pack to the marked terminals, red to red, and the dash flickered awake. I held it until the controller settled, swapped the sagging cell for the spare from the box, and let the EB's own charger top it up. Twenty minutes, and I didn't breathe easy until I pressed my thumb to the screen.

The dash took its half-second to read my print, then lit green, and the visor threw a route line across the street. *"Good. Good. We're good."* I let my forehead drop against the handlebar for one second. It ran better than it had in weeks, thanks to Dad's spares, but the box was a lot lighter than when I'd started. *"If I want to keep this thing alive past the winter, I'm going to need parts I can't pay for with forty-seven thousand yen."* Fixed enough for today.

I clipped the manual into the rear case, pulled the helmet on, tucked my locs in so I could drop the visor, and rode out toward Ginza with the route line leading the way.

• • •

Nexus Enterprises' flagship store took up half a block, glass and brushed steel rising three stories over a plaza crowded with foot traffic. I'd been down here maybe twice in my life, both times as a kid, and both times Dad had held my hand through the doors.

I pushed through into cold, clean air that smelled of new plastic and floor polish.

Third-gen helmets sat on pedestals under warm spotlights, HGO's compass rose etched into the glass around each one. A wall-sized display looped last night's battle, Yuna's voice cutting through the room every time the reel restarted.

"...Thornwatch is London's, and London Spire takes Season Zero..."

*"Of course they're playing that on repeat."* I kept my head down and kept moving.

The pickup counter sat at the back, past a row of demo pods where kids waited their turn at five free minutes. The line moved slower than the clock on my phone could spare.

"Confirmation number," the attendant said, not looking up from his tablet.

I rattled it off from memory, 4472-KAZE, and he disappeared into the back and came out with the same sleeve he'd handed everyone else.

"Drive's pre-loaded with your license." He slid a printed waiver across the counter anyway. "One character, one account. Once you pick, that's it. No refunds, we've had people come back upset. Respec exists." He tapped the line at the bottom.

**[RESPEC FEE: ¥350,000]**

My eyebrows went up on their own. "Most people don't go that route," he said.

"Yeah," I said. "I can see why." I dropped the sleeve into my backpack and turned for the door before he could pitch me a warranty extension.

Outside, the light was going orange. 4:51 PM. Sakura Arcade's window opened in nine minutes, I still had to get across town, and the place might be an abandoned storefront with a listing nobody had taken down. *"If it's a dead end, I just burned the whole afternoon."* I got on the EB before I could talk myself out of it.

The visor clicked into place, the EB caught on the first try, the hum climbing through my palms, and downtown thinned out into streets I actually knew.

• • •

Sakura Arcade sat on a side street between a closed ramen shop and a convenience store hanging on by a thread. The building was older than the blocks around it, brick and wood paneling, warm pendant lights glowing through the front windows.

*"This is it? Doesn't look like much."* I parked the EB around the side, hung the helmet on one handlebar, and went to the door with my backpack on.

Inside, warm lantern lamps hung over brick pillars and steel beams, desks and monitors down one wall, and two tall dark pods standing in the open floor with cable bundles running up their sides and a blue glow in the seams. Everything was clean, everything cared for. The rows ran back through what used to be three separate shops, the walls between them knocked out, and a staircase at the far end led down into rooms with no lights on.

Behind the counter, a guy in his mid-thirties looked up from a tablet, glasses sliding down his nose, dark hair pulled back in a small knot, an unlit cigarette tucked behind one ear and a smear of grease across his thumb.

"Can I help you?" He sat up straighter. "Walk-ins are fine. Please tell me you're a walk-in."

"I'm here for a pod," I said, stepping up. "First-gen, if you've got availability."

"I do." The hope on his face got itself under control. "Before you get excited, they're first-gen Kazuhaya Tech. They don't play well with newer titles. I've turned away a dozen HGO hopefuls since the sale hit, and I'd hate for the free part to scare you off. If you test something newer on it and the pod dies, you're paying for the pod. It's the only moneymaker I've got."

"I know," I said, level, not defensive. "That's fine."

He studied me a second, then shrugged and slid a clipboard across with a pen. "Sign in. Name, time in. You've got the back room to yourself. Only one pod's actually functional, the other one's a shell."

I wrote my name across the top line in the same clean block letters I'd used since middle school. Tatsuya Kazehaya.

He read it as he took the clipboard back. "Kazehaya." He stopped. "Any relation?"

"My dad was Daichi."

He set the clipboard down carefully. "Daichi Kazehaya. Are you serious? You're not messing with me?"

"I'm not."

He took a second, one hand flat on the counter, and then he put himself back together. "Okay. Okay. What can I do for you? Anything."

"Just the pod."

"Nobody's heard a word about Kazehaya since everything got pulled off the market," he said. "That's why the second pod's a shell. I can't get anything for it. Nexus got big off his patents and nobody says his name." He held out a hand across the counter. "Leon. My sister and I run this place. I'm sorry for your loss."

I shook it, firm and brief, and let go before my throat could do anything I wasn't going to deal with right now. "Thanks."

Leon nodded toward a back hallway. "Pods are through there, second door on the left. Take your time. No one else is using them tonight."

• • •

The pod room was clean, the shell polished, the cable bundles tied off. Kazuhaya Tech was stamped in small letters near the base, and I stood there longer than I meant to. *"Dad really did this."* Pride and grief, both, and I wasn't going to look at either one right now.

I dropped my bag beside it and pulled the sleeve out. The drive was smaller than I'd expected, black with a single port and the HGO logo etched in thin gold. While the pod warmed up I opened the manual on the floor. Three sections tabbed in his blocky hand: the first-gen pod, the Sedan, the EB, every schematic and part number he'd ever pinned down. After the last tab the pages went blank, dozens of them. *"Room for my own notes."* All of it in one book. *"No wonder they wanted it."*

I seated the drive in the port beside the headrest and climbed in. The shell hissed shut, and the display came up white on black, a plain menu with nothing fancy on it. I went into the drive. Everything looked normal.

***[KAZUHAYA TECH FIRST-GENERATION VRPOD]***

***[DRIVE DETECTED: HGO ACCESS LICENSE]***

***[LOADING...]***

The display went dark. Then the world did.

Sound came first, a low hum building underneath everything. Then light, bright enough that my eyes watered before the system adjusted. I was standing in a white void, no walls, no floor I could see.

*"Holy shit. It worked."* My shoulders dropped for the first time since I'd woken up, and the grin tried to happen before I caught it. I was actually in.

***[WELCOME TO ERYNDAL]***

A girl stepped out of the white, long white-silver hair falling to her waist, red eyes, a white dress with puffed sleeves, a small smile like she'd been waiting for me.

***[Hi there! I'm Lumi, your guide to Eryndal! Hero's Glory Online is a sandbox, so a lot of it is built by the people in it. Everything you earn stays with you, and nothing resets when you log off.]***

I knew all of it. Taji had recited it at the breakfast table like scripture, and I'd tuned out every word.

***[Eryndal is watched over by five patrons! Seranis, goddess of creation, watches over the demi-humans. Sylthara watches over the elves, Aurenor the humans, and Khazren the dwarves. And Morvaine? Their gifts come with a price, so most adventurers keep their distance! Choose your race, and the patron who favors it will bless you.]***

*"Five gods, and one of them's bad news."*

A wheel formed in front of me, each segment marked with a race name and a small icon. ***Human. Elf. Dwarf. Orc. Beastfolk. Goblin. Frostborn. Drakekin.*** I went straight to the Elf and the stats opened beside it, Wisdom stacked high, exactly what I'd come for.

***[Elf! Graceful, gifted, and born to the old magic!]***

*"That's the one."* But I wanted to see them all first, so I tapped through the others one by one, and each time Lumi said the name and started in on a line about the race. Human, balanced. Dwarf, sturdy. Orc, hard to put down. I read the numbers and let her talk, thumbing past Beastfolk, Frostborn, Drakekin, until Goblin was the last one left, the lowest numbers on the wheel.

A warmth had been creeping up the inside of the shell, and I hadn't noticed until a fan under the headrest whined up and cut out. I tapped Goblin.

***[Goblin! The cunning little—]***

Her voice stretched thin and snapped. The hum cut off mid-note, the light went out, and the white void collapsed into a weightless black. My hand shot out for something solid that wasn't there.

*"What the hell."*

Sound crackled back, warped and wrong, and Leon's voice came through the shell, muffled. "Hey, you alright in there?"

"Yeah," I called back, voice tight. "I'm fine."

"Power flickered on my end. These old pods run hot, so it could've been either." His boots scuffed away. "Let me know if it kicks again."

The pod coughed, the hum came back lower, and the light crept in. The drive in the port beside my head was lit, and it wasn't white anymore. It glowed green. The white void flickered into place around me, and the wheel was back, but it wasn't spinning. Lumi was gone. The Goblin segment burned the same green as the drive, locked in and waiting for me.

***[RACE SELECTED: GOBLIN]***

***[Now, which of the five will you ch—]***

The line broke off mid-word, the letters fraying at the edge like a bad signal. I stared at the empty spot where she'd stood.

***[PATRON ASSIGNED BY DEFAULT: SERANIS, GODDESS OF THE DEMI-HUMANS]***

*"I didn't pick a goddess either. Apparently the game picked one for me."*

***[PROCEEDING TO ATTRIBUTE ALLOCATION...]***

*"No. No no no. That's not what I picked. I wanted the Elf."* I reached for the wheel, trying to go back, but the interface didn't respond. The wheel was gone. Only the confirmation screen remained, and under it, one button glowing soft white.

***[CONTINUE]***

*"This can't be real."* Even as I thought it, I knew it was.

I pressed **[CONTINUE]**, not because I wanted to, but because there was nothing else to press, no back button, no cancel, just forward into whatever this was about to become.

The screen dissolved, and a new set of numbers began to form in front of me, floating at eye level, waiting to tell me exactly how bad this was.

**[END CHAPTER THREE]**
