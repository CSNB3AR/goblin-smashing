# Chapter Four: Into Bloodhurst I

Dirt, cold and gritty against my cheek.

I lay there with my face in it, too heavy to lift. Ash and woodsmoke filled my nose, and under it something wet and mossy. A fire popped and settled somewhere close, its heat lying along my left side like a hand, and past it came the steady hiss of water falling a long way. The ground under my ribs was packed hard and slightly damp.

The noise sorted itself out slowly, a layer at a time. Voices arguing in a Japanese that didn't quite sync with the mouths making it, metal striking metal, fire eating through green wood with a wet crackling snap. Then smell, gamey meat half-burned over a flame, and under it the sharp smell of bodies packed too close in leather that hadn't seen water in way too long.

I got my eyes open and the world swam. Black and orange, a smear of firelight with nothing sharp in it, and at the edges pale rectangles flickering in and out like a screen trying to boot. I squeezed my eyes shut and opened them again until the smear started to hold.

I pushed up onto my forearms and the arms were wrong. Green skin where my own dark brown should have been, clawed fingers at the ends of arms that read too long for the rest of me. Rags hung off me, rough-spun and patched with mismatched leather, the stitching crude and uneven.

*"Shit."*

My chest was going before the thought finished. I stayed on my forearms and didn't move. *"Chill. Don't trip. I've been through way worse than this."*

Dropping out of Tochi. The funeral. A goblin wasn't the worst thing that had happened to me this year. I let my breathing slow, and the first practical thought came in, and I held on to it because it was a way out.

*"Okay, can I fix it? Go back to Nexus, tell them the power cut hit mid-creation, get Leon to back me up."*

I lay there with it. The counter, the attendant with the round glasses, Leon vouching for me with that unlit cigarette behind his ear. It almost sounded like it could work.

Almost. Then I heard the next question coming out of the attendant's mouth.

*"So what was it running on?"*

A first-gen pod, and a game it was never built for. Leon's pod, and Leon had no idea what I'd done to it.

*"Damn, that won't work. To get him to back me up I'd have to tell him about the hardwire, and I'd lose him right there."*

The attendant I could handle. Leon I'd already lied to, and I'd be burning my only open door.

I stayed down until the picture stopped playing. It was a goblin. The Elf was gone, and lying in the dirt wasn't going to change it.

*"Alright. It is what it is. Adjust."*

I got my feet under me and my balance was completely wrong. Everything sat too low, my center of gravity somewhere it had never sat before, the hem of the rags dragging past knees that weren't the right height for it anymore. A little over six feet had turned into maybe five, my eyes level with the shoulders of the goblins by the fire, my chest narrow, my ribs countable through the rags.

A water barrel sat nearby with the firelight rocking on its surface. I stepped closer and the face looking up at me was sharp-featured and green, yellow eyes blinking when I blinked, pointed ears I could feel against the sides of my head when I turned. No locs, no goatee, nothing of me in it but the stare.

I tried to grab for a menu the way the streamers did, a flick of two fingers in the air. Nothing. I swiped the other way and felt like an idiot, then thought *menu* as hard as I could. A pale panel bloomed in front of me and I flinched hard enough that a nearby goblin turned to stare, one eyebrow raised.

***[WELCOME TO ERYNDAL, LUNAREST OUTSKIRTS]***

***[CURRENT LOCATION: BLOODHURST, THRASH CLAN TERRITORY]***

***[HEALTH: 50/50] [STAMINA: 25/25] [MANA: 25/25]***

**[Courage: 4 | Power: 4 | Wisdom: 2 | Speed: 7 | Fortune: 9 | Energy: 4]**

**[UNSPENT POINTS: 2]**

*"Two Wisdom. Man, come on."* I closed the panel. The points could wait until I knew what I was spending them on.

Heat off the nearest fire reached my skin and made me step back from it, and the smell of whatever was cooking behind me made my mouth water even though I hadn't been hungry a second ago. I flexed a clawed hand and watched it move, each finger answering with a sharpness my real hand had never had.

With my eyes clear, the camp came up out of the dark in pieces. Hide-covered tents leaned on heavy timber frames, their roofs gone gray with smoke, and the dirt between them was worn into paths by a lot of boots. A stone-ringed fire pit sat nearest me with a thin line of smoke climbing off it, and further in a bigger fire, built up enough to roast something the size of a pig, lit a cart stacked with hay bales and a ragged line of sharpened stakes along the edge of the clearing. Two blue banners hung limp on their poles. Behind the tents the hillside went up into wet black rock with pines standing against it, and somewhere up there the water I'd heard came down in a long pale thread and ran off into a stream.

Every goblin I could see carried a white marker floating over their head, faint and easy to miss unless I looked directly at it.

*"White tags. Friendly. To each other, at least."*

None of it looked staged. A cluster near the smaller fire sat sharpening blades on whetstones, the scrape of metal on stone landing in a rhythm that kept going whether I was watching or not. Two goblins arm-wrestled for coin near a crate, a small crowd shouting encouragement in a dialect that only half-resolved into Japanese. Someone sang off-key by the big fire, catching thrown scraps of food and laughing when they hit him in the face. An older goblin sat outside a tent mending a torn shirt, needle moving with the patient, practiced motion of someone who'd done this repair a thousand times before. A smaller goblin shot past my legs at a full sprint, cackling, waving a stolen boot over its head.

A notice slid in at the edge of my vision.

**[RARE VARIANT APPLIED: GAMBLER'S GAMBIT]**

*"Wait. Gambler's Gambit?"*

I pulled up the description, thumb moving through the motion without thinking about it anymore.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT - RARE RACIAL ABILITY]**

**[PASSIVE: +3 Fortune (applied via Gambler's Earring, equip required)]**

**[ACTIVE: Once per day, after combat ends, roll for a chance at Rare or higher quality loot. Fortune stat increases success rate.]**

I read it again, slower this time. Once per day I could roll for rare gear. At level one. I had no idea how to trigger it, but for the first time since the wheel stopped, I wasn't only angry.

Still, I was a goblin in rags with two Wisdom, and I needed a minute away from the smoke to think. My finger hovered over the logout command when another notification bloomed in front of me, this one framed in purple instead of white.

**[WORLD QUEST DETECTED IN PROXIMITY]**

**[CALL OF THE BONES - STAGE 1]**

**[AVAILABILITY: GOBLIN RACE ONLY]**

**[ESTIMATED VALUE: WORLD-TIER REWARD]**

**[LOGOUT UNAVAILABLE: WORLD QUEST ACTIVE]**

The race I'd brushed past by accident had handed me a World Quest. *"A world quest? On the weakest race in the game? Come on."* I reached up to scratch my goatee and my claw found smooth skin, and the miss pulled a short, ugly laugh out of me. I didn't love any of this. A world quest was still a world quest.

Six days to show Chiyo something real, and Crownfall's ten million yen still a couple of weeks off, and I had no idea how a level-one goblin got anywhere near it. I winced at my own math. I tapped logout again anyway. Gray. *"Alright, bet. If I'm stuck here, I'm not about to be the worst goblin in this camp."* I'd watched people grind this game for a year. Time to find out if I could. The gamble was already made, and the only way out was through.

Another notification sat quiet beneath it, gold-bordered and easy to miss against everything else demanding my attention.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT ITEM AVAILABLE: GAMBLER'S EARRING]**

I thought about equipping it the same way I'd navigated the menu a minute ago, and a small bronze earring materialized against my right ear, dice carved into its curve in fine detail, a new weight I could actually feel there when I turned my head.

**[GAMBLER'S EARRING - RARE]**

**[+3 FORTUNE]**

**[ABILITY: LUCKY BREAK - Once per day after combat, roll for a chance at Rare or higher quality loot. Fortune stat increases success rate.]**

**[Fortune: 12]**

Twelve Fortune at level one. I touched the earring with one clawed finger and felt it shift under my touch. *"Twelve Fortune on day one. I wonder how I can use this. Whatever I knew about it could've been patched, so I gotta test it."*

I was still taking it in when raised voices cut through the camp noise, sharp enough to pull my attention. One of them was pitched too high to be casual, cracking on the top end the way a voice does right before it either breaks completely or steadies out into something deeper. Something about the sound caught me before I'd consciously registered why.

Two goblins had a smaller one backed against a supply crate near the central fire, close enough the kid had nowhere to go. One of them shoved a claw into the kid's chest hard enough to knock him back, shoulders hitting wood with a dull thud.

"You're in my spot, runt," the bigger one said, voice carrying across the camp. "Move."

Another goblin stepped up beside the first, arms crossed. "You heard him. Move."

"I was here first," the kid said, voice cracking, hands coming up defensively but not actually pushing back.

My eyes swept the camp. Goblins nearby kept working, sharpening blades, tending fires, a few glancing over but nobody moving to step in. The kind of scene people noticed and decided wasn't their problem.

*"Two on one over a fire spot? That's weak."*

I tapped two claws against my thigh, working through whether this was actually my problem or just felt like it. It had felt like mine for a while now, in a lot of places that weren't this camp.

*"I need the reps anyway. Better to find out what breaks now."*

The two enforcers stepped closer to the kid, one reaching for him.

*"Alright, fuck it. Let's see what this build can do."*

I was moving before I'd finished the thought, boots hitting soft dirt as I crossed the open ground, not rushing, just deliberate, committed once I'd started.

The two goblins clocked me coming, turning to look, more curious than concerned.

I stepped between them and the kid, close enough they had to adjust their stance, my weight balanced and my hands loose at my sides.

"Yo, we good here?" I said, my voice coming out higher and rougher than my own. "Don't really need two grown-ass goblins pressed up on a kid over a fire spot."

The nearer goblin looked me up and down, taking in the rags, no visible weapons, the way I was standing as if I knew exactly how much space I was taking up. "Mind your business, new blood."

"Yeah, see, that's the thing," I said, tilting my head slightly. "I'm making it my business. So y'all can back off, or we can figure this out another way."

Both nameplates flickered from white to yellow.

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER - LEVEL 3]**

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER - LEVEL 3]**

*"Yellow. Neutral, I think. Might still flip."*

"You're making a mistake," the first enforcer said, hand drifting toward the blade at his belt.

"Only mistake I see is you two not bathing," I said, letting a grin pull at the corner of my mouth. "Damn, y'all really stink. That's crazy."

The second enforcer barked a laugh, short and mean.

Both tags flipped red.

**[HOSTILE]**

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER HAS INITIATED COMBAT]**

*"There it is. Didn't even need a skill for that."*

Then there wasn't time to think.

I turned and ran.

My legs moved wrong, too short, the stride narrow and choppy, but lighter now, springier with every step, my reduced weight making each push off the ground feel almost effortless next to my real body. *"Speed's doing work. Okay, that's actually clean."*

I cut between two tents at an angle they'd have to widen out to follow, wind whipping at my ears as I used the canvas shadows to break line of sight, boots kicking up small clouds of dust where the ground was packed hard.

Goblins nearby stopped what they were doing to watch, a few of them standing up from their fires or setting down whatever they'd been working on. Not many, the camp wasn't packed, but enough that I could feel the eyes tracking me.

I couldn't die here. The 72-hour death lock surfaced in my mind, something I'd skimmed in the license screen before I pressed accept. *"One mistake and I'm done."*

I brought up my skills menu with a thought, a small translucent window flickering into existence at the edge of my vision, two icons sitting there waiting.

**[GOBLIN GAS]**

**[GOBLIN FLARE]**

I focused on Goblin Gas mid-stride and the description expanded, text appearing in clean white letters.

**[GOBLIN GAS - RADIUS EFFECT]**

**[Releases a cloud of noxious gas around the user. Poison damage over time, stacks up to 4x. Reduces visibility. Can be ignited for increased effect. WARNING: User is not immune.]**

**[COOLDOWN: 45 SECONDS]**

*"Useful. But how do I actually trigger it?"* Boots pounded closer behind me, two sets of feet getting louder, a bead of sweat running into my left eye and stinging. There was no time to figure it out gently. Just use it.

I triggered the ability with a thought.

Pressure built low in my gut, sudden and mortifying even mid-sprint, and then gas vented out behind me in a thick green cloud, from exactly the place nobody wants a cloud to come from.

*"Oh, hell no. That's where it comes from? That's foul."* I covered my own nose with one hand, the smell reaching me even as I kept moving forward, putting distance between myself and the cloud spreading out in my wake. The two enforcers ran straight into it half a second later, unable to stop their momentum in time.

Coughing erupted behind me, harsh and wet. One of them doubled over, the sound of retching clear even over the noise of the camp.

**[POISON DEBUFF: STACK 1]**

I didn't slow down to watch, cutting left around another tent, and the gap between me and them widened for the first time since this whole thing started, my shorter legs eating up ground faster than they should have been able to.

Then the gap closed again. I glanced back over my shoulder and caught one enforcer peeling off down a side lane between tents instead of chasing straight, angling to cut me off.

*"They're not just chasing anymore. They're trying to box me in. Smart."*

I threaded a gap between two tents barely wide enough for my shoulders, my smaller frame making it easier than it would have been at my real height, canvas brushing both arms as I squeezed through. I came out on the other side past the big stew pot, the goblin tending it stepping back out of my way without a word, wooden spoon still in hand, eyes tracking me the whole time with an expression that read more curious than alarmed.

The cooldown on Goblin Gas ticked at the edge of my vision, numbers counting down in steady increments. **[COOLDOWN: 30 SECONDS. 25. 20.]**

Somewhere off to my left, a tent flap snapped open with a sharp crack of canvas on wood. A goblin stepped out into the open air, wild green hair catching the firelight, copper rings hanging from her ears and glinting, arms loose at her sides instead of reaching for any kind of weapon. A gold tag hung over her head, the first I'd seen in the camp. **[WYX THRASH]** Her eyes found me immediately, tracking my movement without any visible concern.

*"Gold. Someone important. And she's not stepping in."*

She didn't move to stop anything that was happening. A couple of the watching goblins picked up on that fast, started calling out to each other, half cheer and half joke, the kind of noise a crowd makes once it's decided nobody's in actual danger and this is just entertainment now.

*"Oh, y'all think this is funny. Cool."*

The cooldown hit zero, the number disappearing from my vision.

I cut around a supply crate, put six feet of clean ground behind me in two quick strides, and triggered Goblin Gas again without hesitation.

Same pressure building low, same mortifying release, same thick green cloud venting out in a radius around where I'd just been standing. This time both enforcers were close enough behind me to catch the worst of it before they could pull up or change direction, both of them running straight into the cloud at full speed.

**[POISON DEBUFF x2]**

**[POISON DEBUFF x3]**

**[POISON DEBUFF x4]**

**[NAUSEOUS]**

Both of them folded over in the green mist almost immediately, one retching outright with his hands on his knees, the other dropping to one hand flat on the ground, his whole body heaving with the effort of not throwing up.

*"Four stacks. Now for the counter."*

I reached into the small leather pocket on my rags, my fingers closing around an item that was materializing in my hand. I read the tooltip that appeared beneath it, text clean and white against the chaos of the camp around me.

**[GOBLIN FLARE - EMERGENCY SIGNAL]**

**[Fires a bright flare into the air, visible to allies on the mini-map within roughly a mile. Distress use only.]**

*"A signal flare."* My claws tightened around it, the casing rough against my palm. I looked at the two enforcers still folded over in the lingering gas, both coughing hard enough that one was spitting into the dirt. But Gas said "can be ignited." And this thing was literally on fire once I lit it. I'd see what happened.

I struck the flare against the rough edge of a nearby crate and it caught immediately, a bright orange hiss of sparks. Then I threw it low, not up into the sky where it was supposed to go, straight into the green cloud still hanging thick between the two enforcers.

The gas caught the instant the flare touched it.

A concentrated burst of flame erupted outward from the point of impact, bright and hot and loud enough that I felt the concussive wave of it hit my chest from fifteen feet away. The explosion lit up the entire camp for half a second, throwing long shadows across the tents and the watching goblins, heat washing back at me hard enough that I raised one arm to shield my face.

**[GOBLIN GAS IGNITED BY GOBLIN FLARE]**

**[CRITICAL HIT]**

**[COMBAT KILL: THRASH CLAN ENFORCER x2]**

**[EXPERIENCE GAINED: 150 XP]**

**[LEVEL UP: 2]**

**[ATTRIBUTE POINTS AVAILABLE: 4]**

**[HEALTH: 50/50] [STAMINA: 20/25] [MANA: 25/25]**

I stood there with my ears ringing, staring at two bodies sprawled in the dirt that weren't moving anymore, smoke still rising off them in thin trails.

*"Holy shit. That actually worked."* My hands trembled slightly when I looked down at them.

I hadn't meant to. I'd been testing whether the tooltip meant what it said, not trying to end anyone, and the quiet where the shouting had been sat wrong with me.

*"Four. Two from the screen I never got to use."* I dropped them into Wisdom and Speed. More of a guess than a plan. I'd find out what they did.

**[Speed: 9]**

**[Wisdom: 4]**

A leather belt sat in the dirt where the smaller of the two bodies had fallen, half-buried in the dust beside two small glass vials that caught the firelight with a faint green glow. I crossed over to them, boots crunching on the packed earth, and crouched down. I touched the belt first, and it lit up faintly in my hand, a soft pulse of light running along its length before the notification appeared in front of me.

**[ITEM CLAIMED: GOBLIN GADGET BELT - COMMON]**

**[ABILITIES: SMOKE BOMB, GAS BOMB (LOCKED, REQUIRES CRAFTING MATERIALS)]**

I turned the belt over in my hands, feeling the weight of it, the rough leather warm from sitting in the dirt near the fire. Fine. Still something. Better than nothing.

I looped it around my waist and felt it settle, the pouches hanging against my hip where I could reach them easily. Then I picked up the two vials, small and delicate in my clawed hands, a number stamped faint beneath the green glass on each one.

**[ITEM CLAIMED: MINOR ELIXIR x2 - UNCOMMON]**

**[RESTORES 30 MANA]**

**[AUCTION PRICE: ¥8,400 EACH]**

*"Eight thousand four hundred yen each."* I turned one vial over carefully. That was sixteen thousand eight hundred total. First real money this game had handed me.

A gold-bordered notification bloomed beneath the item description, brighter than the white text around it.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT PROC: FORTUNE CHECK PASSED]**

There it was. Something in my chest loosened for the first time since I'd watched the word Goblin lock into place.

I let the thought sit there, the two vials still warm in my hands from lying near the bodies. *"This might actually work. The goblin lock, the World Quest, all of it. Maybe this ain't the disaster I thought it was."*

Then the laughing died. I looked up and the whole camp had gone still, every goblin turned my way, and from somewhere behind me came the slow, heavy tread of boots.

**[END CHAPTER FOUR]**
