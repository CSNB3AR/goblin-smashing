# Chapter Four: Into Bloodhurst I

Dirt, cold and gritty against my cheek.

I lay there with my face in it, too heavy to lift. Ash and woodsmoke filled my nose, and under it something wet and mossy. A fire popped and settled somewhere close, its heat lying along my left side, and past it came the hiss of water falling a long way.

The noise sorted itself out slowly. Voices in a mixed Japanese I couldn't make out, half the words bent into something that wasn't quite the language, and under it the smell of smoke and half-burned meat.

I got my eyes open and the world swam, black and orange, with pale rectangles flickering at the edges like a screen trying to boot. I squeezed my eyes shut and opened them again until it held.

The camp climbed away from me up a slope, lit orange by the fires. A ring of stones around a fire pit sat right in front of my face with a thin line of smoke rising off it, and past it hide-covered tents leaned on timber frames, one huge one in the middle with a cart of hay bales beside it. Sharpened stakes ran along the edge of the clearing, two blue banners hung limp on their poles, and at the back a waterfall poured down the rock into a stream. The only way out I could see was one dirt track curving off to the right between the stakes. Everything else was cliff and water. Goblins moved between the tents, more than I could count from the ground.

I pushed up onto my forearms and the arms were wrong. Green skin where my own dark brown should have been, clawed fingers at the ends of arms that read too long for the rest of me. Rags hung off me, rough-spun and patched with mismatched leather, the stitching crude and uneven.

*"Shit."*

Green, clawed, too long for a body this small. There was no other way to read it. I was a goblin. *"Damn it. No way I'm getting the Elf now."*

My chest was going, and I stayed on my forearms and didn't move. *"Chill. Don't trip. I've been through way worse than this."*

Dropping out of Tochi. The funeral. A goblin wasn't the worst thing that had happened to me this year. I let my breathing slow, and the first practical thought came in, and I held on to it because it was a way out.

*"Okay, can I fix it? Go back to Nexus, tell them the power cut hit mid-creation, get Leon to back me up."*

I lay there and let it play out. Me at the counter telling the story, Leon on the line saying the power really did go. The more I ran it, the better it sounded, and the thought started to settle in.

Then the hole in it showed up on its own. Whoever was behind that counter would ask what I'd been running the game on. A first-gen pod, and a game it was never built for. That was the question I'd ask, and it was the one I'd get stuck on.

I shook the thoughts away and went to scratch my goatee. My claw found smooth skin, and I let the hand drop. *"Damn, that won't work."*

Getting Leon to back me up meant telling him the truth about the hardwire, and until I had some way to fix what I'd done to his pod, I couldn't do that. Not yet.

That was it, then. No refund, no respec, no door back. I stayed there a second longer with it.

*"Alright. It is what it is. Adjust."*

I got my feet under me and my balance was completely wrong. Everything sat too low, the hem of the rags dragging past knees that weren't the right height for it anymore. A little over six feet had turned into maybe four, my eyes level with the chests of the goblins by the fire, my ribs countable through the rags.

A goblin by the fire had been staring at me, a strip of meat halfway to his mouth. I gave him a short nod like I did this every night. He held the look a second, shrugged, and went back to chewing.

I needed to see it for myself. The water barrel sat at the edge of the firelight, and I walked over in the wrong stride, short and quick, and leaned over it. The surface rocked, then settled, and the face that came up out of it was green. Short dark green hair standing up in spikes, a narrow face, red eyes that blinked when I blinked, small fangs, pointed ears I could feel against the sides of my head. No locs, no goatee. Nothing of me in it but the stare. I made a face at it and it made one right back.

I tried to grab for a menu the way the streamers did, a flick of two fingers in the air. Nothing. I swiped the other way and felt like an idiot, then thought *menu* as hard as I could. A pale panel bloomed in front of me, a hologram of my body turning slowly in the middle of it, rags and all, with the numbers stacked down one side.

**[GOBLIN | LEVEL 1]**

**[HEALTH: 50/50] [STAMINA: 25/25] [MANA: 25/25]**

**[Courage: 4 | Power: 4 | Wisdom: 2 | Speed: 7 | Fortune: 9 | Energy: 4]**

**[UNSPENT POINTS: 2]**

*"Perfect. So much for the class I had in mind."* From what I'd read, you needed at least six just to cast anything, and that was before any patch. *"Two Wisdom. Damn, they really got me sitting here dumb as hell."*

The joke helped a little. It was still a huge climb, and unless something changed I wasn't casting a thing. I swiped the panel shut harder than I needed to, and the hologram folded away.

I started walking, short quick steps, learning the body as I went. The wind came down off the rock cold and damp. My legs ached, my ribs ached, and under it all my heart was pounding, hard enough that I could feel it in my throat. A heartbeat, in a game. This was what full dive actually was.

Every goblin I could see had a marker floating over their head. I dug back through every stream I'd watched. White was usually NPCs, friendly. Gold meant somebody important, and I saw none. Blue was other players. My own tag was blue, and everybody else's was white.

Two goblins were arm-wrestling by a crate with a crowd shouting them on. A pot boiled in the middle of camp, a goblin stirring it with a long spoon. And the biggest tent sat in the center, wide and tall, the kind of place whoever ran this camp would be. Okay. Now I knew where things were.

None of them moved like scripted characters. The one at the pot tasted the stew, made a face, and added something. The arm-wrestlers cheated with their free hands. Even the goblin with the meat had shrugged me off like I was just another guy walking by. *"Damn. Incredible."* The grin got out before I could stop it. I scratched my head and shrugged the rest off.

I flicked the panel back up to double-check logout. It still didn't answer. The same gray line slid in over it.

**[WORLD QUEST IN PROGRESS. PLEASE FINISH THE CURRENT SCENARIO BEFORE LOGGING OUT.]**

*"A world quest? How did I trigger something like that here?"*

I scrolled back to my stats, then through every menu I could remember the streamers using, hunting for anything on the quest. Quest log, map, notes. Nothing. The inventory held one thing, a small chest glowing faint gold.

**[NEWCOMER MYSTERY BOX - UNOPENED]**

I knew that box. It was the welcome gift HGO handed every new player, a way to help them get started. Most boxes gave something useful, a weapon, a skill line, a passive, an item, and the odds on anything big were low. I'd watched a few players get lucky and pull a rare or even a legendary on stream.

I sat with where I was. No way out, tied to a world quest in the open beta, and nobody to tell me what it wanted. Somewhere in there I realized I wasn't as upset as I should've been. I was more curious than anything.

*"Alright. Open it now, figure out the quest after."* I tapped the box and it popped into my hands, a small gold chest, warm and lighter than it looked, with a latch on the front. I thumbed it open.

A wheel spun up out of the lid, a roulette of glowing icons whipping past, a sword, a scroll, a ring, a flask, each one landing with a click like a slot machine. Weapon. Skill line. Item. Passive. I caught myself leaning in, muttering *"come on, come on."* It slowed. Past the common slots, past blue, past gold, clicking over one notch at a time, until it crept onto a slot that burned white-hot and stopped.

A voice rang out of nowhere, big and bright, like an arena announcer.

**[UNIQUE ITEM! GAMBLER'S GAMBIT!]**

**[PASSIVE ACQUIRED: GAMBLER'S GAMBIT]**

*"Unique?"* I read it twice. Rare, legendary, sure, I'd watched people pull those. Nobody I'd seen had ever pulled a unique out of one of these boxes. I let out a low whistle and tried to play it cool, and didn't do a good job.

*"How's that even possible?"* I flicked back to my stats and stared at the Fortune line. *"Is it the Fortune?"*

The menus had a beginner's guide tucked into a corner, a little manual that explained the game's words. I thought at it and a blue screen floated up in front of me, quicker than the first time. I was getting the hang of making the menus come when I called.

**[FORTUNE: Luck and probability. Affects critical hits, loot quality, random events and how risky gambles land, including chance encounters with NPCs.]**

*"So it's not just loot."* I wasn't going to settle it standing here, but I wanted to test it.

**[PASSIVE: +3 Fortune (applied via Gambler's Earring)]**

**[ACTIVE: Once per day, after combat ends, roll for a chance at Rare or higher quality loot. Fortune stat increases success rate.]**

**[EQUIP PASSIVE? COST: 1 SKILL POINT. PERMANENT.]**

**[SKILL POINTS: 1]**

*"Once a day, a roll at rare loot."* I tapped a claw against my thigh. *"I hate dumping points into something I know nothing about. But I gotta test it."* One point. If it was trash, I'd lose one point.

I pressed equip.

Something small clicked against my right ear. I touched it and found a single bronze die on a short hook, the pips worn smooth, a little weight I could feel every time I turned my head.

**[GAMBLER'S EARRING - UNIQUE]**

**[+3 FORTUNE]**

**[ABILITY: LUCKY BREAK - Once per day after combat, roll for a chance at Rare or higher quality loot. Fortune stat increases success rate.]**

**[Fortune: 12]**

*"After combat. So I still have to farm to get anything out of it."* But if a roll landed something that paid well, I could start chipping at what Dad owed the Tabuchis. Chiyo said she was handling it, but if that balance hit zero they'd have nothing left to come after us with, and maybe they'd finally leave us alone. I was going to need more money than I'd figured. I'd already pushed that pod past what it was built for. If it died, it was on me, and Leon had made clear what that meant. I wasn't letting something Dad built die on my watch, and I didn't know the first thing about putting together a third-gen pod. The only place I'd seen that tech was Tochi. It sat in the back of my head, and I didn't push it away.

Then there was the quest. I didn't know what this one was, but I knew what a world quest was. A world quest was what had ended the season, what sent a prince's soldiers marching through that gate. I knew how big those got. *"As an Elf I wouldn't have gotten either of these."*

It still stung about the build, and I let it. Then I set it down. I couldn't keep staring at what I'd lost, not with a unique passive and a world quest in my lap. *"Alright, bet. Let's see what I can build with this."*

A voice cut through the camp noise, louder than everything else going on.

"You're in my spot, runt. Move."

Out of every conversation in that camp, that was the one that carried. I turned my head. Two goblins had a smaller one backed against a supply crate near the central fire, and one of them shoved a claw into the kid's chest hard enough that his shoulders hit the wood.

"You heard him," the other said, arms crossed. "Move."

"I was here first," the kid said, voice cracking, hands up but not pushing back.

Goblins nearby kept working, a few glancing over, nobody stepping in. I tapped two claws against my thigh, working out whether this was my problem or just felt like it. It had felt like mine for a while now, in a lot of places that weren't this camp.

*"Probably none of my business."* But a camp this size, a world quest hanging over my head, and nobody around me talking about it. *"Somebody here knows something. If this turns into a scene, I want to hear it."*

I didn't rush in. I drifted toward the stew pot like I was after a bowl, a few steps at a time, close enough to catch the words.

The enforcers pushed in on the kid, one reaching for his collar, and the kid went quiet in a way I recognized. It had stopped being an argument.

*"Alright. Two on one, and he's not even fighting back."*

"Hey, nah." I stepped between them and the kid, weight balanced, hands loose at my sides. My voice came out higher and rougher than my own. "I'm not gonna let y'all just do that. If y'all are gonna go two on one, I'll even the odds."

The nearer goblin looked me up and down, taking in the rags, no visible weapons, the way I was standing as if I knew exactly how much space I was taking up. "Mind your business, new blood."

"Yeah, see, that's the thing," I said, tilting my head slightly. "I'm making it my business. So y'all can back off, or we can figure this out another way."

Both nameplates flickered from white to yellow.

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER - LEVEL 3]**

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER - LEVEL 3]**

*"Yellow. Neutral, I think. Might still flip."*

"You're making a mistake," the first enforcer said, hand drifting toward the blade at his belt.

"Only mistake I see is you two not bathing," I said, letting a grin pull at the corner of my mouth. "Damn, y'all really stink. That's crazy."

The second enforcer barked a laugh, short and mean.

Both tags flipped red.

**[HOSTILE]**

**[THRASH CLAN ENFORCER HAS INITIATED COMBAT]**

*"There it is. Didn't even need a skill for that."*

Then there wasn't time to think.

I turned and ran.

My legs moved wrong, too short and choppy, but lighter now, each push off the ground almost effortless next to my real body. *"Speed's doing work. Okay, that's actually clean."*

I cut between two tents at an angle they'd have to widen out to follow, using the canvas shadows to break line of sight.

Goblins nearby stopped to watch. Not many, but enough that I could feel the eyes tracking me.

I couldn't die here. The 72-hour death lock surfaced in my mind, something I'd skimmed in the license screen before I pressed accept. *"One mistake and I'm done."*

I brought up my skills menu with a thought, two icons waiting.

**[GOBLIN GAS]**

**[GOBLIN FLARE]**

I focused on Goblin Gas mid-stride and the description expanded.

**[GOBLIN GAS - RADIUS EFFECT]**

**[Releases a cloud of noxious gas around the user. Poison damage over time, stacks up to 4x. Reduces visibility. Can be ignited for increased effect. WARNING: User is not immune.]**

**[COOLDOWN: 45 SECONDS]**

*"Useful. But how do I actually trigger it?"* Boots pounded closer behind me, a bead of sweat stinging my left eye. No time to figure it out gently. Just use it.

I triggered the ability with a thought.

Pressure built low in my gut, sudden and mortifying even mid-sprint, and then gas vented out behind me in a thick green cloud, from exactly the place nobody wants a cloud to come from.

*"Oh, hell no. That's where it comes from? That's foul."* I covered my nose with one hand and kept moving. The two enforcers ran straight into the cloud half a second later, unable to stop in time.

Coughing erupted behind me, harsh and wet. One of them doubled over retching.

**[POISON DEBUFF: STACK 1]**

I didn't slow down to watch, cutting left around another tent, and the gap widened for the first time, my shorter legs eating up ground faster than they should have.

Then the gap closed again. I glanced back over my shoulder and caught one enforcer peeling off down a side lane between tents instead of chasing straight, angling to cut me off.

*"They're not just chasing anymore. They're trying to box me in. Smart."*

I threaded a gap between two tents barely wide enough for my shoulders and came out past the big stew pot, the goblin tending it stepping aside, spoon still in hand, more curious than alarmed.

**[COOLDOWN: 30 SECONDS. 25. 20.]**

Somewhere off to my left a tent flap snapped open and a goblin stepped out, wild green hair catching the firelight, copper rings glinting in her ears, arms loose at her sides. A gold tag hung over her head, the first I'd seen in the camp. **[WYX THRASH]** Her eyes found me immediately, without any visible concern.

*"Gold. Someone important. And she's not stepping in."*

She didn't move to stop anything. A couple of the watching goblins picked up on that and started calling out to each other, half cheer, half joke, the noise a crowd makes once it decides nobody's in real danger.

*"Oh, y'all think this is funny. Cool."*

The cooldown hit zero, the number disappearing from my vision.

I cut around a supply crate, put six feet of clean ground behind me in two quick strides, and triggered Goblin Gas again without hesitation.

Same pressure low in my gut, same mortifying release, same thick green cloud. This time both enforcers were close enough behind me to catch the worst of it, running straight into it at full speed.

**[POISON DEBUFF x2]**

**[POISON DEBUFF x3]**

**[POISON DEBUFF x4]**

**[NAUSEOUS]**

Both folded over in the green mist, one retching with his hands on his knees, the other down on one hand.

*"Four stacks. Now for the counter."*

I reached into a pocket on my rags and an item materialized in my hand. The tooltip came up beneath it.

**[GOBLIN FLARE - EMERGENCY SIGNAL]**

**[Fires a bright flare into the air, visible to allies on the mini-map within roughly a mile. Distress use only.]**

*"A signal flare."* The casing was rough in my palm. I looked at the two enforcers folded over in the gas. Gas said "can be ignited," and this thing was on fire once I lit it. I'd see what happened.

I struck the flare on the edge of a crate and it caught, a bright orange hiss of sparks. Then I threw it low, not up into the sky, straight into the green cloud hanging between the two enforcers.

The gas caught the instant the flare touched it.

A burst of flame erupted from the point of impact, loud enough that I felt the concussive wave hit my chest from fifteen feet away. The explosion lit the whole camp for half a second, heat washing back hard enough that I raised an arm.

**[GOBLIN GAS IGNITED BY GOBLIN FLARE]**

**[CRITICAL HIT]**

**[COMBAT KILL: THRASH CLAN ENFORCER x2]**

**[EXPERIENCE GAINED: 150 XP]**

**[LEVEL UP: 2]**

**[ATTRIBUTE POINTS AVAILABLE: 4]**

**[HEALTH: 50/50] [STAMINA: 20/25] [MANA: 25/25]**

I stood there with my ears ringing, staring at two bodies that weren't moving anymore, smoke rising off them in thin trails.

*"Holy shit. That actually worked."* My hands trembled slightly when I looked down at them.

I hadn't meant to. I'd been testing whether the tooltip meant what it said, not trying to end anyone, and the quiet where the shouting had been sat wrong with me.

*"Four. Two from the screen I never got to use."* I dropped them into Wisdom and Speed. More of a guess than a plan. I'd find out what they did.

**[Speed: 9]**

**[Wisdom: 4]**

A leather belt sat in the dirt where the smaller of the two bodies had fallen, half-buried beside two small glass vials with a faint green glow. I crossed over and crouched. The belt lit up faintly in my hand, a soft pulse running along its length before the notification appeared.

**[ITEM CLAIMED: GOBLIN GADGET BELT - COMMON]**

**[ABILITIES: SMOKE BOMB, GAS BOMB (LOCKED, REQUIRES CRAFTING MATERIALS)]**

I turned the belt over in my hands, the rough leather still warm from the fire.

I looped it around my waist and felt it settle. Then I picked up the two vials, small and delicate in my clawed hands, a number stamped faint beneath the green glass on each one.

**[ITEM CLAIMED: MINOR ELIXIR x2 - UNCOMMON]**

**[RESTORES 30 MANA]**

**[AUCTION PRICE: ¥8,400 EACH]**

*"Eight thousand four hundred yen each."* I turned one vial over carefully. That was sixteen thousand eight hundred total. First real money this game had handed me.

A gold-bordered notification bloomed beneath the item description, brighter than the white text around it.

**[GAMBLER'S GAMBIT PROC: FORTUNE CHECK PASSED]**

There it was. Something in my chest loosened for the first time since I'd watched the word Goblin lock into place.

I let the thought sit there, the two vials warm in my hands. *"Maybe this ain't the disaster I thought it was."*

A purple line unfolded under the loot.

**[WORLD QUEST: CALL OF THE BONES - STAGE 1]**

Then the laughing died. I looked up and the whole camp had gone still, every goblin turned my way, and from somewhere behind me came the slow, heavy tread of boots.

**[END CHAPTER FOUR]**
