Chapter Eight: Assembling the Assault Team

The smoke reached Tatsuya before the words did, a thin gray thread drifting low along the treeline outside the war tent, and underneath the smell of it he could still taste the ash from an hour ago, Shiv’s ash, clinging to the back of his throat no matter how many times he swallowed. His new hair kept catching the lamplight wrong, white where it should have been dark, and every time it happened his brain still snagged on it for half a second before catching up to what he was actually looking at.

The scout burst through the tent flap before anyone called him in, blood streaked down a sleeve that wasn’t his, chest heaving, one clawed hand already reaching for his knee before his legs gave out. Tatsuya caught the name etched next to the scout’s gray tag a second before the goblin opened his mouth.

**[KESH]**

“Based on his breathing, someone’s been chasing him.” Tatsuya’s eyes stayed on Kesh’s face, watching the goblin’s chest work for air.

“Kesh bring news,” Kesh gasped, his voice choppy, the words coming in bursts between breaths, one clawed hand gripping his knee hard enough the knuckles went pale green. “Kesh and others, scouting the road down the hill. Red tent, big. Intruders. Just sitting.” He scratched at the back of his head with his free hand, claws catching in matted hair, his eyes darting between Sevish and the tent flap like he expected something to follow him through it. “Kesh’s partner, he got caught. Other one too. They got him, they got him bad. Kesh barely, Kesh barely made it back.”

“Every word costs him something.” Tatsuya’s weight shifted forward without him deciding to, drawn half a step closer to the sound of Kesh fighting for air.

Sevish was already moving before Kesh finished, his voice cutting across the tent sharp and clean, the two-handed battle axe leaning against his chair already in reach. “Wyx. Get the barricades up, reinforce the eastern watch, pull everyone back inside the palisade line. Nobody engages past the treeline.” He turned toward Kesh, his scarred face set. “How many on that road, Kesh? Give me a clean count.”

“Four,” Kesh said, still catching his breath, his hand finally coming off his knee to gesture low and wide. “Maybe five. Not farmers. Kesh couldn’t, couldn’t get close enough. They saw Kesh coming, they saw everything.”

Sevish’s stance squared, his scarred hand tightening on the axe shaft. “That’s not a for-sure number, Kesh.” His tone carried an edge now, the kind that said he was running out of patience. “I take it as four for a safe number, and you’re telling me four men gave you all this trouble?”

Kesh scratched at the back of his head again, his claws catching in matted hair, his eyes dropping to the dirt. “They attacked us. Random. They have caster.”

“The wisdom of goblins is really showing at full force.” Tatsuya kept his expression flat, watching Kesh struggle to piece the report together.

Sevish’s eyes tracked to Tatsuya for half a second, something passing behind them, then back to Kesh. “Where exactly? Road shoulder, treeline, where? I need locations, Kesh.”

“Road itself,” Kesh said, his voice steadying slightly now that he’d caught his breath, both hands going to his knees this time. “Big red tent. They set up next to edge, where hill leads to road. Choke spot. Anyone coming down, they see first.”

“Damn. We’re trapped.” Tatsuya’s stomach dropped before his brain finished the thought, his weight rocking back onto his heels.

“They’re using our own rally point against us,” Tatsuya said, half to himself.

Wyx’s head turned sharp at that, her green eyes narrowing with something close to interest. “You’ve seen it.”

“Didn’t have to see it,” Tatsuya said, his voice steady. “Only one road down means whoever’s sitting on it controls whether anyone leaves at all. Whoever’s down there picked the smartest spot on the whole hill. But they don’t know it’s us up here.” He glanced back at Kesh, watching the goblin’s claws still digging into his own knees. “He said it himself, they’re not even bothered enough to move once they’ve got position. They think this is farmland.”

**[AFFILIATION: THRASH CLAN 10% → 12%]**

“Small tick. But it moved.” He kept his expression flat, his eyes on Sevish.

Sevish’s weight rocked back on his heels, his hand tightening on the axe. “Get the camp ready. Stage one defense, full perimeter. Now.”

A notification bloomed at the edge of Tatsuya’s vision, gold-bordered, bigger than anything else that had come through tonight.

**[WORLD QUEST UPDATED: CALL OF THE BONES]**
**[INTRUDERS INVADING BLOODHURST]**
**[THRASH CLAN DEFENDERS: 50/50]**
**[INVASION TIMER: 00:04:37]**

“Fifty goblins. Four minutes in. That number’s going to drop fast if this goes bad.” He dismissed the notification and pulled up his own character sheet instead, the blue UI screen resolving clean in his vision.

**[CHARACTER: TDRAG]**
**[RACE: SHIV GOBLIN]**
**[LEVEL: 7]**
**[TITLE: THE FIRST DESCENDANT OF SHIV]**

**[HP: 65/65] [SP: 32/32] [MP: 32/32]**

**[STATS]**
**[COURAGE: 4]**
**[POWER: 4]**
**[WISDOM: 6] (+4)**
**[SPEED: 10]**
**[FORTUNE: 15] (+6)**
**[ENERGY: 4]**

**[ABILITIES]**
**[BONE SURGE – WORLD TIER]**
**[SHIV-TOUCHED FLARE – TIER 1]**
**[RUNE PRIMING – TIER 3]**
**[CALL OF COMMAND – TIER 1]**
**[GAMBLER’S GAMBIT – PASSIVE]**
**[ANCIENT BONE FLAME BARRAGE – TIER 5]**

“Four points Wisdom, six Fortune. That’s the build. Speed’s sitting at ten, that’s enough for now.” He dismissed the screen and looked back at Sevish, his stance settling back to even.

Sevish was already barking orders, his voice carrying across the tent and out into the camp beyond, goblins scrambling the second his words landed. “Rott, Nab, get to the eastern line, hold position, eyes on that road, do not engage unless they move first. Wyx, I want rotation shifts every thirty seconds, nobody stays exposed long enough to draw fire.”

A second notification bloomed in the corner of his vision, smaller, gray-bordered.

**[STREAM CONNECTION LOST]**
**[RECONNECT?]**

“Stream dropped. Leon’s monitor finally gave out.” He dismissed it without answering, knowing what was coming next.

Leon’s voice cut in a beat later, no longer quiet, no longer careful, the tone tight enough to pull Tatsuya’s focus away from the tent entirely.

“I need you now,” Leon said. “Not in a bit, now. The monitor’s not holding, I can’t see anything on my end, and VRSA’s going to be on my back if I let this slide any longer. The last thing we need is these pods going down when you’re trying to fix the second one. Ten minutes, that’s all I’m asking, but I need them starting the second you can give them to me.”

“There it is. He warned me, and now he’s cashing it in.” Tatsuya rolled his shoulders once before he answered.

“Give me a second,” Tatsuya said, his voice low enough that Sevish barely caught it, then turned back to the chieftain. “Yeah, I’m in. But if you’re expecting me to run down there in prisoner rags, I won’t be very productive. You got anything better I can work with?”

Sevish studied him a beat longer, his scarred face unreadable, then nodded once, sharp. “Understood. We’ll hold stage one defense while you prepare. Wyx, get him something that actually fits.”

“Done,” Wyx said, already moving toward a crate stacked near the tent’s back wall, her hands working the latch before Sevish had finished the sentence.

Sevish’s eyes stayed on Tatsuya, something working behind them. “When you’re back, you go down with Ru. Two others ahead of you, Rott and Nab, holding the line. You end it. That’s the deal.”

“That’s the plan,” Tatsuya said.

Wyx returned a moment later with a bundle in her arms, rough-spun leather dyed a muted brown, stitched together with cord that looked like it had been salvaged from three different sources. She set it on the table between them without ceremony.

**[COMMON GOBLIN LEATHER CHEST – COMMON]**
**[+2 ENERGY]**
**[BASIC CRAFTED GEAR]**

“Common tier. But it’s something.” He picked it up, turning it over in his hands, the leather stiff and worn but intact.

“It’ll do,” Tatsuya said.

Sevish gestured toward the tent flap with his free hand, the axe still gripped in the other. “Rott will show you to a tent. Get your rest while you can. When you’re back, we move.”

Two goblins fell in on either side of him without being told to, one of them the same Rott from earlier, gas pouches still hanging at his belt. They walked him through camp in silence, past goblins hauling crates toward the palisade line, past the fire pit where someone was sharpening a blade on a whetstone, the scrape of metal on stone cutting through the low murmur of voices.

They stopped at a tent near the far edge of camp, smaller than Sevish’s but still standing on its own, a crude nameplate carved into the post beside the flap.

**[WAR TENT – ELDER T-DRAG]**

“Elder. They’re actually calling me that now.” He pushed through the flap and let it fall shut behind him.

The tent was sparse, just a bedroll, a single oil lamp hanging from the center pole, and a small crate stacked against the back wall. Ru was already there, standing near the entrance, his massive frame filling half the space.

“You went stiff again,” Ru said, watching him cross to the bedroll. “Happens every time you leave.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said, sitting down on the bedroll, the straw crunkling under him. “Had to handle something outside. I’m logging off for a bit. Need you to stand guard while I’m gone. If they push the camp, if anything happens, you keep me alive. My body’s going to be here, idle. Can’t move, can’t defend itself. You’re the only thing between me and a seven-day lock if this goes bad.”

Ru’s tusks caught the lamplight as his mouth pulled into something that wasn’t quite a grin. “Understood. Nobody gets past Ru.”

“Good,” Tatsuya said, lying back against the bedroll, the straw crackling louder this time. “Be back soon.” He logged off.

The lid hissed open and the ceiling of the operator’s booth swam into focus, too bright after however many hours his eyes had actually been closed. He sat up slow, joints stiff in a way that made him wince, and the VRSA inspector was already crouched at the monitor bay, panel open, tools laid out in a neat row on a cloth beside him.

Leon stood next to him, one hip braced against the monitor bay, glasses slipping slightly down his nose, and he looked up the second Tatsuya sat up. “Stream dropped the instant you went offline. Number reset to zero. Whatever you built up tonight, it’s not just sitting there waiting for you.”

“Have to earn it back.” Tatsuya pushed himself off the pod’s edge and rolled his neck until something popped.

“Give me the ten minutes,” Leon said, not looking up from where the inspector was working. “Go stretch your legs or something, you’ve been under a long time.”

“Yeah,” Tatsuya said, his legs stiff under him as he crossed toward the hallway. “Bathroom first.”

The bathroom at Sakura Arcade was small and smelled like the same industrial cleaner it always had, harsh enough to catch in the back of his throat after hours of woodsmoke and canvas. Tatsuya splashed water on his face before he even looked at his phone, both hands braced against the sink, needing a second to remember which world he was actually standing in.

His phone had a dozen notifications stacked on the lock screen, more than he’d ever seen at once, the Loop icon buried somewhere in the middle of the pile.

[Your follower count has grown by 2,140 in the last hour.]

“Two thousand. In an hour.” He stared at the number, water still dripping off his chin. “That doesn’t care that I logged off. Following’s not the same as watching.”

He thumbed it open and the feed loaded slow, clip after clip of the same green-skinned figure kneeling in ash and smoke, reposted, quoted, argued over. A handle he didn’t recognize had stitched together every notification bracket from the Shiv scene into a single clip captioned “did anyone else catch this live.” Another had zoomed in on the necklace, freeze-framed, circled in red, captioned “world tier confirmed, somebody check the wiki.” His own name, T-Drag, sat tagged in reply after reply, people arguing over whether it was scripted, whether the AI was real, whether HGO had planted the whole thing. None of them knew it was him behind the account. He scrolled until his thumb cramped and made himself stop, setting the phone on the edge of the sink.

He pulled up his HGO account next, and there it was, a button that hadn’t existed the last time he’d checked this screen.

[Apply for Affiliate Status]

“Five hundred followers. That’s the number. Has to be.” His thumb hovered over it for a second before he tapped it.

The confirmation screen that loaded back wasn’t the instant green light he’d half expected.

[Application received. Affiliate status requires a minimum of 30 days active streaming history. Current eligibility: Day 1.]

“Thirty days.” He set the phone down on the sink’s edge and stared at it. “The follower count doesn’t mean anything yet. It just means I’m allowed to start the clock.”

A quick search turned up the rest of it in under a minute, a forum thread somebody had laid out plain: HGO’s official channel auto-hosts any stream actively progressing a World Quest, standing platform practice, free exposure handed to whoever happened to be live when the algorithm noticed, not a permanent slot, and not something that follows him back online automatically. Coming back live after a break meant starting closer to zero and climbing again, not picking up where he left off.

“None of that was luck.” He picked the phone back up, his grip firming around it. “And none of it just comes back either. Fine. I’ll take it. Can’t count on it being there the second I log back in.”

Then the part that actually stung. Affiliate status wasn’t retroactive. Whatever those seven thousand viewers had been worth, none of it had converted, because he hadn’t cleared the threshold until after the stream that made the number climb in the first place. Every yen of it, gone the second the session ended.

Subs themselves weren’t much once he actually read the fine print. Seven hundred fifty yen a month, and whoever paid it got a couple of cosmetic emotes to throw around, nothing that let them type more than any other viewer could. Not a fortune. Still money nobody else currently had a reason to hand him, once he actually cleared the window for it.

“Should’ve been talking to chat the whole time instead of just surviving it.” He turned the phone over in his hand, once. “Lesson for next time. Actually engage or none of this converts to anything real.”

Two more notifications sat under the Loop pile, both real-world, both waiting.

[Tochi Academy Admissions: Your application has been reviewed. You are invited to sit the entrance interest exam. Reply for scheduling.]

He stared at that one longer than he meant to, his thumb going still against the screen. He’d sent that application off weeks ago, half convinced it would just sit in some queue forever, and now it was sitting on his lock screen like it had been waiting for the worst possible moment to matter.

“Not dealing with that right now.” He locked the screen and set the phone face-down on the sink. “It’ll still be there when I actually have time to think about it straight.”

He picked the phone back up and opened his banking app instead, pulled up the balance sitting at ¥64,120, and moved a piece of it into the account that covered his mother’s ongoing costs.

[Transfer sent: ¥15,000 → Ohema Kazehaya, Medical.]

“Small.” He held the confirmation screen for a second longer than he needed to. “But it’s real, and it’s mine, and it’s not a promise this time.”

He thought about Chiyo seeing that line item whenever she finally checked the statement, and for the first time in a while the thought didn’t make him brace for an argument.

He opened his texts next and typed one out to Gato before he lost the nerve.

[Hey. Gonna need tomorrow off. Been thinking hard about going back to Tochi, actually doing something with it this time. Wanted you to hear it from me instead of me just not showing up. I’ll come by and talk properly when I can.]

The reply came back faster than he expected.

[No worries at all. Proud of you for even considering it. You know you’re always welcome here whenever, shift or no shift. Go handle your business.]

“That’s Gato for you.” The corner of his mouth pulled flat, something close to a smile before it was gone.

He splashed more water on his face, the cold biting against his skin, and gripped the edge of the sink with both hands. Seven days locked out if he died down there. Seven days he didn’t have, not with Winter Bash sitting fifteen days out, not with no duo locked in yet, not with a hospital bill that didn’t care what game he was playing. This was the first real fight he’d ever be in against actual people, not scripted mobs, not a camp of goblins who’d already decided not to kill him, and right now it was still just him and Ru walking into it.

“No zone to hold, no point to capture, but that’s still a team fight.” His grip on the sink tightened once, then loosened. “Real players, real stakes, real chance I walk away from it having actually proven something instead of just surviving another quest chain.”

Something else surfaced while he stood there, a system flag he’d never actually gone looking for. He’d crossed level five somewhere in tonight’s jump without ever checking what that unlocked.

[Arena queue eligibility: unlocked at level 5. Ranked and open-ladder PvP now available.]

“Could’ve been queuing ranked matches for two levels now and never noticed.” He let out a short breath through his nose, his reflection doing the same back at him from the mirror. “Guess tonight’s where I find out if I can actually hang.”

He filed it away, not something to chase mid-crisis, but it sat differently now, a door that had already opened without him.

“Wish I had two more bodies right now instead of just Ru. Stack effects, cover angles.” He pocketed the phone, his hand dropping to his side. “Doesn’t matter. Wishing doesn’t change the math.”

He was about to head back when a Loop headline pushed past everything else on the feed, big enough to cut through even his own name trending underneath it.

[Haruto Murayama announces departure from Kansai Black Lotus, effective immediately.]

“Haruto’s leaving. Actually leaving.” Tatsuya went still in the middle of the bathroom, one hand still in his pocket.

The post underneath was short and careful, nothing like the messy public blowups Loop usually ran on. Haruto thanked Tochi and Kansai Black Lotus for the opportunity, said he still respected what the roster had tried to build, and then, without naming Eren directly, said he wasn’t willing to keep carrying a team whose own signature build he’d started to suspect wasn’t built by the person taking credit for it. Replies underneath were already split between people defending Eren and people reading between the lines exactly the way Tatsuya was.

“He knows.” Tatsuya’s hand came out of his pocket and curled into a loose fist at his side. “Maybe not everything, but enough. And he still didn’t say the name.”

He knew exactly why that mattered, because the same shape of idea had been his, once, sketched out on paper with Eren back before either of them had touched the game for real, before Eren took the whole build and ran with only half of what it could actually do. Eren copied the notes. He never understood them. That gap was the whole reason he’d capped out as a captain instead of anything more, and it was the same gap Tatsuya had been staring at for years.

Another Loop post trailed the first, already speculative, already tagging Dosuke AI, a name that made Tatsuya’s mouth twitch despite everything else going on. Dosuke’s own build had always read a little too close to what he and Eren used to theorize for it to be coincidence, and if anyone in the current pro scene had the eye to actually use Haruto right, it was him.

“Everything Eren built is starting to come apart.” He pushed off the wall and headed for the door. “Not because of me. Because he never earned any of it in the first place.”

A different thought caught him before he reached the hallway, quieter, colder.

“How do I even enter the esports scene with this build?” He stopped just outside the bathroom door, fingers curling around the frame. “Goblin’s a joke race to everyone who matters. World Quest gear gets nerfed in ranked. I’m starting from zero followers every time I log off. What’s the actual path here?”

He didn’t have an answer yet. He pushed off the frame and kept walking.

He was halfway down the hallway, thumb still hovering over his phone, when a voice cut through the noise behind him.

“Hey, T-Drag.”

Tatsuya’s whole body went still for half a second before his brain caught up to whose voice that actually was. Not Leon’s. Different pitch, different rhythm, and it took him a second longer to place exactly why that mattered.

He turned around slow, and there she was. Arya, leaning against the hallway wall just outside the bathroom door, one shoulder against the tile, arms loose at her sides, like she’d been standing there long enough to get comfortable.
