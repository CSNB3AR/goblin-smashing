Chapter Eight: Assembling the Assault Team

The smoke reached Tatsuya before the words did, a thin gray thread drifting low along the treeline outside the war tent, and underneath the smell of it he could still taste the ash from an hour ago, Shiv’s ash, clinging to the back of his throat no matter how many times he swallowed. His new hair kept catching the firelight wrong, white where it should have been dark, and every time it happened his brain still snagged on it for half a second before catching up to what he was actually looking at.

A notification flickered at the edge of his vision before he could stop himself from reading it.

**[VIEWERS: 7,392 → 8,110]**

Chat was still scrolling underneath it, too fast to make out more than fragments, still churning off whatever the Shiv reveal had lit under it a few minutes ago. He shoved it out of view without opening it. Kesh was still bent over his own knee ten feet away, chest working for air, and that mattered a lot more right now than anything chat had to say about it.

Kesh hadn’t moved from the spot he’d skidded to a stop in, one hand still braced hard against his knee, claws gone pale at the tips, his breath coming in short, ragged pulls that hadn’t evened out at all.

“Someone should tell his lungs the danger’s already over.” The corner of Tatsuya’s mouth twitched toward a grin he had no business wearing right now, and he flattened it out again before it could fully surface.

Sevish didn’t wait for anyone to catch their breath. He was already moving, closing the last stretch of ground into the tent in three long strides, Wyx a half step behind him, and Tatsuya followed with Ru crowding close at his shoulder, the flap falling shut behind them on canvas heat, woodsmoke, and old leather instead of open air.

Sevish’s voice cut through it sharp and clean before anyone had really settled, the two-handed battle axe leaning against his chair already in reach the second he crossed the threshold. “Wyx. Get the barricades up, reinforce the eastern watch, pull everyone back inside the palisade line. Nobody engages past the treeline.” He turned back toward the flap, toward Kesh trailing in last, his scarred face set. “How many on that road, Kesh? Give me a clean count.”

“Four,” Kesh said, still catching his breath, his hand finally coming off his knee to gesture low and wide. “Maybe five. Not farmers. Kesh couldn’t, couldn’t get close enough. They saw Kesh coming, they saw everything.”

“Every word costs him something.” Tatsuya’s weight shifted forward without him deciding to, drawn half a step closer to the sound of Kesh fighting for air.

Sevish’s stance squared, his scarred hand tightening on the axe shaft. “That’s not a for-sure number, Kesh.” His tone carried an edge now, the kind that said he was running out of patience. “I take it as four for a safe number, and you’re telling me four men gave you all this trouble?”

Kesh scratched at the back of his head again, his claws catching in matted hair, his eyes dropping to the dirt. “They attacked us. Random. They have caster.”

“The wisdom of goblins really shines in a crisis, doesn’t it.” A laugh threatened at the back of Tatsuya’s throat, and he let it die there instead of on his face, not with Sevish standing right there working through an actual threat.

Sevish’s eyes tracked to Tatsuya for half a second, something passing behind them, then back to Kesh. “Where exactly? Road shoulder, treeline, where? I need locations, Kesh.”

“Road itself,” Kesh said, his voice steadying slightly now that he’d caught his breath, both hands going to his knees this time. “Big red tent. They set up next to edge, where hill leads to road. Anyone coming down, they see first.”

“Damn. We’re trapped.” Tatsuya’s stomach dropped before his brain finished the thought, his weight rocking back onto his heels.

“They’re using our own rally point against us,” Tatsuya said, half to himself.

Wyx’s head turned sharp at that, her green eyes narrowing with something close to interest. “You’ve seen it.”

“Didn’t have to see it,” Tatsuya said, his voice steady. “Only one road down means whoever’s sitting on it controls whether anyone leaves at all. Whoever’s down there picked the smartest spot to single us out.” He glanced back at Kesh, watching the goblin’s claws still digging into his own knees. “He said it himself, they’re not even bothered enough to move once they’ve got position.”

**[AFFILIATION: THRASH CLAN 10% → 12%]**

“I wonder what that means. Have to do more research once I log off for the day.” He kept his expression flat, his eyes on Sevish, not letting the notification distract him.

Sevish’s weight rocked back on his heels, his hand tightening on the axe. “Get the camp ready. Stage one defense, full perimeter. Now.”

A notification bloomed at the edge of Tatsuya’s vision, gold-bordered, bigger than anything else that had come through tonight.

**[BREACH QUEST: DOMAIN DEFENSE]** **[INTRUDERS INVADING BLOODHURST]** **[THRASH CLAN DEFENDERS: 50/50]** **[INVASION TIMER: 00:04:37]**

“Okay. So them attacking us triggered a new quest.” He dismissed the notification and pulled up his own character sheet instead, the blue UI screen resolving clean in his vision.

**[CHARACTER: TDRAG]**
**[RACE: SHIV GOBLIN]**
**[LEVEL: 7]**
**[TITLE: THE FIRST DESCENDANT OF SHIV]**

**[HP: 65/65] [SP: 32/32] [MP: 32/32]**

**[STATS]**
**[COURAGE: 4]**
**[POWER: 4]**
**[WISDOM: 8 (13)] +5 Call of the Bones (Necklace)**
**[SPEED: 9]**
**[FORTUNE: 15 (18)] +3 (Gambler’s Earring)**
**[ENERGY: 4]**

**[ABILITIES]**
**[SHIV-TOUCHED FLARE – TIER 1]**
**[CALL OF COMMAND – TIER 1]**
**[RUNE PRIMING – TIER 3]**
**[ANCIENT BONE FLAME BARRAGE – TIER 5]**
**[GAMBLER’S GAMBIT – PASSIVE]**
**[BONE SURGE – PASSIVE]**

“Four points Wisdom, six Fortune. That’s the build. Speed’s sitting at nine, that’s enough for now.” He dismissed the screen and looked back at Sevish, his stance settling back to even.

Sevish was already barking orders, his voice carrying across the tent and out into the camp beyond, goblins scrambling the second his words landed.

“Wyx, I want rotation shifts every thirty seconds.”

A second notification bloomed in the corner of his vision, smaller, gray-bordered.

**[STREAM CONNECTION LOST]** **[RECONNECT?]**

“Stream dropped. That’s not good.” He dismissed it, already knowing what was coming next.

Leon’s voice cut in a beat later, no longer quiet, no longer careful, the tone tight enough to pull Tatsuya’s focus away from the tent entirely.

“I need you now,” Leon said. “The monitor’s not holding, I can’t see anything on my end, and VRSA’s going to be on my back if I let this slide any longer. Ten minutes, that’s all I’m asking.”

“Damn, has to be at the most inconvenient time. I can’t push dad’s old pods without the repairs, or have VRSA close it down before I can even do that.” Tatsuya rolled his shoulders once before he answered.

“Give me a second,” Tatsuya said, his voice low enough that Sevish barely caught it, then turned back to the chieftain.

Sevish’s eyes stayed on him, holding there in the grim dark like his glare alone was piercing his chest. “So. Will you be bringing help, or facing this alone?”

Tatsuya knew that question was coming, and right now there was only one real answer if he didn’t want to risk the seven-day lock facing them solo.

“Yeah, I’m in. But if you’re expecting me to run down there in prisoner rags, I won’t be very productive. You got anything better I can work with? Also, I need some time to prepare, if you can give me the space for that,” Tatsuya said, readjusting his new frame, which gave him a couple of inches.

Sevish studied him a beat longer, his scarred face unreadable, then nodded once, sharp. “Understood. We’ll hold stage one defense while you prepare. Wyx, get him something that actually fits.”

“Done,” Wyx said, already moving toward a crate stacked near the tent’s back wall, her hands working the latch before Sevish had finished the sentence.

Sevish’s eyes stayed on Tatsuya, something working behind them. “When you’re back, you go down with Ru. Rott and Nab will already be ahead of you, holding the line. You’ll meet them there.”

“Sounds good. I’ll have a plan shortly,” Tatsuya said.

Wyx returned a moment later with a bundle in her arms, rough-spun leather dyed a muted brown, stitched together with cord that looked like it had been salvaged from three different sources. She set it on the table between them without ceremony.

**[COMMON GOBLIN LEATHER CHEST – COMMON]** **[+1 ENERGY]** **[BASIC CRAFTED GEAR]**

“Common tier. But it’s something for now.” He picked it up, turning it over in his hands, the leather stiff and worn but intact.

“It’ll do,” Tatsuya said.

Sevish gestured toward the tent flap with his free hand, the axe still gripped in the other. “Rott will show you to a tent. Get your rest while you can. When you’re back, we move.”

Two goblins fell in on either side of him without being told to, one of them the same Rott from earlier, gas pouches still hanging at his belt. They walked him through camp in silence, past goblins hauling crates toward the palisade line, past the fire pit where someone was sharpening a blade on a whetstone, the scrape of metal on stone cutting through the low murmur of voices.

They stopped at a tent near the far edge of camp, smaller than Sevish’s but still standing on its own, a crude nameplate carved into the post beside the flap.

**[WAR TENT – ELDER T-DRAG]**

“Interesting how much this has changed so fast. So many questions I don’t even have answers to right now, but this is fun.” He pushed through the flap and let it fall shut behind him.

The tent was sparse, just a bedroll, a single oil lamp hanging from the center pole, and a small crate stacked against the back wall.

Ru came trailing in behind him, positioning near the entrance, his larger hobgoblin frame filling half the space.

“What’s next, elder,” Ru said, watching him cross to the bedroll.

“Yeah, I’m going to meditate for a bit. Maybe it’ll give me a chance to channel Shiv once more, for guidance,” Tatsuya said, clearly lying through his teeth, but he didn’t want to give them player terms, not anymore, this was the only way he could earn their trust.

He sat down on the bedroll, the straw crunching under him. “If anything happens while I’m out, you stand guard. If they push the camp, if anything happens, you keep me alive. Understood?”

Ru’s tusks caught the lamplight as his mouth pulled into something that wasn’t quite a grin. “Understood. Nobody gets past Ru, or touches the elder.”

**[AFFILIATION: RU 75%] (TRUSTED)**

“Good,” Tatsuya said, before crossing his legs into a meditation-like position and letting his thoughts open the UI log-off screen.

The lid hissed open and the ceiling of the operator’s booth swam into focus, too bright after however many hours his eyes had actually been closed. He sat up slow, joints stiff in a way that made him wince, and the VRSA inspector was already crouched at the monitor bay, panel open, tools laid out in a neat row on a cloth beside him.

Leon stood next to him, one hip braced against the monitor bay, glasses slipping slightly down his nose, and he looked up the second Tatsuya sat up. “Hey. I think the pods started to overheat, might be from the streaming. We may have to consider not doing that for as long, until we get the new parts in. But we can talk about that later.”

“For sure, man, that was crazy. My heart’s still pounding from the rush.” Tatsuya pushed himself off the pod’s edge and rolled his neck until something popped.

“Oh, I bet that was fun to watch, my dude. I’ll talk more about it next session if you want, hated to interrupt. But give me the ten minutes,” Leon said, not looking up from where the inspector was working. “Go stretch your legs or something, you’ve been under a long time.”

“Yeah, for sure, and speak anytime, be nice to have an outside coach,” Tatsuya said, his legs stiff under him as he crossed toward the hallway. “Bathroom first.”

“Coach, huh? I like the sound of that,” Leon teased, his voice echoing as Tatsuya shifted down the aisles toward the bathroom.

The bathroom at Sakura Arcade was small and smelled like the same industrial cleaner it always had, harsh enough to catch in the back of his throat after hours of woodsmoke and canvas. Tatsuya splashed water on his face before he even looked at his phone, both hands braced against the sink, needing a second to remember which world he was actually standing in.

His phone had a dozen notifications stacked on the lock screen, more than he’d ever seen at once, the Loop icon buried somewhere in the middle of the pile.

[Your follower count has grown by 2,140 in the last hour.]

“I’m not a no-name anymore. I have to move smarter, with people watching my moves now.” He stared at the number, water still dripping off his chin.

He thumbed it open and the feed loaded slow, clip after clip of the same green-skinned figure kneeling in ash and smoke. A handle he didn’t recognize had stitched together every notification bracket from the Shiv scene into a single clip captioned “did anyone else catch this live.” Another had zoomed in on the necklace, freeze-framed, circled in red, captioned “world tier confirmed, somebody check the wiki.” His own name, T-Drag, sat tagged in reply after reply. He scrolled until his thumb cramped and made himself stop, setting the phone on the edge of the sink.

He pulled up his HGO account next, and there it was, a button that hadn’t existed the last time he’d checked this screen.

[Apply for Affiliate Status]

“Five hundred and eight, huh. Not bad for the first stream. Crazy how lucky I’ve been, but I can only expect there’ll be bullshit too. Just wait until Eren finds out about me.” His thumb hovered over it for a second before he tapped it, the dread of the Tabuchis sitting in his chest. “Not to mention telling Chiyo the truth about this. I can tell her about Sakura, but if I make anything big off it, she won’t believe an arcade built on old tech paid me that much.” He shook the thought off and looked at the screen again.

The confirmation screen that loaded back wasn’t the instant green light he’d half expected.

[Application received. Affiliate status requires a minimum of 30 days active streaming history. Current eligibility: Day 1.]

“Thirty days.” He set the phone down on the sink’s edge and stared at it. “Meaning I can gain an audience, but I won’t be able to really make anything until after. That’s fine, subs are nice, but the real money is sponsorships, and that doesn’t have a limit, just have to be smart about how I approach my brand. Can’t tell anyone who I am behind the goblin, not right now, not even family.” Taji and Momo were the first to come to mind, and Chiyo.

A quick search turned up the rest of it in under a minute, a forum thread somebody had laid out plain: HGO’s official channel auto-hosts any stream actively progressing a World Quest, standing platform practice, free exposure handed to whoever happened to be live when the algorithm noticed, not a permanent slot, and not something that follows him back online automatically. Coming back live after a break meant starting closer to zero and climbing again, not picking up where he left off.

“Makes sense why they host World Quests. But it also makes sense how all the big guilds and top leaders grew so fast.” He picked the phone back up, his grip firming around it. “Have to make sure I capitalize on it when the time arrives. Not everything needs to be streamed.”

Subs themselves weren’t much once he actually read the fine print. Seven hundred fifty yen a month, and whoever paid it got a couple of cosmetic emotes to throw around and a special chat tag, unless the streamer set their own perks, nothing that let them type more than any other viewer.

“Be nice to have that now, couldn’t even read that wave of mess earlier. Maybe I can get a setting to limit spam chat or something faster. Waiting thirty days to say anything will be horrible.” He turned the phone over in his hand, once. “Lesson for next time, I suppose.”

Two more notifications sat under the Loop pile, both real-world, both waiting.

[Tochi Academy Admissions: Your application has been reviewed. You are invited to sit the entrance interest exam. Reply for scheduling.]

He stared at that one longer than he meant to, his thumb going still against the screen. He’d sent that application off this morning knowing the deal he had with Leon.

“Not dealing with that right now.” He locked the screen and set the phone face-down on the sink. “It’ll still be there when I actually have time to think about it straight.”

He picked the phone back up and opened his banking app instead, pulled up the balance sitting at ¥64,120, and moved a piece of it into the account that covered his mother’s ongoing costs.

[Transfer sent: ¥15,000 → Ohema Kazehaya, Medical.]

“Small.” He held the confirmation screen for a second longer than he needed to. “But it’s real, and it’s mine, and it’s not a promise this time.”

He thought about Chiyo seeing that line item whenever she finally checked the statement, and for the first time in a while the thought didn’t make him brace for an argument.

He opened his texts next and typed one out to Gato before he lost the nerve.

[Hey. Gonna need tomorrow off. Been thinking hard about going back to Tochi, actually doing something with it this time. Wanted you to hear it from me instead of me just not showing up. I’ll come by and talk properly when I can.]

The reply came back faster than he expected.

[No worries at all. Proud of you for even considering it. You know you’re always welcome here whenever, shift or no shift. Go handle your business.]

“That’s Gato for you.” The corner of his mouth pulled flat, something close to a smile before it was gone.

He splashed more water on his face, the cold biting against his skin, and gripped the edge of the sink with both hands. Seven days locked out if he died down there. Seven days he didn’t have, not with Winter Bash sitting fifteen days out, not with no duo locked in yet. This was the first real fight he’d ever be in against actual players, not scripted mobs, and right now it was still just him and Ru walking into it.

“First real team fight. Nothing like the competitive scene, but if I can’t do this, thinking about that is a pipe dream.” His grip on the sink tightened once, then loosened. “Time to test my skill and not blame Eren or anyone else for not being here. This is my moment.”

Something else surfaced while he stood there, a system flag he’d never actually gone looking for. He’d crossed level five somewhere in tonight’s jump without ever checking what that unlocked.

[Arena queue eligibility: unlocked at level 5. Ranked and open-ladder PvP now available.]

“Can queue in PvP now, which is also good. Will have to figure out my build and not keep points earmarked for that, and there’s the new meta coming out too.” He let out a short breath through his nose, his reflection doing the same back at him from the mirror. “Guess tonight’s where I find out if I can actually hang with the big dogs, if you can call them that.” He chuckled to himself, adrenaline forming.

He filed it away, not something to chase mid-crisis, but it sat differently now, a door that had already opened without him.

Tatsuya was about to pocket his phone when a Loop headline pushed past everything else on the feed, big enough to cut through even his own name trending underneath it.

[Haruto Murayama announces departure from Kansai Black Lotus, effective immediately.]

“Haruto’s leaving. Actually leaving.” Tatsuya went still in the middle of the bathroom, caught off guard by it.

The post underneath was short and careful, nothing like the messy public blowups Loop usually ran on. Haruto thanked Tochi and Kansai Black Lotus for the opportunity, said he still respected what the roster had tried to build, and then, without naming Eren directly, said he wasn’t willing to keep carrying a team whose own signature build he’d started to suspect wasn’t built by the person taking credit for it. Replies underneath were already split between people defending Eren and people reading between the lines exactly the way Tatsuya was.

“He knows.” Tatsuya’s hand came out of his pocket and curled into a loose fist at his side. “Maybe not everything, but enough. And he still didn’t say the name.”

He knew exactly why that mattered, because the same shape of idea had been his, once, sketched out on paper with Eren back before either of them had touched the game for real, before Eren took the whole build and ran with only half of what it could actually do. Eren copied the notes. He never understood them. That gap was the whole reason he’d capped out as a captain instead of anything more, and it was the same gap Tatsuya had been staring at for years.

Another Loop post trailed the first, already speculative, already tagging Dosuke AI, a name that made Tatsuya’s mouth twitch despite everything else going on. Dosuke’s own build had always read a little too close to what he and Eren used to theorize for it to be coincidence.

“Everything Eren built is starting to come apart.” He pushed off the wall and headed for the door. “Bastard better not crash yet. I want to settle everything when I face him in this world.” The words sat heavy, an anger surfacing that he didn’t know he had, but a smile crept onto his face as he realized what was at stake.

“How do I even enter the esports scene with this build?” He stopped just outside the bathroom door, fingers curling around the frame. “Goblin’s a joke race to everyone who matters. World Quest gear gets nerfed in ranked. I’m starting from zero followers every time I log off. What’s the actual path here?”

He didn’t have an answer yet. He pushed off the frame and kept walking.

He was already down the hallway, thumb still hovering over his phone, when a voice cut through the noise behind him.

“Hey, T-Drag.”

Tatsuya’s whole body went still for half a second before his brain caught up to whose voice that actually was. Not Leon’s. Different pitch, different rhythm, and it took him a second longer to place exactly why that mattered.

He turned around slow, and there she was, Arya, leaning against the operator’s desk where Leon should’ve been, but wasn’t.
