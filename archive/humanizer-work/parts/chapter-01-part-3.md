Suddenly a very shiny gold-colored access drive appeared, so big that Kazuku had to be at least ten feet tall to hold it. It could easily be mistaken for an overly elaborate movie prop, but it worked because he was made of CG.

“Christmas Day,” Kazuku said, “prepare yourselves, solo players and guilds alike. With all the commotion at Thornwatch, you would think something is about to be awakened out there. And when that time comes, it is going to take this world by storm. Anybody that has made a new player can be a part of it.” The crowd roared in agreement. He slowly spun around to face the camera while holding up the gigantic access drive. “We are opening up Beta tonight. Everything you earn from the moment you log in will count toward your Season total. Each point is awarded individually, but everything is set up to accommodate guilds, so please come with your guild. If you’re brand new to the world of Eryndal or wanting to get involved in the new economy, bring your business. We’ve never given people this inexpensive a way into the system. For the next seventy-two hours only, each HGO access drive and each rig is discounted fifty percent. As soon as the expansion launches, all characters currently in closed beta will freeze at whatever level they are at now. Your Legacy Points remain accessible to either use or trade once you’re ready. When you start playing on a new character, your old character will be retired.”

The number flashed on the screen underneath him, plain white letters on the black background: **50% OFF - 72 HOURS ONLY**.

I checked twice, the same way I checked Chiyo’s messages, to verify that the sale really was fifty percent and that my tired brain wasn’t hallucinating some sort of hell for me.

*"Half price. For three days."* Math problems about money always worked themselves out right away. Enough? Just barely. And something big waking up on Christmas Day, in the game I had vowed not to play?

*"Something waking up in Thornwatch. There has to be money in that, or nobody would have fought that hard for the spot."* The last prize pool was forty million yen, and a tenth of that would have covered the surgery. *"If this comes even remotely close to that type of money, then it's worth trying to get into this game."*

I brushed it off. *"Tonight is work. Tomorrow is work too. A sale is only a sale."*

I dug my thumbnail into the top of the counter until it went white. Above me, the overhead lights hummed. But my mind kept running the math anyway, a tiny, unyielding motor that simply refused to shut down. I turned it off.

*"I don't earn enough money here."* This job had its limitations and I had hit them six months ago. Chiyo was covering more than she should. Mom’s bills weren’t decreasing. The Tabuchis kept calling us no matter how many times we ignored them. If I didn’t do something, Chiyo was going to make the Tochi decision for me, and I’d be walking back into that school with my head hung low in shame. I took a breath through my teeth and gazed out at the rain beginning to streak the glass.

I wasn’t going to risk losing what little I had on a game I detested hearing about. I grabbed the rag again and told myself that was where it ended.

Red-gold headlights rapidly passed across the front window of the store, and my stomach dropped half a second before I knew why. A black car slowly and deliberately parked in front of the charging bays.

Through the windshield I could make out a figure in dark clothing, driving calmly and gripping the steering wheel firmly as the vehicle came almost to a complete stop.

First, the driver’s side door opened and blond hair caught the streetlight before anything else registered. The black team jacket was zipped all the way up to his neck, a silver lotus crossed with two staves embroidered on his chest, and the logo caught the light.

*"Eren."*
