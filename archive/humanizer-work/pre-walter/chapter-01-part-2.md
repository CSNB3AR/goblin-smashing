The broadcast moved to a view of an abandoned fort in ruin at the edge of a dense forest covered in snow. There were broken walls and fallen towers surrounding crumbling houses, and the only building still standing was an old hall with a banner flapping on its steps. The camera feed was being provided by HGO's orb cameras, small transparent balls floating within Eryndal itself and hovering above the walls and between towers to capture footage of the battle from all angles. Yuna was calling it from Singapore. Along the bottom of the display the overlay read **ZONE: CONTESTED**, and the WattsUp lightning symbol sat on the sponsor bar between two logos I didn't know. I almost laughed. I shifted my shoulder toward the screen, like that would make me stop looking. It didn't.

"And look at how well the Knights are using strategy," Yuna said, her voice dropping low and quick, the way it did when she was about to say something good. "All twenty of their players are cloaked in enchantments and gone to ground inside these ruined structures in small groups, each group covering the breach in the wall from a different angle, hit and fade, hit and fade. This is guerrilla warfare. That is why twenty versus twelve is truly the number that matters, because nobody coming in from outside can change it. Every Aogiri reinforcement who fights through that forest and attempts to cross that breach is a kill, and every kill is six more for London. Behind the walls, Aogiri has twelve players, a guild leader, two officers and nine hands, dug into that old hall as their base, and nobody is burning that down. But they're bleeding out. And magic isn't used for show in Eryndal, so do not be fooled into thinking the glowing wards and advanced equipment are cosmetic."

My thumb dragged along the counter's edge. *"Hit and fade. At least somebody over there knows what they're doing."*

The Knights of the London Spire had retreated into the ruins, twenty of them hidden in partially collapsed buildings and in the shadow of a fallen tower. They weren't defending a position. They were waiting. A lone Spellforger in midnight blue entered the breach and received fireballs and bolts from three separate windows at once and died before his boots even struck the ground. One more player was out for seventy-two hours, and with only two hours left, out for good.

Lin Haoyu, Aogiri's leader, remained trapped in the old hall with the eleven other members of his team, their banner hanging from the hall steps and Haoyu's name on the overlay as its guardian. He was **POISONED**, a tick eating a little health every few seconds, and the Shadowveil players who could have slipped a cure through the narrow way in had all died early. Edmund Vane, London's own leader, guarded their flag from a tent set back from the breach, with the one entrance sealed. The Knights only had to keep the breach bleeding and let the poison work.

I hated how clean the read came. It was the tactics. It had always been the tactics, the reason I'd loved this game in the first place, back when I still thought Eren cared about it as much as I did. That was exactly why I kept my back to it now. I stared at the dark window until my reflection settled, and made myself breathe.

*"And they were playing like boneheads."* He could hand the flag off and meet a runner outside the walls, or break out with the eleven while a fresh wave hit the breach from the other side, but a handoff would show on the overlay and London would swarm whoever held it, and both his officers were DPS who wouldn't last sitting on the steps. Sitting there was the only play that couldn't win. The reinforcements were worse, burning their supplies in the forest and then feeding the breach one at a time instead of massing. The breach was London's single point of failure too, one input, one output, the kind of thing Dad would have glanced at and asked where the redundancy was. Every minute he sat there was a minute of poison, and the flag was his to guard. If he went, it went.

Haoyu did not budge. He sat on the steps of the hall with his shield across his knees and watched his own health tick down, waiting for a fresh wave to hit the breach. My palms landed flat on the counter.

"Pick something," I said out loud, low, to no one, already knowing how this ended, and I hated that I'd said it. I turned my back on the screen and went back to wiping the same spot I'd already cleaned twice tonight. "Break out or run. Do something."

From behind me, sound burst out of the speaker, a double-kill chime that made the crowd erupt, and I grimaced as the volume spiked. Then the noise shifted. Something had gone down on the steps of the hall.

"He's dead!" Yuna yelled over the cheering crowd. "The leader of Aogiri is down, and so is his banner! The Knights are the sole owners of the zone, and the ten-minute clock is running!"

I didn't turn around. I wiped the same spot a third time and a fourth while the cheering climbed and the minutes bled off, until one low tone sounded, the kind that meant a hold timer had run out.

"The zone is secure, Thornwatch belongs to London Spire!" Yuna shouted over the explosion of sound. "Look at that, folks, London Spire's flag is going up over those ruins! And a questline is opening for the Knights! As soon as they finish it off, the points go on the board, and London Spire takes Season Zero and forty million yen in front of a sold-out crowd here in Singapore!"

I turned back around in time to see the camera orbs peel away from the dead leader and drift up over the ruins toward the flag, the way the feed would follow any other fight, while the broadcast cut to Yuna on a virtual stage, her CG avatar waving at the crowd. *"Tough, losing like that."* I felt nothing about it.

*"Forty million."* The thought went exactly where it always went. *"Even a tenth of that pays for the surgery outright. No more Chiyo quietly covering gaps she thinks I don't notice. No more Tabuchi calls."*

The broadcast didn't cut to credits. It cut to a soundstage draped in silver and blue, and the enormous cartoon bear ambled out from behind a curtain to real, delighted applause, both in the arena and, faintly, from the little speaker Gato kept behind the counter for his news when he worked the front.

Kazuku. Nobody knew his real face. Just the bear, round-bellied and grinning, HGO's compass-rose logo stitched across a vest that always looked freshly pressed no matter how many times he wore it on camera. He stood on a stage animated through holographic projection tech Singapore had been pioneering for years, his avatar as real to the crowd there as if he'd walked out in person.

"Huge congratulations to London Spire," Kazuku said, arms stretched wide like he meant to hug the whole arena, his voice warm and genuine in a way that always made me suspicious. "And big kudos to every guild that fought for that stage tonight. You've shown us all again why we compete."

He paused long enough for the cheers to ring out and fade, his expression shifting into something brighter.

"And now, since you've all been so patient with me."

Gold light bloomed across the screen behind him, a map pattern I recognized a half-second before my brain caught up. Not one continent anymore but five. Five separate pieces of land where there had only ever been one.

"Eryndal is expanding," Kazuku said, and the arena noise swelled again, louder this time, people on their feet. "The Hearthfire expansion launches January 1st. Five brand new continents will be unlocked, each one with its own advantages for the people born there. You'll build and defend your own kingdoms and cities in a world where the native inhabitants recognize who you are and notice exactly what you've accomplished so far. Earn their respect and trust, or fail to earn either. And before the real conflicts begin, there's one final surprise waiting at the end of Open Beta, with more of the map opening up behind it."

My weight shifted forward slightly, like I could lean into the screen and see the map more clearly if I just got close enough. Then I caught it and stepped back, and found the counter's edge with my hand again.
