# Chapter 2 — Into the Game (~2,000-2,500 words)

**Status: Not drafted.**

**Opens the same afternoon Chapter 1 ends on**, not the next morning, Chiyo and the twins are already gone on their six-day Kyoto school trip (see `codex/subplots/tochi-academy-return.md`), so this opens with Tatsuya alone in the apartment, the Nexus Enterprises pickup text still fresh. He's not in full panic, but he doesn't just bolt out the door either: get dressed, shower, and actually put together a real plan before he leaves, since going straight to grab the drive without first figuring out where he can even play it just gets him back to square one. This is the natural spot for him to do some real research on his own time (Loop, the HGO Companion App, light numeric/world-detail texture as flavor, decided at draft time), landing on wanting to build a **mage**, explicitly not a goblin, before he ever sits down at Sakura Arcade.

**Available material for this opener (not yet drafted, use if it fits the pacing):** an ordinary morning-news segment, framed as entertainment news, covering last night's World Championship (the prize figure gets a mention) alongside a darker undertone, criminal enterprises using the open in-game economy to launder money, nations that have tried and failed to make direct contact with HGO's ownership. See `codex/lore/hgo-timeline-and-real-world-stakes.md`, keep it exactly as vague and atmospheric there. If a family/Taji beat is still wanted here, it needs to happen before they leave for the trip (a quick phone text exchange, a memory, not an in-person scene, since they're already gone), the Dosuke AI build-recognition beat (see `codex/characters/dosuke-ai.md`) can land this way instead of a face-to-face conversation.

**Real-world half stays trimmed hard from there**, roughly the first third of the chapter, so login lands early rather than near the end. The point of this chapter is momentum into the game; don't let the real-world setup outweigh the payoff.

**The search, compressed:** a paragraph, not a chapter. Bookings come back empty, every third-gen cafe in Tokyo booked solid off Winter Bash hype, second-gen not worth the lag. A pod of his own is flatly out of the question on his money. Sakura Arcade becomes his one real option, and he goes, immediately, no tour.

**Sakura Arcade:** meets Leon, who applies a pressure beat of his own, a cost, a condition, or a warning, before Tatsuya gets anywhere near pod access. Sees the two first-gen Kazuhaya Tech pods, the "Kazuhaya" name hits him hard. Because of Daichi's tinkering knowledge, he gets the override working, the deliberate technical act that lets an incompatible first-gen pod run HGO software at all. Keep this quick rather than a drawn-out technical scene. See `codex/subplots/technical-arc-leon-and-sakura-arcade.md`.

**Character creation and the glitch:** he's browsing race options, per the rare-race-roll mechanic (`codex/lore/playable-races-and-character-creation.md`), wanting a mage, and pauses on goblin, weakest in the game, almost dry curiosity rather than intent, right as the arcade's power blips (the pod's dead internal battery pack means it's running on direct wall power only), sharpening the irony given he walked in wanting a mage. The reboot locks in whatever he was paused on. A hardware accident, not a chosen race, not a targeted hack. Leon scrambles to help when the power drops, at one point referencing or borrowing Arya's own headset while troubleshooting, the first hint she has private hardware separate from the arcade's failing gear.

**The permanent-lock realization** becomes an explicit on-page beat, not just backend mechanics (see `codex/objects/hgo-access-drive.md`). He can't just buy a second drive, having just spent nearly everything on this one at the discount, so there's no do-over available to him at all. His reaction needs to be fast and pragmatic, not a spiral, consistent with his established personality, something closer to "fine, I'm stuck, I make use of this" than a breakdown. What keeps him from spiraling anyway: right around this same beat, "Call of the Bones" gets activated/announced, a call for volunteers, which redirects his attention before the disappointment has room to set in.

**First login, first camp:** the goblin glitch drops him into Lunarest Outskirts. This should land by roughly the midpoint of the chapter, not the final page, leaving real room for the camp scene itself: NPC skirmish, Wyx and Sevish, the three-day ultimatum, and a taste of camp texture before the chapter ends. See `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md`. This is also the hard ceiling on pacing: he needs to actually be in-game and leveling by Chapter 2 or 3, not later. Romance and deep backstory stay seeded gradually from here on, the game and the esports engine stay the throughline, not slice-of-life for its own sake.
