# Chapters 1-7 Summary (Finalized, first person)

One accurate summary per chapter, generated directly from the final committed prose in `chapters/chapter-01.md` through `chapters/chapter-07.md`, not from earlier drafts or planning notes. The chapters are now first person (Tatsuya's head), so this summary uses "I." Meant as a single continuity reference (e.g. for import into NovelCrafter) covering everything locked so far. ## Chapter One: The Countdown Starts

I close a night shift at WattsUp (an EV charging station and convenience store run by Gato) with the charge bays humming, a driverless car charging itself and leaving, and the register job that will not cover Mom's hospital bed on my mind. The screen over the register loops HGO because the WattsUp Corporation sponsors the game, and I have ignored it all day. Chiyo texts that the Tabuchis called again and that Tochi's application window reopens January 4th. Yuna hosts the last battle of Season Zero from Singapore, an in-world fight in a walled medieval snow town. It is guerrilla warfare: the Knights of the London Spire hide inside the walls and ambush anyone from Aogiri who comes in, with a kill zone at the choke. Twelve Aogiri are stuck inside with their banner and a poisoned leader who needs antidotes. Every death is a 72-hour lock, so the reinforcements dwindle and the ones who return only push into the prepared kill zone. The leader never makes a decision, waits too long, and dies, and the banner ends with him. London Spire holds the ten-minute zone timer and wins, 40 million yen, season points 10,500 to 10,250 with 300 on the line. I read it as the tactics I once loved and hate that I do. Mid-broadcast, HGO's CEO Kazuku (a bear avatar, real face unknown) announces the Hearthfire expansion: five new continents, a universal character-lock with Legacy Points for existing accounts, open beta tonight rolling into Season One (points earned from tonight count), a 72-hour, 50%-off sale on the access drive and the rig, the guild-only Crownfall Siege on Christmas Day for 10,000,000 yen, and a pro league as an option for those who want that route. I shrug all of it off. Eren (a former friend, now a rival backed by Tabuchi money, captain of Kansai Black Lotus, which bounced out of the top eight) and his guildmate Haruto (the beta's Breakout Player of the Season) stop to charge; a tense exchange follows, Eren needling me about my hospitalized mother and the Tabuchi debt, Haruto quietly complimenting my read on the way out. I think about how Eren is only the messenger and has no idea what is in the envelope. After closing, my Haya EB moped stutters, loses its charge and dies, and I roll it home. Back home, I go into my father's old work room for the manual to fix the EB's controller, and a letter falls out in Daichi's handwriting: if I am reading it he is gone and did not deal with the Tabuchis, what is in the manual is what they need and I must not give it to them, they have pushed him to sell the business, and if I want the truth I should speak to the bear. It is signed with a bear paw print. "Bring your business here" clicks: the bear is Dad's business partner, and the first-gen pod Dad built makes sense of why the Tabuchis kept pushing. HGO may be my only option. I buy the discounted drive and the rig, wondering how to get the bear's attention. The chapter ends on the purchase.

## Chapter Two: Into the Game

I wake at 3:04 PM the next day, having slept through most of my window; a note reveals Chiyo left that morning with the twins (Momo and Taji) for a six-day trip to Kyoto, and a pickup notification arrives for my drive (code 4472-KAZE). I kill the TV and use the HGO Companion App only for the market and the cheapest way in, not for builds. I avoid the Elf row because that is Eren's build now, and lean toward Human. Third- and second-gen VR cafes are booked out for months on sale demand, but I find Sakura Arcade, a legacy first-gen-pod cafe with a free-after-5PM slot. I pick up the drive at the Nexus Enterprises flagship store in Ginza, then ride to Sakura Arcade and meet Leon, the owner, who recognizes the Kazehaya name and reveals that my late father, Daichi, built the original first-gen pod hardware. Leon leaves me alone with the one working pod. Using Daichi's old technical manual (Dad's letter from chapter 1 still folded inside), I override the pod's hardware incompatibility to get it running at all. During character creation, greeted by Lumi, a bubbly system guide, I tap Goblin by accident just as a power blip hits the building (the backup generator kicking in). Lumi starts the patron step (each player picks a patron to follow, and demi-humans such as goblins follow Seranis) and her voice breaks mid-sentence, so the patron step never finishes and she is gone when the wheel returns, the selection locked in permanently with no reset available. The chapter ends as attribute allocation begins.

## Chapter Three: Into Bloodhurst I

Tatsuya's base goblin stats are confirmed (Courage 4, Power 4, Wisdom 2, Speed 7, Fortune 9, Energy 4) and the race lock is permanent, no reset option exists on his drive. His rare-variant creation roll comes back as Gambler's Gambit (a Fortune bonus plus a once-daily post-combat roll for Rare-or-higher loot), and a World Quest notification fires: "Call of the Bones," goblin-race-only, World-tier reward. He allocates his two starting points into Wisdom and Speed, then fully enters Eryndal at Bloodhurst, the Thrash clan's goblin camp in the Lunarest Outskirts. He equips the Gambler's Earring (+3 Fortune, the Lucky Break ability). He witnesses two enforcer goblins bullying a smaller goblin (nicknamed "Runt") over a fire spot, steps in, and is provoked into a chase through camp. He discovers Goblin Gas by accident (a comedic, bodily-function-based gas ability) and uses it to stack poison debuffs on both pursuers, then ignites the gas cloud with a signal flare (Goblin Flare) he didn't realize was combustible, killing both enforcers in an explosion he hadn't intended. He levels up to 2, puts both points into Speed and Wisdom, and loots a Goblin Gadget Belt (its abilities locked pending crafting materials) plus two Minor Elixirs worth ¥8,400 each off the bodies, with Gambler's Gambit successfully proccing on the loot roll.

## Chapter Four: Into Bloodhurst II

Armed guards escort Tatsuya and the younger goblin to chieftain Sevish Thrash (level 80) and his sister Wyx Thrash (level 25) in the clan's central tent. Sevish confronts Tatsuya over the two kills; Tatsuya defends the intervention, then says goblins have "bigger problems to deal with than fighting each other," a line that visibly affects both Sevish and Wyx and triggers "Call of the Bones, Stage 1." Sevish explains that Myrrhwood's goblin clans have been fractured and leaderless since the founder-figure Shiv disappeared about forty-five years ago, and that three prior attempts to force their way into Lunarest's Grand Library failed. He offers Tatsuya a mission: infiltrate the Grand Library's restricted floors for anything on Shiv, in exchange for permanent Thrash clan protection as an "ally of Thrash." Refusing means being treated as an enemy going forward. Tatsuya asks for three days to decide; Sevish grants it but declares him a prisoner of the Thrash clan, confined to camp boundaries in the meantime. The World Quest formally activates in his UI with a seven-day in-game time limit (a quest timer, not the 72-hour death lock). Leon's voice breaks in externally to warn that the arcade is closing soon. A server-wide announcement fires for the entire playerbase: the World Quest is live. Tatsuya logs out.

## Chapter Five: Consequences in Silence

Tatsuya exits the pod and sells his two Minor Elixirs through the Companion App for ¥16,800, linking the same bank account his WattsUp paychecks already use (new balance: ¥64,120). He sets up a real Loop bio and notices social-media chatter (including a player called PK_Wraith) reacting to the World Quest going live and speculating about who triggered it. At the counter, Leon admits the arcade is struggling against third-gen competition; Tatsuya admits he overrode the pod using Daichi's manual (the cause of the earlier blackout) and pitches repairing the second, currently-dead pod using parts he can access through Tochi Academy. Leon agrees to pursue proper VRSA clearance and offers him informal, ongoing pod access in what both of them frame as a sponsorship arrangement, conditional on staying quiet until clearance is finished. He then rides to the hospital and sits with his comatose mother, Ohema, talking to her about the game and his plan. Leaving, he overhears two nurses (one named Ms. Sugiyama) discussing that Ohema has about six months without surgery and that Chiyo asked them to keep it from the family, which angers him. Rounding a corner, he collides with a girl with twin buns and glasses who helps him set his HGO username to "TDRAG" on the spot; she leaves before he catches her name, tucking a note into his pocket with her friend code and the date December 23rd. The chapter ends with him deciding not to confront Chiyo yet, having settled on his path.

## Chapter Six: VRSA Inspection

Back home, Tatsuya texts Chiyo that he's decided to attend Tochi Academy, citing his father's manual and a new part-time job, without mentioning HGO. Checking his bank balance (¥64,120), he also finds his late father Daichi's frozen Kazuhaya Tech account, deeply negative (-¥3,014,200) with a Tabuchi Holdings collections notice attached, and resolves that the family's debt has to be dealt with first. The next morning, he researches Shiv (finds almost nothing) and the goblin race's unusually open class restrictions, wondering if an unmapped magic path is available to him. He sets his Loop handle to TDRAG with a goblin profile icon and considers the risk/reward of eventually streaming, reasoning his new "ally of Thrash" standing offers him some real protection. He notices on Loop that a recurring event at Lunarest didn't spawn that morning. At Sakura Arcade, he meets Leon's sister Arya for the first time at the counter, who's curious about the secrecy surrounding the pod work but is rebuffed. Leon and a VRSA Inspector are finishing work on the second pod; Leon explains VRSA's role as a legal clearance authority (not a repair role) and the external-monitor/operator requirement for any full-dive session. Tatsuya tells Leon his mother is in a coma and he needs money fast; Leon commits to acting as his operator and real-world backer. The Inspector certifies the first pod for sessions. After a short wait (and a brief exchange with Arya), Leon takes his seat at the operator's booth, and Tatsuya sets his stream to start automatically before diving back in. The chapter ends as he climbs into the pod and the visor seals shut.

## Chapter Seven: The First Disciple

Tatsuya reenters Eryndal exactly where he logged out, in Bloodhurst's war tent, stats unchanged (Health/Stamina/Mana all 50/25/25) and a Rested Travel buff ticking from having slept somewhere safe. The younger goblin from Chapter 3 (nicknamed "Runt") is still there with him, waiting, until he suddenly freezes mid-sentence, paused like the world itself stopped around him. The air pressure changes, all outside camp noise cuts off, and Shiv manifests directly in the tent, an ash-and-smoke figure with a wide-brimmed hat and burning green eyes, a gold, world-tier nameplate. A world-level effect, Flamonta, slows Tatsuya to a crawl just from standing near him, though it never threatens his health outright. Shiv addresses him by his stream handle, T-Drag, shortened to "T," confirming he's aware of Tatsuya's real-world visibility, not just his in-game actions. Mid-conversation, the stream spikes hard: HGO's official channel hosts it, viewers climbing from single digits into the thousands in real time, followers climbing alongside it, all visible to Tatsuya as it happens.

Shiv tells his own history: once a noble named Shivaris Jutsuin at Lunarest's own court, cursed and cast out (not killed) for loving a queen who wasn't his, transformed into what he is now, and only afterward learning goblins, beastfolk, and other demi-humans had already been hunted as "feral" long before him. He built Myrrhwood as proof they could be something civilized rather than what the court called them. He's fading now and needs a real successor, someone who's shown him "the same kind of wisdom" he once had, referencing Tatsuya's own line to Sevish and Wyx in Chapter 4 ("bigger problems to deal with than fighting each other"). He asks permission, not just to bless Tatsuya, but to bring Runt in as well, "already halfway there without knowing it." Tatsuya agrees to both, and in the same beat, gives the kid a real name for the first time: Ru.

Shiv hands over the Call of the Bones Necklace, a bound, unremovable world-tier item, and Tatsuya accepts fully aware it can't be sold, reasoning it through as an investment rather than treasure. The bond hits immediately: the Flamonta debuff clears, his hair turns pure white, his skin lightens, and a new purple title appears above his head, **THE FIRST DESCENDANT OF SHIV**, explicitly singular. A stacked wave of system notifications locks in the changes: +5 Fortune, his racial Wisdom restriction fully negated, a new Bone Surge ability, the Shaman's Veil spirit magic path unlocked, a Whispers of Shiv passive, his race evolving from Goblin to Shiv Goblin, and a jump straight from level 2 to level 7 with ten fresh discipline points, which he immediately splits four into Wisdom and six into Fortune. Goblin Flare evolves into Shiv-Touched Flare, and he gains a new Rune Priming ability. The Whispers of Shiv passive also reveals his companion garrison capacity, currently capped at one slot, about to be filled.

Shiv turns to Ru and blesses him directly, no hesitation this time: Ru transforms from Goblin to Hobgoblin, jumping from level 1 to level 5, gaining a full head of height, his Goblin Gas ability evolving into Toxic Breath, and a new title, **FIRST DISCIPLE OF SHIV**. Shiv warns them both that every goblin who's ever seen even a piece of what he looked like will recognize the mark on sight, generational memory, not just reputation, then dissolves back into ash and vanishes.

Tatsuya steps out of the tent to announce himself to the guards, both of whom go visibly still at the sight of him. He sends word for Sevish, and while waiting, catches the stream's viewer and follower counts still climbing (crossing into the thousands, followers into the hundreds), and glimpses two familiar names scrolling through chat: a handle he half-recognizes as possibly his little brother Taji, which he deliberately doesn't confirm even to himself, and Arya's name, which he clocks silently without replying. Sevish and Wyx arrive, stunned at the transformation in both Tatsuya and Ru; Wyx reads it as a sign to return to Myrrhwood outright. Sevish lifts Tatsuya's prisoner status and movement restriction on the spot, reaffirming him as an ally of Thrash, and the group starts toward the war tent to talk properly. Leon breaks in externally with a quieter, first version of his monitor warning: the external monitor's overheating under the streaming load, a VRSA inspector is coming to check it (though not while Tatsuya's still connected), framed as a heads-up, not yet urgent.

Before they reach the war tent, a scout named Kesh intercepts them at a dead run, blood on his sleeve that isn't his own, speaking in the broken, third-person voice of a genuinely low-Wisdom goblin: the eastern patrol line has been hit, two Bloodhurst goblins already dead before anyone saw the attackers coming. Tatsuya's posture goes rigid at the news. The chapter ends on his private, unspoken fear that his location's already been found, unresolved. (No tent description or exact attacker count lands here anymore, that content now surfaces in Chapter 8 once Sevish presses Kesh for details.)
