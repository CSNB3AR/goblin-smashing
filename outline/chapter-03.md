# Chapter 3 — Into Bloodhurst (First Camp Arc)

**Status: Drafted.** See `chapters/chapter-03.md`. This beat sheet is now stale in a few particulars, the drafted chapter corrects the trigger for the fight (Tatsuya steps in defending a younger goblin, not himself hazed first), drops the "gobshit" line entirely (no forward-facing humor established yet), makes the gas+flare combo genuinely accidental rather than a discovered-and-tested combo, and resolves the World Quest activation as a keyword/philosophy trigger rather than a Fortune roll. See `chapters/chapter-03.md` and `codex/subplots/world-quest-call-of-the-bones-camp-arc.md` for the corrected, current version of events.

## Scene 1 — Login shock, quick recovery

Brief real panic at seeing [RACE SELECTED: GOBLIN] confirmed on-screen, but he doesn't rage-quit. HGO lets players check guides and account info without logging out, so he stays in and checks his options: character reset (confirms what's already established, no reset was ever offered on this drive, see `codex/objects/hgo-access-drive.md`), and notices his physical HGO drive itself now reads green, even though it was inserted by hand back at Sakura Arcade.

## Scene 2 — The World Quest calms him down

Before the panic fully sets in, he clocks that goblin World Quests are a real, rare opportunity, first-mover advantage on something the whole competitive scene will eventually chase (see `codex/lore/world-quests.md`). Crucially, this isn't abstract: World Quests mean real money, tying directly back to his established core motivation (Ohema's surgery, the Tabuchi debt, see `codex/characters/ohema-kazehaya.md`), the same math that got him to buy the drive in the first place. That's the actual tension underneath the panic, not just "this might be interesting," but "this could be the money." He decides to stay logged in and see what this actually looks like rather than logging straight back out over not getting his Elf.

## Scene 3 — Arrival at Bloodhurst

Spawns into Bloodhurst (see `codex/locations/bloodhurst.md`), the Thrash clan's camp within Lunarest Outskirts. First sight of the camp. Because he's an actual player-goblin and not scripted AI, the NPCs around him register something's off, a subtle wrongness in how he moves, setting an uneasy tone before anyone reacts openly.

## Scene 4 — The roast

Two goblin NPCs clock him moving strangely and accuse him of "going feral" (see `codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`), start hazing him. Tatsuya's established humor-as-deflection comes out on the page for the first time in-game: he roasts them back (something in the spirit of "you two smell like you've been digging in each other's gobshit"), other camp goblins laugh, the two harassers don't, and it escalates fast.

## Scene 5 — First combat, first kill

Tatsuya uses Goblin Gas as the igniter and Goblin Flare (normally an SOS/distress ability, repurposed here offensively) to set up a small explosion, killing both goblins. Played for dark comedy but landed with real weight, not treated as trivial. First ability showcase on the page, first level-up, first appearance of attribute-point allocation (2 points/level, per the confirmed formula, see `codex/lore/leveling-and-progression.md`). The kill also procs Gambler's Gambit, the goblin racial Fortune passive (see `codex/lore/deck-building-rarity-and-classes.md`), landing him his first real deck-building item, a Common-rarity goblin gadget belt (see `codex/objects/goblin-gadget-belt.md`), smoke and gas bombs, not a good weapon, but a real tool-based alternative to executing Goblin Gas by hand.

## Scene 6 — Sevish arrives

The commotion draws Sevish Thrash (chieftain, see `codex/characters/sevish-thrash.md`) out. Guards report Tatsuya caused it and is down two of their own. Sevish reacts with interest, not pure hostility, "so we have a fighter," and reveals the camp was already about to attempt their regular, doomed rally march, now they'll proceed with it since they have him. Camp geography lands here too: a defensible hillside/cliffside position with a single narrow path down, the reason they've survived repeated raids from a PK player faction hunting them for easy farm kills.

## Scene 7 — Feral lore, delivered diegetically

Through camp dialogue, not narration, the "feral" stigma gets real texture (see `codex/lore/goblin-clan-culture-and-feral-stigma.md`): an old, setting-wide prejudice against goblins and demi-humans generally, partially broken by Shiv historically, why goblins have any city access at all today, though reputation and Karma still run low for them by default. Ties into why the Thrash clan badly needs a unifying figure and why Myrrhwood's wider clans have fractured and turned on each other without one.

## Scene 8 — The World Quest activates

Distinguished explicitly from the standard failed NPC "goblin rally" event, the one every newbie's heard of, looped uselessly since launch because no real player-goblin has ever been present to trigger it for real. This time it activates because Tatsuya is actually here. Shiv gives the mission: infiltrate a restricted section of Lunarest (library/archive) for intel, real objective is helping the Thrash clan reunify Myrrhwood's fractured, warring clans under Shiv's legacy. Ties to Shiv's Warset (see `codex/objects/shivs-warset.md`), at least two pieces somewhere in Myrrhwood, the rest scattered across the other continents.

## Scene 9 — The three-day ask, and the time jump

Tatsuya asks for three days to think it over. Right as he does, he registers with a jolt that real-world time has moved far faster than he clocked, it's already around 9pm, a concrete, disorienting hit of the established in-game time-dilation mechanic (see `codex/lore/preserved-misc-mechanics.md`) landing on the page for the first time.

## Scene 10 — Taken into custody anyway

Because he killed two of their own, regardless of the mission offer, he's placed in custody as a precaution: a UI notification, [YOU ARE NOW A PRISONER OF THE THRASH CLAN], two guards escort him to a holding tent. The "chained up" beat, previously planned for a later chapter after a real-world return trip, now lands here.

## Scene 11 — Real world breaks back in, chapter ends

Leon calls out that the arcade's closing in about 30 minutes. Tatsuya asks if he can decide in a bit, gets told he has three days but does need to actually decide, and the chapter cuts there, unresolved.
