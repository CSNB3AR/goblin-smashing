# Series structure: Books One to Three (author-stated, latest)

Dictated by the author. Tags: [AUTHOR] stated, [PROPOSAL] mine. Compare `outline/book-one-structure-and-book-two-seeds.md`, which this extends and does not replace.

## The shape [AUTHOR]
- **Book One: the Kingdom of Lunarest.** The first big stretch of the story lives in and around Lunarest. Tatsuya has to level up, do quests, build money, and build a guild along the way, and he stays in the Kingdom of Lunarest until the party heads to the Azure forest (the Crownfall-side forest that leads toward the elves). His long goal in Book One is getting to Myrrhwood.
- **Book Two: Myrrhwood.** It ends up with him reaching Myrrhwood. The clan work and the political work happen here: he gathers the clans and builds an alliance (and partners with the Beast Kingdom).
- **Book Three: Sunmarch.** The alliance built in Book Two makes the push possible. Sunmarch can even work as an expansion-style arc: new raids, Orcs and Lizardmen clans, a Lizardmen head chieftain. By then it will not be unclaimed; other players and guilds will already be claiming it, because it is deep in the story.
- Each kingdom gets its own arc and storyline, the way each nation in Avatar: The Last Airbender did. The kingdoms do not have to open all at once; this is a choice for pacing and "not overwhelming" the reader. [AUTHOR, flexible: "everything is open" in-game, but the books release them one at a time.]

## What the story is [AUTHOR]
- A game inside a game. The main storyline is HGO itself (the world, the princes, the court, the guilds, the world quest). The subplot is the real world (money, family, VRSA, the arcade). Readers get attached to the game story: it is the cake. The real-world material is the icing; nobody attaches to what he does at home outside the people who are there.
- Rule for drafting: the princes, the court, the world quests and the politics must be excellent. They carry the attachment.
